=== DOOM RPG DNA ===

First-person pseudo-3D raycasting: corridors/rooms rendered as vertical wall stripes (Wolfenstein 3D principle).
Entirely turn-based: every step, rotation, attack, item use costs one turn; all enemies act once after.

Level design implications:
- 1-tile-wide hallway fills screen with walls on both sides — use deliberately for tension, open rooms for relief.
- Player sees ~5–8 tiles ahead before distance shading fades to near-black.
- Corners and T-junctions are inherently dramatic — player doesn't know what's around the bend.
- Room scale: 10×10 open room feels enormous; 3×5 room feels tight. Most rooms: 4×6 to 8×10 interior floor space.
- Asymmetry makes spaces feel organic; symmetry reads as deliberate (signals importance — use for boss rooms).

Doom RPG level features:
- Key card gating: colored key cards unlock doors, forcing exploration.
- Secrets: wall segments identical to 'x' but interactable — reveal hidden passages/item caches.
- Computer terminals: shop/lore/puzzle nodes at dead ends or room corners.
- Episode variety: UAC offices → maintenance corridors → demon zones → hellscapes.

=== ROGUELIKE STRUCTURE ===

- Levels procedurally generated each run (future). Hand-crafted templates used now.
- Permadeath: one life per run. Death resets everything.
- Resource scarcity: ammo, health, keys finite per floor. Every room could be the player's last.
- Floor escalation: deeper = more enemies, tighter corridors, less health.
- Safe rooms: one per floor — rest point, shop, lore terminal. Reward cautious exploration.

=== SPATIAL DESIGN PRINCIPLES (roguelike references) ===

Shattered Pixel Dungeon — rooms connected by corridors, clear entry/exit. Every room has purpose
  (combat, loot, puzzle, rest). Corridors are transition spaces, not play spaces.

DCSS — branching layouts; player chooses which path to explore. Dead ends contain rewards.
  Main path never obvious.

Enter the Gungeon — each room is self-contained encounter, cleared fully before moving on.
  Rooms designed around enemy placement, not just geometry.

Brogue — elegant, minimal. Rooms feel hand-sculpted. Pillars, alcoves, irregular shapes break monotony.

=== VISUAL VOCABULARY ===

Long straight corridors → dramatic vanishing-point perspective. Use for grand entrances or final approaches.
90° corners → sudden reveals; player cannot see around. Gate information, create tension.
Open rooms → walls feel distant, ceiling/floor dominate. Use 'v' tiles on room walls to break repetition.
Narrow corridors with 'c' tiles → industrial, cramped, utilitarian. Perfect for maintenance passages.
Terminal alcoves with 't' tiles → visual reward for exploration (terminal glows conceptually).
Distance shading → walls >~5 tiles away fade toward black. Put important things in well-lit (close) spaces;
  put secrets and rewards in darker far corners.
