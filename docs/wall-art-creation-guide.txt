WALL ART CREATION GUIDE — HOW toon3D MAKES ITS WALLS, DOORS, COLUMNS, FLOORS AND CEILINGS
==========================================================================================
PURPOSE: the permanent, single source of truth for HOW environment surfaces are created for this game:
         what a wall is, how a region's wall set is organised, how a wall is designed, sculpted, tiled,
         lit, animated, packed and verified, how often each kind of wall appears in a level, and every
         lesson already paid for (including every piece of owner feedback). Read it before creating,
         changing or reviewing ANY wall, door, column, floor or ceiling texture, and before changing how
         levels choose their walls.

Created 2026-10-22 from the session that prototyped generated walls with the enemy-art kit and agreed the
region-set model with the owner. The implementing series is .claude/agents/ideas/generated-wall-art.txt
(+ -order-1..4); every order of it references this document and updates it in the SAME commit as any
change to the method, the set structure, the placement rules or the engine contract. A section marked
[PLANNED: order N] describes something designed but not built yet; the order that ships it removes the marker.

Relationship to the enemy guide: walls are made with THE SAME KIT (docs/sprite-art-creation-guide.txt
sections 1-4: the non-negotiables, the tools, the rendering technique, the material library). This guide
does not repeat that technique; it covers everything that is different about environment surfaces.

Related: docs/sprite-art-creation-guide.txt (the kit), .claude/skills/enemy-art/SKILL.md (the tool),
         docs/art-direction.txt (the style bible - never contradicted here), docs/environment-tileset-system.txt
         (the tileset subsystem), docs/tile-symbols.txt (the level format), story/05-descent-structure.md.

1. NON-NEGOTIABLES
====================================================================================================
  E1  GENERATED IN CODE with the enemy-art kit (sprite guide N1, N3). No hand-painted pixel, no image-
      model output, no edit of an output PNG. Same input -> byte-identical output. New materials and
      glyphs are APPENDED to kit.py so every enemy sprite stays byte-identical.
  E2  ORIGINALITY. The old wall/door/column/floor textures (lab_wall_*.jpg, lab_door_*.jpg, wall.png,
      the WallRenderer / SurfaceMaterials Pixmap programs) are not reference material - never opened
      for inspiration, traced, recoloured or re-created. Designs come from the story and the room's purpose.
  E3  REGION SEPARATION. Every wall belongs to exactly one region's set. A floor is built only from its
      region's set. Nothing crosses regions - not a hall wall, not a room wall, not a door.
  E4  A BASE WALL HAS NO FOCAL POINT. Base walls (hall base and every room base) repeat along whole
      runs, so nothing on them may draw the eye: no object, no figure, no large glowing gland, no
      single big stain, no protruding machine. Anything the eye locks onto belongs in a spaced slot
      (LIGHT, MARKER, SERVICE, VENT, ACCENT) - section 8.3. (Owner feedback 2026-10-22: the Wound
      prototype's drill "looks ok by itself but when several walls like that are together in line,
      they do not look good with this repeating spike".)
  E5  NO FIGURES INSIDE WALLS. No human body, silhouette, curled print, face or carcass shape in any
      wall, door or surface. (Owner feedback 2026-10-22 on the cradle-bay prototype: concept acceptable,
      the look rejected, and never as a base wall. Its replacement is the soul condenser - section 16 C.)
      The story is told by machinery, material, light and marks, not by bodies on display.
  E6  SEMANTIC COLOURS SACRED (art-direction V5). No saturated red/green/blue/purple/yellow decoration,
      no red medical cross, no hazard-yellow stripes, SUIT_AMBER never. Keycard colours appear only on
      keycard doors (section 11.1).
  E7  READABILITY BEATS MOOD (V8). Walls are the backdrop: mid-value, never brighter than the enemies in
      front of them; emissive pixels small (<= ~6% of a face; doors <= ~12%, section 11.1).
  E8  THE 2D GAME IS UNTOUCHED. Art never adds or removes a tile symbol, never changes walkability, a
      rule, a balance number, a spawn or a supply slot. Which wall picture a tile shows is cosmetic.
  E9  OWNER APPROVAL GATE. No region set enters the game before the owner has seen it (section 14)
      and said yes. The answer is written into the idea file's DECISIONS.
  E10 NO TESTS (CLAUDE.md Testing Policy). Verified by regeneration, previews and run-game captures.

3. WHAT A WALL IS
====================================================================================================
  - One TILE FACE: 256x256 RGB, fully opaque. Row 0 = the ceiling line, row 255 = the floor line, so a
    wall is authored as seen standing in front of it. WallRenderer stretches it over the stripe from the
    floor to the ceiling; a face is roughly square on screen.
  - Texel density matches the enemies (a regular enemy canvas is 240 px per ~1 wall height), so a wall
    and the creature in front of it share one pixel scale.
  - The same face is drawn on all four sides of a wall tile; the engine darkens one axis (face shading)
    and grades/fogs by distance and mood. Author mid-values: the engine supplies darkness.
  - The kit's LIGHT (upper-left-front) is used unchanged. A directional light on a periodic height
    field shades periodically, so lighting never breaks tiling. The cyan RIM catches right-facing
    bevels - "lit by the machine" - and is welcome.


====================================================================================================
4. TILING: THE 3-TILE CANVAS
====================================================================================================
  Walls tile HORIZONTALLY (with themselves and with every other wall of their set). They never tile
  vertically. Floors and ceilings tile in both axes.

  The technique (walls_prototype.py `tiled()` + `bake()`):
    1. Render on a canvas 3 faces wide (768x256). Every shape is stamped three times: at x - 256, x, x + 256.
    2. Crop the middle 256 columns.
  Why the result is seamless: the smooth max, the per-shape grain (sampled in each shape's own frame,
  which moves with the copy), the cavity blur (np.roll wraps a 3-face canvas, which is periodic) and the
  Bayer dither (256 % 4 == 0) are all translation-invariant. Surfaces use the same idea on a 3x3 canvas.

  WALL FACES BAKE SMOOTH (wall-texture-grain-fix, lesson 15.24): bake_face calls kit.render(smooth=
  walls.SMOOTH_SAMPLES = 2) - no Bayer dither, no surface grain, no lonely-pixel cleanup, a continuous ramp
  (interpolated between its six steps), a soft specular edge, and 2x2 samples per texel box-filtered down so
  edges and diagonals are anti-aliased. Same shapes, light rig and ramps; none of the speckle. Floors and
  ceilings (bake_surface) keep the sprite bake. Sampling is per-face and still translation-invariant (a whole
  number of samples per texel), so tiling is unchanged.

  The backing: one full-face slab PER COPY, overlapping its neighbours by ~12 px (`backing()`), never one
  slab spanning the canvas (its grain would not repeat).

  CROSSING THE EDGE SAFELY (order 1, lesson 15.14): a shape that crosses the tile edge overlaps its own copy
  there. Build it with walls.grammar() (band(), rail(), backing() already do): a HARD union (k=0 - a smooth
  max of two equal heights raises the overlap by k/4) and a BRUSHED grain frame (grain varies with y only, so
  both copies carry the same grain where they overlap). Never carve or add across the edge: overlapping
  carves and adds SUM. A joint across the face = two bands with a gap. Surfaces use grammar2d() (a FLAT grain
  frame) because they tile in both axes. Every face of a set is rendered with the region's ONE seed, so the
  shared trim carries identical pixels on every face and two different walls meet cleanly.

  THE EDGE RULE: a shape may cross the tile edge ONLY if it belongs to the region's shared grammar - the
  trim lines (section 5) or a structure every wall of the set continues the same way (the hall's
  manifold, a rail). A FOCAL object (a drill, a gauge, a lamp, a sign) stays fully inside its own face,
  with >= 8 px of margin: its neighbour will be a DIFFERENT wall that does not continue it. (Lesson
  15.6: the prototype drill's collar crossed the edge and was cut off against the next wall.) A builder
  lists its focal shapes in Face(focal=...) and the bake WARNS when one comes within 8 px of the edge.


====================================================================================================
5. TRIM LINES: THE REGION'S GRAMMAR
====================================================================================================
  Each region declares TRIM LINES - the heights where its architecture changes (a cornice, a cable tray,
  a rail, a tile line, a kick plate, a drain). EVERY wall of the region's set honours them: a room wall
  or an accent changes what is BETWEEN the lines, never the lines themselves. That is what lets a LIGHT
  sit beside a BASE, or a MEDICAL wall meet a hall wall at a doorway, and read as one building.

  Region A (shipped, order 1): cornice 0-30, cable tray 24-33, panel field 40-196, bump rail 205, kick plate
  214-249 + floor strip 251-255 (the joint between them). outer_facility_walls.trim() builds them for every A face.
  Regions B-E: declared by the order that builds them, written into section 16 when shipped.
  Organic regions (D, E) keep at least a floor line and the torn-plating top edge (D) / lattice rhythm (E)
  as their grammar.

6.2 What each slot is
  BASE        the dominant wall of halls / of a room. Variants are one design with seeded differences
              (a dent, a different vent, a missing bolt, a scuff, a stencil number): invisible at 4 tiles,
              noticed up close. NO FOCAL POINT (E4).
  STRUCTURE   pilaster / rib / frame - the architecture's rhythm and the door frames.
  LIGHT       the region's light source (lamp, lit slot, gland, synapse), emissive, may animate.
  SERVICE     the building working: pipes, risers, valves, hose reels.
  VENT        grilles, hatches, extraction mouths.
  MARKER      signage, Organization stencils, quota boards - story detail you notice.
  BREACH      the NEXT region leaking through (A: an ichor seep at a seam; B: chitin pushing tiles off;
              C: a soul leak; D: an open wound; E: none - falls back to BASE).
  ACCENT      the room's one telling fixture (a dispenser, a gun locker, a porthole, a breaker panel, a
              terminal, a half-open shutter). Focal by design, so spaced by placement.

6.3 Room families (data: each RoomBlueprint / special generator names its family - never a switch)
  MEDICAL      medical_bay, the MED_BAY floor (MedBayGenerator)
  SECURITY     armory, command_center, the gate airlock (GateAirlockGenerator)
  CONTAINMENT  cryo_chamber, containment_block
  MACHINERY    power_plant, atmospheric_plant
  RESEARCH     server_room, research_lab
  STORAGE      salvage_bay, supply_cache
  SIGNATURE    stellar_observatory, gore_nest, the boss arena, the EVENT room (the region's special look)
  HALL         entrance, standard rooms, corridors, caverns, the start room, hand-made levels (region A)
  Same family, region's style: a MEDICAL room is recognisably medical in every region and always in that
  region's materials (A white livery, B surgical tile, C pattern infirmary, D overgrown, E camp panel).

6.4 The symbol table (21 wall symbols, none added or removed - a symbol names a SLOT; the region set names
    the picture; BASE variants are chosen by the renderer per face, so they need no symbols):
  HALL         x BASE   k STRUCTURE   S LIGHT   c SERVICE   ' VENT   h MARKER   G BREACH

7. HOW OFTEN EACH WALL APPEARS (THE PLACEMENT RULES)                [shipped: order 2]
====================================================================================================
The level picks walls by rule, on its own seeded RNG stream (GameMath.wallSetSeed - never the generator's),
so layout, spawns and supply never change (order 2 proved it: 200 seeds x 7 generators x depths 1-25 + 200
start rooms, walkable mask and every fixed symbol identical). ONE place does it: level/LevelFinisher, which
every generator's final grid goes through. Rates are LevelGenConstants.WALL_SET_*. Design every wall knowing
how often it will be seen:
  P1  REGION: the floor's region picks the set (World.applyFloorWallSet: the route plan's region for the depth,
      like the lighting mood; RegionAmbience.wallSetId); start room, dev runs and hand-made levels use set A.
  P2  ROOMS: a room's perimeter (the exposed walls whose open neighbour lies inside its interior) is its family's
      BASE; its ACCENT on 1-3 tiles (1 in a room narrower than 3), the wall facing the entrance first, never on
      a door frame. Each RoomBlueprint names its family (RoomBlueprint.wallGroup()); HALL rooms are not stamped.
  P3  SPECIAL FLOORS: MED_BAY -> MEDICAL, gate -> SECURITY, event + boss arena -> SIGNATURE for every wall no
      room claims (+ its accents); caverns -> HALL with BREACH density +WALL_SET_CAVERN_BREACH_BONUS; cavern
      chambers name their family like rooms.
  P4  RHYTHM: a RUN is a maximal straight line of rhythm walls (HALL, or a special floor's own group) sharing
      one open side; on a run >= 5 tiles every 4th tile is STRUCTURE (phase seeded per run). The walls flanking
      a door IN LINE with it are STRUCTURE (door frames), whoever owns them.
  P5  LIGHT: no run >= 7 tiles without a LIGHT (placed on the latest base tile before the stretch closes,
      preferring one flanked by base on both sides - a lamp between two panels); never two LIGHTs adjacent.
  P6  VARIETY: MARKER 1-3 per floor (near the arrival 'p', near the exit '>', then anywhere); SERVICE / VENT on
      6-10% of the remaining hall base in 1-3 tile clusters along a line.
  P7  BREACH: hall density = breachBase + floorIndexInRegion x breachStep (RegionWallSet; set A 0.00 + 0.03),
      in seeded clusters; 0 on a region's first floor, ~12% of hall base on its fifth; never on a door frame.
  P8  Only exposed walls (a non-wall 4-neighbour) are dressed; only a wall symbol replaces a wall symbol; the
      finisher first resets every wall to the HALL BASE 'x'.
  P9  SPACING: two FOCAL walls (LIGHT, MARKER, ACCENT) keep a Chebyshev distance > 2 (never adjacent, >= 2
      tiles between); the same focal wall > 4. P9 wins over P5 when nothing is allowed.
  MEASURED (order 2, 40 seeds per generator, exposed walls): rooms floors ~72% BASE (hall 46% + room bases
  26%), STRUCTURE 16-18% (P4's "every 4th" + door frames - more than the old "~10%" estimate), LIGHT ~4%,
  SERVICE + VENT ~4%, breach 0% -> ~6% of exposed walls from a region's first to its last floor; caverns ~80%
  BASE (few straight runs). A STRUCTURE face is therefore seen about as often as a BASE variant: design it as
  quiet architecture, never as a feature.
  Consequence for design: ~65-75% of what a player sees is BASE (in variants), so BASE carries the
  region's mood quietly; ~10% is STRUCTURE/LIGHT rhythm; everything focal is rare and spaced.


====================================================================================================
8. DESIGNING A WALL
====================================================================================================
8.1 THE BRIEF (write it in the order's idea file before code)
    Region + slot       where it lives and how often it is seen (section 7)
    Purpose             what this wall IS in the fiction (a ring corridor panel, an abattoir tile, a
                        refinery plate, wounded muscle, the mind's lattice)
    Trim lines          the region's, honoured
    Materials           from section 9 / the kit library
    Focal or not        BASE: none (E4). Otherwise: the ONE thing it shows
    Story detail        at least one somewhere in the set (a serial, a quota board, a scratched name plate)
    Distinctness        how it differs at 4 tiles from the hall base and from the same slot of other regions

8.2 BUILD ORDER (each step judged at 1x before the next)
    a. Trim lines and the backing.
    b. Big masses (panels, plates, tile field, fibre bundles, lattice) - in ONE layer where they should
       merge, separate layers only where something must read as IN FRONT (a pipe, a rail, a cage).
    c. Detail as add / carve: seams, rivets, grooves, cracks, veins, vents.
    d. Variants: seeded changes that do not move the eye.
    e. Stencils and story detail last (3x5 stencils are close-range only - sprite guide 12.7).
    f. Strip test (8.3), then contact sheet, then in-engine.

8.3 THE REPETITION RULES (the wallpaper problem)
    R-1 Judge every BASE in a strip of 6+ copies (and with its variants mixed). If you can count the
        tiles from a beat in the pattern, the base has a focal point - remove it or lower its contrast.
        A big gland, a vein crossing, a stain, a dent, a sign: each repeats as a beat.
        (Lesson 15.5: the Wound base without the drill still beat on its two big glands.)
    R-2 Contrast budget: on a BASE, the strongest local contrast is the trim lines and panel seams -
        those repeat on purpose (they are architecture). Everything else stays within two ramp steps.
    R-3 Glow on a BASE: none, or a thin line that runs continuously across tiles (a soul trickle in a
        channel, a seam) so it reads as a line, never as a spot.
    R-4 Focal walls are designed to be seen ALONE between base walls (strip: BASE BASE BASE X BASE BASE)
        and must still sit in the trim lines.
    R-5 Room BASE walls repeat around a whole room - the same rules as the hall BASE.

8.4 Room walls must read as their room at 4 tiles by SHAPE and MATERIAL (fixture silhouettes, panel
    division, surface), never by colour alone - check the room's strip beside the hall strip at 1x.

8.5 Symmetry: walls are often symmetric by construction (panels); break it with one off-centre detail
    on focal walls only. A base may be symmetric - it is architecture.

====================================================================================================
10. GLOW AND ANIMATION
====================================================================================================
  - Emissive pixels (emissive materials, and translucent ones where thick) are copied into the GLOW image
    at the same rect (binary alpha). The engine draws them over the graded wall with NORMAL blend,
    exempt from the mood grade (art-direction V6), so a lamp keeps its colour in every region.
  - Glow per face <= ~6% of its pixels (doors ~12%, 11.1); on BASE walls none or a continuous line only (R-3).
  - Animation is a 2-8 frame cycle of the GLOW layer only (the base face stays one image). Bake each
    frame by calling the builder at that frame's glow level; keep only its glow image. A frame level of 0
    ("off") is baked at walls.GLOW_FRAME_FLOOR = 0.06, just above kit.GLOW_DEAD, so the dark tube is IN that
    frame's glow image and covers the lit lamp of the base face (an empty frame would show the base's lamp). Each animated
    face is flagged in the manifest: "animate": "all" (every face of that sprite) or "some" (a seeded
    ~1 in 6 - a failing tube), and the engine offsets the phase per face so nothing pulses in sync.
  - Agreed animated walls: A LIGHT flicker (some), C LIGHT + condenser pulse, D LIGHT heartbeat + a
    containment sac, E LIGHT synapse pulse + the signature accent. Pulses stay under the kit's glow
    ceiling (~1.1).


====================================================================================================
11. DOORS, COLUMNS, FLOORS AND CEILINGS
====================================================================================================
11.1 DOORS - four per region (plain + red/yellow/blue keycard), 256x256 faces, region materials. The
     keycard colour appears ONLY on a lock panel and a frame stripe, emissive, in the exact keycard
     colours (RenderConstants.KEYCARD_AURA_*: red (255,46,41), yellow (255,219,46), blue (56,148,255) - the kit's
     key_red / key_yellow / key_blue, whose top three ramp steps ARE those values), so no mood shifts it; it must read at 6 tiles in the
     region's mood. The plain door must read as a door against its region's walls at 4 tiles by shape
     (frame, seam, porthole), never by colour. Door slide animation is the engine's - author a closed door.
     DOORS ARE NOT BACKDROP (owner feedback 2026-10-23, three passes). The face IS the door leaf, edge to
     edge - NO FRAME (the wall around it is the frame). The leaf is NEAR-WHITE brushed steel (kit material
     steel_lt - a value no wall uses) carrying its LOCKING MECHANISM in plain sight in dark gunmetal on
     dark mounting plates, and each kind has its own mechanism so plain / red / yellow / blue differ by
     shape, not only by ink (region A: a pneumatic latch - two pistons from a centre gear to edge bolts /
     six polished deadbolts on two rack-toothed linkage bars / a big exposed gear driving four corner lock
     arms / two hydraulic rams clamping a lock beam under a pressure gauge). Every KEYCARD door carries a
     small CARD TERMINAL (outer_facility_walls.card_terminal: housing, dark screen with text lines, card
     slot + guide light, status lamp, optional keypad) - the ink lives there and on a few status lamps.
     Not cartoonish: real hardware proportions, thin seams, a tread-plate kick band, no fat neon tubes.
     Door glow budget ~12% of the face.
11.2 COLUMNS - round and square per region, authored as 256x256 faces (the column path draws a stripe). A
     round column is built from tilted STAVES (slabs with tilt = the cylinder's slope at their centre): a
     smooth dome's slow gradient dithers into blotches (lesson 15.17).
11.3 FLOORS / CEILINGS - 64x64, opaque, tiling in both axes (3x3 canvas), no glow (the floor pipeline
     has none). Value bands (art-direction section 6): floors 0.10-0.24, ceilings 0.06-0.18 average
     luminance, so they sit below the walls. No period of 8 texels or less (moire at the far band).
     The image's top-left origin is converted once at load to the pipeline's layout (row 0 = south edge).

12. OUTPUT: ATLAS, MIPS, MANIFEST                                   [shipped: order 1]
====================================================================================================
  - One atlas per region, <region>.walls.png: RGB (no alpha), fully opaque, a power-of-two square
    <= 2048x2048 (region A: 2048). It holds every wall, door and column face (256) plus mips 128 and 64
    (2x2 box filter of the face). Every rect has 2 px of padding filled with its duplicated edge texels
    (Nearest at a column edge never bleeds); unused atlas area is black.
  - PACKING: a deterministic bottom-left skyline. Items go in this order: every face (each followed by its
    extra glow-frame slots, if animated) in manifest order, then every 128 mip, then every 64 mip. Rects are
    explicit in the manifest - the engine never re-derives them. Under a glow-frame slot the RGB atlas holds a
    copy of the face (it stays opaque).
  - One glow atlas, <region>.glow.png: RGBA, binary alpha, the same packing: a face's emissive pixels at
    its rect and at its mip rects (a mip texel glows when >= 2 of its 4 parents do; colour = their mean),
    and each extra glow frame at its own slot. A face without emissives leaves its slots transparent.
  - Manifest <region>.walls.json, version 1 (one face per line):
      { "version": 1, "region": "outer_facility", "size": 256, "mips": 3,
        "atlas": "outer_facility.walls.png", "glow": "outer_facility.glow.png", "atlasSize": [2048, 2048],
        "trim": {"cornice": [0,30], "cableTray": [24,33], "panelField": [40,196], "bumpRail": 205,
                 "kickPlate": [214,255]},
        "breach": {"base": 0.0, "step": 0.03},
        "walls":   [ {"id": "wall_a_hall_base_0", "group": "HALL", "slot": "BASE", "variant": 0,
                      "rect": [x,y], "mipRects": [[x,y],[x,y]], "glowRect": [x,y] | null,
                      "glowFrames": [{"rect": [x,y], "duration": 0.4}, ...] | null,
                      "animate": "all" | "some" | null }, ... ],
        "doors":   [ {"id": "door_a_plain" | "door_a_red" | "door_a_yellow" | "door_a_blue", "rect", "mipRects",
                      "glowRect", "glowFrames", "animate"} ],
        "columns": [ {"id": "column_a_round" | "column_a_square", ...the door fields} ],
        "surfaces": {"floor": "outer_facility.floor.png", "ceiling": "outer_facility.ceiling.png"} }
    rect / mipRects / glowRect are TOP-LEFT pixel corners in the image (row 0 = top; sizes are size >> mip).
    variant is 0 for non-BASE slots. glowFrames[0] is the face's own glowRect; glowFrames carry no mips.
    The order of "walls" is HALL (BASE 0-2, STRUCTURE, LIGHT, SERVICE, VENT, MARKER, BREACH), then per family
    MEDICAL, SECURITY, CONTAINMENT, MACHINERY, RESEARCH, STORAGE, SIGNATURE (BASE 0, BASE 1, ACCENT).
  - Ids are STABLE (wall_<region letter>_<group>_<slot>[_<variant>], door_<letter>_<kind>,
    column_<letter>_<kind>) and never renamed once shipped.
  - Surfaces: <region>.floor.png and .ceiling.png, 64x64 RGB, no mips (the floor pipeline builds its own).
    NOT USED BY THE ENGINE (2026-10-23, owner request): the floor and ceiling were reverted to the procedural
    materials of render/SurfaceMaterials (docs/art-direction.txt section 6); the PNGs are still baked.
  - Deterministic: the same script writes byte-identical files (verify with a second bake and cmp).

13. ENGINE CONTRACT                                                 [shipped: order 2]
====================================================================================================
  - CHOOSING (headless): tileset/WallGroup + WallSlot (per-constant data: id tokens, variant counts, focal flag,
    the R3 fallback ACCENT/BREACH -> BASE, others -> HALL BASE), WallSlotTable, RegionWallSet (ids DERIVED from
    the stable naming of section 12 - wall_<letter>_<group>_<slot>[_<variant>], door_<letter>_<kind>,
    column_<letter>_<kind>) + RegionWallSetRegistry, RegionWallSets.bootstrap() (THE CATALOG: set A real, B-E
    rows that ALIAS it until orders 3-4 - an alias is one row), RegionPaletteBuilder (every wall symbol ->
    its slot's variant-0 face, 'P' -> the set's first column, everything else copied). LevelPalette.wallSetId()
    names the REAL set. The route region names its set (RegionAmbience.wallSetId); startup fails loud on an
    unregistered id.
  - DRAWING: render/tilesetgfx/RegionWallAtlas.loadForFloor(palette.wallSetId()) parses the manifest into dense
    face arrays and loads the two atlases (Nearest); World builds it with the floor and disposes it with the
    floor (one region resident). It cross-checks every id the set names; a manifest that fails, or lacks an
    id, falls back to set A, and if even A fails the walls draw flat - never a crash (R7).
  - WallRenderer.setRegionWallAtlas(atlas, floorSeed) resolves each char to face indices ONCE per level; the
    worker threads read plain int arrays. Per column: BASE variant = GameMath.wallVariantIndex(column, row,
    side, floorSeed, variants) with side 0 W / 1 E / 2 S / 3 N (a corner seen from two halls may show two
    variants); mip = GameMath.wallMipLevel(stripe height, 256, WALL_MIP_BIAS 1.0, 2); srcX = face origin at
    that mip + texel column, srcY = the face's top row + the clipped row (the Y-up rule unchanged). Doors and
    columns use the same atlas; the door slide and keycard logic are untouched. The floor's column picture is
    picked per FLOOR from the set's two columns (one architecture per floor).
  - BASE pass: unblended (the atlas is RGB, R16), equal neighbouring slices merged (R18; the key srcX/srcY/
    height implies the mip), ONE texture bind. Graded at environment strength 1.0 and fogged by depth.
  - GLOW pass (after the muzzle-light pass): the glow atlas at the same rect/mip, NORMAL blend, colour white x
    alpha - exempt from the grade, the red-alert dampen and the muzzle light; alpha = GameMath.
    wallGlowDistanceAlpha (1.0 to 10 tiles, 0.6 at 20 and beyond) x the floor's light multiplier (BLACKOUT dims
    glow by its factor only). A column with a door panel shows only the panel's glow. ONE bind.
  - ANIMATION: at mip 0 an animated face picks its glow frame from the world clock (GameMath.wallGlowFrameIndex)
    with a per-face phase bucket hashed from (tile, side, seed); "animate": "some" runs on a seeded 1 face in
    WALL_GLOW_SOME_ANIMATE_ONE_IN (6), the rest hold frame 0; past mip 0 the static glow mips are drawn.
  - SURFACES: render/SurfaceMaterials registers "<set>.floor" / "<set>.ceiling" for every set id (aliases load
    the borrowed set), read once from the PNG and flipped to the generator layout (row 0 = south); each lighting
    mood binds its region's set surfaces. FloorCeilingRenderer is unchanged.
  - Constants: RenderConstants WALL_* (face size, max mip, mip bias, glow fade, some-animate, hash sides).

15. LESSONS ALREADY PAID FOR
====================================================================================================
  15.1 (2026-10-22, in-engine prototypes) The kit's style reads at wall scale and under every mood; the
       Wound and the Reliquary were the strongest. Soul light and ichor read through the grade.
  15.2 A focal picture on every tile becomes WALLPAPER: the cradle bays and the drill repeated along
       every run. -> E4, P9, the slot model.
  15.3 Owner: the drill wall "looks ok by itself" but repeated in a line looks bad. -> the drill is a
       signature ACCENT only (region D), spaced by P9.
  15.4 Owner: the walls with a person inside (the cradle-bay prototype) - concept acceptable, look
       rejected, never a base wall; replaced by the soul condenser. -> E5, no figures anywhere.
  15.5 Removing the object is not enough: the Wound base without the drill still BEAT on its two big
       glands and a vein crossing. Glands belong to the LIGHT slot; the base keeps fibres and thin,
       low-contrast veins. -> R-1, R-2.
  15.6 The prototype drill's collar crossed the tile edge and was cut off against the next (different)
       wall. -> the EDGE RULE (section 4).
  15.7 Tissue: separate lobes read as sausages; parallel full-height bundles read as planks unless
       constricted and varied in width; glands must be op="add" or the muscle's height buries them.
  15.8 The ring-panel livery read slightly too bright and flat at range: one ramp step darker, grime at
       the panel foot (W7 / E7).
  15.9 The Core lattice read busy at range: fewer, thicker struts, bigger hollows.
  15.10 A hook on every abattoir tile repeats like the drill: one base variant at most, the rest in the
       signature.
  15.11 The old floor/ceiling materials (flat grids, tread plates) clashed with sculpted walls -> floors
       and ceilings are part of every region set.
  15.12 Detailed 256-texel walls alias at range with Nearest sampling -> mips + distance LOD.
  15.13 Rotating in the start room reverses the view (it is a large open room) - capture walls on a
       generated floor reached through the route map (run-game: seed, onDescentRequested, engage; answer
       the briefing exchange and LEAVE IT on the relic offer first).


  15.14 (order 1) Shapes crossing the tile edge overlap their own copy. A smooth max of two equal heights
       raises the overlap by k/4, a grain frame that moves with the copy blends two different grains there,
       and carves/adds sum: all three left small repeating marks every 256 px on the first bake (the rail, the
       kick-plate joint). -> walls.grammar(): k=0 + a brushed frame; joints are gaps between bands.
  15.15 A fixture in the SAME layer as a panel of higher plateau + prio is swallowed: the union paints the
       panel's material (the first doors lost their porthole, lock panel and frame stripe; the first marker
       board showed the panel seams through it). Give fixtures their own layer; paint ON a surface with "add".
  15.16 E5 is about silhouettes, not subjects: a scarf built from round hanging chains read as a limb, two
       equal tails read as trousers. The shipped scarf is a FLAT folded band, one long fringed tail, one short
       fold. Judge every cloth / hanging shape at 1x for a body read.
  15.17 A smooth cylinder (a 128-radius dome) dithers into blotches across its slow gradient; tilted staves
       shade in clean bands (the round column).
  15.18 Surfaces are darker than they look on a sheet: flat darkmetal/gunmetal plates measured 0.20-0.26 mean
       luminance; the ceiling band (<= 0.18) needed concrete_dk. Measure (walls.luminance), never guess.
  15.19 Panel gaps must be EVEN across the tile edge (4..124 / 132..252: 8 px both ways) or the BASE beats as
       pairs of panels.
  15.20 (order 2) Removing a generator's wall pass is not "delete the writes": its RANDOM DRAWS feed every later
       prop, spawn and pickup, and a few later passes drew only when a wall was still plain 'x'. The passes keep
       every draw and write nothing; level/RetiredWallDressing remembers which walls they WOULD have repainted
       so the 'x'-conditioned draws see what they saw. Prove R1 with a full sweep, never a spot check.
       (level-generation-overhaul rebuilt every layout, so the retired passes and RetiredWallDressing are
       deleted; the lesson stands for any future pass removed WITHOUT a rebuild.)
  15.21 A LIGHT placed on "the latest tile before the 7-stretch closes" kept landing beside a STRUCTURE rib
       (k S pairs along every hall). Preferring a tile flanked by base on both sides centres the lamp between
       ribs (k x S x k) and reads as architecture.
  15.22 "animate": "some" is a seeded 1 in 6 PER FACE (side included): ten lamps in a row can all hold still.
       To capture a flicker, find an animating face by its hash (run-game `lookat:S:INDEX:BACK:SIDE`), do not
       sample at random.
  15.23 P4's "every 4th tile" + door frames makes STRUCTURE ~17% of exposed walls - as common as a BASE
       variant. A structure face must stay quiet; it is architecture, not a feature.
  15.24 The sprite bake (Bayer dither, value-noise grain, six hard ramp steps, aliased edges) looked crafted
       in a contact sheet and read as NOISE on a wall in play: the 3D view resamples the face, so the dither
       and grain shimmer into speckle and every diagonal stair-steps. Walls now bake smooth (section 4).
       Never reintroduce dither or grain on a wall face to "add texture" - texture comes from shapes.
  15.25 Doors took three passes (2026-10-23). 1: built from the walls' own grammar (livery leaf, gunmetal
       frame, one ink stripe), one silhouette for all four - a door was hard to find. 2: dark armour, a lit
       frame, chunky stacked plates, an iris and chevrons - it stood out but looked like a cartoon. 3
       (shipped): no frame, a near-white steel leaf with real locking hardware and a keycard terminal. A
       door is a landmark: its own value, readable machinery, a different mechanism per kind (11.1).
