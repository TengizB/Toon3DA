# 00 — Premise

## Setting

**Erebus** is not a planet with a facility on it. Erebus is a **conscious being the
size of a planet**, and the facility — the **Deepworks** — is a wound bored into it.
Over generations the Organization sank shafts, reactors, harvesting galleries, and
habitation rings straight down through the being's body toward the one thing worth
everything: its core, where its mind runs deepest.

For a long time no one knew it was alive. The surveys called the core a "high-yield
energy seam." The being's slow, sleeping patience read as geology. The Deepworks
drilled, extracted, and grew fat, and the planet endured it the way a sleeper endures
a splinter — until the drilling reached tissue that could feel, and the splinter
became a knife, and the being began, at last, to defend itself.

That defense is the game.

## Why you are always going down

The only direction is down, because the story is a descent toward a mind. Each region
deeper is a stratum closer to the being's core, and closer to the truth the
Organization buried with you. The route console frames it as a mission ("reach the
reactor, purge the contaminant"). The planet frames it as a return ("you're coming
back to me"). Both are true.

## The system-as-story table

Every mechanic already in the game carries narrative weight. This is the spine — the
bestiary and the respawn *are* the plot.

| System | Surface meaning (what the Organization tells you) | True meaning (what you learn) |
|---|---|---|
| The descent | Fight down to the reactor and purge the contaminant | You are walking back to the being you died to free |
| **Undead** enemies | Infected crew, hostile | Facility staff killed by the catastrophe and the Deepworks' conditions — victims, some dragged back into motion by raw soul bleeding through the galleries |
| **Insect** enemies | Escaped bio-specimens | Life bred and butchered *from the planet's own tissue* by the harvest program; exploitation made flesh |
| **Machine** enemies | Rogue defense drones | The Organization's own security and failed Cradle-prints — the *oppressor's* immune system, hostile to intruders and to the planet's healing alike |
| **Demon** enemies | Deep infernal corruption | The planet's immune response — its pain and rage given form. Antibodies. Not evil |
| **Golem** enemies | Guardian constructs | The being's own crust and mantle mobilized — its body rising to wall off and crush the infection: you |
| Death + respawn | Standard clone reprint from checkpoint | The Cradle prints a new body from your stored pattern, fuelled by the harvested **soul of the planet**. You keep coming back only because the being is kept enslaved and bled to power the printers |
| The opening card | "Cloning from last checkpoint" boot message | Literally true — and on the very first run it announces the death of your *original self*, moments before the game begins |

Two families (demon, golem) are the **planet defending itself**. Two families
(undead, insect) are the **human cost of exploiting it**. One family (machine) is the
**Organization's machinery**. The moral geometry is built into who you fight.

## The tone

Cosmic horror by way of restraint and dread, not gore. The horror is not that the
planet is a monster — it is that the planet is a *victim*, that you are its betrayer
wearing a stranger's mission, and that the thing you were sent to "save the surface"
from is the only reason you are alive at all. The scares are recognition, complicity,
and the slow collapse of a briefing you believed.
