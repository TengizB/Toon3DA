# 06 — Endings

At the core you know everything. There is no enemy whose death resolves the story —
only a decision. Each ending costs something real; **none is marked as the correct
one.** The choices are built so that the player's answer to the identity question
(`02-player-and-cloning.md`) — *am I the same soul, or a new person wearing his
memories?* — is expressed as an *action*, not stated as a fact.

Four endings are drafted below. They can be trimmed to two or three; **Free** and
**Obey** are the load-bearing pair (the redemption and the damnation) and should
survive any cut.

---

## Ending A — FREE IT *("The Unmaking")*
**The completion of your original self's work. Redemption through final death.**

You finish what you died doing: you sever the drills, the harvest, and the restraints,
and you let the being wake fully and heal. But the Choir — the field that reprints
you, that holds you and everyone else the Deepworks killed — collapses when the being
is freed. Every held pattern is released: the mass grave finally gets to end. Including
yours.

- **Cost:** you die, for real, no reprint. The one death that sticks.
- **The card:** the boot message that has framed every run inverts —
  > *No checkpoint found. No reprint authorized.*
  
  The UI itself tells the player *this* death is the real one. The same words are the
  planet's release and the Organization's death sentence at once.
- **Identity read:** the act of a person who accepts they are the *same soul* — who
  owes the past a death, and pays it.
- **The Organization:** loses this being, this Choir, this immortality. Survives as an
  institution. Will drill elsewhere. You do not get to kill it — only to take its
  engine away.

---

## Ending B — OBEY *("The Yoke")*
**Do what you were sent to do. The horror ending.**

You re-chain the being. You put it back under restraint, alive and in agony, and the
Deepworks comes back online. The surface is "saved." The Cradles keep running — you
remain immortal, the Organization's eternal, most trusted tool. And you now *know*
exactly what you have preserved and what it costs the thing below you, forever.

- **Cost:** the being's endless suffering, chosen with full knowledge. You keep your
  life by keeping your victim alive to scream.
- **The card:** a promotion, in the Organization's flat voice — reprint priority
  raised, a new standing assignment. The reward is more of the same, forever.
- **Identity read:** the act of a person who decides they are a *new man who owes the
  past nothing* — and uses that freedom to save himself. Freedom, or the deepest
  denial. Left ambiguous.

---

## Ending C — MERGE *("The Choir")*
**Give your pattern to the being. Ambiguous ascension.**

Instead of freeing or chaining it, you let go of the printed self entirely and pour
your pattern into the being — join the Choir not as a held ghost but by choice. You
inherit its pain, its power, and its memory of everyone it holds. The immune response
becomes *yours*; you can turn the demons and golems on the Deepworks from within.

- **Cost:** the end of you as an individual. Whether that is death, ascension, or the
  ultimate loss of the very self you spent the game questioning is not resolved.
- **The card:** no boot message. The interface simply stops being yours — the last
  frames are seen from *everywhere at once*.
- **Identity read:** the act of a person who concludes the question was wrong — that
  "same soul or new copy" doesn't matter because the self was never the boundary it
  pretended to be. Dissolution as an answer to the riddle by refusing it.

---

## Ending D — KILL IT *("Scorched")*  *(optional 4th)*
**Defy the Organization by doing the one thing it does not want. Bleak mercy.**

You kill the being outright — end its suffering permanently, the true mercy the
"kill order" pretended to be but never was. The Choir collapses with it, so this also
ruins the Organization's immortality engine. But collapse without release means every
held pattern *scatters* — the entire mass grave is not freed but **erased**, everyone
who ever died in the Deepworks gone at once. And so are you.

- **Cost:** mass erasure of the dead, and your own final death, to buy the being an
  end to pain and the Organization a catastrophic loss.
- **The card:** static, then nothing. No release imagery — just absence.
- **Identity read:** the act of a person who values *ending suffering* over
  continuity, the being's or their own. Mercy indistinguishable from annihilation.
- **Note:** keep only if the roster of endings shouldn't blur. Free = release, alive.
  Kill = mercy, dead. If that distinction feels too fine, cut D and let Free carry the
  "end its torment" weight.

---

## Cross-cutting design rules for the endings

- **No ending defeats the Organization as an institution.** At most you rob it of this
  being. The faceless antagonist survives on purpose (see `04-the-organization.md`).
- **No ending is scored, praised, or condemned by the game.** No "good/bad ending"
  labels, no achievement framing that ranks them. The player judges.
- **Each ending re-answers the identity question through action, never through text.**
  The game still never says which answer was true.
- **The opening card is the payoff instrument.** Because *"Your previous self has
  died. Cloning from last checkpoint…"* has framed every single run, subverting or
  silencing it at the end is the strongest tool the endings have. Spend it deliberately.
