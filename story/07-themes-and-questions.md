# 07 — Themes and Open Questions

## The philosophical spine

The game asks one question in three costumes and refuses to answer any of them:

### 1. Is a pattern a person?
You are printed from a pattern. You die and the body is gone forever, but the pattern
comes back. Are *you* the pattern (continuous, one soul, guilty) or the latest copy of
it (new, free, owing nothing)? The Organization says copy. The planet says soul. The
game never rules. Every ending is the player *acting on* an answer without the game
confirming it (see `06-endings.md`).

### 2. What do we owe for what a previous self did?
Your original self caused the Catastrophe. You did not choose it, do not remember it,
and were built without it on purpose. Are you responsible? If you are the same soul,
yes. If you are a new person, no. The block makes this literal: for most of the game
you *cannot* be guilty because you cannot remember — and then the memory returns, and
the question is whether returning memory also returns guilt.

### 3. Is there a clean side to stand on?
No. This is the "grey" the whole design is built to earn:
- The facility staff (undead) were **victims** — and were **torturers** of the being.
- The being (demons, golems) is a **victim defending itself** — and it **killed your
  original self**, who was trying to save it, and kills you every run.
- **You** freed it out of mercy — and your mercy **caused the Catastrophe** that killed
  thousands and doomed the being to a worse captivity.
- The **Organization** is the one clean antagonist — and it is *faceless*, an
  institution you cannot satisfyingly defeat, that will simply reprint the next
  operator after you.

Everyone is both wronged and wrong. The player is meant to arrive at the core unable
to point at a villain who is *only* a villain, except an institution with no face.

## How the grey is *staged*, not stated

The design never tells the player "it's complicated." It lets them **believe the clean
version first** (Region 1: clear hero, clear contaminant) and then removes one support
per stratum until the clean version collapses under its own recovered facts (see
`05-descent-structure.md`). The player builds the moral certainty themselves, so its
collapse costs them something. Ambiguity delivered as *loss of a belief you held* lands;
ambiguity announced up front does not.

## The deliberately unanswered questions

Keep these open. They are features. Do not resolve them in any text, log, or ending.

1. **Same soul or new copy?** — never answered.
2. **Was the voice trustworthy the whole time, or did it also use you?** — it is the
   victim, and it does return your true memory; but whether a mind that recognized its
   betrayer and stayed silent for strata was *guiding* you or *grooming* you is left
   for the player to feel out. Lean victim, never confirm saint.
3. **Was freeing it ever right?** — your original self's mercy caused mass death. The
   game does not tell you whether the liberation attempt was noble or catastrophic
   hubris. Both readings survive to the end.
4. **Does the original's name matter?** — the name is the last thing the block hides.
   Whether you ever fully recover it, and whether recovering it makes you *more* the
   same person, is left dangling on purpose.

## What the story is finally "about"

A liberator, killed by the thing he freed, rebuilt without his conscience by the
jailers, and sent back to finish the jailing — who has to excavate his own crime to
understand his own mission, and then decide, knowing everything, whether the self who
did the freeing and the self who could undo it are even the same person. It is about
guilt without memory, mercy that becomes catastrophe, and immortality bought with
someone else's endless pain — and it never lets the player off the hook with a clean
answer.
