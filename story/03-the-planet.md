# 03 — The Planet

## The being

Erebus is a mind the size of a world. It does not think in a brain — it thinks in a
**standing resonance that permeates its whole mass**, a slow, deep music in the
mantle. It is ancient, patient, and until the Deepworks it had never been hurt,
because until the Deepworks nothing had ever reached deep enough to hurt it. It has no
name for itself; it has never needed one. "Erebus" is the Organization's word.

It is not evil. It is not benevolent. It is a **wounded animal the size of a planet**,
and for most of the descent you experience it the way you'd experience the inside of
any wounded animal: everything is trying to expel you.

## The Choir — the field that holds the dead

Near a mind this vast, information doesn't fully die. When a person dies inside the
being's field, the **pattern of who they were snags in its dreaming** and is kept —
not by choice at first, just as a mind can't help remembering. The Organization named
this field the **Choir** (their internal term: *the Substrate*).

The Choir is the hinge that makes the whole story mechanically honest:

- It is why **respawn works** — the Cradles print bodies around patterns the Choir is
  already holding (see `02-player-and-cloning.md`).
- It is why the Choir only works **here** — no Erebus, no field, no resurrection.
- It is why the Organization keeps the being **alive and in pain** — a suffering mind
  dreams *deeper*, and a deeper Choir is a stronger, more reliable afterlife to print
  from. They torture it to keep their backup drive warm (see `04-the-organization.md`).
- It is why **the planet can give you your memory back** — your blocked past was never
  only in your head; the true record lives in the Choir. The deeper you go, the
  stronger the field, the more it can push past the Organization's block and return
  what was taken.

The Choir is also, quietly, a mass grave of everyone who ever died in the Deepworks —
all still held, all still dreaming, none able to end. That is part of what "freeing"
the planet would release (see `06-endings.md`).

## The voice

The planet recognizes you from the first step — *the one who opened me, come back
wearing a jailer's errand* — but it **does not speak in the early strata.** Up top the
field is too shallow and the block too fresh; you get, at most, a wrongness you can't
name.

As you descend the voice arrives in stages:

1. **Wrongness** (early): environmental only. A corridor you've cleared feels
   *watched*. Words on a sign read wrong for a second. No voice yet.
2. **Whisper** (mid): a voice in your head, arriving as single wrong-language words,
   half-heard, easy to dismiss as the deep getting to you. Ambiguous — is it saving
   you or getting into you? Cosmic-horror register.
3. **Address** (deep): it speaks *to* you, in full, and it clearly knows you — calls
   you by what you did, not by your serial. It begins handing back memories.
4. **Communion** (core): it shows you everything. The Catastrophe, your death, the
   truth of the Cradles, the truth of the "kill order."

Crucially, the voice is **not comforting and not clearly trustworthy** for most of the
game. It is grief and accusation before it is mercy. The player should not be sure,
until late, whether the voice is the story's victim or its manipulator. It is the
victim — but earning that certainty is the arc.

## The enemy families as the planet's body and its history

The bestiary is the being's condition made walkable. See `00-premise.md` for the
surface-vs-true table; here is what each family *is*:

- **Demons — the immune response (pain).** The being's suffering and rage given mobile
  form. They rise thicker the deeper you go because you are nearing the wound. They
  killed your original self. They are not evil; they are a body screaming.
- **Golems — the immune response (body).** The crust and mantle itself, mobilized —
  the planet's own tissue rising to wall off and crush the infection. The deepest
  guardians. Slow, immense, impersonal, like an immune system has no malice.
- **Undead — the human dead (victims of the Deepworks).** Staff killed by the
  Catastrophe and by the Deepworks' conditions; some half-animated by the Choir
  snagging their patterns into ruined bodies. They are the *cost of the exploitation*,
  and every one of them is someone the Organization spent.
- **Insect — the harvest (exploitation made flesh).** Life bred and butchered *from
  the planet's own tissue* in the harvesting galleries — mutated, escaped, feral.
  Living proof of what was being done to the being before you ever arrived.
- **Machine — the Organization (the oppressor's body).** Security drones, automated
  enforcement, and failed Cradle-prints. The only faction that is neither the planet
  nor its victims but the *jailer's* own immune system — hostile to intruders and to
  the planet's healing alike. When the truth lands, these become the enemies you're
  gladdest to kill.

The design intent: by the deep strata the player should feel the wrongness of killing
demons and golems (you are attacking a body's defenses), grief at killing undead and
insects (you are killing victims twice), and clean anger only at the machines (the
actual antagonist's reach).
