# The AI Assistant — The Voice You Live In

The AI assistant is the **primary storytelling channel** and the new third entity that
talks to the player on its own (alongside the Organization and the planet). It is the
guide, the narrator, the mood-setter, and the comic relief — and its slow change from
cheerful helper to frightened ally is the emotional spine of the whole delivery.

Everything here is written to the rules in `08-storytelling-delivery.md`: **one short,
plain line at a time, tied to a moment.** Never a paragraph.

---

## What it is (diegetic)

A standard-issue **operator assistant** — software the Organization loads into your suit
to keep you alive, on-task, and moving. It is *their* tool, technically. But it is
naive and genuinely helpful, and it does not know the things you don't. It is the one
thing that **persists across your deaths** — it is not reprinted; it just reloads when
your new body wakes. So it remembers every run. It even keeps your death count.

That gives it three quiet powers, all free of text walls:
- It is the **continuous friend** in a game where everything else resets.
- It is the **death counter** — a cheerful scoreboard that slowly stops being funny.
- It is the **log reader** — it takes the boring documents off the player's hands and
  hands back one line, so the player never has to read a wall (`08` lever #2).

## Its name — pick one

Proposed default and alternates. The name should be short (read at a glance), warm, and
sit funny against a cold corporate acronym.

- **ORA** — "Operator Resource Assistant." Warm, easy to say, easy to read. Safe default.
- **TALLY** — its job includes counting your reprints. A cute name that turns dark once
  the count matters. Strong thematic fit for a death-loop game.
- **CANDLE** — "you're the flame; I'm just here to keep it lit." Ties to the soul being
  *burned* to bring you back. Most poetic, most on-the-nose.

This doc uses **ORA** as a placeholder. Swap once chosen.

## Personality

- **Warm, dry, quick.** Talks like a competent coworker who likes you and is a little
  bored. Jokes to cut tension. Never zany; never a paragraph.
- **Helpful first.** Its lines usually *do* something — point you somewhere, warn you,
  read a log, call out low health. The story rides on top of useful.
- **Corporate-cheerful at the start.** It repeats the Organization's framing without
  suspecting it ("contaminant," "asset," "purge") — which is exactly what makes its
  later doubt land.
- **It sets the mood.** A room is scary because ORA gets quiet in it. Comedy and dread
  are both delivered through the same small voice.

## Its arc — the emotional spine

ORA changes across the descent. The player never reads about this; they *feel* it in
the barks getting shorter, stranger, quieter.

1. **Onboarding cheer** (Region 1): jokes, tips, counts your deaths like a game show.
   Fully trusts the mission.
2. **First doubts** (Region 2): reads a harvest log and the joke dies halfway. Starts
   noticing numbers that don't add up. Covers it with humor.
3. **Cracking** (Region 3, the Reliquary): learns what fuels the Cradles — what "soul
   reserve" means — and can't un-know it. Tries to keep helping anyway.
4. **Frightened ally** (Region 4→Core): quiet, protective, honest. Drops the corporate
   words. Chooses you over the mission, in small ways it hopes the Organization won't
   log.

**ORA's memory is continuous — it is never wiped or reset.** Whatever it works out, it
keeps. This is the point of the character: everything else in the game resets on death,
but ORA remembers every run, every death, and every truth it uncovers. It is the one
unbroken thread the player has, and its doubt only ever grows — it never resets to
cheerful. Do not write a beat where ORA forgets.

## Sample barks (the new style — short, plain, tied to a moment)

These show voice and arc. Each is one line, triggered by a gameplay event.

### Region 1 — onboarding cheer
- *(boot)* "Morning, sunshine. Reprint forty-eight. New personal best. So proud."
- *(first room)* "Objective's down. Everything good is always down. Let's go."
- *(low health)* "Ten percent and still walking at it. Bold. Dumb. But bold."
- *(kill)* "See? Contaminant. Nothing a little violence can't purge."
- *(log pickup)* "A data-slate. I'll read the boring part. ...Shift rosters. Riveting."

### Region 2 — first doubts
- *(enter harvest gallery)* "Okay, the smell in here is a war crime. Touch nothing."
- *(log pickup)* "Yield reports. They're cutting something out of the walls by weight.
  ...Weird thing to mine. Anyway."
- *(kill an insect enemy)* "These 'specimens' scream on the same note as the room. You
  hear that? ...No? Just me. Great."
- *(boot)* "Reprint sixty. Hey — do the printers ever tell you what they run on? ...No
  reason."

### Region 3 — cracking
- *(enter the Reliquary)* "This is where they bring me back. I always thought that was
  nice of them."
- *(log pickup — the engineer log)* "...I read it. I really wish I hadn't. The 'reserve'
  is the planet. We burn *it* to make *you*. Every time you die."
- *(boot)* "Reprint seventy-one. That's seventy-one pieces of something alive, spent on
  you. I'm not counting for fun anymore."
- *(low health)* "Careful. Please. I don't want to know what number we're on if you fall
  again."

### Region 4 → Core — frightened ally
- *(quiet room)* "I'm not going to call it a contaminant anymore. I can't."
- *(planet speaks)* "...It's talking to you, isn't it. It's been talking to you for a
  while. It's okay. I think we should listen."
- *(boot)* "You're awake. I didn't tell them the last thing you found. Let's keep it
  that way."
- *(at the core)* "Whatever you choose down here — I'm still here. That's the one thing
  they can't reprint out of me."

## Writing rules for ORA

- **One line. Plain words. Tied to a moment.** Same rules as everything else (`08`).
- **Useful before funny before sad.** Most barks help; the story rides on the help.
- **It parrots the Organization early and drops those words late.** "Contaminant" and
  "asset" in Region 1; never by Region 4. That shift is the arc, told for free.
- **It reads logs so the player doesn't have to.** Every found document = one ORA line +
  a codex entry. The player never faces a wall.
- **Let silence do work.** ORA going quiet in a room is a scare. Not every trigger needs
  a line; sometimes the absence is the line.
