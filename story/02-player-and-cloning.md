# 02 — The Player and the Cradle

## Who you are

You are an **operator** of the Deepworks — the Organization's word, stamped on you
along with a serial. You are a **reprint**: a body the Cradle grew around a pattern
lifted out of the planet's dreaming. You *know* this from the first moment; the game
never pretends otherwise. The opening card tells you your previous self is dead and a
new one is being printed. You have done this many times.

**The character is unnamed and ungendered on purpose.** The Organization refers to
you only by serial ("Operator"). The name of your original self is one of the last
things the block hides; whether it is ever fully recovered is left to the ending you
choose. Use *they/them* in all placeholder text until a name is deliberately assigned.

## What you remember, and what you don't

The reprint is **not a blank slate.** You keep:

- That you are an operator of the Deepworks and know the place.
- The Organization's briefing and standing orders.
- **Every previous run.** Each death and reprint is remembered. You are aware you are
  deep in a long series of yourself, and you carry the accumulated experience of them.

The Organization has **blocked** exactly one region of your pattern:

- The discovery that Erebus is conscious.
- The liberation attempt.
- The Catastrophe you caused.
- Your original self's death by the immune response.

That block is not a hole you can feel at the start. It reads as *settled fact* — you
simply "know" the official version (a containment accident, a contaminant to purge).
The descent is the process of the block failing.

## The Cradle — how respawn works in the fiction

1. You die. Permanently. That body is gone and is never coming back — this is real
   death, every time.
2. Your pattern does not scatter. Because you die inside the **Choir** (the planet's
   dreaming field — see `03-the-planet.md`), the being catches and holds it, updated
   with everything that just happened to you.
3. The Organization's **Cradle** reaches into the Choir, reads the held pattern up to
   the last stable **checkpoint**, and prints a fresh body around it.
4. You wake at the last checkpoint node, mission intact, one death heavier.

So respawn is **both** permadeath and a new person every time — exactly the
double-truth the design wants. The body is mortal and final. The pattern is
continuous. Whether *you* are the pattern or just the latest copy of it is the
question the game refuses to answer.

## The identity question (deliberately unresolved)

The game never tells you whether the reprint is *you* or a stranger wearing your
memories. Instead, three parties **assume** three different answers, and you live
inside the disagreement:

- **The Organization** treats you as **fungible** — an asset, a serial, a print run.
  Their answer: you are a copy. Interchangeable. Not a person.
- **The planet** treats you as **singular** — *the one who opened me*, *the one who
  died*, one soul it has been holding all along. Its answer: you are continuous. One
  person. And therefore guilty, and therefore grievable.
- **You** get to decide which you believe — and the endings let you *act* on that
  belief (a person who owes the past nothing, vs. the same soul who must answer for
  it). Neither is confirmed correct.

This is the philosophical engine. Keep it a question. Never resolve it in text.

## Continuity of runs as a story beat

Because you remember every run, the *repetition itself* becomes narrative. Early runs:
the voice is absent, the world is hostile, the briefing holds. Later runs: the voice
returns memories the last run didn't have, contradictions pile up, and the same
corridors you've cleared a dozen times start showing you things that were always
there. The player's growing mastery of the descent mirrors the character's growing
recovery of the truth. Dying is not just failing — it is how the pattern deepens and
the planet gets another chance to reach you.
