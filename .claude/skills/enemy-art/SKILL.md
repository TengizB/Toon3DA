# Enemy art kit — enemy textures generated in code

Every enemy in toon3D is being rebuilt as a sprite **generated by a script** - sculpted, animated and
reactive - never painted by hand and never made by an image model. The series that does it is
`.claude/agents/ideas/generated-enemy-art.txt` (+ `-order-1..9`). This skill is the tool.

**THE METHOD lives in `docs/sprite-art-creation-guide.txt`** - the technique, materials, how to design
a creature, animation, reactions, effects, the engine contract, verification and every lesson learnt.
Read it first; this page is only the quick reference. Update the guide whenever the kit changes.

It is a **dev tool**, like `run-game`: Python 3 + numpy + Pillow (both are pre-installed in the cloud
container), outside the Gradle build, never shipped. The game ships only the PNGs it writes.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `kit.py` | The renderer (shapes → per-layer height fields → normals → shading → palette → outline → cleanup) plus the animation layer: `Xf` transforms, `Rig`/`Pose`/`Figure`, `Clip`, and the bake (`write_creature`: atlas, glow atlas, manifest, previews). |
| `<name>_rig.py` | One per creature: its rig function, its pose defaults and its clips (`warden_rig.py`, `harvest_tick_rig.py`, `iron_stalker_rig.py`; order 5: `plague_hulk_rig.py`, `eye_tyrant_rig.py`, `gore_biter_rig.py`, `mire_wraith_rig.py`, and `sump_wretch_rig.py` - the first Mire Wraith, kept for its own enemy). |
| `bestiary.py` | The `CREATURES` table of (rig, clips) + the command line. A new creature = one `_rig.py` + one row. |
| `sheet.py` | Contact sheet of a folder of sprites, nearest-neighbour upscaled, on the game's near-black. |
| `effects.py` | The EFFECT sets (generated-enemy-art order 3): per family (`machine`, `insect`, `aberration` - order 5) + the shared `status` set; a creatures row may override a family burst for one creature (`bursts={"RESIST": "RESIST_PLATE"}`, order 4) — particle sprites sculpted by the kit + the burst recipes and per-creature emit points → `<set>.fx.png` + `<set>.fx.json`. Guide section 9. |

Shipped per creature, in `assets/textures/enemies/generated/`: `<name>.atlas.png` (every frame of every
clip, trimmed and packed, binary alpha, <= 2048x2048), `<name>.glow.png` (emissive pixels only, same
packing) and `<name>.clips.json` (the manifest: clips → frames with atlas rect, canvas offset, duration,
loop mode, anchors). Schema: guide section 7.7. Canvas 160x240, ground line 232.

```bash
cd .claude/skills/enemy-art
python3 bestiary.py ../../../assets/textures/enemies/generated            # ship: atlases + manifests (~50 s)
python3 bestiary.py /tmp/out --preview /tmp/out/preview warden            # + one GIF per clip + strip sheet
python3 bestiary.py /tmp/out --stills [--halo] warden                     # rest pose only, for contact sheets
python3 sheet.py /tmp/out /tmp/out/sheet.png 3
python3 effects.py ../../../assets/textures/enemies/generated [--preview /tmp/fx]   # ship: effect atlases + manifests (<1 s)
```
Output is deterministic (every noise source is seeded, the grain hash is integer-only): the same script
writes byte-identical files. Previews go to the owner and are never committed.

## The rules every sprite obeys (the style lock)

1. **Originality.** Never open, trace, recolour or "improve" an existing enemy texture in
   `assets/textures/enemies/` (outside `generated/`). They were made by image models, they do not fit
   the game, and they are not reference material — not for shape, not for palette, not for pose. A sprite
   is designed from: the family's place in `story/00-premise.md`, the archetype's `role()` and
   `tacticalVerb()`, and ORA's bestiary line in `assets/story/story-strings.properties`.
2. **Canvas.** Regular enemies 160x240 (2:3, the aspect the renderer already draws), feet on row ~232,
   centred on x = 80. Bosses 240x360. A floating enemy whose type the renderer does NOT hover bakes the
   hover into the canvas (empty rows under it, like the Warden); a type the renderer hovers
   (`EYE_TYRANT`) stands on the canvas floor (its death falls from the hover - a runtime row).
3. **One light rig, never per sprite.** Key light upper-left-front, FACILITY_CYAN rim from the right
   ("lit by the machine"), wrap shading, cavity occlusion, layer cast occlusion, Blinn specular per
   material. Changing `LIGHT`/`RIM` changes every enemy — that is the point.
4. **Palette.** Only `MATERIALS` ramps (6 steps, hue-shifted: cool shadows, warm lights). Add a
   material rather than tinting a sprite by hand. Semantic colours stay sacred (art-direction V5):
   no pure red / green / blue / purple / yellow as decoration. Flesh (order 5: `flesh`, `flesh_dk`, `sclera`,
   `membrane`, `pupil`) is rose-grey / violet-grey with a `wet` sheen - never red; cap glow near 1.1 (it tops out in white). Family glow inks: MACHINE = `cyan`
   (the Organization's), INSECT + ABERRATION = `ichor` (harvested planet tissue), GOLEM = `magma` (and
   elemental variants), others decided by their order.
5. **Silhouette first.** Readable as a black shape at two tiles; distinct from every other enemy of its
   family; the size reads the role (chaff small, soldier medium, bruiser heavy, mini-elite spiked, boss
   huge). The attack is visible on the body (a ranged enemy shows its emitter, a charger its battering front).
6. **One story detail.** Each sprite carries at least one thing that ties it to the premise — an
   Organization tag, a harvest port, a shackle, a serial stencil (`stencil()`).
7. **Binary alpha.** `EnemySpriteSheets` alpha-cuts at 0.50, so shipped sprites are rendered WITHOUT
   the `--halo` glow; glow belongs to the emissive layer (series order 7), never baked translucency.
8. **Owner approval.** No sprite is wired into the game before the owner has seen its contact sheet
   AND an in-engine screenshot and said yes. The approval is written into the order file's DECISIONS.

## How a sprite is built (kit.py API)

A sprite is a list of LAYERS, back to front. Inside a layer every shape is a signed height field and
shapes are merged with a smooth maximum (`k` = blend radius in px), so limbs grow out of bodies instead
of being glued on. Between layers there is a hard edge with occlusion: put a limb in its own layer when
it must read as IN FRONT.

| Call | Shape |
|---|---|
| `blob(cx, cy, rx, ry, mat, depth=, angle=)` | ellipsoid dome |
| `limb(x1, y1, x2, y2, r1, r2, mat)` | tapered capsule |
| `chain([(x, y), ...], r1, r2, mat)` | bent limb: capsules with a tapering radius |
| `slab(points, mat, bevel=, slope=, tilt=)` | bevelled plate (armour, stone) |
| `spike(x1, y1, x2, y2, r, mat, bend=, tip=, edge=, edge_side=)` | tapered curved blade / spike, optional raised edge material (order 4) |
| `riveted_plate(points, mat, rivet_mat=, spacing=, inset=, ...)` | armour plate + inset rivets; `op="add"` fits it on a body (order 4) |
| `mirror(shapes, axis)` | bilateral symmetry — design one half |
| `eyeball(cx, cy, r, look=, iris=, pupil=, slit=, lid_top=, lid_bottom=, lids=)` | wet sclera, emissive iris, pupil, glint, flesh-cap lids (order 5) |
| `teeth_row(x1, y1, x2, y2, count, length, r, side=, sag=, rake=, seed=)` | seeded teeth along a jaw line; `op="add"` on a face (order 5) |
| `stencil(img, "W-07", x, y, colour)` | 3x5 pixel serial stencil, painted after render |

Shape options: `glow=` (emissive/translucent light: 0 dead, 1 rest, > 1 flare); `op="union"` (default) / `"add"` (raised detail painted in its own material: veins,
rivets, eyes, blisters) / `"carve"` (grooves, panel seams); `prio` biases which material wins where
shapes overlap; `k` the blend radius (0 = hard union).

## Animating a creature (kit.py API — full reference: guide section 7)

| Call | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Xf.move / turn(angle, pivot) / scale(sx, sy, pivot) / reflect_x(axis)` | affine transforms; image space, y DOWN, positive angle = clockwise |
| `Xf.segment(rest_a, rest_b, new_a, new_b, stretch=)` | carry a part riding a limb segment (a clamp on a leg, a spur) |
| `a.then(b)`, `xf(shapes)`, `xf.pt(p)` | compose (child.then(parent)), transform shapes, transform a point |
| `with_glow(shapes, level)` | set the glow of emissive / translucent shapes (eyes die, blisters dim) |
| `Figure(layers, anchors, stencils)` | what a rig returns for one pose |
| `Rig(name, build, defaults, seed)` | `build(pose) -> Figure`; `defaults` names every pose parameter; rest pose = the still |
| `Clip(name, keys, duration, fps=12, mode=, events=)` | keys `[(t, {param: v}, easing)]`, one track per parameter; easing `linear/in/out/inout/hold`; mode `loop/once/hold`; `events={t: "emit"\|"impact"}` marks the strike frame in the manifest |
| `clip.variant(name, speed, keys, fps, **pinned)` | DORMANT / WOUNDED style variants of a clip |
| `jiggle(clip, param, duration, out_param=, freq=, damping=, gain=)` | keys for a damped spring LAGGING `param` (secondary motion: flesh, shrouds, tendrils; order 5) |
| `write_creature(rig, clips, out_dir, preview_dir)` | the bake: atlas + glow atlas + `clips.json` (+ GIFs, strip) |

Grain is sampled in each shape's OWN coordinates (its `frame`), so texture rides a moving part. The
standard clip set (guide 7.3): IDLE, DORMANT, ALERT, MOVE, WIND_UP, ATTACK (<= 0.30 s: bake at fps 20),
HURT_LIGHT, HURT_HEAVY, DEFEND, WOUNDED, DEATH (last frame = corpse), plus ability clips named after the
`SpecialAbility` (BUFF_SELF, AREA_STRIKE - the Iron Stalker). Anchors: ground, hit_centre, eye,
plus muzzle / mouth. Fix every canvas-edge warning by clamping the pose, never by growing the canvas.

## Lessons already paid for

- **Stacked rectangular slabs read as bricks.** The first golem (Mantle Sentinel) was built from flat
  polygon slabs over a glowing backing and the owner rejected it as the weakest. Mineral bodies need
  sculpted, irregular masses (blobs with grain, carved fractures), not rectangles.
- **Emissive intensity comes from the shape's OWN height**, not the layer's merged height — otherwise a
  glowing part sitting on a big body burns flat white. (Fixed in `kit.py`; do not regress it.)
- **Glow is easy to overdo.** Keep emissives to eyes, sacs, emitters and seams; a sprite that is mostly
  glow loses its form.
- **Bayer dither on flat areas turns into stipple.** Dither is 0.08; surface variety comes from
  material grain, not dither.
- **Leave air around the rest pose.** A still that touches the canvas edge clips the moment it moves;
  swing long parts across the body, not out past the edge.
- **Mock-ups lie.** Pasting sprites onto a screenshot ignores the mood grade, fog, contact shadow and
  name tags. Judge in the engine (below).

## Seeing a sprite in the real game

Use the `run-game` skill. Until series order 2 wires a creature's atlas to its `EnemyType`, preview a
pose by writing it with `--stills` and copying that PNG over the texture of an existing type of similar height (in the WORKING TREE only — restore
with `git checkout -- assets/textures/enemies/` afterwards, never commit the swap), and spawn that type
in front of the player with the capture driver's `spawn:TYPE:FORWARD:RIGHT` step (added to
`run-game/capture` by series order 2, together with `alert`, `damage`, `kill` and `burst`). Two tiles ahead is the standard judging distance; also capture
one non-default mood (`--mood amber_hot`).
