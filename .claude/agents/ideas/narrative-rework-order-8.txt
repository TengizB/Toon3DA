PURPOSE
"Now and then" made concrete. Sets the per-floor budget for how much the game may say, which
CHANNEL each kind of fact comes from, and the five region beat maps — the short list of things
that MUST land in each region for the story to be followable. It also fills the two structural
holes order-1 found: nothing explains why the descent repeats, and nothing explains what the
portals and the route map are for.

============================================================
A. FOUR CHANNELS, FOUR KINDS OF FACT
============================================================
Each source gives a DIFFERENT KIND of information. Overlap is the main cause of "the game
keeps telling me things and none of them stick."

  TERMINALS (SYSTEM/paperwork)  what the facility DID, in its own words. Dates, quotas,
                                tickets, sign-offs. Never interpretation.
  ORA (AI)                      what the paperwork MEANS, one sentence, and what to do about
                                the room you are in. Never a document read aloud.
  THE PLANET                    what it FEELS like from underneath. Never facts, never
                                instructions, never more than a fragment.
  THE ORGANIZATION              what you are SUPPOSED to believe. Never true detail, never
                                twice on a floor.

A fact that arrives from the wrong channel is a fault, not a duplicate: if ORA reads a whole
document, the terminals stop mattering; if the planet explains something, it stops being the
planet.

============================================================
B. THE PER-FLOOR BUDGET
============================================================
An ordinary floor may deliver:
    1  terminal take (already capped: STORY_LOG_TAKES_PER_FLOOR = 1)
    2  flavour barks maximum (floor arrival + one of kill/idle/backtrack/low-health)
    0  exchanges — an ordinary floor never blocks
  + any number of CRITICAL beats (teaching, region entry, gate orders, reveal logs), which do
    not count against the budget because a stuck or lost player costs more than a talkative one.

A REGION-GATE floor may add: the region-entry bark, the gate order, and ONE exchange.
A floor never carries two exchanges, and never an exchange within one floor of another.

The existing machinery already enforces most of this (per-trigger cooldowns in minutes,
probability rolls, combat-spike hold, no-repeat memory). What is missing is the CEILING: add
a per-floor delivered-count on BarkSystem for non-critical rows, reset on floor arrival. Two
non-critical lines per floor, then silence until the next floor. That single number is the
strongest anti-annoyance lever in the layer and it does not exist today.

============================================================
C. THE FIVE REGION BEAT MAPS
============================================================
Each region has THREE things that must land, on unskippable channels, plus its texture. If a
beat cannot be placed on region entry, a reveal log, or a control-flow moment the player
cannot route around, it is not a spine beat and must be demoted.

  R1 HABITATION RINGS — belief
     1. This is a workplace and everyone in it is gone.   (floor arrival + roster terminal)
     2. You have a job, a file, and a history you do not remember.  (Org briefing + ORA aside)
     3. Dying is normal here and it is not free.          (first real death card, order-2)
     texture: fluorescent, ordinary, nothing visibly wrong.

  R2 HARVESTING GALLERIES — first doubt
     1. This place was cutting something living, by weight, on a quota. (yield report, one-shot)
     2. The paperwork knew and had a form for it.          (tolerance sheet take)
     3. The Organization corrects your senses rather than the record. (gate order, order-7)
     texture: wet, warm, the same note under everything.

  R3 THE RELIQUARY — the inversion
     1. This is where bodies are printed — yours and ORA's. (region entry + cradle bank)
     2. What they are printed FROM is the thing being cut.  (cradle log, one-shot, CRITICAL)
     3. ORA cannot un-know it, and starts hiding things for you. (trust exchange)
     texture: clinical, bright, humming.

  R4 THE WOUND — grief
     1. The facility stops. The walls are a body.           (region entry)
     2. It has been awake and sedated for forty years.      (sedation schedule, one-shot)
     3. The order changes from "purge" to "restore the restraints". (gate order, order-7)
     texture: warm, red, a slow beat felt more than heard.

  R5 THE CORE — recognition
     1. You were here before. You opened it. You died doing it.  (memory beat)
     2. The "contaminant" is what was done to it, not what it is.
     3. The choice, and what each option costs.             (the ending exchange)
     texture: vast, intimate, almost tender.

============================================================
D. THE TWO STRUCTURAL HOLES
============================================================
HOLE 1 — WHY DOES THE DESCENT REPEAT?
A roguelike player accepts a reset without a reason. A story player asks. The answer must be
said out loud, once, on the second run, in ORA's voice, because it is also the game's premise:
    story.bark.loop.1=They print you at the last checkpoint, not where you died. So we start
                       at the top. Again.
    story.bark.loop.2=The works don't stay cleared either. Whatever's down there fills them
                       back in.
Two one-shot rows, run 2, RUN_START. This is the single missing sentence that makes a roguelike
structure diegetic, and it costs two lines.

HOLE 2 — WHAT IS THE MAP, AND WHY IS THERE A CHOICE?
Order-4 teaches the console's controls. This order gives it its MEANING, one line per region
band, delivered the first time the map opens in that band (one-shot, LORE):
    R1  Nobody built one way down. They built dozens, and kept digging.
    R2  The routes follow the cutting faces. That's why they branch.
    R3  Every road down here ends at the same racks.
    R4  These aren't corridors anymore. We're picking which part of it to walk through.
    R5  It's all one room down here. The map's just being polite.
Five lines, and the route map stops being a menu bolted onto a story.

============================================================
E. THE PLANET'S SCHEDULE (it is the easiest voice to overuse)
============================================================
  R1  silent. No rows. The absence is the design and it must be protected — the first time
      anything speaks that is not ORA or the company, it should be alarming.
  R2  single words, at deep-strata milestones only. Never a sentence. Never a response.
  R3  it addresses you. First full sentences, wrong order, present tense.
  R4  it speaks plainly and it grieves. This is where it gets its exchanges.
  R5  it and ORA are the only voices left.
Hard cap: the planet never delivers more than one line per floor, in any region, ever. Its
power is entirely in scarcity, and it is the one voice with no rate limit of its own today.

============================================================
F. STALENESS AND SILENCE
============================================================
  - A queued non-critical line older than its staleness window is DROPPED, not delivered late.
    (Already implemented; this order raises it to a rule: a line that arrives after its moment
    is worse than no line, because it teaches the player that the voice is not about the room.)
  - Silence is a legitimate result. Every repeatable moment must be able to produce nothing at
    all, and roughly a third of them should. ORA going quiet in a bad room is the cheapest
    horror effect in the game and it is currently unavailable because something always fires.
  - Add one deliberate silence: from Region 4 down, floor arrival has a 40% chance of NO line
    at all, and the pools shrink accordingly. Down there, her not saying anything is the beat.

DEFINITION OF DONE
- An ordinary floor never delivers more than one terminal take and two flavour lines.
- Every region's three spine beats are placed on unavoidable channels and proven by test.
- The player is told, once, why the run resets and what the map is.
- The planet cannot exceed one line per floor, and deep floors are sometimes silent on purpose.

============================================================
AS BUILT — deviations from the spec above
============================================================
1. THE LOOP LINES do not say "checkpoint". `StoryTerm.CHECKPOINT` is not free until run 3 and these
   land on run 2, so the mechanism is described in plain words instead (doctrine D2). They are two
   new `IntroBeat` rows (LOOP_RESTART / LOOP_REFILL) rather than free-standing catalog rows, which
   reuses the existing run-gated cold-open machinery instead of adding a second one; the ids are
   therefore `story.bark.intro.loop_restart` / `.loop_refill`, not `story.bark.loop.1/2`.
2. DELIBERATE SILENCE is a SECOND, independent roll on top of the existing
   STORY_BARK_FLOOR_ARRIVAL_CHANCE (0.40), not a replacement for it — so a deep floor speaks on
   arrival about a quarter of the time. The deep pools were NOT shrunk: order-6's standing rule is
   that a band below its depth goes silent for the rest of the save, which is a different (and
   worse) kind of silence than the one this section wants.
3. THE PLANET RE-BAND moved the two Galleries rows that ADDRESSED the player
   ("...you smell the same...", "...I know this weight...") into the Reliquary as
   `bark.strata.reliquary.5/.6`, where address is the point, and gave the Galleries two more single
   words. R2's rule as written ("single words, never a sentence, never a response") is what those two
   rows were breaking.
4. ONE EXTRA PROMOTION the beat maps forced: R4's beat 2 (the sedation schedule) was a repeatable
   pool row a player could finish the Wound without ever drawing. `bark.log.wound.4` is now a
   one-shot mandatory reveal, alongside the yield report and the cradle note, with
   `bark.log.wound.5` added to hold the repeatable band at four. That is one string id beyond the
   "~7" estimated above (nine in total).
5. THE BEAT-MAP TEST extends `StorySkimmerPathTest` (which already owns "the spine rides unskippable
   channels") rather than adding a test file, per the project testing policy. It also asserts the
   two carriers that are EXCHANGES rather than barks — a blocking prompt is the least avoidable
   channel there is, so R2.3, R3.3 and R5.1/5.3 qualify by construction.
