STATUS: NOT IMPLEMENTED
CATEGORY: Systems
TITLE: Tactical Combat Depth — make fights positioning puzzles, not damage races
---

Idea 4 of 6 in the balance series (see balance_order_1 for the full list).
Depends on ideas 1 & 2; co-developed with idea 3 (it owns the floor TP budget
that scarcity is measured against).

=== THE PROBLEM ===

Combat is currently "stand still, fire best weapon, enemy walks into you, repeat
until one of you is dead." Enemies differ only by HP/damage NUMBERS, not by how
you must FIGHT them. A Shell Brute and a Plague Hulk play identically — walk-and-
hit sponges. There is one correct action every turn (shoot), so there is no
tactic. Tactical depth = the BEST action changes turn-to-turn based on position,
terrain, and which enemies are present. This idea creates that.

Turn-based + tile-based is a GIFT here: every fight is a chess position. The job
is to give the player meaningful moves other than "fire."


=== PILLAR 1 — THE ENCOUNTER BUDGET (difficulty as a dial) ===

The level generator must spend a TP budget (idea 2 primitive 4), not roll
enemies at random. This is what makes difficulty controllable instead of
accidental.

  floorTpBudget(depth) = BASE_TP * depthThreatScale(depth)
  e.g. BASE_TP = 120 at depth 1, scaling 1.08*1.06 per depth (idea 2 invariant).

  COMPOSITION RULES (spend the budget tastefully, not on 20 chaff):
   - Reserve 15-30% of budget for ONE "anchor" enemy (bruiser/mini-elite) that
     defines the floor's hardest room.
   - No more than ~40% of budget in a single enemy TYPE (variety forces the
     player to adapt mid-floor; mono-type rooms are solved once and boring).
   - Per-room TP cap so no single room exceeds ~35% of floor budget unless it is
     a deliberate "gauntlet" climax room.
   - Ranged + melee MIX in the same room creates the core tactical tension:
     close the gap on the ranged enemy while the melee enemy closes on you.

  This budget is ALSO the DEMAND term in the scarcity equation (idea 3). The two
  ideas share this number; that is why they are co-developed.


=== PILLAR 2 — ENEMY ROLE DIFFERENTIATION (stop shipping stat-clones) ===

11 enemy types exist but most are interchangeable. Give each a TACTICAL VERB —
a reason the player must do something DIFFERENT against it. Roles (not numbers)
are the unit of design. Examples mapped onto existing types:

  GORE_BITER   — SWARMER: weak, fast, packs. Verb: "don't get surrounded."
                 Punishes standing still; rewards funneling into a chokepoint.
  EYE_TYRANT   — SNIPER: ranged, cardinal-line only (existing rule). Verb:
                 "break line of sight / step off its column or row." Rewards use
                 of cover and the rotate/strafe moves.
  SHELL_BRUTE  — CHARGER: telegraphs a high-damage rush. Verb: "sidestep the
                 charge, then punish." Rewards the strafe action specifically.
  PLAGUE_HULK  — TANK + AREA DENIAL: slow (moveEvery2) but leaves a hazard
                 (toxic tile) on death/where it stands. Verb: "kill at range,
                 don't melee into its cloud." Ties into the hazard system.
  MIRE_WRAITH  — ARTILLERY/DOT: ranged acid that applies POISON. Verb: "prioritise
                 it; don't let DOT stack." Rewards target prioritisation.
  VOID_SHROUD  — FLANKER: fast, prefers attacking from your blind side (the tile
                 behind your facing). Verb: "keep your back covered / rotate to
                 face it." Rewards spatial awareness.
  IRON_STALKER — MINI-ELITE: hybrid melee+ranged, armored. Verb: "commit
                 resources or avoid." A deliberate spike, not common.
  ACID_DRONE   — HARASSER: ranged, kites (existing). Verb: "corner it / cut the
                 kite path." Rewards using level geometry.

  DESIGN RULE: a new enemy is not approved until you can state its VERB in one
  sentence and that verb is DISTINCT from existing enemies. "It has more HP" is
  not a verb. (Each verb must also respect the cardinal-line constraint for any
  ranged behavior — project invariant.)

  Cheap mechanical hooks that create verbs without new systems:
   - attack-from-behind preference (flanker)
   - telegraph-then-strike (charger): one turn of wind-up the player can read
   - on-death hazard tile (area denial)
   - applies a status effect on hit (DOT/slow/stun — already in StatusType)
   - moves toward cardinal alignment aggressively (sniper)


=== PILLAR 3 — TERRAIN INTERACTION (the emergent-complexity engine) ===

This is the single highest-value lever the game already has half-built. The
hazard chain-reaction system (fire/toxic/explosive) is IN PROGRESS. FINISH IT.
It converts flat rooms into tactical terrain:

  - FIRE tiles: spread along flammable decals; damage anything standing in them
    (player AND enemies). Verb: "lure enemies through fire / don't get cornered
    into it."
  - TOXIC/ACID pools: DOT zones; area denial. Pairs with Plague Hulk / Mire
    Wraith. Verb: "control space, don't path through it."
  - EXPLOSIVE BARRELS (ExplosiveBarrelManager exists): chain detonations. Verb:
    "shoot the barrel, not the enemy — let the room kill the pack."

  BALANCE NOTE: hazards must damage the PLAYER too, or they are just free DPS and
  add no tactics. The tension is "this hazard can win the fight for me OR kill me
  if I misposition." That two-sidedness is the whole point. Put hazard tick
  damage and spread chance in BalanceConfig and count hazard damage in the threat
  math (idea 2): a room full of fire raises its effective TP.


=== PILLAR 4 — POSITIONING INCENTIVES (give "move" a payoff) ===

For repositioning to be worth a turn (and a turn is precious — enemies act every
turn), the geometry must reward it:

  - COVER vs ranged: walls/columns ('P') break line of sight. The cardinal-line
    ranged rule already means stepping off an enemy's row/column denies its shot.
    Make sure levels actually provide cover (generator should place columns in
    open rooms — partly exists via LEVEL_GEN_LARGE_COLUMN_*).
  - CHOKEPOINTS vs swarms: doorways let you fight a pack one-at-a-time. The
    greedy-stepper AI already funnels; ensure layouts offer 1-wide pinch points.
  - FLANK PUNISHMENT: flankers (Void Shroud) make "where am I facing" matter, so
    rotate/strafe become tactical, not just navigational.
  - TURN-COST AWARENESS: each reposition step = one enemy turn of incoming
    damage. The decision "spend a turn moving to safety vs spend it shooting" is
    the core tactical loop. PLAYER_MOVE_DURATION is therefore a BALANCE value
    (faster = you can reposition more freely), not cosmetic — flagged in idea 1.


=== PILLAR 5 — TELEGRAPHS & COUNTERPLAY (fairness) ===

A turn-based game is only tactical if threats are READABLE before they land.
  - Heavy enemy attacks (charger rush, elite slam) should telegraph one turn
    ahead (a wind-up state + visual cue) so the player can react. Damage without
    counterplay is just a dice roll (violates roguelike pillar 1: deaths must
    feel fair, "I made a mistake").
  - Ranged enemies already telegraph via the cardinal-line requirement (you can
    SEE when you're in their lane). Lean into that — it is good design already.
  - RULE: every attack that can deal >25% of player eHP in one hit MUST be
    telegraphed or avoidable. Burst damage without warning is banned.


=== HOW THIS MAKES THE GAME BALANCED *AND* FUN ===

Tactics and balance are the same thing here: when enemies have distinct verbs,
terrain is two-sided, and the budget mixes threats, the "one correct action"
collapses into a real decision tree. That decision tree is BOTH the fun (player
expresses skill) and the balance (skilled play beats the golden-ratio fights of
idea 2; careless play loses them). Scarcity (idea 3) is what makes the decisions
cost something.


=== PREREQUISITES ===
  - Ideas 1 & 2.
  - Finish the IN-PROGRESS hazard chain-reaction system (Pillar 3) — it is the
    biggest single multiplier and is already half-built.
  - Enemy AI hooks for: telegraph/wind-up state, flank preference, on-death
    hazard. These are small additions to EnemyManager, but they ARE new code,
    so flag them as engineering prerequisites for the role differentiation.
  - The encounter-budget generator step is new generator code (LevelGenerator).

=== DEFINITION OF DONE ===
  - LevelGenerator spends a per-depth TP budget with the composition rules.
  - Each enemy type has a documented one-sentence VERB and a mechanical hook
    that enforces it.
  - Hazard chain reactions are finished and damage both sides; hazard damage is
    counted in the threat math.
  - No un-telegraphed attack exceeds 25% of player eHP.
