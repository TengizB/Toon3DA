OVERVIEW
========
The RESONANT PYLON is a stationary demonic-tech organ — a fleshy spire fused to a
UAC power node — that does NO direct damage but radiates a SHIELD AURA: every
enemy on the same cardinal line as the pylon, within range, gains a damage-soaking
overshield each turn. Kill the pylon and the whole pack loses its protection at
once. It is the roster's first SUPPORT enemy: it doesn't threaten you, it forces a
target-priority decision under fire. The current roster has tanks, kiters,
flankers, and a stationary turret, but nothing that makes "who do I shoot FIRST?"
the central question. The Pylon fills that gap and creates the classic roguelike
"break the totem before you fight the room" tactic.

This document OWNS: the Pylon enemy archetype, its aura mechanic, the overshield
layer it grants, its spawn rules, and its readability. It REQUIRES a new enemy
spawn symbol. It LEANS ON the status-effect layer (roguelike_order_8) for the
overshield representation but defines its own simpler shield-pool if that layer is
absent.

GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
1. The Pylon is placed by the level/generator (often near a defended objective:
   keycard, weapon rack, stairs). It is STATIONARY (never moves, never pathfinds)
   and has no attack.
2. On EVERY enemy turn, the Pylon "pulses": for each living enemy that is on the
   same cardinal line as the Pylon (same column OR same row — reusing
   isSameCardinalLine) AND within PYLON_AURA_RANGE_TILES AND with clear LOS along
   that line, that enemy gains/refreshes an OVERSHIELD of PYLON_SHIELD_AMOUNT.
   -> The overshield is a separate hit-point pool drawn ON TOP of the enemy's HP.
      Incoming damage depletes overshield first, then HP.
   -> The overshield does NOT stack beyond PYLON_SHIELD_AMOUNT (refresh-to-cap, not
      accumulate). One pylon = one shield cap per beneficiary.
3. The Pylon itself can be shot. It has modest HP (PYLON_HEALTH) and NO shield of
   its own (it cannot shield itself — it is the obvious weak point).
4. When the Pylon dies: every overshield it was sustaining instantly drops to 0
   (the shields were projected, not owned). A visible "aura collapse" pulse plays.
   The protected pack is now fully vulnerable.
5. If the player ignores the Pylon, every turn the shielded enemies effectively gain
   PYLON_SHIELD_AMOUNT extra durability — the fight becomes a war of attrition the
   player loses. The intended counter is: reposition to break LOS to the pylon, or
   focus-fire the pylon down first.

PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
You round into a containment bay and three Shell Brutes are lit with a sickly cyan
glow — every shotgun blast barely dents them. You scan, spot the pulsing spire
wired into the back wall, and now the room has a PUZZLE: do you push through the
brutes to reach the pylon, or do you back off, break the corridor's sightline so the
aura can't reach, and pick the brutes off in the dark? It teaches target priority
without a tutorial — the cyan glow IS the lesson. Veterans learn to lead with a
piercing weapon (Railgun/Plasma) to kill the pylon through the pack, turning the
Pylon into a satisfying "snipe the support" skill check.

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- PYLON BILLBOARD: a tall fleshy-tech spire — charcoal UAC node base, demonic
  sinew wrapping upward, a glowing cyan resonator crystal at the top that pulses
  once per turn (the pulse frame = "it just shielded the pack"). 0.95x wall height,
  stationary, no walk animation needed.
- AURA BEAMS: on each pulse, draw faint cyan beam segments from the pylon along its
  active cardinal lines to each beneficiary (reuse the wall-stripe/overlay batch;
  short-lived, fades over the turn). This makes the "who is protected" relationship
  legible at a glance — critical for the priority-target read.
- SHIELDED ENEMIES: faint cyan rim tint on the billboard + a thin cyan overshield
  bar above the standard health bar. When overshield is depleting, the bar drains
  cyan before the red HP bar moves.
- DEATH: a bright cyan shockwave ring from the pylon's tile + all rim-tints snap off
  simultaneously + EventText "AURA COLLAPSE." Very satisfying payoff for the snipe.

BALANCE NOTES (placeholders — flag for playtest)
================================================
- PYLON_SHIELD_AMOUNT (12) should feel like "one extra solid hit per enemy per
  turn," not invulnerability. If it makes packs feel spongy/unfun, lower to 8.
- PYLON_HEALTH (40) is intentionally low so the pylon is a PRIORITY, not a second
  tank. The challenge is REACHING/SIGHTING it through the pack, not its raw HP.
- Refresh-to-cap (not stacking) prevents runaway shield growth over a long fight.
- Because the aura is cardinal + LOS-gated, the player has a positional counter:
  step out of the lane or behind cover and the shields stop refreshing. This is the
  intended "skill expression" — reward map reading, don't make it a pure DPS check.
- Piercing weapons (Railgun infinite pierce, Plasma) are the designed hard counter:
  shoot through the front-line enemy and pop the pylon behind it. This gives those
  weapons a clear situational niche — good build-diversity payoff (pillar 5).
- Keep PYLON_MAX_PER_LEVEL = 1 early; two pylons cross-shielding each other's packs
  is a fun ELITE/boss-floor escalation but brutal as a baseline — gate it to deep
  floors or a mini-boss room.

INTERACTIONS
============
1. ENEMY ARCHETYPES (roguelike_order_7): the Pylon transforms existing archetypes —
   a shielded Gore Biter swarm or shielded Shell Brutes become a genuinely new
   encounter without new combat enemies. Strong reuse value.
2. STATUS EFFECTS (roguelike_order_8): does the overshield block DoT (Burning/
   Poison) ticks? Recommend YES — ticks deplete overshield first too, so a burning
   shielded enemy slowly loses shield rather than HP until the pylon dies or the
   shield is gone. Confirm in playtest (see OPEN Q).
3. PIERCING/SPLASH WEAPONS (railgun, plasma-rifle, grenade-launcher): pierce lets
   you hit the pylon through the pack; splash near the pylon can clear shield + pack
   together. Defines a clear weapon-choice incentive.
4. NOISE/STEALTH (noise-propagation): a stationary pylon is a navigational landmark;
   sniping it from outside its room (down a long lane) is a stealth-friendly opener.
5. BOSS FLOORS (roguelike_order_10): a boss arena seeding 1-2 pylons turns the boss
   into a "destroy the supports, then the boss" multi-phase fight — strong synergy.
6. DIFFICULTY SCALING (roguelike_order_16): a clean knob — higher floors raise
   PYLON_SHIELD_AMOUNT or allow a second pylon, escalating without new art.

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
1. Does overshield absorb status DoT ticks, or do ticks bypass it to HP?
   Recommend ticks deplete shield first (consistent), but this slows DoT builds vs.
   pylon packs — playtest whether that's the intended counter or too punishing.
2. Pylon-death shield handling: track source-id per overshield (precise, supports
   multi-pylon) vs. "recompute next pulse + zero now" (simpler, fine for 1 pylon).
   Recommend the simple path for v1, upgrade to source-id when multi-pylon ships.
3. Should the Pylon block movement/LOS like a solid prop (it's a spire), or be a
   shootable-but-walk-through hazard? Recommend SOLID (blocks the tile) so it also
   shapes the room's pathing and can't be body-blocked into uselessness.
4. Can the Pylon shield ITSELF if another pylon is in range (multi-pylon cross-
   cover)? Recommend NO self/peer-shield in v1 to keep the weak point honest; revisit
   for an elite "twin pylon" deep-floor encounter.
5. Visual beam cost on low-end phones: if per-pulse beams are too heavy, fall back
   to just the rim-tint + a single pylon pulse flash. Keep the rim-tint as the
   non-negotiable readability element.
