TITLE: Narrative rework 4 — ORA the guide & the competence model
---

PURPOSE
Fixes failures F4 and F7. ORA currently teaches six controls; the game contains roughly twenty
things a player must understand, including every system players actually fail at — guarding,
reading an enemy's intent, stepping out of a ranged enemy's line, and the route map that
opens the first time they touch a portal. This order gives her all of them, and adds the rule
that keeps her from becoming a nag: SAY IT ONCE, and say it again only when the player has
demonstrably missed it — in different, shorter words.

READ FIRST
- order-1 (D1, D4, D5), order-3 (one idea per panel)
- narrative/ControlHint, BarkCatalog.registerControlHints, world/StoryBarkTickSubscriber
- input/touch/TouchAction (FORWARD..GUARD, USE_MACHINE, INSPECT_WEAPON, SKIP_TURN)
- enemy/IntentVerb + docs/enemy-system.txt (WIND_UP / DEFEND / ATTACK_RANGED telegraphs)
- docs/route-map-system.txt (the FACILITY NAV console the '>' portal opens)
- .claude/CLAUDE.md — the cardinal-line rule for ranged enemies

============================================================
A. FROM ControlHint TO TeachingTopic
============================================================
Rename and widen the enum to narrative/TeachingTopic (ControlHint's six values are kept as
values, so no content is lost). Each topic carries:
    tier      CRITICAL (cannot play without it) | TACTICAL (plays much better with it)
    trigger   the exact world condition that makes it useful FOR THE FIRST TIME
    line      the teaching row (one instruction, plain words)
    again     the re-teach row (different words, shorter, more concrete)
    evidence  what counts as "they missed it" (see part C)

CRITICAL topics fire the first time the thing is possible, and are STORY_CRITICAL so they
land even mid-fight (a dropped critical hint is a stuck player). TACTICAL topics fire at the
first moment the thing would have HELPED — the turn after a telegraphed hit landed, not the
turn a telegraph first appeared. That timing is the whole design: a hint that arrives while
the player is still feeling the problem is advice; the same hint five minutes early is noise.

Twenty topics, twenty lines, one per moment, delivered across the first hour. Fourteen of
them are things a player currently discovers by dying. Note what every TACTICAL line has in
common: it names the COST as well as the action ("costs a turn", "takes a turn", "it sticks
for this run only"), because a tactic without its cost is a trap.

REGION-1 DENSITY GUARD: no more than one teaching bark per THREE player turns, and never two
in the same room, CRITICAL included — a critical that would break the rule is queued, not
dropped (the bark queue already does exactly this and freezes ageing while suppressed).

============================================================
C. THE COMPETENCE MODEL — the rule that stops her nagging
============================================================
A topic is taught ONCE. It may be re-taught AT MOST ONCE MORE, ever, and only on evidence:

  EVIDENCE A — THEY DIDN'T READ IT.
    The teaching bark was closed inside STORY_TELEMETRY_FAST_DISMISS_SECONDS. StoryTelemetry
    already measures this and currently only counts it. Now it names the topic.
  EVIDENCE B — THEY DON'T UNDERSTAND IT.
    Per topic, a concrete failure counted N times in one run:
      HEAL         died with an unused medkit in the bag                        (1x)
      GUARD        took 3 telegraphed heavy hits without ever guarding          (3x)
      BREAK_LANE   took 3 ranged hits while sharing a row/column, never stepped off (3x)
      RELOAD       fired an empty weapon 4 times in a row                       (4x)
      READ_INTENT  took 3 hits from an enemy that had shown its intent          (3x)
      SWITCH_WEAPON  fought 2 whole floors without ever switching, holding two guns
      NAV_MAP      sat on the NAV console over 30 s without picking a node
      LEVEL_UP     an unspent point at the end of a floor
  A topic with no listed evidence rule is never re-taught. Silence is the default.

THE RE-TEACH LINE IS NEVER THE SAME WORDS. Verbatim repetition is precisely what players
report as nagging; a shorter, blunter, differently-angled second telling reads as a person
noticing. Examples:
    GUARD      first:  Guard braces you and ends your turn. Costs a turn, saves most of the hit.
               again:  That one was telegraphed. Guard would have eaten most of it.
    BREAK_LANE first:  It can only shoot straight down a row or a column. Step off its line.
               again:  You're standing in its lane again. One step sideways. Any sideways.
    HEAL       first:  You're carrying a medkit. Use it before the next one lands.
               again:  You died with a medkit in the bag. I'm not going to say anything else.
    READ_INTENT first: See the mark over it? That's what it does next turn.
               again:  The mark means it's already committed. You get a free move. Take it.

RATE LIMITS ON RE-TEACHING
  - at most ONE re-teach per floor, across all topics;
  - never during a combat spike unless the topic is CRITICAL;
  - never within 60 s of the original teaching (someone still processing it is not failing it);
  - a re-teach is one-shot: after it, that topic is silent forever, right or wrong. The game
    does not get a third go at teaching anybody anything.

NEW HEADLESS TYPE: narrative/TeachingSystem — owns the topic registry, the evidence counters
(per run, in memory) and the two persisted flags per topic (taught, retaught) which are just
one-shot beat ids in the existing seen-set. No schema bump. World feeds it the same events it
already feeds the bark layer plus four new facts (guard used, ranged hit taken in-lane,
telegraphed hit taken, medkit unused at death) — all available in EnemyManager / World today.

============================================================
D. THE PORTAL AND THE MAP — the biggest single hole
============================================================
A first-time player finishes floor one, steps on a '>' tile and a node graph takes the screen
with no explanation of what a node is, what the icons mean, why there is a choice, or that
the choice is permanent. Three lines fix it, all CRITICAL, all one-shot:

  seeing the portal   That's the way down. Step on it and I'll bring the map up.
  the map opens       This is the facility. Pick a room to drop into - each one's marked
                      with what's in it.
  second branch       You can take the quiet road or the loud one. Loud pays better.
                      Your call.

Plus ONE piece of lore, delivered the second time the map opens, because this is also the
moment the endless descent needs explaining (order-8 owns the rest):
    story.bark.nav.lore=Nobody built one way down. They built dozens, and then kept digging.

The NAV lines fire from World at the ROUTE_SELECT phase transition. Barks are suppressed
during a hard-pause overlay, so these must be delivered as the console OPENS (the frame the
phase changes) or held until it closes — implement as: queue on open, deliver on the first
PLAYING frame after the node is chosen, EXCEPT the "the map opens" line, which is the one
case worth a dedicated draw inside the console (RouteMapOverlayRenderer already owns a header
band; put it there as a one-shot hint strip, not a bark panel).

============================================================
E. WHAT ORA NEVER DOES
============================================================
- She never repeats an instruction the player is currently executing correctly.
- She never comments on a system the player cannot yet use.
- She never says "remember, you can…" — the phrase that makes every helper voice hated.
- She never gates a mechanic behind a line: the game must remain playable with the story
  layer silenced. Teaching lines are the FIRST place a player looks for information, never
  the only place.

DEFINITION OF DONE

  rule; TeachingCoverageTest enforces all three.

- A player who dies with a medkit in the bag hears about it exactly once, ever.
