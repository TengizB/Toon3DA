=============================================================================
OVERVIEW
=============================================================================
A combat-feedback layer that sits on top of the already-designed enemy system
(enemy_system.txt). It gives every living enemy two pieces of readable juice:
(1) a floating health bar billboarded above the sprite that appears only after
the enemy takes damage, and (2) a hit reaction — a one-shot white damage flash
plus a brief recoil/knockback shudder on the sprite the turn it is struck, with
a distinct death lurch when the killing blow lands. Nothing here changes a
single game rule; it is pure rendering and a few transient per-enemy timers,
and it is the difference between a shotgun blast that feels like a confirmed,
satisfying hit and one that feels like the pellets vanished into fog.

It belongs here because the enemy system resolves damage instantly in tile
space (a shell either reduces HP or it does not), but the player currently has
no way to read HOW MUCH a target has left or even confirm that THAT enemy was
the one hit. In a turn-based crawler where every shell costs a one-step reload,
"will this next blast kill the Corruptor or do I need to back-pedal one more
tile?" is THE core combat decision. Health bars answer it; hit reactions sell
it. This is the readability contract from DCSS (you always know enemy HP state)
delivered through the Doom-RPG billboard aesthetic.

Design lineage: DCSS (health-as-information, monster HP always inspectable),
Doom / Doom RPG (white pain-frame flash on hit, the chunky satisfying recoil),
Hades (every hit reads instantly — flash, shake, number-free legibility).

=============================================================================
GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============================================================================
This feature is feedback-only. It does NOT consume turns, does NOT change AI,
does NOT alter damage. It hooks the two existing damage events from
enemy_system.txt and animates in REAL TIME (over wall-clock frames), the same
way the player pain flash and the red-alert wall pulse already animate
independently of the turn clock.

WHEN THE PLAYER SHOOTS AN ENEMY (player FIRE turn):
1. Player presses FIRE → Shotgun.marchShot walks facing tiles → first tile with
   a living enemy → EnemyManager.applyDamageTo(enemy, dealt) (existing path).
2. INSIDE applyDamageTo, AFTER health is reduced, set transient feedback timers
   on that enemy:
       enemy.healthBarVisibleTimer = HEALTH_BAR_VISIBLE_SECONDS
       enemy.hitFlashTimer         = HIT_FLASH_SECONDS
       enemy.recoilTimer           = HIT_RECOIL_SECONDS
       enemy.recoilDirectionColumn = sign(enemy.tileColumn - playerColumn)
       enemy.recoilDirectionRow    = sign(enemy.tileRow    - playerRow)
   (recoil pushes the sprite AWAY from the shooter along the facing axis.)
3. If health <= 0 → enemy dies (existing removal + corpse 'm' stamp). On the
   frame death is detected, ALSO set:
       enemy.deathLurchTimer = DEATH_LURCH_SECONDS
   and let the renderer play one death lurch before the sprite is removed (see
   DEATH HANDOFF in TECHNICAL NOTES — the sprite must survive a few frames past
   logical death OR the corpse prop must inherit the lurch; recommend the
   former: a short-lived "dying" render entry).
4. EnemyRenderer.render() (already in the frame loop) reads these timers every
   frame, draws the flash/recoil/bar, and decrements the timers by deltaTime.
   NO turn advances because of any of this.

WHEN A VORTEX EYE IS DAMAGED BY A FUTURE SOURCE (chain-reaction barrel, etc.):
- Identical: any call into applyDamageTo arms the same timers. Health bars are
  source-agnostic; they react to the HP delta, not to who caused it.

ENEMIES DO NOT GET HEALTH BARS FOR ENEMY-VS-PLAYER DAMAGE:
- When an enemy hits the PLAYER, the player's existing pain flash fires; enemies
  are unaffected. Health bars are strictly for ENEMY HP shown to the player.

VISIBILITY RULE (the key loop decision):
- A health bar is shown for an enemy ONLY while healthBarVisibleTimer > 0 (it
  fades out HEALTH_BAR_VISIBLE_SECONDS after the LAST hit) OR, optionally, while
  the enemy is the one directly in the player's facing crosshair tile-line
  (ALWAYS_SHOW_BAR_FOR_TARGETED_ENEMY flag — see OPEN QUESTIONS). Default v1:
  damage-triggered only. This keeps the screen clean (no forest of bars) and
  ties the bar to the moment the player actually needs it: right after a shot,
  deciding whether one more shell finishes the job.

=============================================================================
PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=============================================================================
- THE FINISH-OR-FLEE DECISION: the player fires at the Corruptor, the bar pops
  in showing ~one third left. Now the decision is legible: chamber one more
  shell (costs a step the slow bruiser uses to close one tile) or back-pedal and
  reset spacing. Before this feature that read was invisible; the player guessed.
- CONFIRMED HITS: the white flash + recoil is instant, frame-one confirmation
  "I hit THAT one." In a corridor with a Corruptor and a Vortex Eye stacked at
  range, the flash tells you which silhouette ate the pellets — critical when
  the eye is the priority kill.
- SATISFYING KILLS: the death lurch (a sharp recoil + flash + the bar draining
  to empty as the sprite drops to the corpse decal) gives the shotgun weight.
  Doom's whole feel is "the gun is loud and things visibly react." This is that.
- HONEST DEATHS, HONEST KILLS: the player can always reconstruct combat. "The
  eye was at a sliver — I should have spent the shell on it instead of the
  Corruptor that was still near full." The bar makes the misplay nameable, which
  is the DCSS legibility contract this whole project is built on.
- CLEAN SCREEN: because bars only appear on recently-damaged enemies and fade,
  the dread of a dormant room full of still silhouettes is preserved — you do
  NOT see a bar until you commit to a fight. Information arrives exactly when
  earned.

=============================================================================
HEALTH BAR — VISUAL SPEC
=============================================================================
A health bar is a small billboarded quad drawn ABOVE the enemy sprite, scaled
by the same distance projection as the sprite so it shrinks with range and stays
glued to its owner. It is composed of flat colored rectangles (no texture
needed — reuse the existing 1x1 whitePixel texture WallRenderer already owns, or
give EnemyRenderer its own 1x1 white texture).

LAYOUT (three stacked layers, back to front):
  1. BORDER / FRAME  — a 1-screen-pixel dark outline (near-black, e.g. RGB
     20,20,24) drawn as a rectangle slightly larger than the bar on every side.
     Sells it as a UAC HUD element, not a floating smear.
  2. BACKGROUND TRACK — the full bar width in a dim desaturated red/maroon
     (e.g. RGB 60,18,18). This is the "missing health" track that stays visible
     so the player reads the ratio, not just the remaining nub.
  3. FILL — the remaining-health portion, width = trackWidth * (health /
     maxHealth). Color is health-state coded:
         > 60% : bright toxic green   (RGB 90,210,80)   — "healthy"
         30–60%: amber/yellow         (RGB 235,190,40)  — "wounded"
         < 30% : hot red              (RGB 235,55,40)   — "critical", and the
                 fill PULSES (brightness oscillates via sin(facilityTime *
                 BAR_CRITICAL_PULSE_SPEED)) to scream "one more shell."
     The fill is anchored to the LEFT edge of the track (drains right-to-left,
     the universal convention).

POSITION & SIZE (all in screen space, derived from the sprite projection):
  - barCenterColumn = the sprite's spriteScreenColumnCenter (same value the
    sprite uses — bar is centered over the sprite horizontally).
  - barWidthScreen  = spriteScreenWidth * HEALTH_BAR_WIDTH_FRACTION (e.g. 0.9 —
    slightly narrower than the sprite).
  - barHeightScreen = clamp(spriteScreenHeight * HEALTH_BAR_HEIGHT_FRACTION,
                            HEALTH_BAR_MIN_HEIGHT_PX, HEALTH_BAR_MAX_HEIGHT_PX)
    (e.g. fraction 0.06, min 3px, max 14px — never a hairline, never a slab).
  - barBottomScreen = sprite drawTop + spriteScreenHeight *
                      HEALTH_BAR_VERTICAL_GAP_FRACTION (e.g. 0.05 — floats just
                      above the silhouette's head). For the floating Vortex Eye
                      include its hover bob so the bar rides the bob.

FADE: alpha = smooth fade over the last HEALTH_BAR_FADE_SECONDS of
  healthBarVisibleTimer (e.g. linear from 1 → 0 in the final 0.4s). Fully opaque
  while freshly damaged, then dissolves. Apply alpha to ALL three layers
  uniformly so the whole widget fades as a unit.

DISTANCE CULL: do not draw the bar if spriteDepth >
  HEALTH_BAR_MAX_DRAW_DISTANCE_TILES (e.g. 12 — a bar on a barely-visible far
  enemy is unreadable noise). Bars on the nearest threats are what matter.

OCCLUSION: the bar uses the SAME per-column z-buffer test as the sprite. If a
  wall column is closer than the enemy at that screen column, that slice of the
  bar is culled — a bar peeking over a wall corner clips correctly, exactly like
  the sprite body. Use the enemy's spriteDepth for the test (the bar sits at the
  enemy's depth).

READABILITY GUARANTEE: because the bar is built from solid high-contrast rects
  with a dark frame, it stays legible against the dim UAC walls and survives the
  red-alert tint. IMPORTANT: do NOT multiply the bar colors by the red-alert
  tint or the distance shade — the bar is a HUD-style readout pinned to a
  world-space sprite; it must read at full saturation regardless of lighting.
  (The sprite body still shades normally; only the bar ignores shade/tint.)

=============================================================================
HIT REACTION — VISUAL EFFECT
=============================================================================
Three layered effects, all driven by transient per-enemy timers, all real-time:

1. DAMAGE FLASH (the Doom pain frame):
   - For the duration of hitFlashTimer (e.g. 0.10s), tint the enemy sprite
     toward white. flashAmount = hitFlashTimer / HIT_FLASH_SECONDS (1 → 0).
     Blend the per-column sprite color toward white:
         drawColor = lerp(normalShadedColor, WHITE, flashAmount * FLASH_STRENGTH)
     with FLASH_STRENGTH ~0.85 so even at peak a hint of silhouette remains.
   - This overrides the dormant-dampen and red-alert tint for the flash window
     (a hit always reads, even on a freshly-woken enemy).
   - Implementation: SpriteBatch.setColor before drawing the sprite's columns,
     reset to the shaded color after. One setColor per enemy per frame — no
     allocation.

2. RECOIL / KNOCKBACK SHUDDER (cosmetic only — the enemy does NOT change tiles):
   - For the duration of recoilTimer (e.g. 0.14s), offset the sprite's render
     position AWAY from the shooter, then ease back to its tile center.
   - recoilProgress = recoilTimer / HIT_RECOIL_SECONDS (1 → 0).
   - recoilOffsetTiles = RECOIL_MAX_OFFSET_TILES *
                         GameMath.recoilEnvelope(recoilProgress)
     where recoilEnvelope is a quick out-and-back curve (e.g. a half-sine:
     sin(recoilProgress * PI) — peaks mid-window, zero at both ends) so the
     sprite jerks back and snaps home. RECOIL_MAX_OFFSET_TILES ~0.18 (a fifth of
     a tile — a visible shudder, never a teleport).
   - Apply along (recoilDirectionColumn, recoilDirectionRow) added to the
     enemy's render world position BEFORE projection. Pure render offset: the
     authoritative tileColumn/tileRow are untouched, so AI, occupancy, LOS, and
     collision are all unaffected. This is critical — the shudder is a lie told
     only to the renderer.
   - Optional micro-vertical-jitter for extra crunch: add a tiny vertical screen
     wobble (a few pixels) scaled by recoilProgress. Keep subtle.

3. DEATH LURCH (the killing blow):
   - When the hit is lethal, instead of an instant disappear, play one final
     reaction over deathLurchTimer (e.g. 0.20s):
       * A stronger flash (start near full white, FLASH_STRENGTH 1.0).
       * A stronger recoil (DEATH_RECOIL_OFFSET_TILES ~0.30) that does NOT ease
         back — the sprite is knocked away and DROPS: lerp the sprite's drawTop
         downward (sinks toward the floor line) and fade alpha 1 → 0 over the
         window, so it visibly crumples into the corpse decal that is being
         stamped at its tile.
       * The health bar fill drains to 0 and fades with the body.
   - After deathLurchTimer expires, the dying render entry is dropped; the
     corpse 'm' prop (already stamped at logical-death time) remains. See DEATH
     HANDOFF for ordering so the live sprite and the corpse prop do not double-
     draw for those frames.

PER-COLUMN INTEGRATION: all three effects ride the existing per-column sprite
draw loop. Flash = setColor; recoil = a world-position offset applied before
spriteScreenColumnCenter / spriteDepth are computed; death sink = a drawTop/
alpha modifier. None of them add a draw pass — they modulate the pass the
EnemyRenderer already performs.

=============================================================================
TECHNICAL NOTES (LibGDX RAYCASTING RENDERER)
=============================================================================
This feature extends the EnemyRenderer and Enemy classes defined in
enemy_system.txt. It introduces NO new turn logic and NO new package.

ENEMY.JAVA — add transient feedback fields (all default 0; all decremented in
the renderer, NOT in the turn loop):
    float healthBarVisibleTimer;   // seconds remaining the bar is shown
    float hitFlashTimer;           // seconds remaining of white flash
    float recoilTimer;             // seconds remaining of shudder
    float deathLurchTimer;         // seconds remaining of death animation
    int   recoilDirectionColumn;   // -1/0/+1 push axis (away from shooter)
    int   recoilDirectionRow;      // -1/0/+1 push axis
    boolean dying;                 // true once health<=0 but lurch not finished
  Helper:
    void armHitFeedback(int shooterColumn, int shooterRow)  // sets bar/flash/
        recoil timers + recoil direction (sign of tile delta; if both signs are
        0 because shooter and enemy share a tile edge case, default push along
        the larger axis or skip recoil).
    void armDeathFeedback()        // sets deathLurchTimer, dying=true, stronger
        recoil; called when applyDamage drops health to <=0.
  These are PLAIN DATA + setters. No LibGDX state on Enemy (keeps it pure data
  per the enemy_system.txt contract).

ENEMYMANAGER.JAVA — in applyDamageTo(enemy, amount):
    enemy.applyDamage(amount);
    if (enemy.isAlive()) {
        enemy.armHitFeedback(lastShooterColumn, lastShooterRow);
    } else {
        enemy.armDeathFeedback();
        // DO NOT immediately remove from the live list. Move it to a short-lived
        // "dying" set so EnemyRenderer can play the lurch. Clear its occupancy
        // NOW (it no longer blocks movement / cannot be targeted again) and
        // stamp the corpse 'm' NOW (so the tile reads as a corpse to AI/LOS),
        // but keep the dying sprite in the RENDER list until deathLurchTimer
        // expires (EnemyRenderer reports expiry, or EnemyManager ticks the
        // dying timers in a tiny per-frame update — see DEATH HANDOFF).
    }
  applyDamageTo needs the shooter tile. Either pass it through
    (applyDamageTo(enemy, amount, shooterColumn, shooterRow)) or have
    EnemyManager remember the player tile from the last takeTurn / fire. Cleanest
    is to add the two int params to applyDamageTo and the EnemyHitTarget
    interface signature (Shotgun already knows the player tile when it marches).

DEATH HANDOFF (the one ordering subtlety):
  Logical death and visual death must be decoupled so the lurch can play:
    - At logical death: clear occupancy, stamp corpse 'm', set dying=true. The
      enemy is now untargetable (enemyAt() must return null for a dying enemy so
      a second pellet/turn cannot "re-hit" a corpse) and does not act in AI.
    - For deathLurchTimer frames: EnemyRenderer still draws this dying enemy
      (with sink + fade) AND the corpse prop exists at the tile. To avoid a
      double-draw, EITHER (recommended) have PropRenderer skip drawing the 'm'
      corpse at a tile that currently has a dying enemy overlapping it, OR delay
      the corpse stamp until the lurch finishes. Recommend: delay the corpse
      stamp until lurch end — simplest, no cross-renderer coupling. (If AI/LOS
      must immediately treat the tile as walkable corpse, prefer the skip-draw
      approach instead.) FLAG in OPEN QUESTIONS.
    - Ticking dying timers: the cleanest place is a new lightweight
      EnemyManager.updateFeedback(deltaTime) called every frame from World.update
      (NOT from the turn callback). It decrements healthBarVisibleTimer,
      hitFlashTimer, recoilTimer, deathLurchTimer on all enemies (live + dying),
      and when a dying enemy's deathLurchTimer hits 0 it is finally removed and
      the corpse is stamped. This keeps all real-time feedback ticking off
      wall-clock time independent of turns. Alternatively decrement inside
      EnemyRenderer.render using Gdx.graphics.getDeltaTime(); recommend the
      explicit updateFeedback(deltaTime) call for testability and to avoid
      mutating state inside a render pass.

ENEMYRENDERER.JAVA — extend the existing per-enemy billboard pass:
  Per enemy (live or dying), in the existing sorted draw loop:
    1. Compute render world position WITH recoil/death offset:
         offsetTiles = recoil envelope (live) or death recoil (dying)
         renderWorldX = tileCenterX + recoilDirectionColumn * offsetTiles * CELL_SIZE
         renderWorldY = tileCenterY + recoilDirectionRow    * offsetTiles * CELL_SIZE
       (For the Vortex Eye, also add the existing hover bob to renderWorldY's
        screen projection as already specified in enemy_system.txt.)
    2. Compute spriteDepth, spriteScreenColumnCenter, spriteScreenHeight,
       spriteScreenWidth EXACTLY as today, using the offset render position.
    3. Determine the sprite tint:
         base = distance-shaded color (with dormant dampen / red-alert tint as
                today)
         if hitFlashTimer>0 or dying: blend base toward white by
                (timer/duration) * FLASH_STRENGTH  (death uses 1.0)
       batch.setColor(tint.r, tint.g, tint.b, alpha) once before the column loop.
       alpha = 1 normally; for dying, alpha = deathLurchTimer/DEATH_LURCH_SECONDS.
    4. Draw the sprite columns as today (z-buffer test per column unchanged). For
       dying enemies, additionally lower drawTop toward the floor as the lurch
       progresses (sink) — a single lerp on drawTop before the column loop.
    5. HEALTH BAR PASS (only if healthBarVisibleTimer>0 OR dying, and within
       HEALTH_BAR_MAX_DRAW_DISTANCE_TILES):
         - Reset batch.setColor to opaque (bars ignore shade/tint).
         - Compute barCenterColumn = spriteScreenColumnCenter, barWidthScreen,
           barHeightScreen, barBottomScreen as in HEALTH BAR — VISUAL SPEC.
         - Draw three rects with the 1x1 white texture: frame, track, fill.
           Each rect is drawn column-clipped the SAME way as the sprite — for a
           solid rect this means clamping its left/right screen columns to
           [0, screenWidth) and, for occlusion, testing spriteDepth against the
           z-buffer per column the rect spans. A solid bar can be drawn as a
           handful of clipped sub-rects between z-buffer "windows", OR, simpler,
           per-column 1px-wide draws like the sprite loop (a bar is only tens of
           columns wide — negligible cost). Recommend per-column draw for
           pixel-perfect occlusion parity with the sprite.
         - Apply the bar fade alpha to all three rects.
  ZERO PER-FRAME ALLOCATIONS: the bar rects use the pre-existing 1x1 texture and
  fixed scalars. No new arrays. The dying set can reuse a pre-sized array. Any
  Color objects must be pre-allocated fields on EnemyRenderer (reuse a single
  scratch Color, set its components per enemy) — never `new Color()` in render.

  EnemyRenderer needs a 1x1 white texture. Either inject WallRenderer's
  whitePixelTexture (it already owns one) via a getter, or create+own+dispose a
  1x1 white Texture in EnemyRenderer. Recommend EnemyRenderer owns its own to
  avoid lifetime coupling; dispose it in dispose().

GAMEMATH ADDITIONS (pure, with the mandatory derivation comment block):
  - GameMath.recoilEnvelope(float progress) -> float
        Returns the out-and-back recoil magnitude in [0,1]. Half-sine:
        sin(progress * PI). Peaks at progress=0.5, zero at 0 and 1.
        Edge cases: clamp progress to [0,1]; outside the window callers should
        not invoke it (offset is 0).
  - GameMath.healthBarFillFraction(int currentHealth, int maxHealth) -> float
        = clamp(currentHealth / (float)maxHealth, 0, 1).
        Edge cases: maxHealth <= 0 returns 0 (avoid div-by-zero); negative
        currentHealth clamps to 0.
  - GameMath.fadeAlpha(float timerRemaining, float fadeWindowSeconds) -> float
        = clamp(timerRemaining / fadeWindowSeconds, 0, 1).
        Used for both bar fade and death alpha. Edge: fadeWindowSeconds<=0 -> 1.
  (Health-state COLOR selection can stay a tiny private helper in EnemyRenderer
   since it returns a color, not a number; or expose a GameMath function that
   returns a packed int / fills a scratch Color. Keep GameMath LibGDX-free —
   prefer the EnemyRenderer-local color picker.)

=============================================================================
BALANCE / TUNING NOTES  (ALL VALUES PLACEHOLDERS)
=============================================================================
- HEALTH_BAR_VISIBLE_SECONDS 2.5s: long enough to survive a back-pedal-and-
  reload (one shell reload + one step ~ under a second of real time) so the bar
  is still up when the player decides on the follow-up shot, short enough that a
  fled-from enemy's bar clears before the next encounter.
- The three-band color thresholds (60% / 30%) map cleanly onto the shotgun
  damage table from enemy_system.txt:
    Corruptor (60 HP): point-blank shell = 20 dmg. 60->40 (amber, "wounded",
    crossed 60%), 40->20 (red critical, pulsing — "one more!"), 20->0 (death
    lurch). Three shells, three readable states. The bar TEACHES the breakpoints.
    Vortex Eye (18 HP): a point-blank 20 one-shots it — bar never even appears
    (death feedback only). A dist-2 hit (15) drops it to 3 -> instant red pulse,
    telling the player a single chip finishes it. Exactly the right read.
- HIT_FLASH_SECONDS 0.10 and HIT_RECOIL_SECONDS 0.14: tuned just longer than the
  PLAYER_MOVE_DURATION (0.12s) so the reaction is fully visible even if the
  player immediately chains the next action; but short enough it has resolved
  before the enemy's retaliation lands on the same turn.
- RECOIL_MAX_OFFSET_TILES 0.18: must stay well under 0.5 (half a tile) so the
  sprite never visually crosses into an adjacent tile — that would falsely read
  as movement. 0.18 is a meaty shudder that still clearly stays put.
- Critical pulse speed 8 rad/s ~1.27 Hz — a tense, urgent throb, not a seizure.
- DEATH_LURCH_SECONDS 0.20: long enough to feel the kill, short enough not to
  stall the player rounding the next corner. If it ever feels like it gates
  follow-up fire, note that targeting is already disabled at logical death, so
  the player can fire THROUGH a dying sprite at the enemy behind it on the same
  turn — the lurch is cosmetic and never blocks.
- FLAG: if multiple enemies are damaged in one barrel chain-reaction (future),
  many bars + flashes pop at once. Confirm the screen stays readable; if noisy,
  consider capping simultaneously-drawn bars to the nearest N.

=============================================================================
INTERACTIONS
=============================================================================
1. ENEMY SYSTEM (enemy_system.txt): this is a strict superset of the existing
   damage path. armHitFeedback hangs off applyDamageTo; the dying-set + corpse
   handoff refines the "remove + stamp 'm'" step already specified there. The
   four-state EnemyState (DORMANT/ALERTED/CHASING/ATTACKING) is untouched —
   "dying" is a render-only flag, not a fifth AI state.

2. SHOTGUN / WEAPON SYSTEM (shotgun-weapon-system.txt, pump-action-shotgun.txt):
   the bar is the readout that makes the single-shell + one-step-reload cadence
   meaningful — the player can see whether the chambered shell will finish the
   target. FireResult (already planned to carry hit enemy/damage) can feed a
   future on-screen hitmarker, but the bar/flash are sufficient confirmation on
   their own. The flash also disambiguates which of several lined-up enemies the
   march-shot actually struck (the shot stops at the first; the flash marks it).

3. PROP RENDERER / CORPSES (billboard-prop-sprites.txt): the death lurch hands
   off to the existing 'm' corpse decal. The handoff (delay-stamp vs skip-draw)
   is the one coupling point — see DEATH HANDOFF and OPEN QUESTIONS. The bar
   rendering reuses PropRenderer's exact projection + z-buffer occlusion pattern,
   so far enemies' bars clip behind walls identically to prop occlusion.

4. RED-ALERT PULSE (emergency-red-alert-pulse.txt): the sprite body still
   honors the red-alert tint; the health bar deliberately does NOT (it is a HUD
   readout pinned in world space and must stay legible while the room throbs
   red). The hit flash overrides both the red tint and the dormant dampen for
   its brief window so a hit always reads.

5. PLAYER PAIN FLASH / HEALTH HUD (scanline-crt-hud-marine-vitals.txt): mirror
   image — the player's own damage uses the full-screen pain flash and the HUD
   vitals readout; enemies use floating bars. Same visual language (red =
   hurt, drains right-to-left) keeps the combat HUD coherent. Consider matching
   the bar's critical-red exactly to the HUD's low-health red.

6. FUTURE — HAZARD CHAIN REACTIONS (hazard-chain-reactions-fire-toxic-
   explosive.txt): barrel/fire/toxic damage routes through the same
   applyDamageTo, so chain-reaction kills get flashes, bars, and lurches for
   free — a barrel detonation visibly staggering a pack of Corruptors is a big
   payoff with zero extra feedback code.

7. FUTURE — ENEMY VARIETY / ARMOR: if armored enemies arrive, the bar can gain
   a second segmented overlay (a thin blue armor strip above the health fill),
   slotting cleanly into the three-layer layout without restructuring.

=============================================================================
EDGE CASES
=============================================================================
1. DIV-BY-ZERO ON FILL: maxHealth must be > 0; GameMath.healthBarFillFraction
   guards it (returns 0). An enemy spawned with maxHealth 0 is a data bug, not a
   crash.
2. OVERKILL: a hit dealing more than remaining HP clamps health at 0 (existing
   applyDamage clamp). Bar fill clamps to [0,1]; death feedback fires once.
3. RE-HIT A DYING ENEMY: enemyAt() must return null for dying enemies so a
   second pellet on the same march, or a follow-up turn, cannot re-trigger death
   feedback or "kill" a corpse. The shot passes THROUGH to the next tile/enemy.
4. SPRITE FULLY BEHIND A WALL: if every column of the enemy fails the z-buffer
   test, neither sprite nor bar draws — the bar must run the SAME per-column
   occlusion, never draw "on top of" an occluding wall. A bar floating over a
   wall the enemy is hidden behind is the classic bug; per-column z-test fixes it.
5. SPRITE PARTIALLY OFF-SCREEN: the bar center column can be off-screen while
   part of the bar is visible (enemy at the screen edge). Clamp the bar's column
   span to [0, screenWidth-1]; never skip the whole bar because its center is
   off-screen.
6. RECOIL DIRECTION DEGENERATE: if shooter and enemy share a tile (should be
   impossible — you cannot stand on an enemy — but guard anyway), both recoil
   signs are 0; skip recoil (offset 0), keep flash + bar. Never normalize a zero
   vector.
7. RECOIL ACROSS A WALL: the cosmetic recoil offset could push the render
   position into a wall tile visually. That is fine (it is sub-tile, ~0.18, and
   the sprite is a billboard, not collided) — but the z-buffer test still runs on
   the OFFSET depth, so a sprite shoved a hair toward a near wall simply clips a
   touch more. Acceptable. Do NOT clamp the cosmetic offset against walls (would
   add tile lookups to render for no visible benefit).
8. ENEMY DIES WHILE RED-ALERT PULSE IS AT PEAK: death flash uses FLASH_STRENGTH
   1.0 toward white, which dominates the red tint — the kill reads regardless of
   pulse phase. Verified by spec ordering (flash applied after tint).
9. VERY DISTANT ENEMY DAMAGED (e.g. future ranged/explosive): bar is culled
   past HEALTH_BAR_MAX_DRAW_DISTANCE_TILES even if just damaged. The flash on
   the tiny far sprite is still drawn (cheap, and it confirms the far hit), but
   the unreadable bar is suppressed. Confirm this feels right (OPEN QUESTIONS).
10. MANY SIMULTANEOUS DEATHS (chain reaction): each dying enemy holds its own
    deathLurchTimer; the dying set must be sized to the level's enemy count so a
    full wipe in one explosion never overflows or allocates. Pre-size it.
11. FRAME-RATE INDEPENDENCE: timers decrement by real deltaTime, so feedback
    looks identical at 30 or 144 fps. A single huge deltaTime spike (alt-tab) can
    skip an entire short flash — acceptable; clamp deltaTime upstream if the
    project already does so for other animations.
12. TURN CHAINS DURING FEEDBACK: the player can complete several turns while a
    bar is still fading (turns are fast). Each new hit RE-ARMS the timers to full
    (resets the fade), so a steadily-shot enemy keeps a solid bar — correct.
13. ENEMY KILLED THE SAME TURN IT WAKES: a one-shot kill on a still-dormant
    enemy must still play the death lurch (flash overrides dormant dampen). The
    dormant-dampen shade is irrelevant once the death flash strength is 1.0.

=============================================================================
OPEN QUESTIONS
=============================================================================
1. CORPSE HANDOFF: delay the 'm' stamp until the lurch ends (simplest, no cross-
   renderer coupling, but the tile reads as "live enemy" to AI/LOS for ~0.2s) vs
   stamp immediately + have PropRenderer skip a corpse that overlaps a dying
   enemy (immediate AI/LOS correctness, adds one cross-renderer check)? Recommend
   delay-stamp for v1; the 0.2s window is below one turn and harmless.
2. ALWAYS-SHOW-ON-TARGET: should the enemy currently in the player's facing
   tile-line ALWAYS show its bar (pre-shot intel, DCSS-style inspect) even
   before being hit? Default off (damage-triggered) to keep the screen clean and
   preserve dormant dread. Could be a toggle/accessibility option.
3. FAR-ENEMY BAR CULL: is HEALTH_BAR_MAX_DRAW_DISTANCE_TILES=12 right, or should
   bars show at any range an enemy is visible? Recommend the cull; revisit if
   players want long-range HP intel for ranged kiting.
4. NUMERIC HP READOUT: should a tiny "NN" number ride the bar (needs the font/
   BitmapFont from the HUD work) or is the colored bar enough? Recommend bar-only
   for v1 (cleaner, no font dependency in the world pass); numbers can come with
   the inspect/look action later.
5. DEATH SINK STYLE: sprite sinks straight down into the corpse (current spec)
   vs topples/scales-down? Sink is cheapest and reads fine; toppling needs a
   skew the per-column draw does not currently support. Defer toppling.
6. ENEMY-VS-ENEMY DAMAGE (future friendly-fire from barrels): bars react the
   same; confirm we WANT enemies showing bars when hurt by a barrel they stand
   next to (yes — it is the same readable event). No special-casing needed.
7. MINI-MAP: should a damaged enemy's dot on the mini-map also reflect HP (e.g.
   shrink or darken)? Low priority; the world-space bar is the primary readout.
8. SHOULD THE FLASH HAVE A COLOR PER DAMAGE TYPE (white=ballistic, green=toxic,
   orange=fire) once hazards ship? Nice future hook; v1 is white for all.
