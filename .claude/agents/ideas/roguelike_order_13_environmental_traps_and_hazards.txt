TITLE: Environmental Traps & Hazards
---

OVERVIEW
========
A family of floor/environmental hazards that turn level geometry into a second
opponent: Pressure Plates (spikes on step), Toxic Pools (poison-over-time
floor), Explosive Barrels (shoot to detonate, radius damage), Alarm Sensors
(spawn reinforcements on trip), and Acid Drips (ceiling hazard, periodic random
drop). All are turn-based and tile-based — timing is measured in player turns,
never seconds. Hazards damage marine AND demon alike, so a clever player can
WEAPONIZE them (bait an enemy onto a plate, shoot the barrel by the pack).
Players can disarm most triggered traps by approaching and pressing E (timing,
no tools). Discovered traps are flagged on the minimap. This is the
"information management" and "risk/reward" pillars made physical: the floor is
data; reading it keeps you alive, and using it kills your enemies.

RELATIONSHIP TO EXISTING WORK: this CONSOLIDATES and EXTENDS three existing docs:
pressure-plate-trap-tiles.txt (the trigger/effect primitive, telegraph windows,
detection), hazard-wall-tile.txt and hazard-chain-reactions-fire-toxic-
explosive.txt (the fire/toxic/explosive hazard chain). Reuse those primitives.
THIS doc is the unified ROGUELIKE roster (the five specific hazards above), the
disarm mechanic, the minimap-on-discovery rule, the weaponize-against-enemies
rule, and the procgen placement/balance contract. The explosive barrel ALREADY
exists as prop 'E' with the hazard-wall reaction — this doc folds it into the
hazard family.

THE FIVE HAZARDS
================

1) PRESSURE PLATE (triggered spike trap)
   - A floor trigger tile. On STEP it ARMS; resolution is INSTANT by default:
     spikes fire from adjacent walls into the plate tile, dealing
     SPIKE_DAMAGE (20) to whatever stands on it this turn.
   - Variant (DELAYED): a telegraph (rising click + a seam glow) gives the
     player TRAP_TELEGRAPH_TURNS (1-2) to step OFF before spikes fire — a tense
     escape.
   - DISARMABLE: face the plate from an adjacent tile, press E → disarmed
     permanently (one turn). No tools.
   - WEAPONIZE: bait an enemy onto a known plate; it takes the spike damage.

2) TOXIC POOL (persistent floor hazard)
   - A floor tile (reuses the toxic-hazard tile from the chain doc). Standing on
     it deals POISON_DAMAGE (5) per turn AND applies/refreshes a POISONED status
     (lingers TOXIC_STATUS_TURNS, e.g. 3, dealing a small bleed after you leave).
   - SPREADS slowly to adjacent floor over time (chain-doc spread rule), so
     pools can grow and creep — don't loiter.
   - NOT disarmable (it's a spill, not a device) — you route AROUND it, or wade
     through and pay the toll. Can be a deliberate "shortcut costs HP" choice.
   - WEAPONIZE: lure enemies into pools; they take poison too. Acid Corruptor
     boss pools (roguelike_order_16) reuse this exact tile.

3) EXPLOSIVE BARREL (shootable, radius)
   - The existing 'E' prop (solid, blocks movement). Has barrelHp (low, e.g. 1
     shot from most weapons).
   - When destroyed (shot, OR caught in another explosion → chain reaction): it
     EXPLODES, dealing BARREL_DAMAGE (e.g. 40 at center) to all actors within a
     2-tile radius (player AND enemies), and ignites adjacent hazard-wall/oil
     tiles (existing chain). Damage falls off with distance (center > edge).
   - One telegraph turn optional (a "fizzing" warning) before it blows after
     taking lethal damage, OR instant on destruction — pick instant for v1 to
     match Doom feel; the player learns "don't stand next to it."
   - WEAPONIZE: the canonical Doom move — shoot a barrel amid a pack. Big risk if
     the player is also in range. Barrels chain: one detonation pops neighbors.

4) ALARM SENSOR (trip → reinforcements)
   - A floor trigger tile (or a wall-mounted '#' security-camera-linked sensor).
     On STEP, it TRIPS: a klaxon noise event fires and 2-3 additional enemies
     spawn in the room (via the existing enemy spawn path, on free tiles, not on
     top of the player). Ties into the emergency-red-alert pulse system if
     present (escalates the floor's alert state).
   - DISARMABLE: E adjacent → disabled (one turn). A perceptive player spots the
     sensor's blinking lens and routes around or disarms BEFORE stepping on it.
   - NOT directly weaponizable for damage, but a player can deliberately trip it
     to lure/cluster enemies onto a pressure plate or near a barrel (combo play).

5) ACID DRIP (ceiling hazard, periodic)
   - A hazard tile that drips from the ceiling. Every ACID_DRIP_INTERVAL turns
     (3) it has a chance to DRIP onto its tile (telegraphed the turn before by a
     bulging droplet + a "drip" sound). If an actor is on the tile when it drips,
     DRIP_DAMAGE (15). The pattern is RANDOM per the interval (so it's a
     probabilistic chokepoint, not a fixed-rhythm puzzle) — but always
     telegraphed one turn ahead so it's dodgeable.
   - NOT disarmable (it's a corroded ceiling). You time your passage or wait a
     turn. Creates "dance through the drips" corridors.
   - WEAPONIZE: minor — herd an enemy under a dripping tile.


GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
1. Generation places hazards (visible or hidden per type — barrels and pools are
   always visible; plates and sensors can be hidden until detected).
2. Player advances. DETECTION:
   - Visible hazards (barrels, pools, acid drips) are simply seen in the 3D view.
   - Hidden hazards (plates, sensors): if DETECTED (a perception roll on
     adjacency, a faint seam/blinking lens, or a scanner item), a subtle HUD/
     minimap marker appears. If UNDETECTED, nothing — stepping on it triggers it.
3. On reaching a hazard the player chooses:
   - AVOID (route around).
   - DISARM (face it, press E; plates/sensors only; one turn).
   - WEAPONIZE (bait an enemy onto a plate, shoot a barrel by the pack, lure into
     a pool).
   - ACCEPT THE TOLL (wade the pool / dash the drips for a shortcut).
4. Triggers resolve in turns: INSTANT (same turn), DELAYED (after telegraph
   turns — escapable), PERSISTENT (recurring each turn until disabled/expired).
5. All hazard damage applies to ANY actor on the tile — enemies included. Once a
   hazard is discovered, it is flagged on the minimap for the rest of the floor.


DISARM MECHANIC
===============
- Applies to PRESSURE PLATES and ALARM SENSORS (devices). Toxic pools, acid
  drips, and (in v1) barrels are not "disarmed" (barrels you shoot or avoid).
- Player faces the trap tile from an ADJACENT tile, presses E. Consumes one turn.
  On success the trap is permanently inert (visual: plate seam goes dark, sensor
  lens goes from red to dead). No tools, no skill check in v1 — just the decision
  to spend a turn within reach (which, if enemies are near, is itself risky).
- OPTIONAL future: a disarm failure chance scaled by a stat, where a failed
  disarm TRIPS the trap. v1 = always succeeds (keep it readable). Flag in OQ.


PROCEDURAL PLACEMENT & BUDGET
=============================
- HAZARD DENSITY scales with depth. Early floors: mostly barrels (familiar,
  weaponizable, low threat to a careful player). Mid floors add plates and
  sensors. Deep floors add toxic pools and acid-drip corridors.
- Per-floor budget (placeholders, tune):
    Floors 1-2:  0-2 barrels, 0 traps.
    Floors 3-5:  1-3 barrels, 0-1 plate, 0-1 sensor.
    Floors 6-9:  barrels + 1-2 plates, 0-1 sensor, 0-1 toxic pool.
    Floors 10+:  full mix, incl. acid-drip corridors and creeping pools.
- PLACEMENT RULES (solvability + fairness):
  - Hazards must NEVER fully block the only path. A hazard chokepoint must always
    have a survivable crossing (a delayed/escapable trap, a single-turn drip
    window, or a routable edge around a pool).
  - Place barrels near enemy clusters (so weaponizing is tempting) but also so a
    careless player can blow themselves up — the risk is the point.
  - Plates/sensors near loot (guard a cache — risk/reward) or at chokepoints the
    player must read.
  - Acid drips in corridors, not open rooms (they're directional chokepoints).
  - Don't stack so many hazards that a floor becomes unfair; respect the budget.
- HAZARDS IN BOSS ARENAS: none placed by the trap system; bosses create their
  own hazards (Corruptor acid pools, Baron firewalls) using these same tiles.

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- PRESSURE PLATE: a slightly recessed grated floor tile with a faint seam; when
  detected, a subtle yellow corner-marker. On trigger, spikes snap from the
  adjacent wall bases with a "ka-CHUNK"; on disarm, the seam goes dark.
- TOXIC POOL: bubbling sickly-green floor decal, faint rising vapor, soft green
  tile-light. Spreading edge has a darker creep ring.
- EXPLOSIVE BARREL: existing 'E' orange-red drum; on detonation a bright
  fireball billboard + screen flash + chain to neighbors; leaves a scorch decal.
- ALARM SENSOR: a small wall lens (red blink) or a floor pad with a red LED ring;
  on trip, a spinning klaxon light + red alert tint; on disarm, lens goes dead.
- ACID DRIP: a corroded ceiling stain; the warning turn shows a swelling green
  droplet + "drip" audio; the drop is a quick green splash + sizzle on impact.
- MINIMAP: discovered hazards get distinct small icons (spike / toxic / barrel /
  alarm / drip). Undiscovered hazards are NOT shown.
- HUD: a "POISONED" status indicator while the toxic status is active.


BALANCE NOTES
=============
- All numbers are PLACEHOLDERS — tune against player HP at depth.
- FAIRNESS: every damaging trap is either visible, detectable, or telegraphed one
  turn ahead. A player should be able to understand why they took damage. Instant
  hidden plates are the one exception (the lesson is "search/scan suspicious
  ground") — keep their damage non-lethal-in-one-hit at the depth they appear.
- Hazards damage enemies too: this is a feature. Reward the player who thinks
  spatially (bait, barrel, lure-into-pool). Don't nerf self-damage away — the
  barrel that can save you can also kill you.
- Never let a hazard make a floor unsolvable or force unavoidable damage on the
  only path. Always provide a survivable crossing.
- Density scales with depth so early floors teach one hazard at a time (barrels
  first), deep floors layer them.
- Disarming costs a turn within reach — risky if enemies are near, so it's a real
  decision, not a free "delete the trap" button.


INTERACTIONS
============
- ENEMY SYSTEM: hazards damage enemies; alarm sensors spawn reinforcements;
  enemies should path AROUND known-lethal pools/plates where the AI can (and
  blunder into them where it can't — comedy + player exploitation).
- BOSS FLOORS (roguelike_order_16): Corruptor acid pools and Baron firewalls
  REUSE the toxic-pool and fire-hazard tiles defined here — implement these
  primitives first.
- SECRET ROOMS (roguelike_order_19): a secret can be GUARDED by a plate just
  inside the reveal, or be the safe bypass around a hazard corridor.
- SHOP (roguelike_order_18): shop rooms are SAFE (no hazards) — a breather; a
  shop could sell a "scanner" consumable that auto-detects hazards on a floor.
- EMERGENCY RED ALERT PULSE (emergency-red-alert-pulse.txt): alarm sensors
  escalate the floor's alert state and link to that system.
- LOCKED DOORS (roguelike_order_17): a hazard can guard the room behind a locked
  door (double gate: find the key AND survive the trap for the bonus).
- XP/LEVELING (roguelike_order_11): a Perception stat improves passive hazard
  detection (same stat as secret detection); a "demolitions"/agility stat could
  later add disarm-failure resistance.
- PERMADEATH (roguelike_order_1): hazard deaths feed memorable death lines
  ("KILLED BY YOUR OWN BARREL ON FLOOR 4").


OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
1. Toxic pool tile: reuse 'O' oil decal as damaging, or dedicated 'o' symbol?
   Recommended: dedicated 'o' so cosmetic oil and damaging toxic stay distinct.
2. Alarm sensor: reuse '#' camera + floor trigger (no new symbol) vs dedicated
   tile? Recommended: reuse '#' if the trigger can be wired to it.
3. Barrel detonation: instant on destruction (recommended, Doom feel) vs a
   one-turn fizz telegraph?
4. Disarm: always-succeed (v1, recommended) vs stat-based failure chance (later)?
5. Should enemies be ABLE to trip pressure plates / set off alarms themselves, or
   only the player? Recommended: enemies can take plate/pool/barrel damage but do
   NOT trip alarms (only the player trips alarms) to keep alarm logic simple.
6. POISONED status: minimal counter now vs a full status-effect system? Decide
   with whoever owns status effects.
7. Acid-drip randomness: per-tile independent roll vs a synced corridor rhythm?
   Recommended: independent per-tile roll, always telegraphed one turn ahead.
