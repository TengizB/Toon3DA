CATEGORY: Enemies
TITLE: Turn-Based Enemy System — Corruptor & Vortex Eye
---

=============================================================================
OVERVIEW
=============================================================================
The first living threat in the UAC facility. Two enemy archetypes drive the
core combat loop: the Corruptor (a slow, armored melee bruiser of corrupted
tech) and the Vortex Eye (a fast, fragile floating ranged caster). Both are
dormant until the marine wanders too close or steps into view, after which
they hunt relentlessly and never lose interest. Combat is fully turn-based and
runs entirely in 2D tile space — the billboard sprites you see are cosmetic.

This belongs here because the game currently has walls, doors, props, and a
shotgun that marches into empty tiles and hits nothing. Enemies close that gap:
they make every step a tactical decision (advance into a melee bruiser? retreat
and reload? round a corner to break a ranged line of sight?) and they give the
shotgun something to chew on. It is the smallest possible feature that turns a
walking simulator into a roguelike.

Design lineage: Doom RPG (turn-based marine FPS, alert-on-sight enemies,
stat-based combat), DCSS (tile LOS, "everything acts when you act", monsters
that wake and stay awake), Hades (readable telegraphs, distinct enemy roles).

=============================================================================
GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============================================================================
The world advances by exactly one turn whenever the player completes a
turn-consuming action. A turn-consuming action is: a completed tile MOVE
(W / S / Q / E that actually moved), a completed ROTATE (A / D), a FIRE, or a
door INTERACT. Rotations DO consume a turn (Doom RPG rule: turning takes time;
enemies advance while you reorient). A wall-blocked move that never started
does NOT consume a turn.

ONE FULL TURN, step by step:

1. Player presses W (forward). PlayerController enters MOVING, lerps over
   PLAYER_MOVE_DURATION. NO enemy logic runs during the animation.

2. actionProgress reaches 1.0 → PlayerController snaps the player to the
   target tile and, at the END of finishAction(), fires a single callback:
   turnListener.onPlayerTurnCompleted().

3. World receives the callback and calls enemyManager.takeTurn(playerCol,
   playerRow, level, doorManager).

4. EnemyManager processes ALL enemies in two phases:

   PHASE A — PERCEPTION (every enemy, dormant or not):
     For each DORMANT enemy:
       - If chebyshevDistance(enemy, player) <= ALERT_RADIUS_TILES → ALERT.
       - Else if hasLineOfSight(enemy, player, level) → ALERT.
     Any enemy that became ALERTED this phase is pushed to a "newly alerted"
     scratch list for chain propagation (Phase A2).

   PHASE A2 — CHAIN ALERT (propagation):
     For each newly-alerted enemy, wake every DORMANT enemy within
     CHAIN_ALERT_RADIUS_TILES (Chebyshev). Newly woken enemies are appended to
     the scratch list and also propagate (breadth-first), so a scream travels
     across a connected pack in one turn. A globally toggled boolean
     "anyEnemyAlerted" flips World.gameState.redAlert = true the first time any
     enemy wakes (drives the existing red-alert wall pulse).

   PHASE B — ACTION (alerted enemies only, in spawn-index order):
     For each ALERTED enemy:
       - Recompute Chebyshev distance to the player's CURRENT tile.
       - If distance == 1 (orthogonally OR diagonally adjacent — see note) AND
         enemy.canAttackThisTurn():
             enemy enters ATTACKING for this turn → deal damage to the player
             (Corruptor: melee, always lands if adjacent; Vortex Eye: only if
             it also has LOS — it is a ranged unit and will not melee).
       - Else if enemy is a Vortex Eye AND distance <= VORTEX_EYE_RANGE_TILES
         AND hasLineOfSight(enemy, player): RANGED ATTACK (no movement; it
         hovers and fires). It will kite — see Vortex Eye behavior below.
       - Else (CHASING): step one tile toward the player using the greedy
         tile-pick described in MOVEMENT AI. If no legal step exists, hold.

   IMPORTANT — adjacency definition: movement is cardinal only, so an enemy
   can only ever stand orthogonally adjacent to the player after a legal chase
   step. We still test Chebyshev == 1 (which includes diagonals) for the MELEE
   attack gate because two enemies funneling a corridor can leave one on a
   diagonal; a diagonal melee swing is allowed (the bruiser has long arms).
   Cardinal-only is a MOVEMENT constraint, not an attack-reach constraint.

5. After Phase B, EnemyManager checks player.isDead(). If the player's HP hit
   0, World transitions to a death/game-over state (out of scope here; see
   OPEN QUESTIONS). Otherwise control returns to the player for the next turn.

6. Player FIRE turn variant: when the player fires, the shotgun marches tiles
   in the facing direction (existing Shotgun.marchShot). On the FIRST tile that
   contains a living enemy, EnemyManager.applyDamage() reduces that enemy's HP
   by damageAtDistance(distance). If HP <= 0 the enemy dies: it is removed from
   the active list and a corpse decal ('m') is stamped into the level grid at
   its tile (reuses the existing PropRenderer corpse sprite). The FIRE still
   triggers onPlayerTurnCompleted() AFTER the shot resolves, so surviving
   enemies retaliate on the same turn the player shoots.

=============================================================================
PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=============================================================================
- DREAD ON APPROACH: dormant enemies are visible as still billboards. The
  player can SEE a Corruptor slumped in an unlit room before it wakes. Crossing
  the ALERT_RADIUS line is a committed decision — "do I have a shell chambered
  before I round this corner?"

- THE SCREAM: chain alert means waking one enemy in a pack wakes the room. The
  red-alert wall pulse (already implemented) is the audiovisual payoff: the
  facility goes red and you know you are now being hunted.

- KITING DUEL vs THE BRUISER: the two enemy types create opposite pressures.
  The Vortex Eye punishes standing still in open sightlines (retreat, break LOS
  behind a wall/column). The Corruptor punishes retreating in straight lines
  (it is slow but relentless and hits hard up close). Mixed encounters force
  the player to manage both at once — back-pedal from the bruiser while a
  corner blocks the eye.

- READABLE DEATH: because everything is turn-based and adjacency-gated, the
  player can always reconstruct why they died. "I rotated to face the Corruptor
  — that cost a turn — and the Vortex Eye I forgot about got a free shot." The
  player made a mistake they can name. That is the DCSS contract.

- THE DECISION EACH TURN: every move is a question. Rotating to aim costs a
  turn the enemy uses to close. Reloading the single-shell shotgun costs a
  step. Standing in a lit tile is safer to see but worse to be seen in. There
  is no free action.

=============================================================================
MOVEMENT AI
=============================================================================
Greedy "minimize distance, never enter a solid tile" stepper. No A* in v1 —
keep it cheap and predictable (predictability is a feature; the player should
be able to anticipate the step).

stepTowardPlayer(enemy, playerCol, playerRow, level, doorManager, occupancy):
  Candidate steps (CARDINAL ONLY, in this fixed tie-break order):
    1. East  (+1, 0)
    2. West  (-1, 0)
    3. North (0, +1)
    4. South (0, -1)
  For each candidate target tile (enemyCol+dCol, enemyRow+dRow):
    - Reject if level.isBlockedAt(targetCol, targetRow, doorManager) is true
      (walls, solid props, closed doors).
    - Reject if occupancy[targetCol][targetRow] is occupied by ANOTHER enemy
      (no stacking — two enemies may not share a tile).
    - Reject if the target tile is the player's tile (they attack instead of
      stepping onto the player; this branch is only reached when not adjacent,
      so this is a safety guard).
    - Otherwise compute Manhattan distance from the candidate to the player.
  Choose the legal candidate with the SMALLEST Manhattan distance. Ties broken
  by the fixed E/W/N/S order above (deterministic — important for replay and
  for the player learning the AI). If NO legal candidate reduces or holds
  distance, the enemy may still take the best non-increasing legal step; if
  even that is impossible (fully boxed in), it HOLDS (waits) this turn.

  Greedy-stuck mitigation (concave walls): if the chosen step does NOT strictly
  reduce Manhattan distance for STUCK_TURNS_BEFORE_WIGGLE consecutive turns,
  allow a "wiggle": pick a random legal cardinal step (seeded RNG) to slide
  along the wall. This is a cheap escape from the classic greedy local minimum
  without paying for A*. Flag: ENEMY_GREEDY_WIGGLE_ENABLED.

OCCUPANCY: EnemyManager maintains a transient boolean[width][height] (or a
HashSet of packed int tile keys) rebuilt at the start of takeTurn() so that
enemies stepping earlier in the loop block enemies stepping later in the SAME
turn. Update the entry as each enemy commits its step so the pack does not pile
into one tile. Pre-allocate this grid in the EnemyManager constructor — NEVER
allocate inside takeTurn() or render().

SPEED CONTROL (slow Corruptor, fast Vortex Eye): each enemy has a movesPerTurn
budget expressed as a "cooldown" so we keep one-action-per-turn semantics:
  - Vortex Eye: acts every turn (moveCooldownTurns = 1).
  - Corruptor:  acts every OTHER turn for MOVEMENT (moveEveryNTurns = 2) — it
    increments an internal turnCounter and only steps when turnCounter %
    moveEveryNTurns == 0. ATTACKS are NOT throttled — a Corruptor that is
    already adjacent swings every turn. This makes the Corruptor easy to
    out-walk in the open but lethal once it has you cornered.

=============================================================================
ENEMY AI STATE MACHINE
=============================================================================
States (enum EnemyState in entity package): DORMANT, ALERTED, CHASING,
ATTACKING.

  DORMANT  — initial. Not in the action loop. Renders as a still billboard.
             Only the perception phase touches it.
  ALERTED  — transient "just woke" marker. On the same turn it woke, an enemy
             immediately resolves into CHASING or ATTACKING in Phase B; ALERTED
             mainly exists as the flag that gates the action loop and feeds
             chain propagation. Once ALERTED, an enemy NEVER returns to DORMANT.
  CHASING  — alerted and not in attack range this turn → it steps toward the
             player (subject to its move-cooldown).
  ATTACKING— alerted and in attack range this turn → it deals damage. This is a
             per-turn visual/logical pose; next turn it re-evaluates back to
             CHASING or ATTACKING.

Transitions:
  DORMANT → ALERTED      : proximity (Chebyshev <= ALERT_RADIUS_TILES) OR LOS
                           OR chain alert from a nearby waking enemy.
  ALERTED → ATTACKING    : in attack range AND (LOS for ranged) this turn.
  ALERTED → CHASING      : alerted but not able to attack this turn.
  CHASING ↔ ATTACKING    : re-evaluated every turn based on range/LOS.
  (no transition ever returns to DORMANT — "stays alerted for the entire
   encounter" per the spec.)

A single boolean field "alerted" plus a per-turn computed "actionPose" is the
minimal representation; the four-value enum is recommended for readability and
to drive distinct sprites/animations later.

=============================================================================
LINE OF SIGHT ALGORITHM
=============================================================================
Symmetric tile LOS using a Bresenham-style supercover walk in TILE SPACE
(reuse the project's existing DDA conventions; do NOT introduce float world
coords here). Sight is blocked by any solid tile between the two endpoints.

hasLineOfSight(fromCol, fromRow, toCol, toRow, level, doorManager) -> boolean:
  - Walk the integer grid from (fromCol,fromRow) to (toCol,toRow).
  - For each intermediate tile STRICTLY between the endpoints:
      if level.isWall(cell) OR a CLOSED door OR a sight-blocking solid prop
      (lockers 'L', terminals 'T', crates 'C', barrels) → return false.
      (Decals 'm','s','.','O' and floor-light tiles never block sight.)
  - The endpoints themselves are not tested (the enemy's own tile and the
    player's own tile are always "visible").
  - Cap the walk at LOS_MAX_RANGE_TILES (default 16, matches RAY_MAX_LENGTH_
    CELLS scale) — beyond that, return false without walking (cheap reject via
    Chebyshev pre-check).

Implementation note: a clean integer supercover (the variant that includes
every tile the segment touches, not just the thin Bresenham line) avoids the
"shoot through a diagonal wall seam" bug. Provide it as a GameMath helper:

  GameMath.tileLineOfSightClear(int fromColumn, int fromRow, int toColumn,
      int toRow, TileSolidTest solidTest) -> boolean

where TileSolidTest is a tiny functional interface (boolean isSolid(int
column, int row)) so GameMath stays free of Level/DoorManager imports and pure.
EnemyManager passes a lambda: (c, r) -> level.isWall(level.getCell(c,r)) ||
doorManager.blocksSight(c, r) || Level.isPropSolid(level.getCell(c,r)).

Cost: O(maxRange) integer ops per dormant enemy per turn — trivial for the
expected enemy counts (< ~30 per level). Optional optimization: only run LOS
for dormant enemies whose Chebyshev distance <= LOS_MAX_RANGE_TILES.

=============================================================================
ALERT PROPAGATION (CHAIN ALERT)
=============================================================================
Breadth-first wake within a connected pack, resolved in a single turn:

  1. Phase A produces a queue of "seed" enemies that woke via proximity/LOS.
  2. Drain the queue. For each woken enemy E, scan all DORMANT enemies; any
     within CHAIN_ALERT_RADIUS_TILES (Chebyshev) of E becomes ALERTED and is
     enqueued. (Chain alert does NOT require LOS — it represents a scream/roar
     traveling through the room, including around corners within the radius.
     This is intentional and Doom-RPG-flavored.)
  3. Continue until the queue empties. Because each enemy is woken at most once,
     this terminates in O(enemyCount^2) worst case — fine for v1 counts.
  4. The first wake of the encounter sets World.gameState.redAlert = true (the
     red pulse never turns off once combat starts, matching "stays alerted").

Optional flavor: a freshly chain-woken enemy that was NOT a direct seed could
spend its first turn "rousing" (HOLD, no action) to telegraph the wake to the
player. Flag CHAIN_WAKE_COSTS_A_TURN (default false for v1 — keep it punchy).

TWO ENEMY TYPES
=============================================================================
CORRUPTOR  (texture: assets/textures/enemies/enemy_corruptor.png)
  Role: slow, tanky, melee bruiser. The wall you cannot outrun forever.
  - maxHealth        : 60           (placeholder; ~3 adjacent shotgun blasts)
  - moveEveryNTurns  : 2            (steps every other turn)
  - attackType       : MELEE
  - attackDamage     : 14           (placeholder)
  - attackRange      : 1 tile (Chebyshev == 1)
  - attackEveryNTurns: 1            (swings every turn once adjacent)
  - heightMultiplier : 0.95         (nearly full wall height — imposing)
  - alertRadius      : ALERT_RADIUS_TILES (shared default 4)
  Behavior: when alerted, marches straight at the player on its slow cadence.
  Once adjacent, hammers every turn. Dangerous in corridors and corners; soft
  in open rooms where the player can circle it.

VORTEX EYE (texture: assets/textures/enemies/enemy_vortex_eye.png)
  Role: fast, fragile, ranged caster. Punishes open sightlines and standing
  still.
  - maxHealth        : 18           (placeholder; one solid point-blank shell)
  - moveEveryNTurns  : 1            (moves every turn — fast)
  - attackType       : RANGED
  - attackDamage     : 8            (placeholder; chips you down)
  - rangedRange      : VORTEX_EYE_RANGE_TILES (default 5)
  - meleeRange       : it does NOT melee (floats away if cornered)
  - attackEveryNTurns: 1
  - heightMultiplier : 0.55         (floats at eye level; smaller silhouette)
  - hoverBobEnabled  : true         (cosmetic vertical bob — see VISUAL DESIGN)
  Behavior (KITING): each turn, if it has LOS and distance is within
  [VORTEX_EYE_KITE_MIN_TILES, rangedRange] → FIRE (no move). If too close
  (distance < KITE_MIN, default 2) → step AWAY from the player (greedy maximize
  Manhattan distance, same legality rules) AND fire if it still has LOS after
  evaluating. If too far or LOS blocked → step toward the player to re-acquire.
  It will never voluntarily stand adjacent.

Ranged attack resolution (Vortex Eye): hitscan in tile space — if LOS is clear
to the player, the shot lands (no projectile entity, no dodge; turn-based).
This keeps the model simple and readable: break LOS = take no damage. A
cosmetic tracer/orb flash can be drawn for one frame (see VISUAL DESIGN) but it
does not travel over multiple turns in v1.

      where interface EnemyHitTarget { Enemy enemyAt(int col, int row); }

        enemies.applyDamageTo(enemy, dealt);

WEAPON DAMAGE TABLE (existing shotgun, coeff 0.18, floor 0.15):
  dist 1: 20  | dist 2: 15 | dist 3: 11 | dist 4: 7 | dist 5: 4
  vs CORRUPTOR (60 HP): ~3 point-blank shells (20+20+20). Reload is one step
    between each → the player back-pedals-and-reloads while the slow Corruptor
    (every-other-turn mover) keeps pace but cannot close faster than the player
    retreats. Tense but winnable.
  vs VORTEX EYE (18 HP): one point-blank (20) kills; dist 2 (15) leaves it at
    3 — chip it again or it kites. Encourages closing the gap on the eye fast,
    which is exactly the wrong instinct against a ranged unit guarded by a
    Corruptor. Good tension.

RENDERING
=============================================================================
Enemies render as billboard sprites through a NEW renderer that mirrors
PropRenderer almost exactly (same projection math, same z-buffer occlusion,
same painter's-algorithm depth sort, ZERO per-frame allocations).

EnemyRenderer (render package) responsibilities:
  - Holds the two enemy Textures (loaded once in constructor via the same
    loadOrGenerate pattern PropRenderer uses; both PNGs already exist so no
    procedural fallback is needed, but keep the fallback for safety).
  - setPlayerState(worldX, worldY, dirX, dirY, fovRadians) — identical body to
    PropRenderer.setPlayerState (computes planeX/planeY).
  - setAlertPulse(pulse) — for red-alert tinting consistency.
  - render(camera): pulls the live enemy list from EnemyManager (passed in
    constructor or via a setter that hands over the List<Enemy> reference held
    by EnemyManager — NOT a copy). For each LIVING enemy:
      * Use enemy world center (enemy.tileColumn*CELL_SIZE + CELL_SIZE/2, same
        for row) as the billboard position. While a tween between tiles is
        desired for smooth movement, v1 can snap (sprite jumps one tile per
        turn) OR lerp using the same actionProgress idea as the player (see
        OPEN QUESTIONS — recommend a per-enemy renderX/renderY lerped over the
        turn animation window for polish).
      * Reuse GameMath.spriteDepth, spriteScreenColumnCenter, spriteScreenHeight,
        wallShade exactly as PropRenderer does.
      * Apply enemy.heightMultiplier as the prop height multiplier analog.
      * Per-column z-buffer test against wallRenderer.getZBufferAt(column) — an
        enemy behind a wall column is culled per-column, just like props.
      * Depth-sort all visible enemies farthest-first (reuse the insertion-sort
        from PropRenderer). Pre-allocate sortedIndices/sortedDepths sized to the
        spawn count.
  - DORMANT vs ALERTED tint: dormant enemies render slightly darker / desaturated
    (multiply shade by DORMANT_SHADE_DAMPEN, e.g. 0.7) so a woken enemy visibly
    "lights up". Alerted enemies get the standard red-alert tint blend.

Render order in World.render (insert enemies in the same band as props, after
walls so the z-buffer is populated, before the weapon HUD):
   1. floorCeilingRenderer.render
   2. wallRenderer.render            (populates z-buffer)
   3. propRenderer.render
   4. enemyRenderer.render           <-- NEW (props and enemies both billboard;
                                          either order works since both z-test
                                          per column; draw enemies after props
                                          so a corpse-prop doesn't overdraw a
                                          live enemy on the same tile)
   5. weaponHudRenderer.render
   6. levelRenderer.render (mini-map)
   7. player.render (mini-map dot)

MINI-MAP: LevelRenderer can optionally draw enemy dots (red = alerted, dim gray
= dormant) — low priority, list under OPEN QUESTIONS.

=============================================================================
LEVEL PLACEMENT
=============================================================================
New spawn tile characters (walkable floor underneath; the spawn char is
consumed at load and replaced with a lit floor ' ' in the grid so the tile is
walkable and the enemy is tracked separately by EnemyManager):
   '1'  → Corruptor spawn
   '2'  → Vortex Eye spawn

LevelLoader change: when it encounters '1' or '2', it records the spawn
(column, row, type) into a list exposed via Level.getEnemySpawns() (return a
List<EnemySpawn> where EnemySpawn { int column; int row; EnemyType type; }),
THEN writes ' ' (lit floor) into the grid cell so walls/movement/raycasting
treat it as normal floor. EnemyManager reads Level.getEnemySpawns() in its
constructor to build the initial enemy list.

Level.isWall / isProp / isBlockedAt do NOT need '1'/'2' cases because they are
rewritten to ' ' at load. (If a designer prefers the spawn char to survive in
the grid, add them to a "treated as floor" branch — but the rewrite approach is
cleaner and keeps the renderers untouched.)

Placement guidance for level designers (game-level-designer agent):
  - Corruptors ('1'): guarding chokepoints, doorways, dead-end loot rooms, or
    blocking the only path forward. They are obstacles — place them where the
    player must engage, often on 'u' unlit tiles for ambush dread.
  - Vortex Eyes ('2'): in OPEN rooms with long sightlines, ideally paired with
    columns ('P') the player can use to break LOS. Place 2-3 around a room's
    perimeter so they triangulate. Avoid placing eyes in tight corridors where
    their ranged advantage is wasted.
  - Mixed packs: 1 Corruptor + 2 Vortex Eyes in an open room behind a door is
    the signature encounter — the player opens the door, the proximity/LOS wake
    chains the whole room, and they must manage the bruiser while breaking the
    eyes' lines. Use sparingly; it is a set-piece.
  - Density: target 6-12 enemies per level for v1. Do not exceed ~30 (perf and
    difficulty).

DATA FLOW SUMMARY (the turn-notification callback pattern):
  Input → PlayerController.update advances the action; on completion
  finishAction() fires turnListener.onPlayerTurnCompleted()
  → World.onPlayerTurnCompleted() calls enemyManager.takeTurn(playerTile,
  player) → EnemyManager runs perception/chain/action phases, mutating enemy
  positions, player.health, and the level grid (corpses) → control returns to
  the player. Rendering is independent: World.render() draws the current enemy
  list every frame via EnemyRenderer; AI only runs on completed turns.

VISUAL DESIGN
=============================================================================
- Both enemies use their existing PNGs, drawn as distance-shaded billboards via
  the same wallShade falloff as walls/props for lighting consistency.
- CORRUPTOR: tall (0.95x wall height), grounded (drawBottom at floor line like
  props). Dormant: shaded * 0.7 (a dim hulk in the dark). On wake: snaps to full
  shade + the red-alert tint blend pulses across it. Optional 2-frame idle/step
  animation later (sprite sheet) — v1 is a single static frame.
- VORTEX EYE: smaller (0.55x), floats — render its drawBottom raised by a
  HOVER_OFFSET (e.g. drawBottom + lineHeight * 0.25) so it sits at eye level,
  not on the floor. Cosmetic bob: add sin(facilityTime * BOB_SPEED) * BOB_AMP
  to its render Y each frame (pure render-time, no turn cost, no allocation).
- RANGED ATTACK FLASH (Vortex Eye fires): on the turn it attacks, tint the
  sprite brighter for one render pass OR draw a one-frame cyan orb decal at the
  player-facing column. Keep it to existing batch draws; no new entities.
- PLAYER PAIN FLASH: when the player takes damage, push a translucent red
  full-screen quad for a short real-time fade (HUD layer, drawn over the world,
  under the mini-map). Intensity scales with damage taken. Reuse whitePixel /
  a 1x1 texture; no allocation in render (pre-create).
- HEALTH HUD: numeric "HEALTH 100" readout near the existing ammo readout
  (WeaponHudRenderer or a new HealthHudRenderer). Turns red and could pulse
  below 25. Specify exact placement with libgdx-specialist.
- DEATH: enemy collapses to the existing corpse decal 'm' (reuses PropRenderer
  corpse sprite). A blood decal '.' could also be stamped on an adjacent floor
  tile for splatter flavor (optional).
- MINI-MAP (optional): alerted enemy = solid red dot, dormant = dim gray dot,
  drawn in LevelRenderer's overlay pass.

BALANCE NOTES   (ALL VALUES ARE PLACEHOLDERS — tune in playtest)
=============================================================================
- Shotgun-vs-Corruptor math (above) targets ~3 point-blank shells with a
  forced one-step reload between each, so a 1v1 Corruptor fight is a fighting
  retreat the player wins if they manage spacing. If it feels too easy, raise
  CORRUPTOR_MAX_HEALTH to 80 (4 shells) or reduce its moveEveryNTurns to 1 (it
  matches player speed, no out-walking).
- Vortex Eye at 18 HP dies to one point-blank shell — intended; it is a glass
  cannon. Its threat is COMPOUNDING chip damage when ignored, not burst.
- ALERT_RADIUS_TILES = 4: large enough that the player rarely sneaks past, but
  leaves room for a careful approach to pick the first engagement. CHAIN radius
  5 > alert radius so a woken seed reliably wakes its immediate packmates.
- Rotations cost a turn: this is the single most impactful balance lever. It
  makes facing an enemy a real cost (the eye gets a free shot while you turn).
  If testers find it too punishing, consider making the FIRST rotation after an
  enemy wakes "free" — but default to costing a turn (Doom RPG fidelity).
- Player 100 HP vs Corruptor 14/hit = ~7 adjacent hits to die; vs Vortex Eye
  8/hit = ~12 hits. A mixed pack should kill an unprepared player in ~5-6 turns
  of standing still — fast enough to be scary, slow enough to recover from one
  mistake.
- Enemy count 6-12/level. The chain-alert set-piece room should be the deadliest
  single moment; rest of the level is 1-2 enemy skirmishes.
- FLAG: confirm whether decals/floor-lights block LOS (they must NOT). FLAG:
  confirm CLOSED doors block both movement (yes, already) and sight (yes).

=============================================================================
INTERACTIONS
=============================================================================
1. DOORS (DoorManager): closed doors block enemy MOVEMENT (via isBlockedAt) and
   block LOS (new DoorManager.blocksSight). An OPEN door lets enemies path and
   see through — opening a door to fight can let a far pack see you. Enemies in
   v1 do NOT open doors themselves (they path around or wait); this is a
   deliberate simplification and a design lever (doors are a safe wall you can
   close behind you — see OPEN QUESTIONS on enemy door use).

2. RED ALERT / WALL PULSE (GameState.redAlert + existing alertPulse): the first
   enemy to wake flips redAlert on permanently for the encounter, lighting the
   facility red. EnemyRenderer and the existing wall/floor/prop renderers all
   already consume alertPulse, so the whole scene reacts cohesively when combat
   starts. No new visual system needed — enemies plug into the existing one.

3. SHOTGUN / WEAPON SYSTEM (Weapon, Shotgun, FireResult, PlayerInventory): the
   shotgun's stubbed enemy-hit path (Shotgun.java:45-51) is finally satisfied.
   The single-shell + one-step reload cadence is the heart of combat pacing —
   it forces the back-pedal-and-reload rhythm that the slow Corruptor is
   designed around. FireResult should be extended to carry the hit enemy /
   damage dealt for future HUD feedback.

4. PROPS (PropRenderer, Level prop chars): explosive barrels ('E') become a
   tactical tool — a future "shoot the barrel near a Corruptor" interaction
   (barrels are already solid and block LOS/movement). Solid props also create
   LOS-breaking cover the Vortex Eye must path around. Corpses ('m') and blood
   ('.') stamped on enemy death reuse the existing prop sprites and renderer.

5. FLOOR LIGHTING (lit/normal/unlit/flickering tiles): unlit 'u' tiles are
   ideal Corruptor ambush spots (dormant + dim shade = nearly invisible until
   it wakes). The game's existing lighting becomes a stealth-vs-visibility
   tension layer for enemy placement.

6. LEVEL GENERATION (future roguelike generator): the '1'/'2' spawn chars and
   getEnemySpawns() API give a procedural generator a clean contract for
   placing enemies. EnemyManager builds purely from spawns, so a generator only
   needs to emit the two chars onto floor tiles.

OPEN QUESTIONS
=============================================================================
1. SMOOTH ENEMY MOVEMENT: snap one tile per turn (simple, readable, "Doom RPG"
   crisp) vs lerp renderX/renderY over the player's action-animation window
   (smoother, more work, risk of desync if turns chain fast)? Recommend snap
   for v1, add lerp as a polish pass.
2. GAME OVER FLOW: when player.isDead(), what happens? A death screen, instant
   restart, or a roguelike run-end summary? GameState needs a gameOver flag and
   a Screen transition — out of scope for this enemy doc; flag for screen work.
3. CORPSES: stamp the corpse as a level-grid prop 'm' (simple, but the corpse
   then occupies the enemy's death tile permanently and can't overlap a live
   enemy that walks onto it) vs an EnemyRenderer-owned "death frame" with a
   fade? Recommend grid 'm' for v1.
4. ENEMY DOORS: should enemies open doors (path through) or treat them as walls?
   v1 = treat closed doors as walls (lets the player use doors defensively).
   Revisit once doors-as-tactics feels stale.
5. RANGED PROJECTILE vs HITSCAN: v1 Vortex Eye is hitscan (LOS = instant hit).
   A future multi-turn projectile (an orb that travels one tile/turn and can be
   out-walked or blocked) would add depth but needs a projectile entity and
   render pass. Defer.
6. DIAGONAL MELEE: confirm with the team that allowing a Chebyshev-1 (diagonal)
   melee swing is acceptable given cardinal-only movement. Alternative: strict
   Manhattan-1 (orthogonal only) melee — cleaner rule, but a diagonally-funneled
   enemy then can't hit you, which may feel like a bug. Recommend Chebyshev-1.
7. ALERT DECAY: spec says enemies never go dormant again. Confirm there is no
   "lose the player and give up" behavior desired (DCSS has wandering/sleep
   re-entry; Doom RPG does not). Default: permanent alert per spec.
8. PERF CEILING: O(enemyCount^2) chain alert + per-enemy LOS is fine at <30
   enemies. If the generator ever wants 100+, switch chain alert to a spatial
   grid query and gate LOS behind Chebyshev pre-checks (already noted).
