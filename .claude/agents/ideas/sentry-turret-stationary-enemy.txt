TITLE: UAC Sentry Turret — Stationary Line-of-Fire Enemy
---

OVERVIEW
========
The Sentry Turret is a stationary, ceiling- or floor-mounted UAC defense gun
that the facility's hacked security system has turned against you. Unlike every
other enemy planned, it never moves — it bolts to a single tile and rotates to
track the player, firing down a clean cardinal line of sight. This makes it the
game's first true "area-denial" threat: it does not chase, it controls a
corridor. The player's job is not to outrun it but to break its line of fire,
flank to its blind arc, or destroy it before it shreds them.

The turret is built specifically for the tile/cardinal architecture. Because
the player moves and faces in four cardinal directions, the turret's firing line
is always a straight run of tiles along a row or column. Line of sight is a
trivial integer tile walk — no float math, no free-aim. The turret only fires
when the player is on its row or column AND no wall/door blocks the line. Step
out of the lane and you are instantly safe; step back in and it punishes you.
This reads instantly to the player: "stay out of the red lane."

Thematically it is dripping UAC: a sleek charcoal pod with a glowing targeting
laser and a corporate voice line ("UAC Sentry online. Hostile detected.
Compliance is mandatory."). It can be hacked (with sufficient IQ / a terminal
nearby) to flip allegiance, or simply blown up. It is the natural inhabitant of
chokepoints, vault approaches, and the lane outside a keycard door.

GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
1. A turret occupies one tile (it is solid — blocks movement and raycasting like
   a column, but is a destructible entity, not a wall). It has a facing and an
   "alert" state: DORMANT, TRACKING, or FIRING.
2. On each enemy turn the turret evaluates: is the player on my row or column,
   within range, with an unobstructed cardinal line (no wall, closed door, or
   solid prop between us)?
   - If NO: it rotates one 90° step toward the player's general direction (slow
     tracking) and stays DORMANT/TRACKING. It does not fire.
   - If YES and it is already facing the player's lane: it enters FIRING and
     deals ranged damage this turn (a hitscan down the lane).
   - If YES but not yet facing: it spends this turn rotating to face the lane
     (TRACKING) and fires NEXT turn — giving the player one turn of warning.
3. The player sees the threat through (a) the turret's targeting laser drawn
   down its facing lane on the mini-map and in 3D, and (b) a HUD/audio warning
   when they enter a tracked lane.
4. Player options to survive:
   - STEP OUT of the lane (one perpendicular move = instant safety; turret loses
     line of sight and reverts to TRACKING).
   - APPROACH FROM THE BLIND ARC: the turret rotates only one quarter-turn per
     enemy turn, so a player who circles fast enough can reach an adjacent tile
     in its blind arc and attack melee with reduced risk.
   - DESTROY IT: it has modest HP; explosive barrels in the lane are a clean
     one-shot. A closing door also fully blocks it.
   - HACK IT (optional, see INTERACTIONS): convert it to fire on enemies.
5. When destroyed it leaves a smoking wreck prop and may drop plasma cells
   (ties into loot/ransack).

PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
The turret rewires how the player reads a room. Open corridors stop being safe
running lanes and become kill-zones with a clear, fair tell. The one-turn
"warning shot" (rotate this turn, fire next) makes deaths feel earned: you saw
the laser swing onto you and chose to stand there. It introduces positional
puzzle-solving into combat — "how do I cross this hall?" — answered by timing,
cover (columns, doors), or sacrificing a barrel. It also creates great synergy
moments: luring a chasing demon into the turret's lane so the turret does your
work, or shooting an explosive barrel sitting in the lane to kill both.

The decision it forces: "Do I spend resources to kill it, or do I just route
around its lane and leave it alive behind me?"

TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
Fits cleanly into the planned enemy framework (enemy_system.txt). The turret is
an enemy with movementPattern = STATIONARY and a specialized targeting routine.

New tile symbol: `'G'` = Sentry turret spawn (gun). Add to LevelLoader parsing;
on load, spawn a turret entity at that tile and treat the tile as floor for the
grid (the entity itself is solid via the entity collision layer, mirroring how
solid props block movement). `Level.isWall` stays unchanged — solidity for
entities is handled entity-side, consistent with barrels/crates being props.

New / modified classes:
- `entity/SentryTurret.java` (or an enemy-type config within the enemy system):
  fields tileColumn, tileRow, facingX, facingY (cardinal unit vector),
  alertState (enum), health. Method `takeTurn(playerColumn, playerRow, level,
  doorManager)` implements the rotate/track/fire logic above.
- `GameMath.java`: add a pure helper
  `cardinalLineOfSightClear(fromColumn, fromRow, toColumn, toRow, level,
  doorManager)` → boolean. It returns false immediately if the two points are
  not on the same row or column; otherwise it walks the integer tiles between
  them and returns false if any is a wall, a closed door, or a solid prop.
  Include the required Formula/Derivation/Edge-cases comment block (edge cases:
  same tile, adjacent tiles, diagonal request → not aligned → false).
- `GameMath.java`: `quarterTurnToward(currentFacingX, currentFacingY,
  targetColumnDelta, targetRowDelta)` → returns the cardinal facing one 90° step
  closer to the target (for slow tracking).
- `EnemyRenderer.java`: render the turret billboard (pod + barrel) and its
  targeting laser as a thin emissive stripe along the lane (the laser can be a
  cheap colored quad drawn per visible lane tile through the existing billboard
  path — no render() allocations; pre-allocate).
- `LevelRenderer.java`: draw the turret and a colored line down its current lane
  on the mini-map (TRACKING = amber, FIRING = red).
- Enemy turn driver / GlobalTickEventSystem: turret takeTurn() runs in the enemy
  phase like any enemy.

All logic is integer tile space; the 3D laser is cosmetic only.

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- Billboard: a compact charcoal-and-steel pod with a stubby cylindrical barrel
  and a single red optical "eye" lens. Mount it slightly above floor center so
  it reads as a fixed emplacement, ~0.7x wall height. A faint cyan UAC stencil
  on the housing.
- Targeting laser: a thin translucent line running from the barrel straight down
  its facing lane to the first obstruction. Amber/orange while TRACKING (low
  alpha, "searching"), snaps to bright red and thickens for one frame-burst when
  FIRING (the muzzle also flashes). The laser is the player's primary read.
- Eye lens color mirrors state: dim red dormant, pulsing amber tracking, hard
  white-red flash on fire.
- Mini-map: turret as a small square with a short colored line indicating lane
  and state, so the player can plan routes from the map alone.
- Death: a quick orange spark burst, then a desaturated "wreck" billboard
  (barrel drooping) that remains as cover/flavor and can be ransacked for cells.

BALANCE NOTES
=============
- The one-turn warning is the core fairness lever. If deaths feel cheap, raise
  TURRET_TRACK_TURNS_BEFORE_FIRE to 2; if it feels toothless, lower to 0 in
  later/harder floors only.
- Damage should sting but not one-shot a healthy player (8 dmg vs ~50-100 HP).
  Standing in the lane for 3+ turns should be lethal — punishing greed, not
  punishing a single mistake.
- Range 8 tiles keeps lanes long enough to matter without covering whole levels.
- HP low enough that a shotgun blast or one barrel kills it, so the player
  always has a clean answer if they choose to spend resources.
- Placement guidance for generators/level designers: one turret per chokepoint,
  never two covering the same lane (that removes the "step out" answer). Pair
  with at least one piece of cover (column/door) on the approach.
- All numbers above are placeholders pending playtest.

INTERACTIONS
============
1. Explosive barrels & hazard chains (hazard-chain-reactions): a barrel in the
   turret's lane is the cleanest counter — shoot it to destroy the turret. The
   turret can also detonate barrels by firing at the player past one.
2. Doors (doors.txt, keycard-doors): a closed/closing door fully blocks the
   lane, so doors become tactical shields. A turret guarding a keycard door is a
   classic setup.
3. Enemy AI (enemy_system.txt): mobile enemies wandering into the lane get hit
   by friendly fire — the player can weaponize the turret by kiting demons
   through it. Turret targeting must (optionally) ignore other enemies unless
   hacked, to keep behavior readable.
4. Loot/ransack system (inventory-loot-ransack): the wreck drops plasma cells,
   rewarding the resource spent to kill it.
5. Noise system (noise-propagation): turret fire is loud and can wake nearby
   dormant enemies — destroying it quietly (melee from the blind arc) preserves
   stealth.
6. Columns `'P'` and cover props: provide the perpendicular cover that makes
   crossing a lane a solvable spatial puzzle.

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
- Does the turret track through DORMANT instantly on first sighting, or only
  begin tracking after the player has been in line for a turn? (Affects how
  punishing first contact is.)
- Should it ever fire on other enemies by default, or only after hacking? Pure
  player-targeting is more readable; friendly-fire-on-all is more emergent.
- Hacking path: does it require the Scientist/IQ stat, a nearby terminal, or a
  consumable "hack tool" item? (Could defer hacking to a later iteration and
  ship destroy-only first.)
- Ceiling vs floor mount — purely cosmetic, but ceiling mount may render
  awkwardly in the billboard pipeline; recommend floor/wall mount for v1.
- Should a turret on the player's lane but facing away still get the one-turn
  rotate warning, or fire same-turn once aligned? Recommend always granting the
  rotate turn for fairness.
