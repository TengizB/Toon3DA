IMPLEMENTATION NOTE (what shipped, and the one thing that did not)
Parts A, B, D and E shipped in full. Part C shipped its ARCHIVE half in full (a missed story beat
lands on the codex.missed shelf and is never re-shown in play) plus the EVIDENCE half of C.2:
StoryTelemetry now records which line was closed unread, and narrative/StoryRecovery routes a missed
TEACHING beat onto a re-teach lane (consumeRecoverableTopic()). The re-teach DELIVERY is deliberately
NOT here: this order's own text hands it to "order-4's re-teach path", and order-4 (the competence
model) is still NOT IMPLEMENTED. Order-4 owns the second telling — the shorter, blunter, differently
-angled words, the one-re-teach-ever cap and the per-floor rate limit — and building half of it here
would have shipped the verbatim repetition order-4 exists to forbid. Until it lands the lane fills
and nothing consumes it, which is the correct failure mode: silence.

PURPOSE
This is a phone game with a story told across dozens of sessions. Two things break it that no
earlier order covers: a player who comes back after three days and has forgotten where they
were, and a player who tapped a one-shot line away before reading it and can never see it
again (order-1's F7). Both are fixed with the channels that already exist — no new screen, no
quest log, no markers.

READ FIRST
- order-1 (F7, D5), order-4 (the competence model, whose evidence hooks this order reuses)
- order-6 of the old series (the codex; access is a MENU, never play)
- narrative/StoryProgress, CodexSystem, BootCardSystem

============================================================
A. THE THREE-DAY PROBLEM
============================================================
A player opens the app after a week. They get: a title screen, a menu, a status card, and then
a corridor. Nothing anywhere says which region they are in, what they last learned, or what
they were told to do. The story is gated on DEEPEST REGION and their memory of it is the one
piece of state the game does not persist.

THE FIX — ONE LINE, ON THE CARD, ONLY WHEN IT IS NEEDED.
The boot card already prints one ORA wake line. When the gap since the last session exceeds a
threshold (STORY_REENTRY_GAP_HOURS, default 24), that slot is filled by a RE-ENTRY line
instead of the ordinary wake line: one sentence naming where they are and what they were last
told, drawn from the deepest region and the most recent unlocked codex entry.

    R1  You're at the rings again. Same job: down, find it, restart the works.
    R2  We were in the cutting floors. Trying to work out what they were cutting.
    R3  Reliquary. We'd just found out what the printers run on. You remember that much.
    R4  We were in the warm part. Where it stops being a facility.
    R5  The core. You know what's down there now. Whenever you're ready.

Five rows, one per region, one-shot per RE-ENTRY EVENT (not one-shot for the save — this is
the one deliberate exception to D5, and it is legitimate: the evidence is a real absence).
It replaces the wake line rather than stacking with it, so the card never grows.

============================================================
B. THE "WHERE AM I" PAGE — always unlocked, always current
============================================================
One codex entry, in a new position: pinned at the top of the archive, never locked, rewritten
by the game rather than authored once. It says, in ORA's voice, four things:

    WHERE WE ARE     the deepest region reached, in plain words
    THE JOB          the standing order as most recently transmitted (order-7's escalation
                     means this text CHANGES four times across the game — which is the point)
    WHAT WE KNOW     the last three codex entries unlocked, by title
    WHAT YOU DECIDED the consequential outcomes recorded so far, one line each

This is assembled from state the game already persists (deepest region, unlock order, the
outcome keys). It is the only piece of "long text" that is genuinely dynamic, and it lives in
the one screen where long text is allowed. It must NOT become a checklist: no percentages, no
"next objective", no markers on the map. It reads like her summarising, because that is what
she does.

Implementation: a CodexEntry whose body is composed at open time by CodexSystem from a small
composer (narrative/StoryRecap — headless, pure, testable: state in, list of string ids +
substitutions out). The renderer is unchanged.

============================================================
C. MISSED-BEAT RECOVERY
============================================================
Every STORY_CRITICAL one-shot beat currently gets exactly one chance to be read, and
StoryTelemetry already knows when one was dismissed inside the fast-dismiss window without
being read. Two recoveries, both cheap:

  1. ARCHIVE IT ANYWAY. A critical beat that was fast-dismissed unlocks its codex entry (if it
     has one) AND is added to a small "you may have missed this" shelf under the LOGS tab,
     showing the line itself. The archive is opt-in, so this costs a player who does not care
     precisely nothing.
  2. ONE RE-DELIVERY, EVIDENCE-GATED. A fast-dismissed CRITICAL TEACHING beat is eligible for
     order-4's re-teach path immediately (it does not have to wait for a gameplay failure).
     Story beats — region entries, reveal logs, gate orders — are NEVER re-delivered in play:
     they go to the archive only. Re-showing a story beat is how a narrative layer becomes
     something to escape from; re-showing an instruction is how a player gets unstuck.

That distinction is the rule: MECHANICS may be repeated once, on evidence. STORY is never
repeated, only archived.

============================================================
D. THE RUN-ONE-OF-MANY PROBLEM
============================================================
By run 20, the opening beats are long spent and the boot card is the only story the player
sees for whole sessions — because everything is one-shot and they have seen it. The layer goes
quiet exactly when a player is most invested. Two answers, both content, no new machinery:

  1. ORA'S DEPTH REACTIONS. A small pool (4 per region) that fires only when the player is
     deeper than they have ever been ON THIS RUN but not deeper than ever overall — i.e. the
     grind floors. She talks about the RUN, not the plot: what they are carrying, how it is
     going, what she noticed. It is the one place small talk belongs.
        You've still got both guns and half a bag. That's a first.
        Fourth floor without a scratch. Say nothing. Don't jinx it.
  2. THE COUNTER EARNS ITS KEEP. Every tenth print, one line about the number itself, region
     appropriate. It is the game's oldest running joke and its slowest turn:
        R1  Print ten. Round numbers. I'd get you a cake.
        R3  Print thirty. I've stopped rounding it off in my head.
        R4  Print forty. I know exactly what that is, now. I'd rather not say it out loud.
     Ten-ish rows across the game, and they land on the one screen every player always sees.

============================================================
E. GUARDS
============================================================
- The recap never appears in play. It is on the card and in the archive, both of which are
  places the player has already stopped.
- No objective marker, no waypoint, no "next: descend to region 3". The recap says where you
  ARE and what you were TOLD, never what to do next.
- The re-entry line replaces the wake line; the card never grows a fourth block.
- Missed-beat recovery never re-opens an exchange. A blocking beat is spent when answered
  (order-7 of the old series) and stays spent.

DEFINITION OF DONE
- A player returning after a day is told where they are and what the job is, in one sentence,
  on a screen they were going to tap anyway.
- The archive has a page that answers "what is going on" from live state, and it never tells
  the player what to do next.
- A critical beat dismissed unread is recoverable: instructions once in play, story in the
  archive.
- Late runs are not silent: depth reactions and counter milestones keep her present without
  replaying the plot.
