OVERVIEW
========
The Lurker is a fast, fragile demon that refuses to fight you face-to-face. It
skitters along ceilings and walls just out of view, circles the player, and only
strikes the tile DIRECTLY BEHIND your current facing — your blind spot. To the
player it is a predator that vanishes the instant you turn toward it and lunges
the instant you turn away. It exists to make the player's FACING DIRECTION a
live tactical resource, not just a way to aim. Because the player can only face
four cardinal directions and every rotation costs a turn, "which way am I
looking?" becomes a constant defensive decision.

This pairs the enemy with a small, reusable subsystem: the FLANK / BLIND-SPOT
model. Any enemy can be tagged as a "flanker" that deals bonus damage from your
rear arc, and the player gains a readable "threat-behind" warning. The Lurker is
the showcase enemy for it, but the system also retroactively deepens every
existing melee enemy: getting surrounded now genuinely matters, and turning to
face a threat is a meaningful trade (you expose your back to whatever was there).

It is dripping with Doom-RPG dread: you hear claws scuttling overhead, the screen
edges darken behind you, and a UAC bio-scanner voice mutters "Motion — behind
you." The counterplay is pure positioning: back into a corner so you have no
blind spot, use a wall as your "sixth wall," or bait the lunge and spin to meet
it.

GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
Core definitions:
- A tile's REAR TILE = the tile immediately behind the player, i.e. player tile
  minus the facing vector: (playerColumn - directionX, playerRow - directionY).
- BLIND ARC = the rear tile plus the two diagonally-rear tiles (3 tiles). Logic
  stays cardinal; diagonals are only used for the "is this enemy flanking me"
  check, never for movement.

Lurker behavior, evaluated once per player turn during the enemy phase:
1. STALK: while not adjacent, the Lurker pathfinds toward the player BUT always
   tries to end its move on a tile inside the player's current blind arc. If a
   blind-arc tile is reachable this turn, it moves there; otherwise it moves one
   step closer along the normal path. While in the player's front/side view it is
   rendered semi-transparent and "climbing the wall" (see VISUAL) — a tell, not a
   full reveal.
2. POSITION → COIL: when the Lurker reaches the player's REAR TILE specifically,
   it does not attack immediately. It spends one turn "coiling" (a clear wind-up
   pose + audio hiss). This is the player's warning window.
3. POUNCE: on its next turn, if it is STILL in the player's blind arc, it deals a
   heavy backstab hit (LURKER_BACKSTAB_DAMAGE) and recoils back one tile.
4. PLAYER COUNTER — the whole point:
   - ROTATE TO FACE IT: rotating so the Lurker is now in your FRONT arc cancels
     the pounce, staggers the Lurker for one turn (STAGGERED state, can't act),
     and lets you attack it while it's exposed. BUT rotating costs your turn and
     exposes your NEW rear tile to anything else.
   - BACK INTO COVER: if your rear tile is a wall/door/solid prop, you have NO
     blind spot — the Lurker cannot reach a rear tile and is forced into your
     front arc to engage, where it is far weaker. Corners (two walls behind) are
     safest of all.
   - JUST MOVE: stepping forward breaks the coil (the rear tile changes), but the
     fast Lurker re-establishes position quickly, so running only delays it.
5. DEATH: very low HP — one solid hit from most weapons kills it. Drops a small
   amount of credits/ammo (ties into loot/ransack). It is a glass cannon: lethal
   if ignored, trivial if faced.

PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
The Lurker turns the camera into a survival instrument. The player constantly
asks "what is behind me right now?" — a question this genre rarely forces because
most dungeon crawlers let you see threats you're walking toward. It creates a
delicious tension loop: you WANT to keep facing forward to progress and aim, but
something is exploiting exactly that. The fix (rotate to face it) feels great —
a hard spin-and-blast that staggers the thing — but it always costs you tempo and
re-exposes a different flank, so it never feels free.

The decision it forces every encounter: "Do I give up my forward progress / my
aim on the enemy in front to deal with the thing behind me, or do I find a wall
to put my back against?" It also teaches spatial literacy: experienced players
will fight Lurkers in corridors and corners, not open rooms. Death to a Lurker
always reads as "I let it sit behind me too long" — fair, legible, your fault.

TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
Builds on the planned enemy framework (enemy_system.txt) and the enemy turn
driver (global-tick-event-system.txt). All logic is integer tile space; visuals
are cosmetic overlays through the existing billboard/raycast pipeline. No
allocations in render().

New tile symbol:
- 'k' = Lurker spawn (k for "skitter"; 'l'/'L' are taken by floor/locker). Add to
  LevelLoader; spawn a Lurker entity, treat the tile as floor for the grid. Like
  other enemies, solidity is entity-side, so Level.isWall stays unchanged.

New / modified classes:
- entity/Lurker.java (or a config + behavior strategy in the enemy system):
  fields tileColumn, tileRow, facing (for its sprite), state enum
  {STALKING, COILING, STAGGERED, RECOILING}, health, coilTurnsRemaining.
  Method takeTurn(playerColumn, playerRow, playerDirectionX, playerDirectionY,
  level, doorManager).
- entity/FlankComponent.java (the reusable subsystem): a small data tag
  { boolean isFlanker; float rearDamageMultiplier; } that ANY enemy can carry.
  The damage-resolution code checks, when an enemy attacks, whether the enemy
  occupies the player's blind arc and applies the multiplier. This is what lets
  the Lurker model generalize to all melee enemies.
- util/GameMath.java — add pure helpers (each with the mandatory
  Formula/Derivation/Edge-cases comment block):
  * rearTile(playerColumn, playerRow, playerDirectionX, playerDirectionY) ->
    returns the column/row of the tile directly behind the player.
    Derivation: rear = player - facing unit vector. Edge case: facing is always a
    cardinal unit vector, so result is always an integer-adjacent tile.
  * isInBlindArc(enemyColumn, enemyRow, playerColumn, playerRow,
    playerDirectionX, playerDirectionY) -> boolean. Compute the vector from
    player to enemy; project onto the facing vector; enemy is in the blind arc if
    the dot product is negative (behind) AND it is within the 3-tile rear cluster
    (rear + two rear-diagonals). Returns false for tiles in front or directly to
    the side. Edge case: enemy on the same tile (dot == 0) -> not behind.
  * Reuse cardinalLineOfSightClear(...) if added by the turret idea, for the
    Lurker's "can I see/path to a blind tile" checks.
- render/EnemyRenderer.java: render Lurker billboard with state-driven appearance
  (transparent/climbing while in view, opaque + coil pose when behind). Because a
  billboard behind the player is off-screen, the "behind you" threat is conveyed
  by the HUD overlay below, NOT by drawing the Lurker on screen.
- render/HudRenderer.java (or a new render/ThreatVignetteRenderer.java): draw a
  REAR-THREAT indicator — a red/orange darkening + claw-mark vignette on the
  BOTTOM edge of the 3D view (the bottom edge reads as "near/behind the camera")
  whenever an enemy is in the player's blind arc. Intensity ramps from STALKING
  (faint) to COILING (bright, pulsing). Pre-allocate the vignette texture/quad in
  create(); never allocate in render().
- render/LevelRenderer.java: on the mini-map, draw the player's blind arc tiles
  with a faint tint so the player can read their own vulnerable rear from the map,
  and draw the Lurker normally (mini-map shows it even when "hidden" in 3D —
  rewarding map-watchers; or gate this behind a stat/upgrade for stealth depth).

Constants to add (Constants.java) — ALL PLACEHOLDER:
- LURKER_HEALTH = 10
- LURKER_BACKSTAB_DAMAGE = 18
- LURKER_FRONT_DAMAGE = 4        (weak chip if forced to fight you face-on)
- LURKER_COIL_TURNS = 1          (warning window before the pounce)
- LURKER_STAGGER_TURNS = 1       (free hit window after you spin to face it)
- LURKER_MOVE_RANGE_TILES = 1    (one tile per enemy turn; it's fast via good pathing, not multi-step)
- FLANK_REAR_DAMAGE_MULTIPLIER = 1.5  (generic flanker bonus for other enemies)
- THREAT_VIGNETTE_BASE_ALPHA / _PULSE_ALPHA (rear-threat overlay colors/alpha)

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- Sprite: a low, spider-limbed demon-imp the color of dried blood and ash —
  hunched, too many joints, a single sweep of pale eyes. Reads as "wrong, fast,
  cowardly."
- In-view (front/side arc) state: rendered at ~45% alpha and pressed flat against
  a wall/ceiling, limbs splayed ("climbing"). The semi-transparency says
  "not fully here yet / can't hit me like this." Slight jitter animation.
- Coil state (in your blind arc): full opacity, body drawn back like a spring,
  pale eyes flaring. Accompanied by a sharp hiss SFX and the rear-threat vignette
  spiking to bright pulsing red on the bottom screen edge.
- Pounce: a one-frame claw-slash overlay sweeping across the lower screen + screen
  shake; the Lurker is then drawn briefly in the front arc as it recoils.
- Stagger (after you spin to face it): it's caught mid-air/exposed, eyes dim, a
  white hit-flash outline — clearly "now's your chance."
- Rear-threat HUD vignette: claw-mark silhouettes darkening the bottom 1/5 of the
  3D view. STALKING = faint amber, steady. COILING = saturated red, ~2 Hz pulse.
  This is the single most important readability element — the player fights what
  they cannot see, so the vignette IS the enemy's on-screen presence.
- Audio: directional scuttling that grows louder as it nears your rear; a
  bio-scanner voice barks "Motion — behind you" on first coil of an encounter.
- Mini-map: small fast-moving dot; player's blind-arc tiles faintly tinted red.

BALANCE NOTES
=============
- Fairness lever = LURKER_COIL_TURNS. At 1, the player always gets exactly one
  turn of warning (vignette spikes) before the backstab — enough to spin or step.
  Set to 0 only on the deepest floors for terror; raise to 2 if early deaths feel
  cheap.
- Glass-cannon tuning: HP 10 means nearly any weapon one-shots it, so the threat
  is purely positional, not a damage sponge. Backstab 18 is scary vs ~50-100 HP
  but not an instant kill from full; two ignored backstabs should kill, punishing
  sustained neglect rather than a single slip.
- Front damage must stay low (4) so that forcing the Lurker into your front arc
  (via cover) feels like a hard counter, reinforcing the "back to a wall" lesson.
- Stagger 1 turn guarantees the spin-to-face counter always yields a free hit,
  making the skillful response feel rewarding.
- Spawn guidance for generators/level designers: Lurkers shine in OPEN rooms and
  multi-exit junctions (blind spots everywhere) and are near-useless in tight
  1-wide corridors (player's back is a wall) — place them to push the player OUT
  of safe corridors into exposed space. Never spawn 2 Lurkers such that facing
  one always exposes you to the other unless that's a deliberate late-floor
  gauntlet. Cap ~1-2 per room early game.
- All numbers above are placeholders pending playtest.

INTERACTIONS
============
1. All melee enemies (enemy_system.txt): the FlankComponent generalizes — give
   any chaser isFlanker=true and surrounding the player suddenly matters. The
   Lurker is the tutorial that teaches the rule that then applies game-wide.
2. Cover: columns 'P', doors 'd', solid props (barrels/crates/lockers) and walls
   all serve as "rear blockers." This makes the existing prop/cover layout
   tactically richer at zero new art cost. A locker at your back is a panic button.
3. Sentry Turret (sentry-turret-stationary-enemy): brutal synergy — a Lurker
   forces you to keep spinning, and a turret punishes you for facing the wrong
   lane. The two together create a true positional puzzle; level designers should
   use the pairing deliberately and sparingly.
4. Noise propagation (noise-propagation-and-3d-sound-cues): the Lurker's scuttle
   is a directional audio cue, and loud weapons that wake it should let it flank
   from stealth — silent counters (melee spin) preserve quiet.
5. Hazards (hazard-wall-tile, pressure-plate-trap-tiles, hazard chains): baiting a
   Lurker's pounce so it recoils onto a hazard tile or pressure plate is an
   emergent kill — rewards spatial setup.
6. Progression / boons (levelup-stim-boon-choice): natural boon candidates — a
   "rear motion radar" that shows blind-arc enemies on the mini-map, a "spinal
   plate" that caps backstab bonus damage, or a "whirl" boon that lets a rotation
   also attack the tile you're turning past. Strong build-identity hooks.
7. Berserk / melee builds: a high-Strength melee player who learns to spin-and-one-
   shot Lurkers plays completely differently from a ranged player who walls up —
   feeds the build-diversity pillar.

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
- Does the Lurker appear on the mini-map while "hidden" in 3D, or is map-visibility
  a stealth-relevant property gated behind IQ / a radar upgrade? (Showing it on the
  map is more readable; hiding it makes the audio/vignette tells matter more.)
- Should rotating to face it ALSO require you to then attack, or is the stagger
  enough of a reward on its own? (Auto-flinch on facing vs. flinch-only-on-hit.)
- Blind arc = 3 tiles (rear + rear diagonals) or strictly 1 (rear only)? Three is
  more dangerous and forgiving to the enemy's pathfinding; one is more precise and
  punishing to face. Recommend 3 for v1, tune down if it feels unfair.
- Should backing into a corner make it give up entirely (wander off) after N turns,
  or hover and re-engage the moment you step out? Wandering off rewards patience but
  can feel anticlimactic.
- Climbing-on-walls visual: render it as a wall-attached sprite (needs a wall-decal
  draw path) or just a floating semi-transparent billboard? Recommend billboard for
  v1 to reuse the existing pipeline; upgrade later.
- Does the front-arc "forced fight" use a separate weak attack, or does it simply
  flee and re-flank? Forced weak fight is more decisive and readable; recommend it.
