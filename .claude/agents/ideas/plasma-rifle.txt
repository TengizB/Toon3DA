OVERVIEW
========
The Plasma Rifle is the player's mid-tier energy weapon and the first gun that
rewards positioning over panic. It fires a blue-cyan plasma bolt that PIERCES
every enemy in a straight line ahead (no penetration cap — the bolt does not
stop on the first body), carries a 4-shot clip, reaches 8 tiles with a gentle
0.10 damage-drop coefficient, and pays for all that power with a slow 3-tick
reload. Where the shotgun is "get close, one big hit, reload," the plasma rifle
is "line them up down a corridor and melt the whole column." It reuses the
existing Weapon abstraction (clipSize / reloadTime / damageDropCoefficient),
the AmmoType.CELLS pool already reserved in the weapon system, and the
WeaponEventBus / muzzle-flash compositing path. Its weapon sprite and muzzle
bolt are PROCEDURALLY GENERATED (Pixmap) — no asset file required, matching the
textureless direction of the procedural HUD.

Why now: the shotgun ships the whole weapon system (Weapon base class, ammo
pools, fire-resolution code path, WeaponSlot, muzzle/flash compositing). The
plasma rifle is the cleanest possible second weapon to prove the abstraction
generalizes — it changes only the stat block, the line-march logic (pierce vs.
penetrate-1), the muzzle color, and the sprite. It also gives CELLS a consumer
and gives the player a reason to manage two ammo economies (shells vs. cells).


GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
PRE-PICKUP STATE
- Player may already have fists + shotgun (see pump-action-shotgun.txt).
- The plasma rifle is found in the level (placed on a corpse `m`, in a locker
  `L`, or as a dedicated weapon decal — see OPEN QUESTIONS for the tile char).

PICKUP
1. Player steps onto / interacts with the tile holding the rifle.
2. After the move completes (post-snap, IDLE), World checks the tile.
3. Rifle is added to inventory in WeaponSlot.SECONDARY. Cells: +12 (3 clips).
4. HUD pickup banner: "PLASMA RIFLE  +12 CELLS" — 2-turn fade.
5. Auto-equip only if the player currently holds fists (don't yank a loaded
   shotgun out of their hands mid-fight). Otherwise it stays holstered until
   the player swaps to it.

AIM (free)
- Rotating (A / D) to line the bolt up with a row of enemies is FREE — it does
  not advance a reload tick (identical rule to the shotgun). The player WILL
  spend rotations lining up a corridor before firing. This is the core skill.

FIRING (one full turn)
1. Player presses the attack key (Constants.KEY_ATTACK = SPACE) with the rifle
   equipped.
2. PlayerController checks state == IDLE and weapon.canFire() (shotsInClip > 0
   and state == NORMAL). If FALSE (mid-reload or empty clip), play a dry energy
   "fizzle" click, flash the HUD ammo readout red 0.15s, consume NO turn.
3. If TRUE, transition to ATTACKING. Weapon.fire(player, level, enemyManager,
   eventBus) resolves instantly in game logic:
   a. Build a "bolt ray" starting at the player tile, marching forward one tile
      at a time along (directionX, directionY), up to PLASMA_RANGE_TILES (8).
   b. For each tile crossed:
        - If it contains a WALL (Level.isWall): the bolt stops at that tile.
          The wall absorbs the bolt (a scorch/flash decal is optional polish).
        - If it contains a CLOSED door: bolt stops (does not open it).
        - If it contains one or more enemies: register a hit on each, apply
          damage with falloff, and CONTINUE marching (PIERCE — no cap).
   c. Damage at tile distance D: PLASMA_BASE_DAMAGE × dropMultiplier(D), where
      dropMultiplier(D) = max(MIN, 1 - PLASMA_DROP_COEFFICIENT × (D - 1)).
      See BALANCE for the resolved per-tile table.
   d. shotsInClip -= 1.
4. Visual: weapon sprite plays the FIRE frame for ~0.12s; a blue-cyan plasma
   bolt streaks the full traveled length (see VISUAL DESIGN); a cool-toned
   cyan screen flash at low alpha pops for 0.05s; every hit enemy plays its
   hurt animation simultaneously. A high "VWAP" energy-discharge sample plays.
5. Turn ends. Enemies take their turn.
6. If shotsInClip is now 0, the weapon swaps NORMAL -> RELOADING and arms
   ticksRemaining = PLASMA_RELOAD_TICKS (3). If shotsInClip > 0, the weapon
   stays NORMAL and can fire again next turn.

RELOAD (tick-driven — slow, this is the cost)
1. While RELOADING, the rifle needs PLASMA_RELOAD_TICKS (3) MOVEMENT ticks to
   re-energize the cell chamber. A tick = one COMPLETED tile step:
     - W / S / Q / E that actually change tiles: +1 tick.
     - A / D (rotate): NOT a tick — aiming stays free.
     - A blocked move (bumped wall, no tile change): NOT a tick.
2. Each completed step calls weapon.onMovementTick() -> ticksRemaining -= 1.
3. When ticksRemaining reaches 0: shotsInClip = min(PLASMA_CLIP_SIZE, cells in
   pool); deduct that many from the CELLS pool; state -> NORMAL.
4. RATIONALE: 3 ticks is deliberately punishing. After dumping a 4-bolt clip
   down a hallway you are exposed for three of your own moves. The player learns
   to retreat to safety BEFORE the clip runs dry, or to fire in disciplined
   2-bolt bursts and reload early in a lull.

EMPTY / OUT-OF-CELLS
- Fire with shotsInClip == 0 (reload not yet finished): dry fizzle, no turn.
- Reload completes with 0 cells in pool: clip stays empty, HUD shows "PLASMA
  0/4" in red, firing keeps fizzling. Player must swap weapon. Fists remain the
  never-softlock fallback (per shotgun doc).

  Min Damage Floor:                       0.30  (× base; bolt never fizzles to 0)
  Effective Range:                        8 tiles (bolt stops at 8 or at a wall)
  Penetration:                            UNLIMITED (pierces all enemies in line)

  Ammo per Shot:                          1 cell

  Pickup Cells:                           12 (3 clips)
  Max Cells Carried:                      40 (later upgrades raise this)
  Ammo Type:                              CELLS (shared with future BFG/plasma guns)

WHY PIERCE (NOT PENETRATE-1)
----------------------------
The shotgun penetrates exactly ONE enemy (pellet flavor). The plasma rifle is
defined by the OPPOSITE: a coherent energy bolt that does not lose itself in
flesh. Mechanically this makes the rifle the answer to the shotgun's weakness —
lines of enemies in a corridor. The shotgun wants enemies bunched at point
blank; the rifle wants them strung out down a hall. Two guns, two ideal
geometries, same grid. This is the whole reason the rifle exists.

DAMAGE FALLOFF (8-tile reach, coefficient 0.10)
-----------------------------------------------
dropMultiplier(D) = clamp(1 - 0.10 × (D - 1), 0.30, 1.0)

  Tile distance D   Multiplier   Damage (base 18)
    1                1.00          18
    2                0.90          16   (16.2 -> round)
    3                0.80          14   (14.4)
    4                0.70          13   (12.6)
    5                0.60          11   (10.8)
    6                0.50           9
    7                0.40           7   (7.2)
    8                0.30           5   (5.4, floored at 0.30×)

Store the resolved per-tile multipliers in
Constants.PLASMA_DROP_BY_TILE = {0f, 1.00f, 0.90f, 0.80f, 0.70f, 0.60f, 0.50f,
0.40f, 0.30f} (index = tile distance; index 0 unused). This mirrors the
shotgun's SHOTGUN_FALLOFF_BY_TILE pattern so the fire-resolution code can share
a "lookup multiplier by distance" path. Note the shotgun's array is empirical
per-tile; the plasma array is the coefficient formula pre-evaluated — keep BOTH
as explicit arrays so designers tune numbers, not formulas, in playtest.

TECHNICAL NOTES
===============

REUSES EXISTING WEAPON SYSTEM (from shotgun-weapon-system.txt)
--------------------------------------------------------------
No new package. Lives in ge.tbegvadze.toon3d.weapon alongside Weapon, Fists,
PumpActionShotgun. The base Weapon class already exposes the stat block fields
(clipSize, reloadTime, damageDropCoefficient), the WeaponState machine
(NORMAL / FIRE / RELOADING / EMPTY), shotsInClip, ticksRemaining,
canFire(), onMovementTick(), update(deltaTime), and hudAmmoString(). The plasma
rifle only overrides what differs.

NEW CLASS
---------
  weapon/PlasmaRifle.java extends Weapon
    - Constructor sets: displayName = "PLASMA RIFLE"; ammoType = AmmoType.CELLS;
      clipSize = Constants.PLASMA_CLIP_SIZE; reloadTime =
      Constants.PLASMA_RELOAD_TICKS; baseDamage = Constants.PLASMA_BASE_DAMAGE;
      damageDropCoefficient = Constants.PLASMA_DROP_COEFFICIENT;
      rangeTiles = Constants.PLASMA_RANGE_TILES; penetration = UNLIMITED.
    - Override fire(Player, Level, EnemyManager, WeaponEventBus): the bolt-march
      loop described in GAMEPLAY LOOP step 3. Marches tile-by-tile, applies
      dropMultiplier from Constants.PLASMA_DROP_BY_TILE, does NOT break on enemy
      hit (only on wall / closed door / max range). Posts onShotFired with the
      hit list and onHit per enemy via the WeaponEventBus.
    - Override hudAmmoString(): returns "PLASMA <shotsInClip>/<clipSize>",
      e.g. "PLASMA 3/4". (Build into the weapon's reusable StringBuilder; no
      per-frame allocation — the HUD reads this string each frame.)
    - needsReload()/onMovementTick() inherited from Weapon (tick decrement),
      because the 3-tick logic is identical to the shotgun's 1-tick logic with
      a different count. The ONLY difference is the constant.

GAMEMATH ADDITIONS (util/GameMath.java)
---------------------------------------
  plasmaDamageDropMultiplier(int distanceTiles, float coefficient,
                             float minMultiplier) -> float
    /*
     * Formula: linear damage falloff with a floor
     * Derivation: multiplier = 1 - coefficient * (distanceTiles - 1);
     *   at D=1 -> 1.0 (point blank, no drop); each tile beyond subtracts
     *   `coefficient`. Clamp to [minMultiplier, 1.0] so a long bolt still bites.
     * Edge cases: distanceTiles < 1 -> treat as 1 (clamp); coefficient 0 ->
     *   flat 1.0 at all ranges; large coefficient -> floor dominates quickly.
     */
  (The shotgun uses a precomputed array; this formula is what generated
   PLASMA_DROP_BY_TILE. Keep the method for tuning and for future energy guns,
   but the runtime fire loop should read the precomputed array for speed and
   designer-tunability — never compute inside render().)

PROCEDURAL WEAPON SPRITE GENERATION (no asset file)
---------------------------------------------------
Generate the weapon sprite into a Pixmap ONCE at load (in the renderer's
constructor / show()), upload to a Texture, then draw it like any sprite. NEVER
generate inside render(). This mirrors the procedural-HUD philosophy: every
pixel is code.

  weapon/ProceduralWeaponSprites.java  (or fold into the existing weapon sprite
  renderer from the shotgun doc — recommended, keep one weapon-sprite owner)
    - Builds a small set of Pixmaps for the plasma rifle frames and converts
      each to a Texture (disposed on shutdown). Frame canvas ~480x320 (match the
      shotgun sprite size so HUD layout / centering is unchanged).
    - FRAMES:
        idle      — rifle held barrel-up, coils dim (PLASMA_COLOR_GLOW at low
                     brightness), chamber dark.
        fire      — coils flare to PLASMA_COLOR_CORE, chamber white-hot, the
                     emitter muzzle replaced by a bright cyan burst.
        reload    — chamber open, a spent cell ejecting, coils dim/flickering.
    - DRAWING RECIPE (all rectangles / lines / filled circles — no external art):
        1. STEEL BODY: a chunky angular receiver. Fill a trapezoid-ish stack of
           rectangles in PLASMA_COLOR_STEEL; add a one-pixel PLASMA_COLOR_STEEL
           _DARK bottom/right edge for a beveled metal lip (same two-tone bevel
           trick as the HUD chrome). Marine-hand grip block at bottom-center.
        2. PLASMA CHAMBER: a vertical glass cylinder set into the body near the
           top. Draw as a filled rounded rect (compose with filled circles at
           the corners). Fill it with a vertical gradient PLASMA_COLOR_GLOW ->
           PLASMA_COLOR_MID -> PLASMA_COLOR_CORE (interpolate the color row by
           row down the chamber). In the FIRE frame, bias the whole gradient
           toward CORE (white-hot). This is the focal "energy" read.
        3. ENERGY COILS: 3-4 horizontal coil rings wrapping the barrel, drawn as
           stacked thin ellipses / filled rects in PLASMA_COLOR_MID with a
           PLASMA_COLOR_CORE highlight pixel on top. In FIRE, brighten coils to
           CORE; in RELOAD, dim to GLOW and skip every other coil (flicker).
        4. EMITTER MUZZLE: a small ring at the barrel tip. Idle = dark; FIRE =
           filled PLASMA_COLOR_CORE disc with a PLASMA_COLOR_MID halo ring.
        5. GLOW BLEED: optionally, after drawing, blit a few semi-transparent
           PLASMA_COLOR_GLOW rects around the chamber/coils for a bloom feel.
      Keep it readable as a chunky pixel sci-fi rifle: STEEL BODY + GLASS PLASMA
      CHAMBER + GLOWING COILS + EMITTER. The blue-cyan glow is the identity.

PROCEDURAL MUZZLE / BOLT (no orange flame — blue-cyan plasma)
-------------------------------------------------------------
  - MUZZLE BURST (on the weapon sprite): an additively-blended quad over the
    emitter for PLASMA_BOLT_TRACER_DURATION. Procedural: a small PLASMA_COLOR
    _CORE disc, PLASMA_COLOR_MID mid-ring, PLASMA_COLOR_GLOW outer halo. NO
    orange, NO yellow — explicitly the cool palette. This is the single most
    important visual contrast vs. the shotgun's warm muzzle flash.
  - ON-SCREEN BOLT STREAK (in the 3D view): the plasma bolt is fired along the
    player's facing, which in the first-person view is dead-center. Render a
    brief vertical-ish cyan streak / glow column up the center of the screen
    for PLASMA_BOLT_TRACER_DURATION, additively blended, fading out. Because the
    player only ever fires straight ahead (no free-aim), this is a fixed
    center-screen effect — a tall thin PLASMA_COLOR_CORE rect with a
    PLASMA_COLOR_GLOW falloff on each side, drawn over the world but under the
    HUD. No per-tile world projection needed; it is a stylized tracer, and the
    actual hits are resolved in tile space.
  - SCREEN FLASH: cool cyan, PLASMA_SCREEN_FLASH_ALPHA (0.22 — noticeably
    dimmer/cooler than the shotgun's 0.40 warm-white). Snaps off, no fade.
  - LIGHTING BUMP: like the shotgun, briefly bump nearby-wall shade in
    WallRenderer for the flash window, but tint the bump COOL (boost blue
    channel) rather than warm. Implemented as a transient shade multiplier with
    a blue bias; composited over the existing lighting calc.

PLAYERCONTROLLER / TURN INTEGRATION
-----------------------------------
- No new states beyond the shotgun's ATTACKING. Firing the plasma rifle uses
  the same ATTACKING action (Constants.PLAYER_ATTACK_DURATION) — the weapon
  decides what fire() does.
- Rotation does NOT tick the reload (free aim). Completed W/S/Q/E steps tick.
  This is the inherited Weapon.onMovementTick() contract.
- Weapon swap (KEY_WEAPON_NEXT / _PREV from the shotgun doc) selects the rifle
  if owned. Swapping does NOT reset reload progress (ticksRemaining persists on
  the weapon instance).

NO-ALLOCATION COMPLIANCE
------------------------
- Pixmaps/Textures built once at load, disposed at shutdown.
- hudAmmoString() writes into a preallocated StringBuilder (cleared with
  setLength(0)); never concatenates in render().
- Bolt-march loop uses a preallocated hit buffer (int[] enemy ids + parallel
  damage array) sized to a max-hits cap; no per-shot allocation. fire() runs in
  game logic, not render(), but keep it allocation-free regardless.


VISUAL DESIGN
=============
WEAPON SPRITE (bottom-center, ~480x320, procedural)
  - Identity silhouette: steel rifle body, a GLOWING vertical plasma chamber
    set into the top of the receiver, 3-4 energy coils wrapping the barrel, a
    dark emitter ring at the muzzle. Marine glove grip at bottom-center.
  - Palette is deliberately COOL: steel grays for the body, blue-cyan gradient
    for anything energized. The chamber is the brightest element at idle so the
    gun reads as "charged and dangerous" even when not firing.
  FRAMES:
    idle    — coils dim glow, chamber softly lit (GLOW->MID gradient).
    fire    — coils + chamber flare to CORE white-cyan; emitter pops a bright
              disc; weapon kicks UP ~6px (a buzz, not the shotgun's heavy lurch).
    reload  — chamber breech open, a spent cylindrical cell ejecting up-right,
              coils flickering (every other coil dimmed), chamber dark/refilling.

MUZZLE & BOLT
  - Cool blue-cyan ONLY. Core near-white-cyan, mid cyan, outer deep electric
    blue. No orange, no yellow, no smoke. Optional faint white "spark" pixels.
  - Center-screen vertical bolt streak for 0.10s, additive, fading.

SCREEN FLASH & SHAKE
  - Cyan flash, alpha 0.22, instant off.
  - Light vertical buzz shake, 2px peak, decays over ~0.12s. The rifle should
    feel electric and controlled, not concussive.

AUDIO
  - FIRE: a sharp high "VWAP" / "ZWIP" energy discharge with a brief tail.
  - RELOAD: an electric whine that rises over the 3 ticks (one rising whine
    blip per completed tick reads as "recharging"), ending in a "ka-CHK" cell
    seat on the final tick.
  - DRY FIRE: a flat low "fzzt" fizzle (failed discharge), distinct from the
    shotgun's metallic dry click.

HUD
  - Right panel ammo string from hudAmmoString(): "PLASMA 3/4". Big digits show
    shotsInClip; the "/4" is clip size. The HUD's existing yellow change-glow
    fires on each bolt; the empty (0/4) state pulses red per the procedural HUD
    doc. Weapon name strip: "PLASMA RIFLE".
  - Reload feedback: while RELOADING, the ammo digits can render in the dim
    "recharging" color (PHOSPHOR_DIM from the HUD palette) until the clip refills
    to 4, then snap bright with the change-glow. (Optional polish; the existing
    HUD empty/glow logic already conveys the state.)


BALANCE & TUNING
================
ROLE
- The plasma rifle is the corridor-control / line-clear weapon and the player's
  first sustained-DPS option (4 bolts before a reload vs. the shotgun's 1).

PER-CLIP OUTPUT (full clip, point blank, vs. a single target)
- 4 bolts × 18 = 72 damage before reload. Against a typical zombie (8 HP) that
  is gross overkill on one body — the rifle is NOT meant for single weak enemies
  (waste of cells). It shines when those 4 bolts each pierce 2-3 enemies in a
  line: 4 bolts × ~2.5 enemies × ~14 avg = ~140 distributed damage per clip.

THE RELOAD IS THE BALANCE
- 3 movement ticks of exposure after 4 bolts. Net cadence when dumping a full
  clip: 4 attack-turns of fire, then 3 move-turns defenseless = a 7-turn cycle.
  If enemies are still alive after the clip, those 3 reload steps are when you
  take damage. Discipline (fire 2, reload early in a lull, keep distance) is the
  intended counterplay. PLACEHOLDER — if reload feels oppressive, drop to 2.

CELL ECONOMY (separate from shells — TWO economies to juggle)
- Pickup gives 12 cells (3 clips). Max carry 40 (10 clips).
- Cells should be RARER than shells per level so the rifle stays a "pick your
  moment" weapon, not the default. Suggested ~8-16 cells per level beyond the
  initial pickup. The player is meant to clear trash with the shotgun and SAVE
  cells for dense rooms / a boss line-up.

DIFFICULTY LEVERS (single knobs)
- PLASMA_BASE_DAMAGE — global damage knob.
- PLASMA_DROP_COEFFICIENT — raise toward shotgun-like steepness to nerf long
  range; lower toward 0 for a flat railgun.
- PLASMA_CLIP_SIZE / PLASMA_RELOAD_TICKS — the cadence/exposure dial.
- Cell drop rate — the "how often can you use it" dial.

PLACEHOLDER FLAGS
- 18 base / 0.10 coeff / 4 clip / 3 ticks are all first-pass numbers. The
  intended FEEL is "noticeably stronger and longer-ranged than the shotgun, but
  you can't spam it and you can't afford to waste cells on stragglers."


INTERACTIONS
============
SHOTGUN / WEAPON SYSTEM (pump-action-shotgun.txt, shotgun-weapon-system.txt)
  - Drop-in second weapon: same Weapon base, same fire-resolution path, same
    tick-driven reload, same WeaponEventBus events, same WeaponSlot system. The
    rifle goes in SECONDARY; the shotgun in PRIMARY. Swapping preserves each
    weapon's reload progress. This doc is the proof the abstraction generalizes.
  - Deliberate anti-redundancy: shotgun = cluster/point-blank/penetrate-1;
    plasma = line/range/pierce-all. See the comparison table in MECHANICS.

ENEMY SYSTEM (enemy_system.txt) + HEALTH BARS / HIT REACTIONS
  - PIERCE means a single bolt can trigger multiple enemies' hurt animations and
    health-bar updates in ONE turn. The hit-reaction system must handle N
    simultaneous hits from one shot (the shotgun already does for its cone, so
    the path exists). Order hits front-to-back along the bolt so any death
    animations / corpse drops resolve in a sensible sequence.
  - Enemy weaknesses: if an enemy type defines an energy/plasma weakness
    multiplier (mirroring the zombie's shotgun 1.5×), apply it in fire() when
    resolving each pierced target. Reserve a per-enemy-type weakness lookup.

PROCEDURAL HUD (procedural-hud-no-textures.txt)
  - hudAmmoString() "PLASMA 3/4" flows into the right-panel ammo digits +
    weapon name exactly like the shotgun's "8/24". The empty-state red pulse and
    change-glow logic are reused unchanged. No HUD code changes required beyond
    reading a different weapon's string.

HAZARD CHAIN REACTIONS (hazard-chain-reactions-fire-toxic-explosive.txt)
  - A piercing bolt that crosses a tile containing an explosive barrel (`B`)
    should detonate it — the bolt passes THROUGH and can detonate a barrel AND
    hit enemies beyond it in the same line. This makes the rifle a fun
    chain-reaction trigger down a corridor lined with barrels. Decide whether
    the bolt stops at the barrel or continues past the explosion (recommend:
    continue — the bolt is coherent energy; the explosion is a separate AoE).
  - Radioactive / toxic tiles are unaffected by the bolt (no interaction).

DOORS (doors.txt, uac-keycard-doors-security-tiers.txt)
  - The bolt does NOT open or destroy doors (preserves keycard gating). A closed
    door stops the bolt like a wall. An OPEN door tile lets the bolt pass.

EMERGENCY RED ALERT PULSE (emergency-red-alert-pulse.txt)
  - The plasma discharge is a loud "noise event" the alert/hearing system can
    subscribe to (like the shotgun BOOM), raising local alarm state. The rising
    reload whine is NOT a combat noise event.


OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
1. PICKUP TILE: what represents the rifle on the map? Options: reuse a corpse
   `m` / locker `L` as a container, or add a dedicated weapon-decal char (e.g.
   reuse the `s` pattern with a new glyph for "energy weapon"). Recommendation:
   add a single new walkable weapon-decal char for clarity, generated as a
   glowing-cyan-rifle billboard (procedural, matches the sprite palette).
2. PIERCE THROUGH EXPLOSIONS: does the bolt continue past a barrel it detonates,
   or stop? Recommendation: CONTINUE (coherent energy); the AoE is separate.
3. MAX PIERCE TARGETS: truly unlimited, or cap at, say, 6 to bound the hit
   buffer? Recommendation: cap the BUFFER at a generous number (range is only 8
   tiles, so at most ~8 enemy tiles anyway) — effectively unlimited, but
   allocation-safe.
4. RELOAD TICK COUNT: 3 is the design intent (punishing). If playtest finds it
   miserable with fast enemies, drop to 2. Do NOT go to 1 — that erases the
   identity (it would just be a 4-shot shotgun).
5. PARTIAL RELOAD: if the player has only 2 cells in the pool when a reload
   finishes, the clip refills to 2/4 (min(clipSize, pool)). Confirm this matches
   the shotgun's pool-deduction behavior. Recommendation: YES, identical.
6. BOLT TRACER: stylized fixed center-screen streak (recommended, cheap) vs. a
   true 3D-projected bolt traveling down the corridor (expensive, needs the
   billboard/raycast pipeline). Recommendation: stylized center streak for v1;
   upgrade to a projected bolt in a polish pass if desired.
7. SHOULD AIM-FREE ROTATION DURING RELOAD ALSO APPLY HERE: confirmed yes
   (inherited). Flag only if it feels exploitable with the longer 3-tick reload
   (it should be fine — you still can't fire while reloading).
