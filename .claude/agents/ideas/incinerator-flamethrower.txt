TITLE: Incinerator — short-range cone + damage-over-time burn (FUEL)
CREATED: 2026-06-05
---

OVERVIEW
========
The Incinerator is the player's short-range crowd-melt weapon and the only gun
that applies a PERSISTENT damage-over-time effect. Pulling the trigger sprays a
gout of fire that fills a short WIDENING CONE directly ahead — the tile in front
(1 wide), then the two tiles beyond (3 wide), then the front of that (a final
band) — hitting every enemy in that fan for modest immediate damage AND setting
them ON FIRE. Burning enemies take BURN damage at the start of every subsequent
turn for several turns, even after the player stops firing or looks away. It is
the answer to a swarm of weak enemies pouring down a corridor, to "I cannot kill
them all this turn but I can light them all up and let the fire finish them,"
and to creating a wall of flame the player retreats behind. It reuses the Weapon
abstraction and a cone-shaped marchShot (like the shotgun's spread, but multi-
tile-deep) plus a new per-enemy BURN status that the global tick system advances.

Why it belongs: the existing weapons all resolve their entire damage in the turn
they fire. Nothing leaves a lingering effect, nothing rewards "tag everything and
disengage," and nothing fills a 2D area in front of the player at melee range
with sustained pressure. The shotgun is a single point-blank burst; the
incinerator is a sustained cone that keeps killing on its own. It is the swarm
answer and the only weapon whose damage outlives the trigger pull.


GAMEPLAY ROLE
=============
- Niche: short-range area suppression + damage-over-time. The ONLY weapon that
  deals damage on turns AFTER you fire (the burn), and the only one that fills a
  multi-tile cone in front of the player.
- Ideal geometry: a swarm of weak enemies bunched and advancing at close range
  (a corridor mouth, a doorway flood). Light them all, back up, let them burn.
- Anti-niche (deliberate weakness): single tough/armored targets (the per-hit
  immediate damage is low and burn is a slow drip — inefficient vs one beefy
  foe), and long range (the cone reaches only 3 tiles; useless across a hall).
  This keeps it clearly distinct from the railgun (single armored target at
  range) and the plasma rifle (a line at mid range).
- Role differentiation table:

                    Shotgun        Grenade Launcher   Incinerator
  Damage area       1D cluster     2D plus-splash     2D widening CONE
                    (point blank)  (at impact tile)   (sustained, in front)
  Range             5 tiles        6 tiles (lobbed)   3 tiles (short cone)
  Persistence       none           none (instant)     BURN over ~4 turns
  Best vs           1 close enemy  a clump / corner   a SWARM at the doorway
  Damage timing     all this turn  all this turn      a little now + a lot
                                                      over the next turns
  Ammo              shells common  grenades RARE      FUEL common-ish but
                                                      drains fast (3/shot)


GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
PRE-FIRE (free aim)
- Rotating to face the swarm is FREE (no tick, no turn), as with all weapons.

FIRING (one full turn)
1. Player presses KEY_ATTACK with the incinerator equipped.
2. canFire() requires fuelInClip >= FUEL_PER_SHOT (3) and not RELOADING. If not:
   a hollow "spit / sputter" (no flame), HUD fuel flashes red, NO turn consumed.
3. If true: transition to ATTACKING. marchShot() resolves the cone in tile space:
   a. Build the cone tiles relative to facing (facingStepColumn, facingStepRow)
      and the perpendicular axis (perpColumn, perpRow) = (-facingStepRow,
      facingStepColumn):
        Depth 1 (1 tile ahead):  the single tile directly in front.
        Depth 2 (2 tiles ahead): the front tile PLUS its two perpendicular
                                  neighbours (3 tiles wide).
        Depth 3 (3 tiles ahead): the center tile PLUS its two perpendicular
                                  neighbours (3 tiles wide).
      Total up to 7 cone tiles forming a widening fan. (See FLAME_CONE_OFFSETS in
      TECHNICAL NOTES for the exact offset table.)
   b. The cone is BLOCKED BY WALLS along each depth ray: if depth 1's center tile
      is a wall, the flame stops (nothing past it on that ray). Flame does not
      pass through walls. Compute reachable cone tiles by walking each depth ray
      from the player and stopping a ray at the first wall (so a doorway naturally
      funnels the flame).
   c. For every reachable cone tile, for every enemy on it:
        - Apply FLAME_IMPACT_DAMAGE immediately (modest — see STATS), reduced
          slightly at depth 3 (FLAME_FALLOFF) for the cone's far edge.
        - Apply or REFRESH the BURN status on that enemy: set its
          burnTurnsRemaining = FLAME_BURN_TURNS (4). Re-spraying a burning enemy
          REFRESHES the timer (does not stack damage, resets duration).
   d. fuelInClip -= FUEL_PER_SHOT (3 per shot — fuel drains fast).
4. Visual: weapon sprite plays the continuous FIRE frame (nozzle gout), a layered
   orange-yellow flame cone billows from the bottom-center of the screen, a warm
   orange screen-edge glow pulses, and every hit enemy ignites (a small flame
   overlay billboard on each burning enemy persists until the burn ends). A
   roaring "FWOOOOSH."
5. Turn ends. Enemies act. BURN ticks resolve (see BURN below). If fuelInClip <
   FUEL_PER_SHOT, weapon -> RELOADING with FLAME_RELOAD_TICKS armed.

BURN STATUS (the signature mechanic — damage over time)
- Each enemy carries burnTurnsRemaining (int, default 0). The global tick / world
  step processes burns at the START of every turn AFTER the player's action
  (i.e. when the world advances):
    For each enemy with burnTurnsRemaining > 0:
      apply FLAME_BURN_DAMAGE_PER_TURN to that enemy;
      burnTurnsRemaining -= 1;
      when it reaches 0, remove the flame overlay.
- Burn damage is applied REGARDLESS of where the player is or faces — the fire
  keeps working after you disengage. This is the "tag and retreat" payoff.
- Burning is visible: each burning enemy renders a small animated flame overlay
  (orange flicker) on its billboard so the player can read who is on fire and for
  roughly how long (intensity can dim as burnTurnsRemaining decreases).
- A burning enemy that walks does NOT spread fire to tiles (v1 — keep it simple;
  see OPEN QUESTIONS for optional floor-fire spread via the hazard system).

RELOAD (tick-driven)
- Standard tick model: each completed tile step decrements ticksRemaining;
  rotations/blocked bumps do not. On 0: fuelInClip = min(clipSize, pool); deduct
  from FUEL pool; state -> NORMAL. Reload (3 ticks) = swapping/repressurizing the
  fuel canister.

EMPTY / OUT-OF-FUEL
- Fire with fuelInClip < FUEL_PER_SHOT (mid-reload or low pool): sputter, HUD red,
  no turn.
- Reload finishes with insufficient fuel: clip stays low/empty, HUD red, keeps
  sputtering. Swap weapon. Fists are the never-softlock fallback.
- IMPORTANT: enemies already burning continue to burn even when the player is out
  of fuel — the DoT you already applied still resolves. A clutch dynamic: light
  the swarm, run dry, and the fire still finishes them.

SPECIAL MECHANICS
=================
1. DAMAGE OVER TIME (BURN) — the ONLY weapon whose damage continues on turns
   after you fire. Tag an enemy, and it loses HP every turn for 4 turns even if
   you disengage, retreat, or run out of fuel. Rewards "light them up and back
   off." Refreshing (re-spraying) resets the timer; it does not stack.
2. WIDENING CONE — fills a 2D fan in front of the player (up to 7 tiles), the
   only weapon that area-suppresses melee range. A doorway funnels it (walls
   block cone rays), turning a chokepoint into a kill zone.
3. SUSTAINED PRESSURE / SOFT CONTROL — even modest immediate damage plus burn
   means the incinerator wears down a whole swarm over several turns. It does not
   delete enemies instantly (low per-hit) but guarantees attrition.
4. FUEL BURN RATE — 3 fuel per spray means the clip empties in 10 sprays; the
   player must be economical and time the disengage. The tension is "how many
   more sprays before I have to reload and retreat."
5. SYNERGY WITH HAZARDS — flame can ignite oil pools / explosive barrels in the
   cone (see INTERACTIONS), turning the environment into part of the weapon.


VISUAL DESIGN
=============
WEAPON SPRITE (procedural FrameBuffer, 192x134 canvas, Quake-1 top-down angle)
  Identity silhouette: a bulky nozzle weapon with a visible FUEL CANISTER and a
  pilot-light flame at the nozzle tip. Industrial, not military — reads as a
  repurposed UAC incineration / pest-control tool (dark humor: "approved for
  vermin extermination"). Warm ballistic-ish palette but with an orange pilot
  flame and a fuel-tank accent.

  View mode: TOP-DOWN, convergence ~0.65, muzzle CAP (no bore ellipse). The
  nozzle is WIDE (it is a sprayer), wider than a rifle barrel, narrower than the
  grenade tube.

  DRAW LAYERS (back to front, Y-up, centerX = 96):
    1. RECEIVER / HOUSING: a wide industrial body Y=14..60, dark gunmetal
       rgba(0.24,0.26,0.28,1), with a 3px top highlight rgba(0.42,0.46,0.50,1)
       and 3px bottom shadow rgba(0.12,0.13,0.15,1). Add a couple of rivet dots
       rgba(0.36,0.38,0.42,1) for an industrial read.
    2. FUEL CANISTER: a cylindrical tank mounted on the LEFT-back of the housing
       (Y=16..50, centered around centerX-30), drawn as a rounded rect (rect +
       circles at corners) in rust-red rgba(0.55,0.20,0.12,1) with a faded HAZARD
       label band rgba(0.85,0.70,0.10,1) and a dark fuel-gauge stripe. This
       asymmetric tank is the incinerator's signature silhouette (most weapons are
       symmetric; the tank breaks it — but keep the NOZZLE centered on centerX for
       the centered-weapon look; only the tank decoration sits off-axis). NOTE:
       per the guide weapons should read symmetric about centerX; if strict
       symmetry is required, mirror a small twin canister on the right, or accept
       the tank as a centered detail. See OPEN QUESTIONS.
    3. FEED HOSE: a curved dark rubber hose rgba(0.16,0.16,0.18,1) from the
       canister to the nozzle base (a few short connected rects approximating a
       curve), selling the fuel path.
    4. NOZZLE TUBE: a wide-ish tube from base Y=60 (half-width ~16px) to muzzle
       Y=120 (half-width ~16*0.65 ~10px) via drawSymmetricTrapezoid. Crown
       highlight rgba(0.45,0.49,0.54,1); outer-edge shadows rgba(0.10,0.11,0.13,1).
    5. IGNITER RING: a small ring at the nozzle tip Y=116, steel
       rgba(0.40,0.44,0.50,1), where the pilot flame sits.
    6. PILOT FLAME (idle): a tiny ever-present orange flicker at the nozzle tip
       (Y=120..126), a small triangle/teardrop rgba(0.95,0.55,0.15,1) with a
       yellow core rgba(1.0,0.85,0.30,1). Even at idle the gun shows a live pilot
       light — it reads as "ready to burn."
    7. MUZZLE CAP: 2px bright steel band at Y=120, muzzle width. NO bore ellipse.

  FRAMES:
    idle    — pilot flame small and steady, canister full.
    fire    — a large flame gout erupts from the nozzle (handled mostly by the
              cone fire effect over the world); on the sprite, the pilot flame
              swells, the nozzle glows orange, weapon shakes/vibrates ~4px
              (continuous spray rumble, not a single kick).
    reload  — canister detaching/swapping (the tank rect drops/gaps), pilot flame
              guttering low, a fresh canister sliding in over the ticks.

FIRE EFFECT (custom — the flame cone is the whole identity)
  - FLAME CONE OVER THE WORLD: a layered, animated orange-yellow flame fan rising
    from bottom-center of the screen and billowing UP and slightly OUTWARD,
    representing the cone (which is always dead-ahead). Build from stacked
    semi-transparent triangles/ellipses in three color bands:
      outer  rgba(0.85,0.30,0.05,0.7) (deep orange-red)
      mid    rgba(0.98,0.55,0.12,0.85) (orange)
      core   rgba(1.0,0.85,0.30,0.95) (yellow-white)
    Animate by jittering the flame tongue heights/offsets per frame (use a small
    precomputed jitter table to stay allocation-free). This is the most
    persistent on-screen effect — it should fill more of the lower screen than
    any other muzzle effect.
  - SCREEN GLOW: a warm orange screen-edge glow, alpha ~0.30, that pulses while
    firing (NOT a snap flash — a sustained warm bath).
  - SHAKE: a low continuous rumble FLAME_SHAKE_INTENSITY ~4px while firing (the
    sprayer vibrates), decaying after the spray ends.
  - BURNING-ENEMY OVERLAY (on enemy billboards): each enemy with
    burnTurnsRemaining > 0 gets a small animated flame overlay drawn on its
    billboard (a few flickering orange triangles at its base), dimming as the
    burn timer runs down. This is rendered by EnemyRenderer, not the weapon HUD.

HUD
  - hudAmmoString(): "FUEL <fuelInClip>/<clipSize>", e.g. "FUEL 21/30". Reuses
    the right-panel digits + weapon name "INCINERATOR". Low-fuel (cannot afford a
    spray) pulses red via existing logic.

AUDIO
  - FIRE: a roaring "FWOOOOSH" that sustains for the spray, with a crackle tail.
  - BURN (ambient): a soft crackle loop while any enemy is burning (low volume).
  - RELOAD: a hiss (depressurize), a clunk (canister swap), a hiss (repressurize)
    over the 3 ticks.
  - DRY FIRE: a sputtering "fft-fft" (pilot light, no fuel pressure).


TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
NEW CLASS
  entity/Incinerator.java extends Weapon
    Constructor:
      super("INCINERATOR",
            Constants.FLAME_IMPACT_DAMAGE,
            Constants.FLAME_CLIP_SIZE,
            Constants.FLAME_RELOAD_TICKS,
            Constants.FLAME_DAMAGE_DROP_COEFF, // 0.0; depth falloff handled explicitly
            Constants.FLAME_RANGE_TILES);

  marchShot() OVERRIDE — cone fan, not a line. Walks each depth ray, stops a ray
  at the first wall, applies impact damage + burn. Pseudocode (integer tiles,
  allocation-free; perpendicular axis from facing):

    int perpColumn = -facingStepRow;   // 90-degree CCW of facing
    int perpRow    =  facingStepColumn;
    // For each cone tile (depth, lateralOffset) in FLAME_CONE_OFFSETS:
    //   tileColumn = playerTileColumn + facingStep*depth + perp*lateral
    //   tileRow    = playerTileRow    + facingStep*depth + perp*lateral
    // But first determine reachable depth per lateral ray (wall-blocking):
    for each lateralOffset in {-1, 0, +1}:
        for (int depth = 1; depth <= range; depth++) {
            int coneColumn = playerTileColumn + facingStepColumn*depth + perpColumn*lateralOffset;
            int coneRow    = playerTileRow    + facingStepRow*depth    + perpRow*lateralOffset;
            // gate: this tile only exists in the cone if its depth is allowed
            //  (lateral=0 reaches depth 1..3; lateral=+-1 reaches depth 2..3 only)
            if (!coneTileExists(depth, lateralOffset)) continue;
            char coneCell = level.getCell(coneColumn, coneRow);
            if (Level.isWall(coneCell)) break; // wall stops this lateral ray
            if (enemyHitTarget != null) {
                Object hitEnemy = enemyHitTarget.enemyAt(coneColumn, coneRow);
                if (hitEnemy != null) {
                    int damage = (depth >= range)
                        ? Constants.FLAME_FALLOFF
                        : Constants.FLAME_IMPACT_DAMAGE;
                    enemyHitTarget.applyDamageTo(hitEnemy, damage);
                    enemyHitTarget.applyBurn(hitEnemy, Constants.FLAME_BURN_TURNS);
                }
            }
        }
    return FireResult.MISSED; // or a hit result; cone has no single "stop" tile

  - coneTileExists / FLAME_CONE_OFFSETS: define the fan shape as a static offset
    table so it is data, not branches. Suggested 7-tile fan:
      depth1: (1, 0)
      depth2: (2,-1) (2,0) (2,+1)
      depth3: (3,-1) (3,0) (3,+1)
    where each entry is (forwardDepth, lateralOffset). The reachability/wall-block
    above is computed per lateral ray; the offset table defines membership.
  - enemyHitTarget.applyBurn(enemy, turns): NEW method on the EnemyHitTarget /
    enemy-damage interface — sets enemy.burnTurnsRemaining = max(existing, turns)
    (refresh, not stack). The enemy system owns the burn field and ticking.

  hudAmmoString() override: "FUEL " + fuelInClip + "/" + clipSize, built into the
  preallocated StringBuilder.

BURN TICK (enemy system + global tick)
  - Add int burnTurnsRemaining to the Enemy (or a Burn status component if ECS).
  - In the world/enemy turn-advance (the same place enemies take their turn —
    see enemy_system.txt and global-tick-event-system.txt), BEFORE or AFTER enemy
    actions, process burns:
      if (enemy.burnTurnsRemaining > 0) {
          enemy.takeDamage(Constants.FLAME_BURN_DAMAGE_PER_TURN);
          enemy.burnTurnsRemaining -= 1;
      }
    Order relative to enemy movement is a tuning choice (recommend burn FIRST so a
    burning enemy can die before acting). A killed-by-burn enemy drops loot/corpse
    normally.
  - This hooks the global-tick-event-system: a "burn tick" can post an event for
    the flame overlay / crackle audio.

BALANCE NOTES
=============
- DESIGNED FOR SWARMS, NOT SINGLES. Per-hit immediate damage is low (8) by
  design; the value is in (a) hitting up to 7 tiles at once and (b) the burn
  (6/turn x 4 = 24 follow-up per tagged enemy). Against a 5-enemy swarm, one
  spray = 5 x 8 immediate + 5 x 24 burn potential = 40 now + 120 over the next
  4 turns. That is the highest TOTAL multi-target damage in the game — but spread
  over time, low per-instant, and only at melee range. Strong vs swarms, weak vs
  a single tank (8 + 24 over 4 turns is slow for one beefy target).
- BURN BALANCE: 4 turns x 6 = 24 is the key knob. If swarms melt too easily, drop
  burn turns to 3 or damage to 5. If the burn feels negligible, raise to 8/turn.
  REFRESH (not stack) is deliberate — prevents a player from stacking infinite
  burn by spamming; instead it maintains pressure. If stacking is wanted later,
  cap stacks at 2.
- FUEL ECONOMY: 3 fuel/spray, 30/clip = 10 sprays per clip; 120 max = 40 sprays.
  Fuel should be moderately common (it is a workhorse swarm tool) but drains fast
  enough that the player cannot hold the trigger forever. Suggested ~40-60 fuel
  found per level.
- RANGE 3 is the hard weakness. The incinerator cannot touch anything past 3
  tiles — it forces the player into dangerous close range, which the burn-and-
  retreat loop is meant to mitigate. Do NOT extend range past 3-4; long range
  would erase its identity and overlap the plasma/chaingun.
- DIFFICULTY KNOBS: FLAME_BURN_DAMAGE_PER_TURN x FLAME_BURN_TURNS (DoT power),
  FLAME_IMPACT_DAMAGE (burst), FUEL_PER_SHOT and clip/max (sustain), cone offset
  table (area), FLAME_RANGE_TILES (the weakness dial).
- ALL NUMBERS ARE FIRST-PASS PLACEHOLDERS. Intended FEEL: "the crowd answer —
  when the doorway floods, you bathe the whole mouth in fire, back up, and watch
  them cook. Low immediate punch, relentless attrition, scary at melee."


INTERACTIONS
============
1. GLOBAL TICK / ENEMY SYSTEM (global-tick-event-system.txt, enemy_system.txt):
   the BURN status is the first per-enemy DoT and requires the enemy turn-advance
   to process burn damage each turn. This is a clean extension of the existing
   tick loop — a new "process burns" step. Burn ticks can post events for the
   crackle audio / overlay via the event bus.
2. HAZARD CHAIN REACTIONS (hazard-chain-reactions-fire-toxic-explosive.txt): the
   incinerator is the NATURAL fire source for the hazard system. Flame in a cone
   tile containing an OIL POOL (`O`) ignites it (spreading floor fire if that
   system supports it); flame on an explosive barrel (`B`) detonates it; flame in
   a toxic/radioactive cloud could ignite it. This is the deepest environmental
   synergy of any weapon — the incinerator turns the level's hazards into part of
   its kit. Recommend: cone tile with `O` -> ignite floor-fire tile; cone tile
   with `B` -> detonate.
3. ENEMY HIT REACTIONS / HEALTH BARS (enemy-health-bars-...): burning enemies
   show the flame overlay AND continue updating their health bar each burn tick.
   The multi-target spray reuses the existing multi-hit reaction path.
4. SHOTGUN / WEAPON SYSTEM: drop-in via the Weapon base, same tick reload, same
   WeaponEventBus. Deliberately non-redundant: shotgun = single point-blank
   burst; incinerator = sustained melee-range cone with DoT.
5. EMERGENCY RED ALERT PULSE (emergency-red-alert-pulse.txt): the FWOOOSH is a
   loud noise event raising local alarm. The ambient burn crackle is NOT a combat
   noise event.
6. ENEMY weakness/resistance: a "flammable" enemy type (e.g. a gas-bloated demon)
   could take bonus burn damage or a longer burn; a "fireproof" type (a hell
   knight?) could resist burn entirely — apply a per-type burn multiplier in
   applyBurn, mirroring the shotgun/plasma weakness pattern. This gives level
   designers a reason to mix enemy types against the incinerator.


OPEN QUESTIONS
=============
1. SPRITE SYMMETRY VS FUEL TANK: the off-axis fuel canister breaks the guide's
   "symmetric about centerX" rule. Options: (a) keep the nozzle centered and
   accept the tank as an off-axis detail (most readable, recommended); (b) mirror
   twin tanks on both sides (symmetric but less iconic); (c) center a single tank
   behind the nozzle. Recommendation: (a).
2. BURN STACK VS REFRESH: confirmed REFRESH (re-spray resets to FLAME_BURN_TURNS,
   does not add). If playtest wants more reward for sustained spraying, allow up
   to 2 stacks. Recommendation: refresh-only for v1.
3. FLOOR FIRE SPREAD: should a burning enemy or an ignited oil pool leave a
   damaging fire tile that the hazard system spreads/ticks, hitting the player
   too? This is powerful but couples tightly to the hazard system. Recommendation:
   defer floor-fire to the hazard-chain-reactions feature; v1 incinerator only
   burns ENEMIES directly, with oil/barrel ignition delegated to that system if
   present.
4. BURN TICK ORDER: process burn BEFORE enemy movement (a burning enemy can die
   before acting) or AFTER (it always gets one action)? Recommendation: BEFORE —
   the player who lit them up should be rewarded with potential pre-action kills.
5. FRIENDLY FIRE / SELF-BURN: can the player catch fire (e.g. standing in ignited
   oil)? For v1 the cone only points away, so no direct self-burn. If floor-fire
   spread is added later, the player CAN be burned by standing in it — a good
   risk for the retreat-behind-flame fantasy. Defer with #3.
6. CONE WIDTH/DEPTH: the 7-tile fan (1/3/3) is the proposed shape. Alternatives:
   a narrower 1/1/1 straight 3-tile blast (more shotgun-like) or a wider 1/3/5
   fan (more area, harder to balance). Recommendation: 1/3/3 (the {{1,0},{2,-1},
   {2,0},{2,1},{3,-1},{3,0},{3,1}} table) — clearly a cone, clearly melee, clearly
   distinct from every other weapon's pattern.
