STATUS: IMPLEMENTED
CATEGORY: Systems
TITLE: Encounter density: spend the budget in BODIES, keep packs whole
---

## THE COMPLAINT

"Levels seem too empty, too few enemies are spawning — or maybe there is a bug
where they are somehow killed by themselves, because there are corpses. One level
was generated without any enemies."

Both halves of that report are real defects, and neither is what it looks like.
Nothing is dying on its own. The floors genuinely are near-empty — but not because
the Threat-Point budget is too small. The budget is fine; it is being CONVERTED
into bodies badly, and on a low-budget floor it is barely converted at all.

Every number below was measured headlessly against the generators on this branch
(seeds 1..200, depths 1/2/3/5/8/12), not estimated.


## THE EVIDENCE

### A. A CACHE floor contains exactly ONE enemy

CacheProfile builds a FULL-SIZE dungeon — GeneratorId.ROOMS_MST, 80x45, ~1,100
walkable tiles — and applies EnemyBudgetOverride.calm() = ROUTE_CALM_BUDGET_SCALE
= 0.28. Measured over 100 seeds:

    CACHE/CALM 0.28   depth=1   avg=1.0  min=1  max=1   (100 seeds)
    CACHE/CALM 0.28   depth=3   avg=1.0  min=1  max=1   (100 seeds)
    CACHE/CALM 0.28   depth=8   avg=3.0  min=3  max=3   (100 seeds)

Not "about one" — EXACTLY one, on every seed, with zero variance. One Shell Brute
in a thousand-tile dungeon. CACHE is NODE_WEIGHT_CACHE=14 of 100 route weight, so
roughly one floor in seven is this.

The trace that explains it (depth 1, scale 0.28):

    budget = 126    anchor SHELL_BRUTE = 96    leftover = 30
    maxPerType = 126 * ENCOUNTER_MAX_SINGLE_TYPE_FRACTION(0.40) = 50
    cheapest chaff PACK = CRAWLER 16.56 * CHAFF_PACK_MIN(2) = 33  >  leftover 30
    => chooseFill returns null on its first call => roster = [SHELL_BRUTE]

Two independent bugs stack to produce it:

### B. PROBLEM 1 — the anchor reserve is bypassed on small budgets

EncounterBudgetPlanner.chooseAnchor picks a reserve fraction in
[ENCOUNTER_ANCHOR_BUDGET_FRACTION_MIN 0.15, _MAX 0.30] and takes the largest anchor
that fits it. When nothing fits, it falls through to:

    "Nothing fits the reserve — place the cheapest anchor that fits the WHOLE
     budget so the floor still has a defining threat"

On a 126 TP budget that fallback spends 96 TP — 76% of the floor — on one body.
The 15-30% reserve rule, the whole point of composition rule 1, is not a rule at
all: it is a preference with an unbounded escape hatch. A "defining threat" that
consumes three quarters of the floor does not define the floor, it IS the floor.

### C. PROBLEM 2 — the remainder is unspendable, and silently discarded

CHAFF_PACK_MIN = 2 (order-5 pack coherence) means chooseFill only OFFERS a chaff
type when a full minimum pack fits. That is correct on a full floor. On a small
floor it means the last 20-30% of the budget can never be spent: the leftover buys
one chaff but not two, so the fill loop breaks immediately and the change is thrown
away. The budget was authorised; the bodies were not delivered.

Measured budget utilisation in BODIES (not TP) is 76% on a CALM floor. On a
standard floor it is ~99%, which is why the bug is invisible at scale 1.0.

### D. PROBLEM 3 — placement is FRAGILE, but was not actually losing enemies

CORRECTED FINDING. An earlier draft of this document claimed ~10% of planned
enemies were being dropped in placement. That number was a MEASUREMENT ARTIFACT and
is withdrawn: it compared the generator's placed count against a roster re-planned
from a fresh Random(seed), which is a different RNG stream than the generator's own,
so it was diffing two unrelated rosters. Instrumenting the generator itself
(counting inside placeBudgetedEncounter, 300 seeds x depths 1/3/8/12) shows:

    planned == placed exactly, loss = 0.00%, at every depth, BEFORE the fix.

The real defect here is a latent one, not an observed loss. tryPlaceEnemyInRoom
gives up after 40 random tile probes, and placeEnemyLoadBalanced then marks that
room roomTilesExhausted PERMANENTLY — even though a probe miss is not evidence the
room is full. Today the rosters are small enough relative to room area that the
probes essentially always succeed, so nothing is lost; the failure mode is real but
dormant, and it would surface exactly when rosters grow (which FIX 1/2 do, and which
the depth body-count ramp in NON-GOALS would do much more).

So the fix below is hardening, not a bug fix, and is worth doing on that basis
alone: after the change a room is retired only when a deterministic full-interior
scan proves it has no eligible tile. Loss stays 0.00% and can no longer become
non-zero as rosters grow.

### E. PROBLEM 4 — packs are formed, then shredded

The planner goes to real trouble to add chaff in coherent packs of
CHAFF_PACK_MIN..CHAFF_PACK_MAX (2..4), because the golden-band chaff exemption
EXPLICITLY assumes packs: "a lone chaff reads a harmless ~9 golden ratio, balanced
by pack TP". placeEnemyLoadBalanced then drops each roster entry into whichever
room currently has the LOWEST depth-weighted load — which is a near-perfect
algorithm for guaranteeing that the three members of a pack land in three
different rooms.

    mean manhattan distance to the nearest OTHER enemy:
      depth  1  ->  13.9 tiles
      depth  5  ->  13.5 tiles
      depth 12  ->  10.8 tiles

Fourteen tiles is fourteen player turns. Every enemy is fought alone. The pack
that the pricing model assumes exists has never once existed in play, which means
the chaff exemption has been pricing a fight the game does not contain.

This is also the single biggest contributor to the FEEL of emptiness, independent
of the raw count: 12 enemies met one at a time across 1,100 tiles reads as an
abandoned facility. The same 12 in four groups of three reads as a defended one.

### F. PROBLEM 5 — enemy COUNT is mathematically frozen across depth

    GameMath.floorThreatPointBudget = base   * depthThreatScale(hp, dmg, depth)
    GameMath.enemyThreatAtDepth     = baseTP * depthThreatScale(hp, dmg, depth)

The SAME factor. Therefore budget/cost — the number of bodies the budget buys — is
depth-invariant by construction. Confirmed empirically: the spawn histograms at
depth 1 and depth 2 are byte-identical, and so are depth 3 and depth 5. Count only
moves at all because the region-band multiplier and the elite-anchor depth gate
perturb it:

    depth  1 -> 12.4 enemies     depth  8 -> 13.5 enemies
    depth 12 -> 15.8 enemies

Twelve floors of descent buy 3 extra bodies. Deeper floors are stronger, never
busier, and the composition is literally the same roster.

NOTE: this one is DOCUMENTED HERE BUT DELIBERATELY NOT FIXED IN THIS PASS — see
NON-GOALS. It cannot be fixed by adding budget without moving the R-DEPTH coupling
curve, and that deserves its own order with its own audit run.

### G. PROBLEM 6 — the corpse symbol lies to the player

This is the "enemies killed themselves" half of the report, and it is a pure
semantics bug.

EnemyManager.killEnemy stamps 'm' at the dead enemy's tile — 'm' is the game's
DEATH MARKER. The generator ALSO stamps 'm' as ambient atmosphere, at
LevelGenConfig.corpseWeight = 0.14 of the prop budget. Measured per floor at
depth 3:

    corpse decals 'm' : 16.4        LIVE enemies : 11.7
    blood '.'         : 16.7        oil 'O'      : 35.6
    => 1.4 pre-placed corpses for every living enemy

The player walks into a room they have never visited, sees corpses that are
indistinguishable from the ones their own kills leave behind, and correctly infers
that something died here — then reasonably concludes the enemies are killing
themselves. The game told them that. There is no bug in the enemies; there is a
bug in the vocabulary.

Verified there is genuinely no self-kill mechanic: the Plague Hulk's self-destruct
blast (applySelfDestructBlast) only ever tests the PLAYER's tile, and enemy ranged
attacks do not detonate explosive barrels. Barrels damage enemies only when the
PLAYER shoots them.

### H. PROBLEM 7 — dead constants: EVENT / GATE budget scales

    BalanceConfig.EVENT_BUDGET_SCALE = 0.15    GATE_BUDGET_SCALE = 0.10

EventProfile and GateProfile pass these through as EnemyBudgetOverride.scaled(...).
EventRoomGenerator and GateAirlockGenerator ignore the config entirely and hardcode
an empty spawn list ("ZERO enemies — a story beat"). Meanwhile RouteEconomics
prices both node rows at budgetScale(0f).

Three sources of truth; two of them agree on 0 and the third claims 0.15/0.10. The
constants are dead and actively misleading — anyone tuning EVENT difficulty would
change a number that does nothing.

(These floors being enemy-free is CORRECT and stays: MedBay/Event/Gate build small
curated rooms — 131-222 walkable tiles, not 1,100 — so they read as a deliberate
pacing beat, not an empty dungeon. Only the lying constants are the defect.)

### I. PROBLEM 8 — the determinism gate is already RED

LevelGeneratorSnapshotTest expects 42003771e27256491f15580cef9bff6bab221016eec8e456
e6347697e1ace00c; the generator actually produces e8d312a1f01b1c88bd21b35e058dbf77
ad08a4ba485de61f3ad28ce63d05f03d. Generation IS still deterministic — the digest is
byte-stable across three separate JVM runs — the baseline was simply never
re-based after a previous behaviour change. `./gradlew test` fails on master.


## THE FIX

Design constraint that shapes everything below: **every change is
Threat-Point-NEUTRAL.** No floor's total TP spend moves. This matters because the
route economics ledger (RouteEconomicsModel, R-CALM-COST, R-RISK-PREMIUM) prices
nodes off budgetScale, and the depth-coupling rule R-DEPTH prices the floor's total
threat. Converting the SAME budget into more, better-arranged bodies changes what
the player meets without moving a single number the audit reads.

### FIX 1 — the anchor reserve becomes a real ceiling

Delete the "cheapest anchor that fits the whole budget" fallback. An anchor is
placed only when its cost fits ENCOUNTER_ANCHOR_BUDGET_FRACTION_MAX of the budget.
If no anchor fits that ceiling, the floor simply has no anchor and the full budget
goes to fill — which is the honest description of a CALM floor anyway ("light
stragglers", per CacheProfile's own doc comment).

Standard floors are unaffected: at scale 1.0 depth 1, the ceiling is
0.30 * 450 = 135 and SHELL_BRUTE costs 96, so the anchor still lands every time.
Only budgets too small to afford a bruiser within its own reserve lose it.

### FIX 2 — spend the remainder in bodies

After the main fill loop, add a REMAINDER PASS: while budget is left, add the
cheapest affordable single enemy (pack minimum relaxed to 1 for this pass only,
per-type cap still enforced). This is the ONLY place a lone chaff may be created,
and it exists precisely so authorised TP always becomes a body.

Projected CALM depth-1 result: no anchor, ~126 TP of chaff => 6-8 light enemies
instead of 1 bruiser. Same TP, same ledger, a floor that is actually populated and
that finally matches the "light resistance" the node claims to be.

### FIX 3 — placement retries instead of retiring rooms (HARDENING)

- Move the hardcoded 40-probe budget into LEVEL_GEN_ENEMY_SPAWN_PROBE_ATTEMPTS
  (and LEVEL_GEN_CAVE_SPAWN_PROBE_ATTEMPTS for the cave, which probes the whole
  grid rather than one room).
- On probe exhaustion, fall back to a DETERMINISTIC full scan of the room interior
  (whole grid, for the cave) before declaring it exhausted. A room is retired only
  when it truly has no eligible tile.

Target: keep placement loss at the 0.00% it already measures, and keep it there as
rosters grow. Not a fix for an observed loss — see the corrected PROBLEM 3.

### FIX 4 — packs land together

Add placePackTogether: when a chaff type appears as consecutive roster entries
(which is exactly how the planner emits packs), place the whole pack into ONE room,
on tiles near each other, and charge the room the pack's full TP. The load balancer
still chooses WHICH room — it just receives the pack as one indivisible unit
instead of N independent placements.

This is what makes the chaff golden-band exemption honest for the first time, and
it is the change that most directly answers "the level feels empty": the player
meets groups.

### FIX 5 — 'm' becomes exclusively a death marker

The generator stops stamping 'm' as ambient decor. LevelGenConfig.corpses defaults
to false and its prop weight is redistributed to the blood-stain decals, which
already mean "combat happened here" without implying a body the player did not
make. 'm' now means one thing only: SOMETHING DIED HERE DURING THIS RUN.

No new symbol, no renderer change, no docs/tile-symbols.txt budget change — 'm'
keeps its entry and its sprite, it just stops being generated as scenery. Blood
'.', alt-blood 's', oil 'O' and scorch 'e' carry the aftermath atmosphere, which is
what they are for.

### FIX 6 — delete the lying constants

EVENT_BUDGET_SCALE and GATE_BUDGET_SCALE go to 0f with a comment stating that
these generators emit zero spawns BY DESIGN and that the ledger rows agree. The
number now matches the behaviour and the price.

### FIX 7 — re-baseline the determinism digest

Re-base LevelGeneratorSnapshotTest from the post-change generator, with the reason
recorded in its javadoc as every previous re-baseline has been. This is a
deliberate, reviewed behaviour change: fixes 1-5 all perturb the RNG draw sequence.


## NON-GOALS (deliberately out of scope for this pass)

- **PROBLEM 5, depth-invariant body count.** Fixing it means giving the floor
  budget a per-depth multiplier that is NOT applied to per-enemy cost, which moves
  total floor threat and therefore the R-DEPTH coupling curve. That is a balance
  change with its own audit obligations, not a bug fix. It belongs in its own
  order alongside a re-derived depth curve.
- **The waived simulator bands.** 6 of 8 behavioural bands are waived because the
  scripted policy stalls on 88% of floors (median death depth 2.0 against a target
  of 8-14). That is the sim NAVIGATOR's competence, not the game's balance, and the
  waiver already says so. Worth noting here only because it explains why none of
  the above was caught: the one gate that could measure "floors feel empty" is not
  currently measuring anything.
