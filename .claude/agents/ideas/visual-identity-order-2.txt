IMPLEMENTATION NOTE (2026-10-20, reviewer-approved deviations)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  - R8 formula replaced. clamp01(s x tint + light) clamped the light away (near surfaces already sit at
    multiplier ~1.0; measured +2..3/255). The light is added AFTER the clamp, as albedo x light: walls,
    doors, columns, props, pickups, the shop body and enemy bodies get a second draw of the same texture
    slice with blend (GL_SRC_ALPHA, GL_ONE), colour = lightAdd, alpha 1, using the same wall z-test and
    skipping columns a nearer recorded sprite covers. Floor/ceiling use
    GameMath.applyRgbShadeAndLightToPackedRGBA (only the final channel clamps). A door-panel column lights
    the panel only (deliberate).
  - R7 peaks raised: WARM 0.35 -> 1.50, ENERGY 0.35 -> 1.50, FIRE 0.40 -> 1.70 — the materials are dark
    (order-1 R9), so a multiplier on albedo needs a peak above 1 to be visible. Colours, duration (0.10 s),
    radius (4 tiles) and falloff unchanged. VISUAL DESIGN's "+35%" is superseded: a surface at the
    player's feet briefly reaches ~2.5x its lit colour.
  - R10 superseded: while a pulse is active, each lit wall column and lit sprite slice costs one extra
    additive quad (two blend switches per frame for walls, two per lit sprite). At intensity 0 everything
    is skipped by a branch, as specified.
  - AS6 restated: the peak is no longer 0.35. The flash is local (squared falloff to 4 tiles), never
    full-screen, lasts 0.10 s and never stacks; repeated chaingun fire repeats the pulse. Revisit if
    photosensitivity is raised.
  - R4/R6 extension: every FLOATING billboard pickup (weapon pickups, credit chips, and the medical /
    armour / ammo / keycard tile pickups) gets its floor shadow at CONTACT_SHADOW_HOVER_ALPHA_SCALE (0.55),
    R6's own reasoning for the hovering eye; at full alpha they read as stray blobs.
  - R3: the alpha cut runs in place on the sheet's pixel buffer at load; measured 9 sheets in 379 ms
    (desktop/llvmpipe). The 0.50 cut is kept for every sheet: the 4.5-16% mid-alpha texels are
    anti-aliased edges in body colour, not a dark glow (offline composites).
  - Verified on screen: halo cut, prop/pickup shadows, the forced-pulse light in warm/cyan/orange.
    Verified by code read only: enemy shadows (A5/A6), enemy lighting (A7), chaingun refresh (A8).

OVERVIEW
--------
Makes the billboards look like they are in the same room as the walls. Three changes,
all built on order 1's AtmosphereGrade:
  (a) The enemy PNG sheets carry a baked translucent dark halo: 11-18% of their pixels
      are partially transparent smudge. They are conditioned at load with a hard
      alpha cut, so every enemy has a crisp pixel-art silhouette. The conditioned
      sheets are cached for the app session instead of being reloaded every floor.
  (b) A soft contact shadow sits under every upright billboard (enemies, solid props,
      pickups, the shop machine), so nothing floats.
  (c) Firing a gun briefly lights the nearby world (walls, floor, ceiling, props,
      enemies) in the weapon's colour, through the same grade.

What was SAID: "consistent art direction across ... props, enemies, weapons". What is
NEEDED: sprites look pasted on. A halo around a monster against a lit wall, and a
barrel with no contact with the floor, are the two clearest "not finished" signs left
once order 1 has unified the colour.


SCOPE
-----
IN
  - Load-time alpha conditioning of every enemy texture loaded from
    assets/textures/enemies (EnemyRenderer.loadSheetOrFallback path). The PNG files
    are untouched (V1).
  - render/EnemySpriteSheets: a session-lifetime holder of the conditioned enemy
    textures, built lazily on first use and disposed in Main.dispose(). EnemyRenderer
    stops loading and disposing them per floor.
  - Contact shadows in EnemyRenderer, PropRenderer and ShopMachineRenderer.
  - Muzzle light: a weapon-fired seam on PlayerController, a per-weapon light catalog,
    and an additive light term in AtmosphereGrade read by every graded surface.
  - docs/art-direction.txt sections: SPRITE CONDITIONING, CONTACT SHADOWS, MUZZLE LIGHT.

OUT (deliberately not doing now)
  - Outlines or rim light on enemies. A dilation pass on 1024-1536 px sheets is too
    slow at load on phones. A draw-time outline needs 5x the draws. The halo cut plus
    the half-strength grade (order 1) already separate enemies from the haze.
  - Light from explosions, barrels, plasma bolts, hazards or enemy attacks. Only the
    player's own shot lights the world. Point-light positions for other sources would
    need world-space distance per column (cost, and it is not identity).
  - Dynamic shadows, or shadows that react to light direction. A contact shadow is a
    fixed soft blob.
  - Shadows under decals, hazards (fire/toxic), the portal, wall-mounted props, glow
    and aura sprites, and corpses drawn flat.
  - Per-weapon ground sprites (weapon-ground-sprites.txt owns them).
  - Replacing the boss placeholder sprite mapping or any enemy art.
  - Weapon viewmodel re-authoring.


GAMEPLAY LOOP
-------------
Purely visual. No turn, rule or input change.
  1. First EnemyRenderer construction of the session: EnemySpriteSheets loads each
     enemy PNG into a Pixmap, conditions it (R1), uploads a Texture, disposes the
     Pixmap, and keeps the Texture for the session. Later floors reuse it.
  2. Every frame, each upright billboard first draws its contact shadow (R4-R6), with
     the same per-column z-buffer occlusion as its sprite, then draws the sprite.
  3. The player taps FIRE and a shot is actually fired (ammo spent: the same moment
     PlayerController plays the weapon's fire sound). PlayerController notifies its
     weapon-fired listener with the weapon's ItemType. World calls
     AtmosphereGrade.pulseMuzzleLight(itemType). For MUZZLE_LIGHT_DURATION_SECONDS of
     real time (cosmetic animation, not a turn rule), every graded surface within
     MUZZLE_LIGHT_RADIUS_TILES gains the light term (R8). An empty-click, a reload, a
     railgun CHARGE turn and every melee swing emit no light.


RULES
-----
  R1  ALPHA CUT. For each texel of an enemy sheet: if alpha < ENEMY_SPRITE_ALPHA_CUT
      (0.50), the texel becomes fully transparent (RGBA 0). Otherwise alpha becomes 1.0
      and RGB is kept. Applied in one pass over the Pixmap's ByteBuffer, no per-texel
      allocation. Applies to every PNG the enemy layer loads. The procedural fallback
      sheet (generateFallbackSheet) is already opaque and is skipped.
  R2  SESSION CACHE. EnemySpriteSheets.get(path, fallbackRed, fallbackGreen,
      fallbackBlue) returns the cached conditioned Texture (Nearest filter, as today).
      EnemyRenderer's constructor gets its textures from it, and its dispose() no
      longer disposes them. Main.dispose() calls EnemySpriteSheets.disposeAll(). The
      cache must also survive World disposal and recreation (death -> new run), which
      is the point: a floor build no longer reloads 9 large PNGs.
  R3  LOAD-TIME BUDGET. Conditioning runs once per session. The holder logs
      "EnemySpriteSheets conditioned N sheets in M ms". On desktop, M must be under
      400 ms. If not, the builder optimises the loop (bulk IntBuffer read/write)
      before moving on.
  R4  WHICH BILLBOARDS GET A SHADOW. Every live enemy (bosses included) while its
      sprite is drawn upright; every SOLID prop (the TileCategory solid-prop symbols
      and the generator-placed props drawn by the same solid-prop path); every ground
      item / pickup billboard; the shop machine. Never: decals, hazard fire/toxic
      frames, the portal, aura/glow draws, corpse decals.
  R5  SHADOW GEOMETRY. A shared procedural texture CONTACT_SHADOW_TEXTURE_WIDTH 64 x
      HEIGHT 32: an ellipse whose alpha is (1 - r^2)^2 for normalised radius r in
      [0,1] and 0 outside. Linear filter. Built once per renderer at construction and
      disposed with it (each of the three renderers owns its copy; it is tiny).
      Drawn centred on the billboard's screen X, with its vertical centre on the
      billboard's FLOOR LINE: the sprite's draw bottom BEFORE any hover or lunge
      offset (EnemyRenderer: the GameMath.wallStripeDrawBottom value at the sprite's
      depth). Width = projected billboard width x CONTACT_SHADOW_WIDTH_FRACTION_ENEMY
      0.70 / _PROP 0.85 / _PICKUP 0.60 / _SHOP 0.90. Height = width x
      CONTACT_SHADOW_HEIGHT_RATIO 0.22.
  R6  SHADOW TONE. Colour ArtDirection.WORLD_SHADOW. Alpha = CONTACT_SHADOW_ALPHA 0.50
      x (1 - CONTACT_SHADOW_FOG_FADE 0.80 x fog), where fog is
      GameMath.atmosphereFogFactor at the billboard's distance (order 1). Hovering
      enemies (the eye-tyrant hover offset) use alpha x CONTACT_SHADOW_HOVER_ALPHA_SCALE
      0.55, so the gap between body and shadow reads as height. The shadow is not
      graded (it is already the darkest value) and it IS occluded per column by the
      wall z-buffer, exactly like its sprite.
  R7  MUZZLE LIGHT CATALOG. render/MuzzleLightCatalog maps weapon ItemType -> light
      (r, g, b, peak), one register() row per binding. The fallback for an unbound
      weapon is WARM, so a future gun is never dark. Rows:
        WEAPON_PISTOL, WEAPON_SHOTGUN, WEAPON_DOUBLE_BARREL, WEAPON_CHAINGUN,
        WEAPON_ASSAULT_RIFLE, WEAPON_ROCKET ........ MUZZLE_WARM   (1.00,0.82,0.55) peak 0.35
        WEAPON_PLASMA, WEAPON_RAILGUN, WEAPON_ARC_CANNON  MUZZLE_ENERGY (0.60,0.90,1.00) peak 0.35
        WEAPON_INCINERATOR ............................ MUZZLE_FIRE   (1.00,0.55,0.20) peak 0.40
        WEAPON_FIST, WEAPON_KNIFE, WEAPON_HAMMER,
        WEAPON_CHAINSAW ............................... NONE (peak 0: no pulse)
      Colours live in ArtDirection, peaks and timing in EffectConstants.
  R8  MUZZLE LIGHT TERM. While active, AtmosphereGrade holds intensity
      i = peak x (1 - elapsed / MUZZLE_LIGHT_DURATION_SECONDS 0.10), floored at 0. For
      a surface at distance d (tiles):
        light = GameMath.muzzleLightFalloff(d, MUZZLE_LIGHT_RADIUS_TILES 4.0) x i
                (falloff = max(0, 1 - d/radius)^2)
        channel = clamp01(s x tint.channel + light x muzzle.channel)
      It applies to walls, doors, columns, floor, ceiling, props, decals, pickup
      bodies, the shop machine body and enemy bodies, all at full light regardless of
      grade strength (a lit enemy is the point). It is never applied to emissive-exempt
      draws (order-1 R4) or to the weapon sprite (its own frames carry the flash).
      The floor renderer evaluates it per backdrop row, like the tint. A new pulse
      restarts the clock (chaingun fire refreshes it, never stacks).
  R9  The pulse clock advances in World.update with real deltaTime. It is cosmetic
      animation timing (allowed: "animation timings are cosmetic"). It is paused
      whenever the world is hard-paused (any overlay phase that freezes the world).
      It resets to zero on floor build.
  R10 PERFORMANCE. No allocation in render. The shadow is one extra quad per occluded
      column run of each billboard (same draw strategy as the sprite). The light term
      costs one multiply-add per channel and is skipped entirely (branch) when i == 0.


PLAYER EXPERIENCE
-----------------
Monsters stop wearing a brown fog. Barrels, crates and pickups sit on the deck. Every
shot throws a brief splash of light down the corridor: warm for slugs, cold cyan for
energy weapons, orange for the incinerator. Firing into a dark corridor now briefly
shows its shape, which is the most "Doom" moment a raycaster can have, and it costs
nothing in rules. Nothing to teach.

VISUAL DESIGN
-------------
Shadow: a soft, near-black ellipse that is flat and wide, never a hard disc. At
distance it fades with the haze. Muzzle light: a single 0.10 s splash, peak +35%
toward the light colour at the player's feet, falling to zero 4 tiles out. It must
never look like a screen flash: the HUD, mini-map and touch buttons are untouched,
and the hit vignette is unrelated.

INTERACTIONS
------------
  - Order 1 AtmosphereGrade: extended, not replaced. Order-1 emissive exemptions
    still hold.
  - Audio: the light fires at the same site as the fire sound, so sight and sound are
    one beat.
  - Floor twists: BLACKOUT floors get the most out of muzzle light. That is
    intentional and changes no rule.
  - Railgun charge turn: plays its own charge sound, not playPlayerWeapon, so no
    light.
  - EnemyAttackEffectSystem / ImpactEffect particles: unchanged and ungraded.
  - World recreation on death: EnemySpriteSheets survives it (R2).


EDGE CASES
----------
  - Enemy texture missing: the procedural fallback sheet is used and cached like any
    other.
  - Enemy partially behind a wall: the shadow columns use the same z-test, so the
    shadow is clipped where the sprite is.
  - Enemy in its lunge animation: the shadow stays on the floor line (pre-lunge
    bottom).
  - Two billboards on adjacent tiles: shadows draw in the same back-to-front order as
    the sprites. No sorting change.
  - Fire during the last frame before an overlay opens: the clock pauses with the
    world (R9) and resumes after.
  - Floor transition mid-pulse: reset on build (R9).
  - App pause/resume: EnemySpriteSheets textures are Pixmap-built (unmanaged), like
    every procedural texture in the game. Behaviour on GL context loss matches
    existing practice. Not in scope to change.


ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
-------------------
  A1  Running the game, no enemy shows a translucent dark halo or rectangle against a
      lit wall. Every enemy edge is crisp (alpha 0 or 1 only: code read of R1).
  A2  The log shows "EnemySpriteSheets conditioned N sheets in M ms" once per app
      session, M < 400 on desktop. It does NOT appear again on the second floor or
      after a death and new run.
  A3  EnemyRenderer.dispose() no longer disposes the sheet textures.
      EnemySpriteSheets.disposeAll() runs in Main.dispose().
  A4  Every enemy, solid prop (barrel, crate, locker, terminal, ...), pickup and the
      shop machine has a soft ellipse shadow on the floor line. Decals, fire/toxic
      hazards and the portal have none.
  A5  An enemy half hidden behind a wall corner has its shadow clipped at the same
      column as its sprite.
  A6  The hovering eye enemy's shadow is on the floor, lighter, with a visible gap to
      the body.
  A7  Firing the shotgun in a dark corridor briefly (about 0.1 s) warms walls, floor,
      ceiling and any enemy within ~4 tiles. The plasma rifle does the same in cyan,
      the incinerator in orange. Melee swings, an empty click and the railgun charge
      turn produce no light.
  A8  Holding fire with the chaingun keeps the light topped up (restarts) without
      getting brighter than one pulse.
  A9  The HUD, mini-map, touch buttons, health bars and intent icons do not change

  A10 `./gradlew build` + `./gradlew test` green. No new test file or method.

RISKS AND UNKNOWNS
  - Painted edges: a 0.50 cut may chew thin soft details (wisps, glows) on some sheets,
    notably vortex eye and boss overseer. If a sheet visibly loses detail, add a
    per-path override ROW (ENEMY_SPRITE_ALPHA_CUT per path in a small table in
    EnemyConstants, default 0.50), never an if on the path in code.
  - Premultiplied fringe colours: some sheets carry dark RGB under low alpha. Those
    texels become fully transparent, which is the goal.
  - Confirm which PropRenderer loop draws "solid props" vs decals vs glows before
    adding the shadow, so R4's exclusion list holds by construction.

ASSUMPTIONS
-----------
  AS1  The halo in the enemy PNGs is unwanted bake-in (soft drop-glow from the source
       art tool), not intended atmosphere. It is 11-18% partial-alpha texels per
       sheet (measured on the shipped files).
  AS2  A hard 0.50 alpha cut suits the art: the sprites are drawn with Nearest
       filtering, so they are pixel art by intent.
  AS3  The shadow's floor line equals the existing wall-stripe draw bottom at the
       billboard's depth (FLOOR_CAMERA_Z 0.5, horizon at screen centre), as
       EnemyRenderer already computes it.
  AS4  Each renderer owning a 64x32 shadow texture is cheaper to wire than sharing one,
       and costs nothing measurable.
  AS5  The light seam is the existing playPlayerWeapon call in PlayerController, so
       "a shot was fired" means exactly what the audio layer already means by it.
  AS6  MUZZLE_LIGHT_DURATION 0.10 s is short enough never to read as a strobe. Peak 0.35
       stays below any photosensitivity concern (no full-screen luminance flash).
  AS7  Grenade launcher (WEAPON_ROCKET) launch light is warm. The explosion itself
       emits no light in this order (OUT).
  AS8  A future weapon without a catalog row gets warm light, by design (never silent,
       never dark: the SoundRegistry precedent).
