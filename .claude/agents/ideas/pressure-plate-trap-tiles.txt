CATEGORY: Level Design
TITLE: Pressure Plates & Triggered Trap Tiles
---

OVERVIEW
========
A family of floor-based traps that turn the act of walking through the facility
into a tactical reading exercise. The core primitive is a TRIGGER tile (a
pressure plate, tripwire, or pad) that fires an EFFECT when the player steps
onto it. Effects range from immediate damage (a vent gout of flame, a crushing
ceiling slab, an electrified grate) to environmental sabotage (slamming a door
shut behind you, dropping you through a trapdoor to a deeper sub-level, raising
an alarm that wakes every enemy on the floor). Traps are the level itself
fighting back — the UAC's failed automated defenses, half-broken and
indiscriminate, hurting marine and demon alike.

Because everything is turn-based and tile-based, trap timing is expressed in
turns, not seconds. A pressure plate "arms" when stepped on and resolves either
the same turn (instant trap) or after a fixed number of player turns (telegraph
window), letting the careful player step back off in time. Crucially, traps are
detectable — a perceptive player (or one with the right stat/tool) sees a faint
seam, scorch ring, or laser tripline before committing. This is the
"information management" pillar made physical: the floor is data, and reading it
keeps you alive.

The hazardous-tile work already specced for walls and the hazard chain-reaction
system are siblings to this; pressure plates are the *triggered, one-shot or
re-armable* counterpart living in the floor, and they deliberately hook into
those hazards (a flame trap ignites an oil pool, a trapdoor drops a barrel).

GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
1. Level generation/design places trigger tiles and links each to an effect
   (some traps are visible, some hidden until detected).
2. Player approaches. If the trap is DETECTED (visible seam / they passed a
   perception check / they used a scanner), the HUD shows a subtle marker and
   mini-map flags the tile. If UNDETECTED, nothing is shown.
3. Player steps onto a trigger tile → the step resolves as one turn, and the
   trap ARMS:
   - INSTANT trap (e.g. electrified grate, spike grate): effect resolves this
     same turn — damage applied immediately. Lesson learned the hard way.
   - DELAYED trap (e.g. flame vent winding up, ceiling crusher rising): a
     telegraph plays and the effect fires after TRAP_TELEGRAPH_TURNS player
     turns. The player may step off in time to avoid it — a tense escape.
   - PERSISTENT trap (re-armable hazard, e.g. an arc-field that pulses every N
     turns): occupies the tile as a recurring danger until disabled.
4. Effect fires → routed through the appropriate system: deal damage, spawn a
   fire/toxic hazard, slam a linked door, raise an alarm (noise event), or
   trapdoor → transition the player down a floor.
5. Some traps are RE-ARMABLE (reset after firing) and can therefore be exploited
   by the player: lure an enemy onto a flame vent, or back a demon onto a
   crusher plate.
6. Disarming (optional, stat/tool gated): a detected trap adjacent to the player
   can be DISARMED with the INTERACT key, spending one turn, removing the danger
   and possibly salvaging components (credits/cells).

PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
Traps make movement itself risky and deliberate. The player learns to scan the
floor, to distrust the obvious straight path, and to weigh "rush through this
corridor while enemies chase me" against "slow down, I might be running onto a
plate." The delayed-trap telegraph creates pure roguelike drama: you hear the
flame vent spool up and have two turns to scramble clear while an imp blocks
your exit. Detected traps become tools — the satisfaction of luring a Hell
Knight onto the crusher you cleverly avoided is a peak emergent moment.

The decision this creates repeatedly: "Is the fast route worth the risk, and can
I turn this trap against my enemies instead of just avoiding it?"

TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
New package: `…toon3d.trap` (or fold into a broader `…toon3d.hazard` package if
one is created for the existing hazard ideas — coordinate with those docs).

New tile symbols (all are walkable floor for movement; danger is entity/overlay
side, like props):
- `'^'` = generic pressure plate (effect defined by level metadata / a parallel
  trap-link layer, see below)
- `'~'` = flame vent trap (delayed gout)
- `'='` = electrified / spike grate (instant damage)
- `'V'` = trapdoor (drop to next floor) — capital V to avoid clash with `'v'`
  vent wall
- `'A'` = alarm pad (raises facility alert / wakes enemies via noise event)
Re-check these against LevelLoader's existing symbol set before finalizing to
avoid collisions; adjust glyphs as needed. `Level.isWall` is unaffected (traps
are walkable). Solidity/effect handled by a TrapManager, mirroring how the
DoorManager owns door runtime state.

Linking traps to effects: because a plain `.txt` grid cannot encode "plate X
slams door Y," support an OPTIONAL second metadata block appended after the
grid (e.g. lines after a `---TRAPS---` separator: `^ 12 7 -> door 20 7 close`).
LevelLoader parses these into TrapDefinition objects. Generators can emit the
same format. Unlinked trap glyphs use their default self-contained effect.

New / modified classes:
- `trap/TrapType.java` (enum): PRESSURE_PLATE, FLAME_VENT, SHOCK_GRATE,
  TRAPDOOR, ALARM_PAD — each with: isInstant/isDelayed/isPersistent,
  telegraphTurns, damage, reArmable, detectionDifficulty.
- `trap/Trap.java`: { tileColumn, tileRow, TrapType type, armed, turnsUntilFire,
  detected, optional linkTargetColumn/Row, optional linkAction }.
- `trap/TrapManager.java`: owns active traps; `onPlayerEnterTile(column,row)`
  arms/resolves; `tick()` advances delayed traps each turn and fires them;
  `trapAt(column,row)`; `tryDisarm(column,row)`; `tryDetect(...)`. Lives in the
  enemy/turn phase of the global tick.
- `trap/TrapDefinition.java`: parsed-from-file template used to instantiate
  Traps at load.
- `render/TrapRenderer.java` (or extend PropRenderer): draws floor decals
  (plate seams, scorch rings, grate texture, trapdoor outline) as walkable
  billboards, plus telegraph effects (flame vent glow ramping over its
  telegraph turns; arc sparks on shock grates). No render() allocations.
- `LevelLoader.java`: parse new glyphs + optional `---TRAPS---` metadata block.
- `LevelRenderer.java`: flag DETECTED traps on the mini-map.
- `PlayerController.java`: stepping onto a tile notifies TrapManager; INTERACT
  key can disarm an adjacent detected trap (one turn).
- `GameMath.java`: a pure `perceptionDetectChance(...)` helper if detection is
  stat-based (with the required comment block), or detection can be purely
  proximity-based (always reveal within 1-2 tiles) for a simpler v1.

All timing is in turns; nothing is real-time. Telegraph "animations" are
cosmetic interpolations driven by render time, but the *fire* moment is the
turn counter hitting zero.

Constants to add (`Constants.java`):
- TRAP_TELEGRAPH_TURNS = 2 (placeholder, delayed traps)
- TRAP_FLAME_DAMAGE = 15, TRAP_SHOCK_DAMAGE = 12 (placeholders)
- TRAP_DETECT_RANGE_TILES = 2 (if proximity detection)
- TRAP_DISARM_SALVAGE_CHANCE = 0.5f
- ALARM_PAD_NOISE_LOUDNESS (ties to noise system loudness scale)

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- Pressure plate: a square floor decal with a recessed seam and four corner
  bolts, slightly darker than surrounding floor; when armed it depresses 1-2px
  and a thin amber edge-glow appears.
- Flame vent: a circular grille decal; telegraph = a deepening orange glow and
  rising heat-shimmer over its telegraph turns, then a bright vertical flame
  billboard on fire turn (reuse fire hazard visuals).
- Shock grate: a metallic grid decal; instant arc of cyan-white electricity
  snapping across it on trigger.
- Trapdoor: a square seam with hinge marks; on trigger the seam "opens" (dark
  hole) and a fall transition plays before loading the next floor.
- Alarm pad: a red-and-yellow hazard-striped pad; on trigger the whole view
  briefly pulses red (hook the existing emergency-red-alert-pulse) and a klaxon
  plays.
- Detected traps gain a faint outline marker visible in 3D and a flag on the
  mini-map; undetected traps render as plain (or near-plain) floor.

BALANCE NOTES
=============
- Trap damage must be meaningful but rarely instantly lethal at full HP — the
  punishment for missing a tell, not a coin-flip death. 12-15 dmg vs ~50-100 HP.
- Delayed traps with a 2-turn telegraph are "fair"; instant traps must therefore
  carry strong visual tells (you should be able to see a shock grate). Reserve
  fully-hidden traps for higher floors and lower their damage.
- Trapdoors should be opt-in surprises, not run-enders: dropping a floor early
  skips loot but isn't death — frame it as a shortcut/penalty, not a kill.
- Re-armable traps are the fun ones; bias generation toward re-armable flame/
  shock traps near enemy patrol routes so players can exploit them.
- Disarm salvage keeps disarming worthwhile vs just walking around.
- Density guidance: ~2-5 traps per floor early, scaling up; never block the ONLY
  path with an instant-lethal trap (navigability pillar).
- All numbers above are placeholders pending playtest.

INTERACTIONS
============
1. Hazard chain reactions (hazard-chain-reactions-fire-toxic-explosive): a flame
   vent trap igniting an oil pool or an explosive barrel is the headline combo —
   traps are first-class ignition sources.
2. Doors (doors.txt, keycard-doors): pressure plates can slam, lock, or open
   linked doors — enabling "trap room" setups and puzzle gates.
3. Enemy AI (enemy_system.txt): enemies path across traps too; flame/shock traps
   damage them, and a clever player herds enemies onto re-armable traps. Decide
   whether smart enemies avoid known traps.
4. Noise system (noise-propagation) & red-alert pulse (emergency-red-alert):
   the alarm pad is literally a noise/alert trap — one tile that wakes the floor
   and triggers the red-alert visual.
5. Loot/ransack & progression (inventory-loot-ransack, levelup-stim-boon):
   disarming yields salvage; a perception/IQ stat or a "trap scanner" item gates
   detection, making trap-reading a build axis.
6. Turrets (sentry-turret-stationary-enemy): a trap that slams a door can also
   cut a turret's firing lane — layered chokepoint design.

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
- Detection model: proximity-based (always reveal within N tiles, simple and
  fair) vs stat/roll-based (deeper, but can feel arbitrary)? Recommend proximity
  for v1, add a stat modifier later.
- Should the linked-trap metadata block be required, or ship v1 with only
  self-contained traps (no linking) to reduce LevelLoader complexity?
- Do enemies trigger pressure plates, or only the player? Enemy-triggering is
  far more emergent but needs careful tuning to avoid traps clearing the floor
  for free.
- Trapdoor destination: always the next sequential floor, or a special
  "dropped-in" mini-area with a risk/reward cache?
- How many trap glyphs are too many for level designers to track? Consider one
  generic `'^'` plate whose behavior is set entirely by the metadata layer,
  keeping the symbol set lean.
- Confirm chosen glyphs don't collide with current/planned LevelLoader symbols
  (especially `'A'`, `'V'`, `'='`).
