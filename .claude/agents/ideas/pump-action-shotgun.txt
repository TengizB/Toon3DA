TITLE: Pump-Action Shotgun (first weapon + weapon system foundation)
CREATED: 2026-05-27
---

OVERVIEW
========
Introduces the Pump-Action Shotgun — the player's first real weapon and the
keystone that stands up the entire weapon system. One shotgun shell consumes
one turn, deals heavy point-blank damage, falls off with distance, and locks
the player into a one-turn "pump" reload animation that the enemies absolutely
will use to close on you. This is the Doom RPG opening loop: you find the
shotgun on a corpse, you shoot a zombie at 2 tiles, you pump, a second zombie
slaps you, you shoot again, you live. Every weapon shipped after this
(plasma rifle, chainsaw, BFG, pistol) reuses the same Weapon abstraction,
ammo-pool system, attack-resolution code path, and muzzle-flash compositing.

Why now: the `s` (dropped shotgun) decal already exists on the tile spec, the
zombie design doc already calls out shotgun as a 1.5× weakness, the turn engine
exists, and the player currently has no way to actually fight back. This is the
single highest-leverage feature in the backlog after enemies themselves.


GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
PRE-PICKUP STATE
- Player spawns unarmed (fists do 1 damage, used as fallback).
- A shotgun is placed in the level at one or more tiles marked `s`.

PICKUP
1. Player steps onto a tile containing the `s` decal.
2. World checks the tile after the move completes (post-snap, IDLE state).
3. If `s`: shotgun is added to inventory, tile char is rewritten to whatever
   floor char it was before (default ` `). Shells: +8 (one box).
4. HUD pickup banner: "PUMP-ACTION SHOTGUN  +8 SHELLS" — 2-turn fade.
5. Weapon is auto-equipped (player had no weapon).

FIRING (one full turn)
1. Player presses SPACE (attack key — NEW input, see Constants).
2. PlayerController checks state == IDLE and weapon.canFire() (has ammo + not
   in pump cooldown). If yes, transitions to ATTACKING state.
3. Weapon.fire(player, level, enemies) resolves instantly in game logic:
   a. Build a "shot ray" starting at player tile, marching forward
      (directionX, directionY) one tile at a time, up to SHOTGUN_RANGE_TILES.
   b. For each tile crossed: if it contains a wall, stop. If it contains an
      enemy, register hit, apply damage with falloff, continue (the shotgun
      pellets penetrate one enemy — pellet-pierce flavor; see BALANCE).
   c. Damage at distance D: base × falloffMultiplier(D). See BALANCE.
4. Visual: weapon sprite plays MUZZLE_FLASH frame for 0.10s, screen flashes
   warm-white at 0.40 alpha for 0.05s, hit enemies play their hurt animation
   simultaneously. A short "BOOM" sample plays.
5. Turn ends. Enemies take their turn. Now player is in PUMP_PENDING.

PUMP RELOAD (next turn)
1. Player presses ANYTHING (move, rotate, attack — anything).
2. Before the action resolves, if weapon.needsPump() == true: the input is
   consumed by the pump action instead of the requested action. Weapon plays
   pump animation (sprite frames forward → back, 0.20s), distinctive
   "CHK-CHUNK" sample plays, and the turn ends normally. Enemies act.
3. Weapon.canFire() becomes true again the moment pump animation completes.
4. RATIONALE: this is what makes the shotgun *feel* heavy and tactical — you
   literally lose a turn after every shot. The player learns to back into a
   chokepoint *before* shooting, not after.

EMPTY-CLICK
- If player presses SPACE with 0 shells, weapon plays "click" sample at 0.40
  volume, NO turn is consumed, HUD ammo counter flashes red for 0.15s. Free
  to retry / switch weapon / run.

OUT-OF-AMMO FALLBACK
- If player has shotgun but 0 shells, SPACE swaps weapon to FISTS for one
  attack (1 damage, range 1, no pump). This way the player is never softlocked.


MECHANICS
=========

SHOTGUN STAT BLOCK (placeholders, tune in playtest)
---------------------------------------------------
  Base Damage (per pellet, 7 pellets):       2
  Pellet Spread:                             1 tile cone (see below)
  Effective Range:                           4 tiles (full damage)
  Falloff Range:                             5–6 tiles (50% damage)
  Max Range:                                 6 tiles (no damage past this)
  Penetration:                               1 enemy (kill or wound first one,
                                              continues to second behind it)
  Ammo per Shot:                             1 shell
  Starting Shells (on pickup):               8
  Max Shells Carried:                        24 (later upgrades raise this)
  Pump Time:                                 1 turn (forced, after every shot)
  Zombie Weakness Multiplier:                1.5× (already in zombie doc)

PELLET CONE
-----------
Tile-space pellet pattern (player at @ facing right):

    . . p . . .
    . p P P P .       p = side pellet (deals 1× damage)
    @ P P P P P       P = center pellet (deals 2× damage)
    . p P P P .
    . . p . . .

A simpler integer model (preferred for v1):
- 5 pellets along the forward axis (1 per tile up to range 5).
- 1 pellet 45° off each side at range 1 only (catches diagonal-adjacent
  enemies — important since the player can't strafe-shoot).

The cone is processed left-to-right by tile distance, applying damage in order
so that hit-stop / camera shake fires only once per shot.

DAMAGE FALLOFF
--------------
Distance (tiles)   Damage Multiplier
  1                 1.50  (point blank — devastating)
  2                 1.00
  3                 1.00
  4                 0.75
  5                 0.50
  6                 0.25
  7+                0.00

Stored as a small float array Constants.SHOTGUN_FALLOFF_BY_TILE for easy tuning.

WHY NO HITSCAN ARC / WHY TILES
------------------------------
Because the player faces strictly N/S/E/W and enemies live on a grid, a true
"cone" with angles would be lying — the underlying logic must remain integer
tile checks for consistency with LOS and pathfinding. The cone above is a
visual approximation; the *game logic* is "march forward N tiles, also check
the two diagonals at distance 1."


TECHNICAL NOTES
===============

NEW PACKAGE
-----------
  ge.tbegvadze.toon3d.weapon
    Reason: weapons are gameplay objects with state (ammo, pump, equipped),
    they don't fit in `entity` (not on the map) or `item` (which doesn't exist
    yet — items will go in `…toon3d.item` later). Keep them isolated.

NEW CLASSES
-----------
  weapon/Weapon.java           (abstract base class)
    - String displayName
    - int ammoInReserve, maxAmmo
    - WeaponState state         (READY, FIRING, PUMP_PENDING, PUMPING, EMPTY)
    - float animationProgress   (0..1 driven by deltaTime in update())
    - abstract void fire(Player, Level, EnemyManager, WeaponEventBus)
    - abstract boolean needsPump()
    - boolean canFire()         (state == READY && ammoInReserve > 0)
    - void update(float deltaTime)
    - String hudAmmoString()    e.g. "8 / 24"

  weapon/PumpActionShotgun.java extends Weapon
    - implements the pellet cone in fire(...)
    - holds reference to Sound assets (boom, pump, click)
    - in update(): when state == FIRING && animationProgress >= 1, transitions
      to PUMP_PENDING. Pump itself is started by PlayerController on next input.

  weapon/Fists.java extends Weapon
    - infinite ammo, range 1, damage 1, no pump
    - serves as default fallback

  weapon/WeaponSpriteRenderer.java
    - Draws the equipped weapon sprite at the bottom-center of the screen each
      frame, using current weapon + current frame index.
    - Pre-allocates a Sprite[] per weapon (idle, fire, pump 1/2/3). No allocs
      in render().
    - Position: centered on x = WORLD_WIDTH / 2; bottom edge at y = 0; size
      tuned to feel like a marine arm + barrel (placeholder 480×320 sprite).

  weapon/WeaponEventBus.java
    - Tiny callback registry: onShotFired(damage, hits[]), onHit(enemyId,
      damage), onEmptyClick(), onAmmoChanged(weapon).
    - HUD subscribes (ammo counter), camera-shake subscribes, screen-flash
      subscribes, audio subscribes. Decouples weapon logic from presentation.

  entity/PlayerInventory.java
    - Owned by Player.
    - Map<WeaponSlot, Weapon> weapons; WeaponSlot currentSlot.
    - Ammo pools shared per ammo-type, not per weapon (shells live here, not
      on the shotgun itself — future: shotgun + super-shotgun share shells).
    - Methods: equip(slot), next(), previous(), addAmmo(AmmoType, amount),
      hasAmmo(AmmoType, amount), consumeAmmo(AmmoType, amount).

NEW ENUMS
---------
  weapon/AmmoType { SHELLS, BULLETS, CELLS, ROCKETS, NONE }
  weapon/WeaponSlot { MELEE, PRIMARY, SECONDARY, HEAVY, SPECIAL }
  weapon/WeaponState { READY, FIRING, PUMP_PENDING, PUMPING, EMPTY }

PLAYERCONTROLLER CHANGES
------------------------
- Add new PlayerActionState value: ATTACKING.
- In pollInput(): before checking W/A/S/D/Q/E, check SPACE (Constants
  .KEY_ATTACK). If pressed and currentWeapon.canFire(), start ATTACKING.
- If currentWeapon.needsPump() and ANY action key pressed: divert to PUMPING
  action (consumes the turn, doesn't move/rotate).
- ATTACKING uses Constants.PLAYER_ATTACK_DURATION (0.18s — slightly longer
  than move so the muzzle flash reads).
- TurnEngine.onPlayerActionStarted() is still called once per action — pumping
  counts as a turn.

LEVEL.JAVA CHANGES
------------------
- `s` is already a walkable decal. Add new method consumeDecalAt(tileColumn,
  tileRow) which atomically reads the decal char and rewrites it to the
  underlying floor char (default ` `). Stores the original floor char in a
  parallel sparse map so we don't lose unlit/flickering state when a decal is
  picked up over them. (Alternative: store decals in a separate layer entirely —
  recommended long-term; the per-tile-char approach is convenient for now.)

WALLRENDERER / WEAPON SPRITE INTEGRATION
----------------------------------------
- WeaponSpriteRenderer.render() is called AFTER WallRenderer and AFTER
  enemy/billboard rendering, but BEFORE LevelRenderer (mini-map). The weapon
  is always on top of the world.
- Uses its own SpriteBatch (or shares the HUD batch — see HUD idea).
- Muzzle flash is a separate additive-blended quad drawn over the weapon
  sprite for 0.10s.

SCREEN SHAKE
------------
- New util class: render/ScreenShake.java
  - shakeAmount, decayPerSecond.
  - When shotgun fires: shakeAmount = SHOTGUN_SHAKE_INTENSITY (placeholder 6f).
  - WallRenderer reads shake offset before rendering and applies a 2D translate
    to its batch (camera offset only, no world-space change). Snap to integer
    pixel offsets so the pixelated look isn't muddied.

VISUAL DESIGN
=============

WEAPON SPRITE (idle)
- A gloved marine arm holding a black/grey pump-action shotgun, barrel pointing
  toward the top of the screen. Visible furniture: wooden pump-grip, ribbed
  heat shield over barrel, blued-steel receiver. Bottom of sprite anchors at
  the screen bottom — barrel takes up the lower ~30% of the screen.

WEAPON SPRITE FRAMES (5 total, all 480×320 pixel-art canvas)
  1. idle           — barrel raised, hand on pump grip
  2. fire           — barrel kicks UP 12 pixels, muzzle replaced with bright
                       orange-white flash + smoke, ejected shell mid-flight
  3. pump-back      — pump grip slid back 20 pixels, breech open, smoke wisp
  4. pump-forward   — pump grip mid-return, ejected shell falling
  5. pump-idle      — back to idle pose (re-uses frame 1)

The hand-pump-back motion is the iconic "I just killed a zombie" feedback.

MUZZLE FLASH
- A second additively-blended quad over the weapon, ~96px tall, palette
  shifts orange → yellow → white in 3 frames over 0.10s.
- Brief warm-tinted lightening of nearby walls — implemented as a shade
  multiplier bump in WallRenderer during the muzzle-flash window. Falls off
  with distance.

SCREEN FLASH
- Full-screen white quad at 0.40 alpha for 0.05s. Snaps off — does not fade.

CAMERA SHAKE
- Up-down only (a real shotgun kicks vertically). 6px peak, decays over 0.18s.

AUDIO
- "BOOM" — short, low, punchy. Mono. ~0.4s tail.
- "CHK-CHUNK" — two-stage pump click. ~0.5s with a small silence between
  the two clicks. This silence is the *gameplay tell* — enemies hear the pump.
- "Click" — empty-chamber dry firing. Hollow, slightly metallic.
- "Pellets impact wall" — wet thwacks on hit-tile wall (future polish).

HUD ELEMENTS
- Bottom-right ammo counter: large yellow digits "8" with smaller "/ 24" below.
  Font: pixel display, 32px high, slight outer glow when ammo just changed.
- Bottom-left weapon name strip: "PUMP-ACTION  SHOTGUN" fades in/out on swap.
- Both elements go through the HUD idea (scanline CRT HUD).

CORPSE/PICKUP VISUAL
- `s` decal sprite: dark steel-grey shotgun lying flat on the floor, slight
  bloom/highlight on barrel so it's visible in dim tiles. Already part of the
  billboard-prop-sprites design.


BALANCE & TUNING
================

EARLY GAME (no shotgun)
- Player has fists. Fists do 1 dmg, range 1. Zombies have 8 HP. 8 turns to
  kill a zombie with fists, taking 4–8 chip hits from the zombie in return.
- The shotgun MUST be findable within the first 1–2 rooms of level 1.

WITH SHOTGUN, FULL DAMAGE TILE (distance 2)
- 7 pellets × 2 dmg × 1.0× = 14 base, but pellet cone usually hits ~3 pellets
  per enemy in the center column. Realistic per-shot damage = 4–8.
- Apply 1.5× zombie weakness: 6–12 effective. Zombies have 8 HP → most one-shot,
  some two-shot.
- Point-blank (distance 1): 3 pellets × 2 × 1.5 × 1.5 (zombie) = 13.5 — always
  one-shots a zombie. THIS is the tactical reward for letting it get close.

PUMP PENALTY
- Effective cadence: one shot every 2 player turns.
- Net DPS = ~7 zombie-effective damage / 2 turns = 3.5 / turn. Zombie does
  2 / turn melee. Net: player wins 1-vs-1 zombie trades easily *if* they have
  the shells. Player struggles 1-vs-3 in open rooms (correct).

SHELL ECONOMY
- Average shells per level: 12 (pickup) + 8 from boxes scattered in rooms.
- 20 shells / 12 zombies per level = 1.67 shells per kill budget.
- If the player misses, panics, or fires at distance, they will run out.
- This pressures the player to (a) get close, (b) use chokepoints, (c) save
  shells for tough rooms.

DIFFICULTY LEVERS
- SHOTGUN_BASE_DAMAGE_PER_PELLET (single knob for everything)
- SHOTGUN_PENETRATION_COUNT (0 = harder; 2 = anti-horde monster)
- SHOTGUN_PICKUP_SHELLS / shell box size
- Pump duration (could be removed entirely on a hypothetical "rapid pump" upgrade)

CROSS-FEATURE INTERACTIONS
--------------------------
  ZOMBIE ENEMY
    - Already specifies "Weapon Weakness: Shotgun (1.5×)". This is where that
      multiplier finally has a consumer. Apply when Weapon.fire() resolves
      damage on a target whose enemy type defines a weakness.

  DOORS
    - Shotgun cannot blast doors open in v1 (would trivialize keycards).
    - FUTURE: a weak "wooden barricade" door type could be shotgun-breakable.
    - Pump-CHUNK noise WILL alert any enemy within hearing range, including
      through closed (but not locked) doors. Hooks into noise propagation.

  LABORATORY LIGHTING
    - Muzzle flash temporarily brightens nearby walls. Implemented as a global
      shade bump in WallRenderer for ~0.10s after fire — independent of and
      composited over the existing lighting calculation.

  BILLBOARD PROP SPRITES
    - The `s` decal is already speced as a billboard floor decal — no new
      sprite work, just pickup logic. The dropped-shotgun sprite is reused
      from the same texture if the player later DROPS the shotgun (out of
      scope for v1 but architecturally clean).

  RED ALERT PULSE
    - Shotgun BOOM is a "noise event" the alert system can subscribe to,
      raising local alarm state in the future.

  FLOOR/CEILING TEXTURE RENDERING
    - Ejected shell visual is a tiny 1-frame brass particle that lands on
      the floor; can be implemented as a transient decal that fades after
      a few turns, or skipped entirely in v1.


OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
1. Should the shotgun pellet "penetration" stop at walls or pass through
   1-thick wall fragments? Recommendation: STOP at walls. No exceptions.
2. Should pumping be cancellable by weapon-swap? E.g. swap to fists mid-pump
   to avoid wasting a turn? Recommendation: NO — pump completes once started;
   prevents abuse and keeps weapons feeling weighty.
3. Should pump noise wake distant enemies (hearing range > sight range)?
   Recommendation: YES, 6-tile omni-directional alert radius. Noise mechanics
   are a high-value future system.
4. Should the player start with fists, or empty hands? Recommendation: fists.
   "Empty hands" creates a softlock-feeling moment if all weapons run dry.
5. Pellet cone diagonal pellets — do they go through walls at distance 1?
   Recommendation: NO. A wall directly N of the player blocks the NW + NE
   pellets at distance 1. Standard LOS rules apply.
6. Multi-shotgun pickup behavior — second `s` pickup gives shells only, not
   a second weapon. Code path is identical to a future "shell box" pickup.
7. Should we ship a "Super Shotgun" variant in the same idea, or save for
   a follow-up doc? Recommendation: separate doc — Super Shotgun is its
   own decision (double-barrel, 2 shells per shot, no penetration, 16 pellets).
