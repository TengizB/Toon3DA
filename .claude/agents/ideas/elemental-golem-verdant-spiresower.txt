IMPLEMENTATION NOTES (delta from the spec below)
================================================
Shipped as designed, with two deliberate deviations documented here:

  1. SPIRE TILE — the spec's RECOMMENDED path was RECIPE A (register the spire as a tileset SOLID_PROP
     sprite bound to a symbol). In practice that path is unreliable for a RUNTIME-STAMPED tile: the legacy
     palette every hand/generated level carries never binds the spire symbol, so EnvironmentTextureSet
     never realizes its texture and textureFor() throws. It also breaks the tileset drift-guard tests
     (SymbolCategoriesTest / TilesetRegistriesTest hold Level.isPropSolid in lockstep with the category
     system). So the spire uses the spec's stated FALLBACK: a FIXED runtime symbol (':') handled by its own
     Level.isSpire checks (in isBlockedAt / isPropOccluder / the enemy LOS + isLanceBlocked / Weapon.is-
     ShotBlockingCover), exactly like the 'i'/'q' hazard decals, with an owned PropRenderer texture. Documented
     in docs/tile-symbols.txt. This keeps it robust and leaves level generation byte-identical.

  2. WEAPON DAMAGE ROUTING — routed through the existing EnemyHitTarget seam (isSpireAt / damageSpireAt,
     called from Weapon.hitSpireCover at each weapon's cover-stop and from the grenade splash), per the
     spec's "extend the existing hit-target seam, no parallel damage path" instruction. SpireManager
     implements the entity/SpireHitTarget seam so the enemy package never depends on hazard.

Key classes: hazard/SpireManager, entity/SpireHitTarget, EnemyType.VERDANT_SPIRESOWER,
EnemyManager (computeSowerPlan / executeSow / advanceSowerRegen / stepTowardPreferringCover / spawn '}'),
the SPIRESOWER_* constants in BalanceConfig + EnemyConstants. Tests: enemy/SpiresowerTest,
level/SpireReachabilityTest. Gates: ./gradlew test passes (balance audit green; the pre-existing
LevelGeneratorSnapshotTest failure is unrelated and unchanged by this feature).

OVERVIEW
========
The third ELEMENTAL GOLEM (EnemyFamily.GOLEM). A low, four-legged mass of emerald
crystal with a wide bladed mane and two hot yellow-green eyes, crouched in a ring of
crystal shoots pushing up through the floor.

It is the first enemy in the game that EDITS THE LEVEL. It plants crystal SPIRES —
solid, sight-blocking tiles — and drains life from every spire that is still standing.
Shoot the golem and it heals. Shoot the spires and it stops healing, but the room is
already a maze. The player has to decide what to shoot, and the wrong answer is a
fight that never ends.

FAMILY & ROLE
=============
  family()               EnemyFamily.GOLEM
  role()                 EnemyRole.SOLDIER          (TP band 36-66)
  positionalMultiplier() BalanceConfig.POSITIONAL_MULT_MELEE (1.00)
  isRanged()             false
  tacticalVerb()         "SOWER: break the spires first — they heal it and wall you
                          in, and it will never die while one is standing."

Distinct from every shipped archetype: the Blight Corruptor SUMMONS bodies you kill
the normal way; the Spiresower creates TERRAIN. Its adds cannot chase you, cannot be
dodged, and change the geometry the rest of the encounter is fought on. Nothing else
in the bestiary makes the room itself the problem.

SPIRE TILE SYMBOL — REUSE, DO NOT INVENT
========================================
The spires are NOT a new tile symbol. They are runtime-spawned SOLID PROP tiles
stamped into the level grid by a manager, exactly the way HazardManager stamps 'i'
and 'q'. Reuse an existing solid-prop symbol from the SOLID PROPS list (recommended:
'g', the radioactive barrel slot, re-skinned per-level is WRONG — instead register a
new crystal-spire SPRITE via RECIPE A and bind it to the prop symbol the spire
manager uses).

  RECOMMENDED PATH (cheapest, no symbol cost):
    - RECIPE A (docs/environment-tileset-system.txt §9): register ONE
      EnvironmentSpriteDefinition "prop_crystal_spire" in the solid-prop category plus
      its procedural texture generator. That is a single register() line each.
    - The spire manager stamps a solid-prop symbol whose palette binding for that level
      resolves to the spire sprite.
  If per-level palette variation makes that binding unreliable in practice, the
  fallback is a new FIXED symbol via the STRICT RULE checklist — but try RECIPE A
  first. Adding a symbol is the expensive option and the tileset system exists
  specifically so that art no longer costs one.

THE ABILITY — SOW SPIRES
========================
  TRIGGER   Every SPIRESOWER_SOW_CADENCE_TURNS (4) enemy turns, when ALERTED and at
            least one legal target tile exists.
  COMMIT    IntentVerb.WIND_UP with triggerTelegraph() — one full player turn of
            warning, and the target tiles are marked (see VISUAL EFFECTS) so the
            player can step out or pre-empt.
  RESOLVE   Spawns up to SPIRESOWER_SPIRES_PER_SOW (2) spires. Target tiles are chosen
            deterministically (the existing seeded enemy RNG — never Math.random) from
            tiles that are ALL of:
              - within SPIRESOWER_SOW_RADIUS_TILES (3) of the golem,
              - walkable floor (not wall/prop/door/hazard),
              - not the player's tile, not any enemy's tile,
              - not the ONLY remaining path between the player and the room exit.
            THE LAST RULE IS NOT OPTIONAL. A spire that seals the player in a dead end
            turns a tactical puzzle into an unwinnable one. Implement it as a flood
            fill from the player's tile to the room's exits with the candidate tile
            treated as solid; reject the candidate if any exit becomes unreachable.
  CAP       SPIRESOWER_MAX_LIVE_SPIRES (4) per golem. At the cap it does not sow.

SPIRE PROPERTIES
  - SOLID: blocks player movement, enemy movement and line of sight, exactly like an
    existing solid prop. It costs the Lancer its lane and the player their sightline
    equally.
  - DESTRUCTIBLE: SPIRESOWER_SPIRE_HIT_POINTS (30) HP, damaged by any weapon that hits
    its tile. Dies to roughly one shell — cheap, but every shell spent on a spire is a
    shell not spent on the golem. That is the whole decision.
  - DECAYS: expires after SPIRESOWER_SPIRE_LIFETIME_TURNS (8) turns even if untouched,
    so a fled fight cannot leave a permanently mazed room.
  - HEALS ITS SOWER: at the end of each of the golem's turns it regains
    SPIRESOWER_REGEN_PER_SPIRE (2) HP per LIVING spire within
    SPIRESOWER_LEY_RANGE_TILES (5), capped at maxHealth. Four spires = 8 HP/turn,
    which is ~32% of REFERENCE_PLAYER_DPT — enough to matter, never enough to
    out-heal focused fire.
  - DOES NOT ATTACK. A spire is terrain, not an enemy: no health bar, no AI, no XP, no
    entry in the enemy list, no encounter-budget cost.

BEHAVIOUR — COMMIT / EXECUTE INTEGRATION
========================================
The turn engine does not change (docs/enemy-system.txt, PRE-COMMITTED INTENT MODEL).

  computeNextPlan()  -> add computeSowerPlan(enemy, ...) beside computeChargerPlan /
                        computeFlankerPlan. Preference order: sow (cadence due, legal
                        tiles, under cap) > melee attack if adjacent > step toward the
                        player, PREFERRING tiles that keep a living spire between it
                        and the player (a one-line scoring tweak on the greedy stepper:
                        break ties toward cover).
  phaseBExecute()    -> executeSow() stamps the spires; end-of-turn regen tick.

SPIRE OWNERSHIP: a new small manager in the hazard package (SpireManager, mirroring
ExplosiveBarrelManager/HazardManager) owns spire tiles, their HP, their lifetimes and
the level-grid stamping/unstamping. World owns it and wires it into the tick bus. It
must clear on level teardown, and every stamped tile MUST be restored on expiry or
death — a leaked solid tile is a permanently broken level.

VISUAL EFFECTS (renderer spec — EnemyRenderer / a spire renderer / ImpactEffectSystem)
======================================================================================
Procedural only, apart from the RECIPE A spire sprite.

  1. LEY-LINES — THE SIGNATURE. A thin, pulsing green line drawn from the golem to
     each living spire in range: the visible proof of the heal link. Drawn in the
     billboard pass at floor level, tile-quantised, alpha pulsing at
     SPIRESOWER_LEY_PULSE_SPEED, brightening on each regen tick so the player SEES
     the heal happen. Kill a spire and its line snaps with a green spark. This single
     effect teaches the whole mechanic without one word of tutorial.

  2. SOW TELEGRAPH. On the commit turn, the target tiles get a green floor rune that
     grows over the turn — a ring of shoots pushing up. Same floor-decal draw path as
     the Lancer's aim line.

  3. SPIRE BIRTH. An upward shard burst, a bright green flash and a short screen shake
     as each spire erupts. The spire itself has a slow internal glow pulse SYNCED to
     the golem's (same clock, same phase) — they visibly belong to each other.

  4. EYE BRIGHTNESS = SPIRE COUNT. The golem's eye glow (an additive quad over the
     sprite's eye position) scales with the number of living spires: dim at zero,
     blazing at four. A second, redundant readout of the same state — readable even
     when the ley-lines are occluded.

  5. SPIRE DEATH. A shatter burst of emerald shards, the ley-line snapping, and the
     golem FLINCHING (reuse the existing hit-flash on the golem itself, at low
     strength) — feedback that hurting the terrain hurt the enemy.

  6. GOLEM DEATH — WITHERING. Every living spire shatters in sequence over ~0.6s,
     ley-lines whipping out, then the golem collapses into a spray of green shards.
     The room visibly returns to normal, which is its own reward.

COUNTERPLAY
===========
  - SPIRES FIRST. A player who focuses the golem while four spires are up is fighting
    a 132-eHP enemy that regenerates; a player who clears the spires is fighting a
    100-HP soldier. Same enemy, two very different fights, and the choice is theirs.
  - Blast weapons (Grenade Launcher, Double Barrel) clear spires and golem together —
    the first enemy that makes area weapons feel smart rather than sloppy.
  - Close FAST. It sows at range-3 and prefers cover; in melee it has nothing.
  - Or leave. Spires decay in 8 turns, and it is slow. Disengaging is a legitimate,
    intended answer for a player low on ammo — very much in line with the roguelike
    pillars.

BALANCE — TP DERIVATION
=======================
  Regeneration priced as an ARMOUR POOL. Regen is extra effective HP, and the model
  already has the right primitive for that — armorPool() feeds
  GameMath.enemyEffectiveHitPoints directly. Expected regen over a fight:
      SPIRESOWER_REGEN_PER_SPIRE (2) * expected living spires (~2, averaged over the
      fight — it starts at 0 and is being shot) * expected fight length (~5 turns)
      = 20 HP
  So armorPool() = 20f, with that derivation written into the comment above it.

  eHP = enemyEffectiveHitPoints(100, 20, 0, 0, REFERENCE_PLAYER_DPT=25)
      = 100 + 20 = 120.0
  survivalTurns      = 120.0 / 25 = 4.8
  basicDamagePerTurn = 13 / 1 = 13.0
  TP = 13.0 * 4.8 * 1.00 = 62.4     -> SOLDIER band (36-66) OK, upper-mid

  The spires' TERRAIN value (blocked tiles, blocked LOS) is deliberately unpriced: it
  hurts its own family as much as the player, and the model has no positional-denial
  channel for enemies. Flag it for review if playtesting says the Spiresower is
  over-performing its 62 TP.

  Credits 13 sits between Void Shroud (12) and Mire Wraith (15) — consistent with TP.
  XP is derived; never add an XP constant.

  GATE: BalanceConfig change -> ./gradlew test AND ./gradlew balanceSim
  (docs/game-balance-authority.txt SECTION 7).

FLOOR BANDS & SPAWNING
======================
  Floors 1-10+. Alongside the Rimeshell Lancer, one of the two golems present from
  depth 1 — its mechanic is legible immediately and it is slow enough to teach on.

  Rarely more than one per floor: two Spiresowers can cross-heal through each other's
  spires (both are in ley range of both spire sets), which is a genuinely nasty
  emergent encounter. Allowed, but the per-room TP cap should keep it to a deep-floor
  rarity — verify it does.

  EVERY-FLOOR PRESENCE: the four golems are banded so at least one GOLEM archetype is
  available at every depth (Lancer 1+, Spiresower 1+, Colossus 2+, Sentinel 3+); they
  join the normal mixed roster immediately, with no generator change.

INTERACTIONS
============
  - LINE OF SIGHT: spires are opaque, so they feed GameMath.tileLineOfSightClear for
    free — no LOS change needed, and ranged allies lose their lanes to them too.
  - RANGED ALLIES: a Spiresower actively degrades a Rimeshell Lancer's lane. Good:
    the family should not be a strictly-better stack.
  - HAZARDS: fire does NOT spread through a spire tile (it is solid). Spires can wall
    off a fire, which is a nice player tool next to a Cinderforge Colossus.
  - LEVEL GENERATION: it needs floor space to sow. The encounter planner should avoid
    placing it in one-tile corridors — where it is nearly harmless — but no code
    change is required for correctness, only for quality.
  - ENCOUNTER BUDGET: ordinary SOLDIER; spires cost no budget (they are terrain).

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
  1. Do spires block ENEMY movement as well as the player's? RECOMMEND YES — the
    symmetry is honest, readable, and gives the player a reason to let one live.
  2. Should a spire drop anything on destruction? RECOMMEND NO — free loot from a
    renewable source is an exploit (farm the golem, harvest the spires).
  3. Two Spiresowers cross-healing: cap total regen per golem regardless of spire
    count, or let it ride as a deep-floor spike? RECOMMEND letting it ride and
    watching the sim bands first.
  4. Should killing the golem instantly shatter its spires (as specced) or leave them
    to decay? Instant shatter is more satisfying and prevents leftover terrain; keep
    it unless level flow argues otherwise.
  5. If RECIPE A binding proves unreliable, fall back to a new FIXED symbol via the
     STRICT RULE checklist — and update docs/tile-symbols.txt in that same commit.
