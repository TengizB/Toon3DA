MASTER INDEX of the generated-enemy-art series. The work itself lives in
generated-enemy-art-order-1.txt .. generated-enemy-art-order-7.txt, built strictly in ascending

the series rules, the roster plan, the owner decisions already taken and the lessons already paid for.
An order that contradicts this file is wrong; change this file first (with the owner) instead.


OVERVIEW
--------
Every enemy texture in assets/textures/enemies/ was made with image models. They share no light, no
palette, no outline, no resolution and no shape language, so every enemy looks pasted into the
facility from a different game. The owner's need: one bestiary that looks like it was made by one hand
and belongs in the "LIT BY THE MACHINE" world, made the way the rest of the game's content is made -
generated in code.

On 2026-10-21 the approach was prototyped in a working session: a Python sprite kit that sculpts
sprites from height-field shapes and renders all of them through one shared light rig, palette and
outline rule. Two of its sprites - the WARDEN (machine) and the HARVEST TICK (insect) - were approved
by the owner after in-engine screenshots and are already committed as textures. A third (a stone
golem) was rejected. This series turns that prototype into the game's enemy-art pipeline and replaces
all 20 enemy textures, family by family.


THE TOOL (already committed)
----------------------------
  .claude/skills/enemy-art/            the enemy-art SKILL (read its SKILL.md first, every order)
    kit.py                             the renderer: layers of signed height fields merged by smooth max,
                                       gradient normals, one light rig, 6-step hue-shifted material ramps,
                                       specular, cavity + cast occlusion, selective outline, cleanup pass
    bestiary.py                        one function per sprite + the ENEMIES table
    sheet.py                           contact sheets
  assets/textures/enemies/generated/   the shipped PNGs: warden.png, harvest_tick.png (160x240, binary alpha)

Python 3 + numpy + Pillow, outside Gradle, never shipped (a dev tool exactly like run-game). The game
only ever sees the PNGs. Output is deterministic: re-running bestiary.py rewrites byte-identical files.


SERIES RULES (every order, repeated by reference)
-------------------------------------------------
  G1  ORIGINALITY. The existing enemy textures (everything in assets/textures/enemies/ outside
      generated/) are NOT reference material: never opened for inspiration, traced, recoloured,
      "cleaned up" or re-created, not for shape, palette, pose or detail. A new sprite is designed only
      from: the family's place in story/00-premise.md, the archetype's role() and tacticalVerb(), its
      ORA bestiary line (story.bark.enemy.<type> in assets/story/story-strings.properties), its
      mechanics (abilities, ranged or not, hover) and this file. The order's design brief says what the
      creature IS; it never says "like the current one".
  G2  GENERATED IN CODE. Every shipped enemy texture is the output of bestiary.py. No hand-painted pixel,
      no image-model output, no manual edit of a PNG. To change a sprite, change its function and
      regenerate.
  G3  ONE STYLE LOCK. The kit's light rig (key upper-left-front, FACILITY_CYAN rim from the right),
      outline rule, cleanup pass and dither are shared and never overridden per sprite. Materials are
      added to kit.MATERIALS, never as one-off colours.
  G4  CANVAS. Regular enemies 160x240 (2:3, the aspect the renderer already draws), feet near row 232,
      centred on x = 80. Bosses 240x360 (2:3). A floating enemy the renderer does not hover bakes its
      hover into the canvas (empty rows below it); EYE_TYRANT, which the renderer hovers, stands on the
      canvas floor. heightMultiplier() values are NOT changed by this series; sprites are composed to
      fit them.
  G5  SEMANTIC COLOURS ARE SACRED (art-direction V5). No pure red / green / blue / purple / yellow as
      decoration, and SUIT_AMBER is the player's ink - no enemy uses it. Glow inks are per family
      (table below) and must stay visibly apart from those hues.
  G6  SILHOUETTE FIRST. Each sprite reads as a black shape at two tiles, differs from every other
      member of its family, and its size reads its role: CHAFF small, SOLDIER medium, BRUISER heavy,
      MINI_ELITE spiked and larger, BOSS huge. The attack is on the body: a ranged enemy shows its
      emitter, a charger its battering front, a flanker its reach.
  G7  ONE STORY DETAIL per sprite at least - an Organization tag, a harvest port, a shackle, a stencilled
      serial - that ties it to the premise (the moral geometry of who you fight is the plot).
  G8  BINARY ALPHA. EnemySpriteSheets alpha-cuts at 0.50; shipped sprites are rendered without the
      kit's --halo glow. Glow returns only through order 7's emissive layer.
  G9  RENDERER STATE MUST STILL READ. Hit flash, matchup words, health bars, name tags, the Auric /
      Cinderforge / Rimeshell state tints and the boss afterimage are drawn over the sprite by
      EnemyRenderer. A sprite keeps mid-value body colours so every tint and flash still shows.
  G10 NAMES, NUMBERS AND BEHAVIOUR DO NOT CHANGE. displayName(), stats, abilities, ORA lines, sounds,
      spawn symbols: untouched. A sprite that would need its enemy renamed or re-written to make sense
      is redesigned, not the enemy.
  G11 OWNER APPROVAL GATE. Every order generates its sprites, sends the owner a contact sheet AND
      in-engine screenshots (two tiles, default mood + one other mood), and STOPS. Nothing is wired into
      EnemyRenderer until the owner says yes; the approval (or the requested changes) is written into
      that order's DECISIONS. "It looks fine to me" from an agent is never the gate.
  G12 NO TESTS (Testing Policy). Not balance-bearing; verified by ./gradlew build + run-game screenshots.
      No balance gate is owed because no balance file is touched.

Why this order: lowest risk first. Order 1 proves the wiring with sprites the owner already approved.
Order 2 finishes a family the kit already handles (chitin). Order 3 is soft flesh, which the kit's
smooth union does best. Order 4 needs a humanoid rig and cloth - new kit work. Order 5 is the stone
the prototype FAILED at, so it comes after the kit has grown. Order 6 is the largest canvas and the
most important sprites, built last with everything learnt. Order 7 cleans up.

Every order leaves the game building and playable with a mix of old and new art; old textures stay
on disk until order 7.


LESSONS ALREADY PAID FOR (2026-10-21 session)
---------------------------------------------
  L1  Stacked shapes read as a toy. The first kit drew separate ellipses and capsules; legs looked
      glued on. Smooth-max height fields (kit.py) fixed it - keep everything organic in one layer
      and use layers only for parts that must read as IN FRONT.
  L2  Rectangular stone slabs read as bricks. The rejected Mantle Sentinel golem was polygon slabs
      over a glowing backing - the owner's weakest of the three. Mineral bodies need irregular sculpted
      masses, fractures carved INTO them, and glow kept to seams and a core.
  L3  Emissive intensity must come from the shape's own height, not the merged layer height, or glow
      parts burn flat white (fixed in kit.py).
  L4  Glow is easy to overdo; keep it to eyes, sacs, emitters, seams and cores.
  L5  Pasted mock-ups lie (no mood grade, fog, contact shadow, name tag). Judge only in-engine.
  L6  The owner reads story into anatomy: a missing leg read as a missing leg, not as a design
      feature, and was asked back. Story details go on the body (tags, clamps, ports), not by
      removing anatomy the creature should have.
  L7  At 160x240 a 3x5 stencil is legible at close range and invisible at four tiles - right for a
      story detail, never use it for anything the player must read.


DECISIONS (owner, 2026-10-21)
-----------------------------
  D1  Enemy textures are generated by Claude in code, not by image models.
  D2  The existing enemy textures must not be copied, recreated or used as inspiration.
  D3  The WARDEN and the HARVEST TICK are approved and go into the game (order 1).
  D4  The Harvest Tick must have all six legs (the prototype's clamped stump was removed; the
      Organization tag clamp now sits as a band on an intact front leg).
  D5  The golem prototype is dropped; golems are redesigned from scratch in order 5.
  D6  The goal is to replace ALL existing enemy textures with generated ones.


ASSUMPTIONS (series-wide; the owner objects cheaply here)
---------------------------------------------------------
  AS1  The Warden becomes the ACID_DRONE texture (the only non-boss MACHINE, a ranged hovering
       harasser - the Warden is a ranged hovering drone). Its name tag keeps reading "Acid Drone" (G10).
  AS2  The Harvest Tick becomes the SHELL_BRUTE texture (INSECT BRUISER, a straight-line charger: a
       heavy, low, front-facing body). Its name tag keeps reading "Shell Brute".
  AS3  Single frame per enemy for the whole series. Idle / attack / death frames are a later series
       (they need the renderer to pick frames, which is a separate design).
  AS4  The kit stays Python and offline. Porting it to Java runtime generation was considered and left
       out: it would duplicate ~400 lines of numerics in Java, add load time on phones, and buy nothing
       the owner asked for; the PNGs are small (a 160x240 sprite is ~15-30 KB vs the 1024x1536 originals).
  AS5  Enemy display names are not changed even where a new sprite would suggest a better one.
  AS6  An order may grow the kit (new materials, primitives, a humanoid rig). It must regenerate every
       earlier sprite and confirm they are unchanged (byte-identical) unless the owner approved a change.
