OVERVIEW
========
The Neural Leash is a one-handed UAC prototype device that lets the marine HIJACK a
single living enemy and turn it into a temporary, leashed companion that fights at the
player's side for a limited number of turns. It is the spiritual descendant of Doom RPG's
mind-control collar (Hellhound pet), rebuilt for a procedural roguelike: the leash is a
build-defining resource, not a guaranteed pet. You aim it in your facing direction, spend
a charge, and the closest enemy in that lane becomes "DOMINATED" — it walks behind you,
body-blocks incoming fire, and attacks other enemies. When its leash timer expires it
either dies (overload) or breaks free and turns hostile again, mid-fight, possibly behind
you. It belongs here because it converts the existing turn-based enemy AI into an ally with
zero new movement rules, and it creates the kind of high-risk/high-reward, "do I trust this
thing" decision the design pillars demand (Pillars 3, 4, 5, 7).

This is a SYSTEM + ITEM, not a weapon. It occupies an inventory slot (works with
inventory-loot-ransack-system.txt) and consumes a new resource: NEURAL CHARGE.


GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
Acquire:
1. Leash starts EMPTY (0 charges) — found as a rare drop / shop item, or as a meta-unlock
   starting item. Neural Charge cells are picked up off the floor and from ransacked corpses.

Hijack:
2. Player faces a direction and presses the LEASH action key (proposed: F). This consumes
   one full turn AND one Neural Charge.
3. Engine fires a tile-space lane scan from the player's tile in the facing direction (same
   straight-line LOS walk used by the sentry turret and ranged enemies — reuse it). The FIRST
   living, non-dominated enemy in that lane within LEASH_RANGE_TILES is the target.
4. Roll dominate chance:  successChance = LEASH_BASE_CHANCE - target.tier * LEASH_TIER_PENALTY
   (clamped to [LEASH_MIN_CHANCE, LEASH_MAX_CHANCE]). Scale up with player IQ/Agility stat if
   stat system exists (levelup-stim-boon-choice-progression.txt).
   - SUCCESS -> target enters state DOMINATED. Set target.leashTurnsRemaining = LEASH_DURATION_TURNS.
     Spawn a cyan tether beam (see VISUAL). Target's faction flips to ALLY.
   - FAIL -> charge is still spent, target is ENRAGED for LEASH_ENRAGE_TURNS (moves+attacks
     toward player every turn, ignores normal wander). Punishes spraying the leash blindly.
5. If no valid target in lane: charge is NOT spent (dry-fire click, quarter feedback), turn is
   still consumed.

Companion behaviour (runs in the existing enemy turn phase, AFTER player acts):
6. A DOMINATED enemy acts once per player turn like any enemy, but with ALLY logic:
   - Target selection: nearest HOSTILE enemy by tile (Manhattan) within its aggro range.
   - If a hostile is adjacent -> it attacks that hostile using its own normal attack.
   - Else if a hostile is visible -> it steps one tile along the shortest cardinal path toward it
     (reuse enemy pathing).
   - Else (no hostiles known) -> it FOLLOWS the player: steps toward the player's tile but never
     onto it, preferring the tile directly behind the player's facing.
7. Body-block: a DOMINATED ally is solid to hostile movement and to hostile ranged shots — a
   hostile lane scan that hits the ally stops there and damages the ally instead of the player.
   This is the core tactical payoff: a meat shield you steer.

Leash decay & break:
8. Every player turn, decrement leashTurnsRemaining. Tether beam color shifts cyan -> yellow ->
   red as it nears 0 (telegraph).
9. When leashTurnsRemaining hits 0, roll LEASH_BREAK_OUTCOME:
   - OVERLOAD (default, prob LEASH_OVERLOAD_PROB): ally's brain fries. It dies, dropping a small
     Neural Charge fragment LEASH_OVERLOAD_REFUND of the time. Clean, no betrayal.
   - BREAK FREE (1 - LEASH_OVERLOAD_PROB): faction flips back to HOSTILE and it gets a free
     "betrayal" attack if currently adjacent to the player. This is the gotcha — never fully trust it.
10. Re-leash: pressing LEASH on an already-dominated ally with leashTurnsRemaining low spends a
    fresh charge to TOP UP the timer back to full (no re-roll). Lets a charge-rich build maintain
    a permanent pet at high cost.

Death of the ally during combat:
11. If a DOMINATED ally is killed by hostiles, it dies normally and drops nothing extra. The tether
    snaps with a visual zap. No penalty beyond losing the shield.


PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
The leash is a gamble dressed as power. The fantasy: walk into a room, turn the biggest demon
in it into your bodyguard, and let it soak the crossfire while you reposition. The tension:
every charge spent is a charge not saved for the next room, the dominate roll can fail and
ENRAGE the target, and even on success the thing might turn on you the moment its timer pops —
ideally while it's standing right behind you. Decisions created:
- "Do I leash the Demon now as a shield, or save the charge and just shoot it?"
- "Its tether is red — do I burn another charge to keep it, retreat, or kill it myself for the refund?"
- "Two hostiles left and my pet is between them — if it breaks free I'm flanked. Reposition first."
It also defines a whole build: a charge-hoarding "Beastmaster" run that fights almost entirely
through dominated proxies plays nothing like a shotgun-and-medkit run (Pillar 5).


TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
New enum / state (entity package):
- Add DOMINATED and ENRAGED to the enemy state enum (see enemy_system.txt / docs/enemy-system.txt).
- Add a Faction enum { HOSTILE, ALLY } or a boolean isDominated on the enemy entity.
- Enemy fields to add: int leashTurnsRemaining; (0 when not leashed).

New class: entity/NeuralLeash.java  (package ge.tbegvadze.toon3d.entity)
- Holds: int neuralCharges; int maxNeuralCharges;
- Method tryHijack(Player player, EnemyManager enemies, Level level) -> HijackResult enum
  { DOMINATED, FAILED_ENRAGED, NO_TARGET, NO_CHARGE }.
- Lane scan: reuse the existing straight-line tile walk used by ranged enemies / sentry turret
  (sentry-turret-stationary-enemy.txt). Convert player worldX/Y to tile space first (CELL_SIZE).
  Walk one tile per step in facing (directionX, directionY rounded to {-1,0,1}); stop on wall via
  Level.isWall; first enemy tile hit is the target. Respect LEASH_RANGE_TILES.

Companion turn integration:
- In the enemy update/turn phase (the loop that ticks every enemy once per player turn — see
  global-tick-event-system.txt / tick-system.txt), branch on faction:
    HOSTILE  -> existing hostile AI.
    DOMINATED-> ally AI (target nearest HOSTILE, else follow player).
- Body-block: in the hostile ranged lane scan, an ALLY tile is treated as a blocking, damageable
  occupant exactly like the player tile. Reuse whatever "enemy occupies tile" collision the mover
  already uses so two allies/hostiles never stack.
- Pathing: reuse enemy cardinal step-toward-target. No new pathfinder. No diagonal — all logic in
  2D tile space (grid is sacred).

Math (put in util/GameMath.java with derivation comment):
- dominateSuccessChance(int tier, float playerStatBonus): clamp(LEASH_BASE_CHANCE
  - tier*LEASH_TIER_PENALTY + playerStatBonus, LEASH_MIN_CHANCE, LEASH_MAX_CHANCE).
- tetherColorForTurns(int turnsRemaining, int maxTurns): lerp cyan->yellow->red by ratio. Pure.

Constants to add (util/Constants.java):
  LEASH_RANGE_TILES        = 6
  LEASH_DURATION_TURNS     = 8
  LEASH_ENRAGE_TURNS       = 4
  LEASH_BASE_CHANCE        = 0.85f
  LEASH_TIER_PENALTY       = 0.18f   // per enemy difficulty tier
  LEASH_MIN_CHANCE         = 0.15f
  LEASH_MAX_CHANCE         = 0.95f
  LEASH_OVERLOAD_PROB      = 0.6f    // chance leash end = death not betrayal
  LEASH_OVERLOAD_REFUND    = 0.5f    // chance a fried ally drops a charge fragment
  LEASH_MAX_CHARGES        = 3
  NEURAL_CHARGE_FRAGMENT   = 1       // charges per pickup
  LEASH_TETHER_WIDTH_PX    = 4f

Render (no allocations in render()):
- Tether beam = a single screen-space line/quad drawn in the existing sprite/shape pass between
  the player HUD anchor and the dominated enemy's projected billboard center. The enemy is already
  a billboard via EnemyRenderer — reuse its computed screen X and the z-buffer for occlusion so the
  tether is hidden behind walls. Pre-allocate the color objects in create()/show().
- DOMINATED enemies get a cyan rim/tint added in EnemyRenderer (multiply sprite by a cool tint).
  ENRAGED enemies get a red tint + the existing hit-flash style pulse (enemy-hit-impact-effects.txt).


VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- Leash device HUD: small inventory-slot icon, a clawed collar with a cyan power coil. Charge count
  shown as 0-3 cyan pips (procedural, matches procedural-hud-no-textures.txt — ShapeRenderer pips).
- Hijack flash: a brief cyan radial pulse over the targeted enemy billboard on success; a red jagged
  crackle on failure/enrage.
- Tether beam: animated electric arc, cyan when fresh, lerps to yellow ~3 turns left, red at 1 turn,
  flickering rapidly the turn before break. 4px wide, additive blend. Occluded by walls via z-buffer.
- Dominated enemy: cool cyan rim light tint so allies are instantly readable as "yours" in a crowded
  room (information management, Pillar 7). Enraged enemy: hot red tint + faster idle bob.
- Break-free zap: white flash on the tether snap + brief screen-edge red vignette pulse if it lands a
  betrayal hit (reuse emergency-red-alert-pulse.txt vignette path).
- Color language stays consistent: CYAN = under your control, RED = hostile/danger, YELLOW = warning.


BALANCE NOTES (all placeholders — tune in playtest)
===================================================
- LEASH_DURATION_TURNS = 8 is short on purpose: a shield, not a free win. A full leash on a strong
  enemy should feel like ~one room's worth of help.
- LEASH_TIER_PENALTY makes weak enemies near-guaranteed (cheap fodder shields) and bosses nearly
  impossible to leash (don't let players trivialize the Facility Overseer — flag bosses with an
  isLeashable=false hard block to be safe).
- LEASH_MAX_CHARGES = 3 keeps it scarce; a Beastmaster build leans on meta-unlocks / shop charges.
- LEASH_OVERLOAD_PROB = 0.6f: 40% betrayal keeps trust low. If players feel cheated, raise to 0.75f.
- Failure ENRAGE is the anti-spam tax. If too punishing for new players, gate ENRAGE behind floor depth.
- PLACEHOLDER: dominate-vs-stat scaling needs the final stat system before tuning playerStatBonus.


INTERACTIONS (at least 3)
=========================
1. Hazard chain reactions (hazard-chain-reactions-fire-toxic-explosive.txt): a dominated enemy can be
   walked/lured into fire or shot near an explosive barrel; conversely a betrayal-mid-fire is brutal.
   Allies take environmental damage too — steer them carefully.
2. Inventory / ransack (inventory-loot-ransack-system.txt): Neural Charge is a stackable consumable in
   a slot; fried allies (OVERLOAD) leave a ransackable corpse like any kill, so leash-kills still feed
   the loot economy.
3. Noise propagation (noise-propagation-and-3d-sound-cues.txt): a dominated ally's attacks make noise
   and can pull OTHER hostiles' aggro onto the ally instead of you — a stealth tool: leash one enemy,
   let it loudly aggro the room, slip past.
4. Boss encounter (boss-encounter-facility-overseer.txt): bosses are isLeashable=false, but adds the
   boss spawns CAN be leashed and turned against it — a designed counterplay moment.
5. Level-up boons (levelup-stim-boon-choice-progression.txt): natural boon hooks — "+1 max charge",
   "+15% dominate chance", "overload always refunds a charge", "betrayal disabled but duration -2".


OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
- Should a dominated ally be steerable directly (a "guard here" / "attack that" command costing a turn)
  or stay fully autonomous? Autonomous is simpler and keeps the chaos; manual command adds depth but
  needs targeting UI. Recommend autonomous for v1.
- Can the player hold MORE than one dominated ally at once (multi-leash build), or hard cap at 1?
  Cap at 1 for v1 to keep the AI/render and tactics readable; multi-leash as a late meta-unlock.
- Does re-leashing (top-up) re-roll the break outcome or just reset the timer? Spec says reset only;
  confirm in playtest.
- On betrayal, should the enemy keep any damage it took while dominated (HP carries over) — yes,
  recommended, so a weakened pet is easy to finish off after it turns.
- Key binding: F proposed. Confirm no clash with future "use/interact" key for doors/terminals.
