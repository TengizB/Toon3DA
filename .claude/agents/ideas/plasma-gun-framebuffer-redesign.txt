OVERVIEW
========
The current procedural plasma gun in WeaponHudRenderer is drawn with Pixmap
fillRectangle calls. Pixmap can only stamp axis-aligned rectangles, so the
silhouette is a stack of boxes: it reads as "pile of grey crates," not a gun.
This document specifies replacing the Pixmap generator with a one-time render
pass into an offscreen FrameBuffer using ShapeRenderer, which supports
arbitrary triangles and polygons. That unlocks tapered barrels, an angled grip,
a curved trigger guard, and a circular emitter — a real first-person plasma
rifle silhouette in the classic Quake 1 / 1990s-FPS centered-weapon style.

Scope is purely the static "normal" sprite (the `normalTexture` field). The
FIRING / RELOADING animation logic, the recoil offset, the slide-down on
reload, and the live plasma muzzle burst (renderPlasmaEffect) are NOT touched —
they already use ShapeRenderer in world space and look good. We are only
swapping how the gun-at-rest texture is generated.


GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
This is a visual-only change; it adds no new player action and consumes no
turn. The relevant "loop" is the rendering lifecycle:

1. WeaponHudRenderer constructor runs once (PlasmaRifle equipped, no asset file
   on disk) -> calls the new generatePlasmaRifleTexture().
2. generatePlasmaRifleTexture() creates a FrameBuffer at PLASMA_SPRITE_PIXEL_*
   resolution, binds it, clears to transparent, and draws the gun with a
   ShapeRenderer using an orthographic projection sized to the FrameBuffer in
   PIXEL space.
3. The FrameBuffer's color texture is copied into a standalone Texture (so the
   FrameBuffer itself can be disposed and we are not holding a GL FBO for the
   lifetime of the renderer). Returns that Texture as `normalTexture`.
4. Every frame, render() draws normalTexture with SpriteBatch exactly as today:
   batch.draw(normalTexture, drawX, currentOffsetY, WEAPON_HUD_WIDTH,
   WEAPON_HUD_HEIGHT). drawX is already (WORLD_WIDTH - WEAPON_HUD_WIDTH)/2 =
   450, so the gun is horizontally centered. No call-site changes.


PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
The player should instantly recognize the equipped weapon as an energy rifle,
not a generic block. Centered framing reads as "heavy two-handed weapon braced
in front of the marine" — the Quake 1 nailgun / Doom plasma-rifle pose. The
glowing cyan emitter and energy coils telegraph "this shoots plasma" before a
single shot, reinforcing the weapon's identity (pierce-down-a-corridor energy
gun, per plasma-rifle.txt). When idle, the coils and emitter give a faint
"charged and dangerous" read; the existing FIRING burst then erupts from the
emitter tip, which now visually lines up with a real muzzle instead of a box
corner. Decision created: none directly — but clearer weapon silhouettes help
the player track which gun is equipped at a glance during fast fights.


SILHOUETTE BREAKDOWN
====================
Design target: classic 90s sci-fi plasma rifle, first-person, CENTERED and
roughly bilaterally symmetric about the vertical centerline of the sprite. The
barrel points UP-and-slightly-forward (toward top of sprite = into the scene),
muzzle at top, grip at the bottom — same vertical orientation the current
Pixmap uses (Pixmap row 0 = top = muzzle).

IMPORTANT coordinate note: the FrameBuffer ShapeRenderer works in PIXEL space
with a bottom-left origin (standard LibGDX FBO + Ortho camera). So inside the
generator, +Y is UP and the muzzle is at the TOP (high Y). When the resulting
texture is drawn by SpriteBatch with normal (non-flipped) UVs, it appears
upright on screen. (This is the opposite vertical convention from the old
Pixmap code, where row 0 was the top — keep that in mind when porting numbers;
do NOT blindly reuse the old Y values.)

All coordinates below are given as FRACTIONS of the sprite pixel dimensions
(spriteWidth W, spriteHeight H) so the artist/dev can pick the resolution.
centerX = 0.5 * W is the symmetry axis. Recommended resolution
PLASMA_SPRITE_PIXEL_WIDTH = 256, PLASMA_SPRITE_PIXEL_HEIGHT = 192 (4:3-ish,
matches the 380x263 world target aspect ~1.44; 256/192 = 1.33 — close enough,
SpriteBatch stretches to fill, see OPEN QUESTIONS for exact aspect match).

Shapes, from structural to detail. Each "shape" is one or more ShapeRenderer
primitives (triangle / rect / circle / arc). ShapeRenderer.rect is fine for
true rectangles; use triangle pairs for trapezoids/parallelograms; circle for
the emitter; arc/curve approximated by a fan of triangles for the trigger
guard. Coordinates are (x, y) with y-up.

(1) MAIN BODY / RECEIVER — the central mass.
    A wide rounded-rectangle block, centered.
    - Rect: x from 0.30W to 0.70W, y from 0.30H to 0.62H.
    - This is the heaviest visual element; it anchors the centered composition.

(2) TAPERED BARREL — extends UP from the body toward the muzzle, narrowing.
    A symmetric trapezoid (wide at bottom where it meets the body, narrow at
    top near the muzzle). Built from 2 triangles.
    - Bottom edge (wide): from (0.36W, 0.60H) to (0.64W, 0.60H).
    - Top edge (narrow):  from (0.43W, 0.86H) to (0.57W, 0.86H).
    - Triangle A: (0.36W,0.60H), (0.64W,0.60H), (0.57W,0.86H).
    - Triangle B: (0.36W,0.60H), (0.57W,0.86H), (0.43W,0.86H).
    Symmetric about centerX so it reads as a clean tapered barrel.

(3) EMITTER HOUSING + PRONGS — at the very top (muzzle).
    - Short narrow rect (muzzle collar): x 0.42W..0.58W, y 0.84H..0.90H.
    - Two emitter prongs (thin verticals flanking the glow), one each side of
      centerX, slightly taller than the collar:
      left prong rect  x 0.40W..0.435W, y 0.86H..0.96H.
      right prong rect x 0.565W..0.60W, y 0.86H..0.96H.
      (Mirror images about centerX.)

(4) CIRCULAR PLASMA EMITTER — the glowing muzzle eye, between the prongs.
    Concentric filled circles, centered at (0.5W, 0.90H):
    - Outer glow circle  radius 0.085W, color outer-blue, alpha ~0.55.
    - Mid circle         radius 0.060W, color bright-cyan.
    - Hot core circle    radius 0.032W, color white-cyan.
    - Pinpoint           radius 0.014W, pure white.
    This is the focal point and the anchor the FIRING burst emits from.

(5) ENERGY COILS — cyan bands wrapping the barrel.
    3 to 4 thin horizontal rects across the tapered barrel. Because the barrel
    tapers, each coil should span only the barrel width AT THAT HEIGHT (so they
    don't stick out past the trapezoid edges). Compute barrel half-width at a
    given y by linear interpolation between the wide bottom and narrow top:
      halfWidth(y) = lerp(0.14W at y=0.60H, 0.07W at y=0.86H).
    Coil heights (y): 0.64H, 0.70H, 0.76H, 0.82H. Each coil rect:
      x = centerX - halfWidth(y), width = 2*halfWidth(y), height = 0.012H.
    Add a half-alpha fringe rect 1px above and below each coil for glow bleed.

(6) ANGLED GRIP — the pistol grip, extending DOWN from the body, raked back.
    A parallelogram (not axis-aligned) so it reads as an ergonomic raked grip.
    Built from 2 triangles. Rake the bottom of the grip toward the player's
    side; since the gun is centered, rake it slightly toward screen-right
    (positive X) for a natural right-handed hold, OR keep it vertical-symmetric
    for a pure-centered look (see OPEN QUESTIONS — recommend a SLIGHT right rake
    of ~0.04W to avoid a sterile look while staying centered).
    - Top edge:    (0.44W, 0.32H) to (0.56W, 0.32H).
    - Bottom edge: (0.48W, 0.06H) to (0.60W, 0.06H)  (shifted +0.04W = rake).
    - Triangle A: (0.44W,0.32H), (0.56W,0.32H), (0.60W,0.06H).
    - Triangle B: (0.44W,0.32H), (0.60W,0.06H), (0.48W,0.06H).

(7) TRIGGER GUARD — curved loop in front of the grip.
    A C-shaped / D-shaped loop below the body, ahead of the grip. ShapeRenderer
    has no stroked-arc-with-thickness primitive, so approximate the curve as a
    thick arc band: draw an outer filled arc and then "cut" the inside by
    drawing a smaller transparent... NOTE: you cannot erase on an FBO with
    alpha blend the way Pixmap can. Instead build the guard as a ring of small
    quads: sample N points (N=10) along a half-circle of radius 0.10W centered
    at (0.42W, 0.26H), and connect inner radius 0.07W to outer radius 0.10W
    with quads (each quad = 2 triangles). This yields a real curved guard with a
    hollow center, no erasing required. Sweep from ~200deg to ~340deg so the
    opening faces up toward the grip/trigger.
    (Simpler fallback: a single thin curved band of triangles, accept a solid
    look. See OPEN QUESTIONS.)

(8) TARGETING / DETAIL ACCENTS (optional polish, draw last):
    - Top bevel highlight on the body: thin bright rect along body top edge.
    - Bottom shadow on the body: thin dark rect along body bottom edge.
    - A small power-cell indicator: 3-4 short cyan charge bars stacked on the
      right shoulder of the body (x ~0.62W..0.70W). Mirrors the existing
      "charge bars = full clip" read.
    - A subtle scope/sight bump: small rect centered on top of the body where
      the barrel meets it (x 0.46W..0.54W, y 0.58H..0.64H).

LAYER DRAWING ORDER (back to front inside the FrameBuffer)
==========================================================
ShapeRenderer Filled mode. Later draws paint over earlier (no depth test).
Draw order so silhouette structure sits behind detail and glow sits on top:

  1. Trigger guard ring (GUARD_METAL)        -- behind everything else, lowest
  2. Grip parallelogram (GRIP_BASE)
  3. Grip highlight edge (GRIP_HIGHLIGHT)
  4. Main body rect (BODY_BASE)
  5. Body top bevel (BODY_HIGHLIGHT) + bottom shadow (BODY_SHADOW)
  6. Tapered barrel trapezoid (BARREL_BASE)
  7. Energy coils (COIL_FRINGE first, then COIL_CYAN on top)
  8. Emitter housing collar + prongs (BODY_BASE / BODY_SHADOW)
  9. Charge-bar indicator + scope bump + green LED
 10. Circular emitter: EMITTER_OUTER -> EMITTER_MID -> EMITTER_CORE ->
     EMITTER_POINT (largest first, smallest last so the hot core sits on top)

Rationale: the emitter glow is the brightest, smallest, foreground-most element
and must not be overpainted, so it is drawn dead last.

FrameBuffer generation (static helper, runs once at construction):
  import com.badlogic.gdx.graphics.glutils.FrameBuffer;
  import com.badlogic.gdx.graphics.Pixmap.Format;  (Format.RGBA8888)
  import com.badlogic.gdx.graphics.OrthographicCamera;
  import com.badlogic.gdx.graphics.glutils.ShapeRenderer;

  Steps:
    int width  = Constants.PLASMA_SPRITE_PIXEL_WIDTH;   // e.g. 256
    int height = Constants.PLASMA_SPRITE_PIXEL_HEIGHT;  // e.g. 192
    FrameBuffer frameBuffer = new FrameBuffer(Format.RGBA8888, width, height, false);
    OrthographicCamera spriteCamera = new OrthographicCamera(width, height);
    // place origin at bottom-left: setToOrtho(false, width, height)
    spriteCamera.setToOrtho(false, width, height);
    spriteCamera.update();
    ShapeRenderer spriteShapes = new ShapeRenderer();
    spriteShapes.setProjectionMatrix(spriteCamera.combined);

    frameBuffer.begin();
      Gdx.gl.glClearColor(0, 0, 0, 0);            // transparent background
      Gdx.gl.glClear(GL20.GL_COLOR_BUFFER_BIT);
      Gdx.gl.glEnable(GL20.GL_BLEND);
      Gdx.gl.glBlendFunc(GL20.GL_SRC_ALPHA, GL20.GL_ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA);
      spriteShapes.begin(ShapeRenderer.ShapeType.Filled);
        ... draw all layers (order above), using width/height to expand the
            fractional coordinates from SILHOUETTE BREAKDOWN ...
      spriteShapes.end();
    frameBuffer.end();

  Capture into a standalone Texture so we can dispose the FBO:
    Texture fboTexture = frameBuffer.getColorBufferTexture();
    // The FBO texture is Y-FLIPPED relative to normal Texture sampling.
    // Two options:
    //  (A) Keep the FBO and its texture alive for the renderer's lifetime, and
    //      compensate the flip in the batch.draw UVs (draw with flipY=true via
    //      a TextureRegion). Simpler GL, but holds an FBO.
    //  (B) Copy to an independent Texture via Pixmap readback:
    //         Pixmap copy = Pixmap.createFromFrameBuffer(0,0,width,height);
    //         Texture result = new Texture(copy);
    //         copy.dispose();  frameBuffer.dispose();  spriteShapes.dispose();
    //      createFromFrameBuffer already returns top-left-origin pixels, so the
    //      resulting Texture draws upright with the EXISTING batch.draw call
    //      (no UV flip needed). RECOMMENDED — keeps render() untouched.
    result.setFilter(TextureFilter.Linear, TextureFilter.Linear);
    return result;

  NOTE on filtering: ShapeRenderer triangles are not pixel-art; Linear filtering
  gives smooth tapered edges when stretched 256->380. If a crisp retro look is
  preferred, use Nearest (matches the old Pixmap call). RECOMMEND Linear here
  because the whole point is smooth angled/curved edges. See OPEN QUESTIONS.

  IMPORTANT: this generator runs at CONSTRUCTION time, not in render(). The
  "no allocations in render()" rule is satisfied — the FrameBuffer, camera, and
  temporary ShapeRenderer are all created and disposed inside the one-time
  static helper. The per-frame render() still only does batch.draw of a cached
  Texture.

  WARNING — FBO state save/restore: binding a FrameBuffer during construction
  is fine because the renderer is built during World setup, not mid-frame. If
  this generator is ever called inside an active SpriteBatch/ShapeRenderer
  begin() block, the FBO bind will corrupt that batch. Guard: only call from
  the constructor / a dedicated init pass with no active batch.

  Pixmap.createFromFrameBuffer must be called WHILE the FrameBuffer is bound
  (between frameBuffer.begin() and frameBuffer.end()), because it reads the
  currently-bound framebuffer via glReadPixels. Sequence:
    frameBuffer.begin();
      ...clear + draw...
      Pixmap copy = Pixmap.createFromFrameBuffer(0, 0, width, height);
    frameBuffer.end();
    Texture result = new Texture(copy);
    copy.dispose();
    frameBuffer.dispose();
    spriteShapes.dispose();

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- Net read: a centered, bilaterally symmetric energy rifle braced in front of
  the marine, muzzle high. Cool steel body, cyan coils, a single bright cyan
  emitter eye at the top center.
- Centering: the sprite itself is drawn symmetric about its own centerX. The
  SpriteBatch draw places it at drawX=450 (already (1280-380)/2), so the gun's
  centerline lands on the screen centerline (x=640). The 440px clear HUD gap
  (420..860) fully contains the 380px-wide sprite — no occlusion.
- Vertical placement: WEAPON_HUD_HEIGHT=263 drawn from y=currentOffsetY (0 at
  rest). Grip bottom sits near screen bottom; emitter at ~0.90H lands around
  y=237 world units — comfortably above the 130px HUD panels.
- The existing FIRING burst (renderPlasmaEffect) emits from
  barrelY = currentOffsetY + WEAPON_HUD_HEIGHT * WEAPON_BARREL_TIP_Y_FRACTION
  with WEAPON_BARREL_TIP_Y_FRACTION = 1.0, i.e. the very top of the sprite
  (y=263). Our emitter circle center is at 0.90H = ~237. There is a ~26px gap
  between the emitter art and where the burst spawns. RECOMMEND nudging
  WEAPON_BARREL_TIP_Y_FRACTION to ~0.90 so the burst originates exactly at the
  emitter eye. Flag this as a one-line tuning change for the implementer.
- Animations affected: none authored here. Recoil (slide up/down) and reload
  slide both translate the whole cached texture — they keep working unchanged.


BALANCE NOTES
=============
No gameplay balance impact (cosmetic). "Balance" here = visual tuning, all
PLACEHOLDER and to be eyeballed on screen:
  - Sprite resolution 256x192 (placeholder). If edges look soft when stretched
    to 380x263, bump to 320x240. If performance/memory matters (it won't for one
    sprite), 192x144 is the floor.
  - Coil count 4 (placeholder 3-4). 4 reads as "more energized."
  - Grip rake +0.04W (placeholder). 0 = sterile symmetric; 0.06W = aggressive.
  - Trigger-guard arc 200deg..340deg, radii 0.07W..0.10W (placeholder).
  - Emitter glow alpha 0.55 outer (placeholder); raise toward 0.7 if the eye
    looks weak against the bright HUD-gap background.
  - Linear vs Nearest filtering: start Linear; switch if it looks "blurry mush"
    rather than "smooth metal."

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
1. Exact sprite resolution / aspect. Target world rect is 380x263 (~1.44).
   256x192 is ~1.33, so SpriteBatch stretches X slightly. Match aspect with
   256x178 or 380x263? Recommend 256x178 (true aspect, no distortion) unless
   178 height feels low-res — then 380x263 (1:1 world units, crisp, more fill).
   Decision left to implementer after eyeballing.

2. Y-flip handling: confirm Pixmap.createFromFrameBuffer returns top-origin
   pixels on the target platform (it does on LWJGL3). If a flipped sprite
   appears in testing, switch to the TextureRegion flipY approach (option A) or
   call pixmap row-flip. Verify on first run.

3. Trigger guard: full hollow ring-of-quads (nicer, more code) vs single solid
   curved band (simpler, slightly chunky)? Recommend the ring-of-quads; fall
   back to solid band if it looks noisy at 256px.

4. Grip rake direction: pure-symmetric (rake 0, most "Quake centered") vs
   slight right rake (+0.04W, more natural right-handed hold). Recommend slight
   right rake; revisit if it breaks the centered feel.

5. Filtering: Linear (smooth, recommended) vs Nearest (retro pixel, matches old
   look and the rest of the game's Nearest textures). The game's other
   procedural sprites use Nearest — a consistency argument for Nearest. Decide
   based on whether smooth gun edges clash with pixelated walls.

6. Should the fallback weapon texture also move to FrameBuffer/ShapeRenderer
   for consistency, or stay a cheap Pixmap crash-guard? Low priority.

7. Whether to expose the silhouette layout fractions as Constants (tunable, but
   ~30 new constants of pure art layout) or keep them local to the generator.
   Recommend local; confirm with code-reviewer's "no magic numbers" stance —
   these are art coordinates, arguably exempt, but flag it.
