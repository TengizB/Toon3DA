============================================================
WHAT ALREADY WORKS — DO NOT REBUILD IT
============================================================
The architecture is sound and this series changes almost none of it. Everything below stays:
  - the five channels (boot card / bark / exchange / codex / framing) and their precedence;
  - "adding a line is ONE register() call", never a switch statement;
  - story-gating by DEEPEST REGION EVER REACHED, persisted — death never rewinds the story;
  - no timers, ever; one tap always advances; nothing auto-dismisses;
  - strings externalised to assets/story/story-strings.properties by stable id;
  - the headless brain / dumb renderer split.
This rework is about CONTENT, TEACHING and SEQUENCE. Orders 2-9 add at most two headless
classes and NO new renderer. If an order finds itself designing a screen, it has gone wrong.

============================================================
THE SEVEN FAILURES (with evidence)
============================================================
F1 — THE STRANGER PROBLEM
   story.bark.coldopen.1 = "You're up. I'm ORA. I do the talking down here."
   She gives a name and nothing else: not what she is, not why she is in the player's ear,
   not why she is on their side. A name is not an introduction. For the rest of the game the
   most important character in it is a stranger who keeps talking.

F2 — THE JARGON WALL
   Inside the first five minutes the game says, undefined: instance, reprint, soul reserve,
   checkpoint, operator, contaminant, cradle, pattern, yield. Nine proper nouns, no
   definitions, on channels that cannot be re-read. The codex defines them, and the codex is
   opt-in by design — so the words that carry the whole plot are explained only to the players
   who already care.

F3 — A DEATH THE PLAYER DID NOT SEE AND NOBODY EXPLAINS
   The launch screen opens on "PREVIOUS INSTANCE - TERMINATED / SOUL RESERVE .......... SUFFICIENT".
   docs/story-ui-system.txt claims a first-timer "reads it as flavour". They do not. They read
   two unexplained words and a status readout for a thing they have never heard of, about an
   event that happened off screen to somebody they have not met. The single best hook in the
   game — you are already dead and the game opens on the receipt — is being spent on a player
   with no context to spend it on. Worse: "SOUL RESERVE" is the Region-3 reveal, printed in
   full on screen one, years of story ahead of the beat that makes it horrifying.

F4 — ORA DOES NOT TEACH THE GAME
   ControlHint covers six things: MOVE, FIRE, RELOAD, HEAL, SWITCH_WEAPON, INVENTORY. The game
   also contains, all unexplained: the GUARD stance (TouchAction.GUARD, directional defense),
   enemy intent telegraphs (IntentVerb WIND_UP / DEFEND / ATTACK_RANGED, drawn over the enemy),
   the cardinal-line rule that makes stepping out of a lane a dodge, SKIP_TURN, USE_MACHINE,
   INSPECT_WEAPON, doors and keycards, explosive barrels, level-up rewards, and the '>' portal
   that opens the FACILITY NAV console — the branching route map, which is the entire shape of
   a run. A player reaches the end of floor one and is handed a node graph nobody mentioned.
   These are exactly the systems players fail at, and the game's only teacher is silent on all
   of them.

F5 — JOKES WITH NOTHING UNDER THEM
   "A lunch order, dated the last day. Soup. Bold." — the joke is the whole line.
   "Okay, the smell in here is a war crime." — she is software in a suit; she cannot smell,
   and "war crime" is a modern idiom sitting badly in a cosmic-horror register.
   "Morning, sunshine. Reprint {instance}. New record." — the friend of the character is
   scoring their deaths on the screen that exists to tell them they died.
   The rate is not the problem (the LORE/LEVITY weighting handles rate). The problem is jokes
   that reference nothing and jokes aimed at the player's own death before the story has
   earned that tone.

F6 — LINES THAT ASSUME FACTS THE PLAYER HAS NOT BEEN TOLD
   "Crew bunks. Serial tags on them, same format as yours." — the player has never been told
   they have a serial. "New body, same job. Down we go." fires on region entry as the FIRST
   region beat, throwing away the cloning reveal in a subordinate clause. "You're leaking. The
   Cradle bill for this is mine." uses Cradle in Region 1, two regions before the Reliquary.

F7 — NO RECOVERY PATH
   Every teaching line is one-shot for the life of the save. A player who taps the X while the
   panel is still fading in has permanently lost the only explanation of the heal button, and
   nothing in the game measures whether anybody understood anything. StoryTelemetry already
   counts fast dismissals; nothing acts on the number.

THE DOCTRINE — the rules orders 2-10 are written against
============================================================
D1  A LINE DOES ONE OF THREE JOBS: it TEACHES (how to play), it REVEALS (what this place is),
    or it LANDS A CHARACTER BEAT (who ORA is becoming). A line that does none of the three is
    cut. A line that does two is worth four that do one.

D2  PLAIN BEFORE PROPER. No proper noun of this fiction may appear in any channel before its
    plain-language introduction has been delivered. "They keep a copy of you and print a new
    body" comes first; the word "reprint" comes after. Enforced as data + a test (order-3).

D3  THE GAP IS THE LICENSE. This print came back with holes in its memory. That single fact
    makes everything ORA explains — controls, enemies, the facility, the job — diegetic
    instead of a tutorial voice, and it is later revealed to be no printing defect at all.
    (Order-2 owns it, including the deviation from story/02-player-and-cloning.md.)

D4  USEFUL BEFORE FUNNY BEFORE SAD. Kept from story/dialog/ai-assistant.md, now with a floor
    under the funny — THE JOKE POLICY:
      - a joke rides a TRUE OBSERVATION about the room, the paperwork or the job; the
        observation is the line, the joke is the delivery;
      - no joke about the player's death before Region 3, and none at all below the Reliquary;
      - no joke that needs an idiom, a pun or outside-world knowledge to parse;
      - a joke is never the only content in a line, and never one-shot STORY_CRITICAL;
      - if it is only funny the second time you read it, it is not funny on a phone.
    ORA stays dry and quick. She stops being glib.

D5  SAY IT ONCE. Repetition is earned by EVIDENCE, never by a timer: a beat may be said a
    second time only when the player demonstrably missed it (dismissed inside the fast-dismiss
    window) or demonstrably does not understand it (they keep dying to the thing it explains),
    and the second telling uses DIFFERENT, SHORTER, MORE CONCRETE words. Verbatim repetition
    is the thing players call nagging. (Order-4's competence model.)

D6  ORA IS A FRIEND WHO IS ALSO STAFF. She is on the player's side from the first line —
    warm, competent, useful, and honest about what she is allowed to say. Her arc is NOT
    "cold assistant becomes friend"; it is the company varnish coming off a friend who was
    always there. She never mocks the player, never withholds a mechanic to be mysterious,
    and never says something she believes is false without the player being able to feel it.

D7  THE ORGANIZATION IS UNDERSTOOD BEFORE IT IS HATED. A voice that only threatens is
    scenery. It gets a mission, a rationale a reasonable person could accept, and claims the
    world later disproves — so the player catches the lie themselves. (Order-7.)

D8  IF IN DOUBT, CUT. The catalog should come out of this series SMALLER and clearer. Deleting
    a weak row is always cheaper than a knob, a toggle or a setting.

============================================================
THE COMPREHENSION BAR — the acceptance criteria for the whole series
============================================================
A player who has never read a word of the codex must be able to answer these, unprompted, at
these moments. Order-10 automates this as a scripted transcript test; every "answered by" cell
must name a line on an UNSKIPPABLE channel.

  | By when                    | They can answer                                    |
  |----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
  | 60 seconds in              | Who is the voice? What do I press? Which way is on? |
  | 5 minutes in               | What am I fighting? What happens when I die?        |
  |                            | What is this place? Who is giving me orders?        |
  | End of floor 1 (portal)    | What is the map? Why do I get to choose a path?     |
  | First real death           | Why am I back? Does anything carry over?            |
  | Run 2                      | Why do I keep coming back? What is ORA, exactly?    |
  | Region 2                   | What was being harvested here, and by whom?         |
  | Region 3                   | What does bringing me back cost, and who pays it?   |
  | Region 4                   | What is the thing at the bottom? What did I do?     |
  | The ending                 | What do my three options actually do?               |

Anything a player cannot answer at its row is a hole, and the fix is a line on a channel they
cannot skip — never a codex entry.

============================================================
ORA — THE VOICE CHARTER (supersedes the samples in dialog/ai-assistant.md)
============================================================
WHAT SHE IS, in her own first words: the assistant running in the player's suit. Not a person,
not a ghost, not a mystery. Company software that has been in this suit through every one of
their bodies, which is why she is the only one who remembers.

  DO                                        DON'T
  one line, plain words, tied to a moment    a paragraph, or two beats in one panel
  tell them the useful thing first           bury the instruction under the mood
  admit what she is not allowed to say       lie flatly to the player
  name a thing before commenting on it       comment on a thing they cannot see yet
  go quiet when it is bad                    fill silence because a trigger fired
  count the deaths, and mean it later        celebrate them, ever
  be dry                                     be zany, punny, or ironic about their death

SIX LINES THAT SET THE RANGE (Region 1 -> Core):
  "That's you awake. I'm ORA - the assistant in your suit."
  "It's slow. Back up a step and let it walk into your shot."
  "Shift roster. Forty-one people signed out. Nobody signed back in."
  "Print eleven. I know what that costs now. I'd rather you didn't make it twelve."
  "I'm not calling it a contaminant. I can't."
  "Whatever you pick down here, I'm still here."

============================================================
WHAT THIS SERIES WILL NOT DO
============================================================
- No tutorial screen, no help menu, no tooltip layer. The teacher is a character. (F4 is fixed
  by giving her more to say, not by building UI.)
- No timers, no auto-advance, no "skip story" toggle. (Anti-skip, order-6 Part C of the old
  series, still governs.)
- No quest markers, no objective tracker, no minimap ping. Order-9's recap is prose in her
  voice, not a HUD element.
- No making the codex mandatory. It stays the archive; if a fact only exists there, it is a
  bug this series must fix by moving the fact onto a channel.
- No rewrite of the fiction in story/*.md, with ONE recorded exception: order-2 changes how
  the memory block is EXPERIENCED at the start (story/02-player-and-cloning.md), and must
  update that file in the same commit.

DEFINITION OF DONE (the series)
- Every row of the comprehension bar is answered on an unskippable channel, proven by test.
- No proper noun ships before its plain introduction (order-3 test green).
- Every teachable system in the game has an ORA line and a re-teach line (order-4 test green).
- The FLAVOUR pools are smaller than they are today (order-6 cuts nine rows outright), the
  teaching and reveal rows are far more numerous, and 100% of shipped rows do one of D1's
  three jobs. Every surviving joke passes D4.
- docs/story-ui-system.txt updated in the same commits, per its own UPDATE CONTRACT.
