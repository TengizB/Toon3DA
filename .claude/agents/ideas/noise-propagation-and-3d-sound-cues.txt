CATEGORY: Systems
TITLE: Noise Propagation & Directional Sound Cues
---

OVERVIEW
========
A unified audio-and-noise system that makes sound a first-class tactical
resource. Every player and enemy action emits a "noise event" with a loudness
value that floods outward through the 2D tile grid (walls block it, doors
muffle it). Enemies that are still asleep wake and path toward the loudest
recent noise they can "hear"; the player, in turn, gets directional audio cues
(panned/attenuated) for off-screen threats — footsteps, growls, distant
gunfire, alarm klaxons. This turns the silence-vs-firepower tension at the
heart of Doom RPG stealth into a readable, exploitable system that ties
directly into Pillar 7 (information management) and Pillar 3 (emergent
complexity).

Because the renderer is first-person and the mini-map only shows explored
tiles, sound is the primary channel for "something is behind that wall."
Loud weapons (shotgun, plasma) trade burst damage for a wide noise radius that
can pull a second pack into the fight; melee, the Holy Water Pistol's hiss, and
a future silenced pistol stay quiet. Knocking over a barrel or triggering a
hazard becomes a deliberate noisemaker to lure enemies away from a chokepoint.

GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
1. Player performs an action on their turn (step, attack, reload, use item,
   break a prop). The action declares a loudness value (0..100) and an origin
   tile.
2. A NoiseEvent is pushed into the noise system for that turn.
3. During the world tick, the noise floods the grid via a bounded BFS:
   effective loudness at a tile = sourceLoudness - distanceInTiles * FALLOFF,
   minus an extra penalty each time the flood crosses a closed door
   (DOOR_MUFFLE). Walls block flood propagation entirely (BFS only steps
   through non-wall tiles, so sound travels down corridors, not through rock).
4. Each enemy reads the loudness at its own tile. If loudness >=
   enemy.hearingThreshold AND the enemy is currently UNAWARE, it transitions to
   INVESTIGATING and stores the noise origin as its target tile.
   - This reuses the existing enemy alert/aggro hooks from enemy_system.txt;
     noise is just a second way (besides line-of-sight) to flip an enemy awake.
5. For the player: every noise event NOT originating from the player is
   evaluated for audibility at the player tile. If audible, play the matching
   sound with volume = f(loudness) and stereo pan = f(bearing of origin
   relative to player facing). Player hears a growl off to their right before
   the imp rounds the corner.
6. Klaxon / red-alert state (emergency-red-alert-pulse.txt) emits a recurring
   high-loudness ambient noise that suppresses player situational awareness
   (everything is "loud") — a deliberate downside of triggering the alarm.

PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
The player learns to "listen to the dungeon." Creeping forward with a melee
weapon, they hear faint shuffling panned hard-left and choose to peel left to
ambush rather than walk into the open. After firing the shotgun, a chorus of
distant growls tells them they just woke the next room — do they push the
advantage or fall back to the corridor chokepoint? The core decision created:
"How much noise is this action worth?" Every loud action is a gamble against
the unknown population of adjacent rooms.

TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
New package: ...toon3d.audio

New classes:
- audio/NoiseEvent.java
    Immutable-ish value object: int originColumn, originRow, float loudness,
    NoiseSource source (enum: PLAYER_STEP, PLAYER_GUNSHOT, PLAYER_MELEE,
    ENEMY_GROWL, ENEMY_STEP, PROP_BREAK, ALARM, EXPLOSION). Pre-allocate a
    pool (NOISE_EVENT_POOL_SIZE) — never allocate during a tick.
- audio/NoiseSystem.java
    Owns the per-tick flood. Method: floodAndDispatch(Level level, DoorManager
    doors, List<Enemy> enemies, Player player). Uses a reusable int[] or
    float[] scratch grid sized levelWidth*levelHeight, cleared per flood (not
    reallocated). BFS uses a pre-allocated int ring-buffer queue.
- audio/SoundDirector.java (libgdx-specialist territory)
    Maps NoiseSource -> Sound asset, computes volume + pan, and calls
    Gdx.audio sound.play(volume, pitch, pan). Holds Sound handles, disposes
    them. NO allocation in playback path.

Modified classes:
- input/PlayerController.java — on completing an action, ask the active
  weapon / action for its loudness and push a NoiseEvent. Stepping pushes a
  small PLAYER_STEP event (footstep loudness, very low).
- world/World.java — owns NoiseSystem; calls floodAndDispatch() exactly once
  per world tick, after the player action resolves and before enemies choose
  moves (so enemies react same turn). Integrates with global-tick-event-system.
- entity/Enemy (from enemy_system.txt) — add int hearingThreshold and reuse
  existing investigate/target-tile fields. No new state machine needed if
  enemy_system already has UNAWARE/INVESTIGATING/AGGRO.

Math (math-expert):
- Flood loudness falloff: loudnessAtTile = sourceLoudness
      - tilesTraveled * NOISE_FALLOFF_PER_TILE
      - closedDoorsCrossed * NOISE_DOOR_MUFFLE.
  BFS distance = tiles traveled along the open-tile graph (NOT Euclidean), so
  a noise around a corner correctly attenuates by path length, giving free
  "sound goes around corners" behaviour.
- Stereo pan: bearingRadians = atan2 of (originTile - playerTile) relative to
  player facing; pan = clamp(sin(relativeBearing), -1, 1). Volume =
  clamp(loudnessAtPlayer / 100, 0, 1) shaped by a mild curve. Provide a pure
  GameMath method: stereoPanFromBearing(...) and audibleVolume(...).

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
Audio-first, but reinforce with optional minimal visuals:
- A brief directional "ping" arc on the mini-map edge pointing toward an
  unseen loud noise (thin colored chevron, fades over ~3 turns). Color-coded:
  red chevron = enemy noise, white = environmental.
- When the player makes a loud noise, a faint expanding ring drawn on the
  mini-map (only over explored tiles) for one turn — readable feedback that
  "you were loud here." Drawn by LevelRenderer using existing ShapeRenderer
  pass; no new texture.
- HUD status line (procedural-hud) can show a tiny "!" speaker icon when a
  fresh off-screen enemy noise was heard this turn.

BALANCE NOTES (placeholders — TUNE)
===================================
- Footstep loudness (12) must sit BELOW default hearing threshold (20) after
  even one tile of falloff, so simply walking does not wake the whole floor —
  walking is "quiet but not silent": a sleeping enemy in the same room two
  tiles away might stir, one across a wall will not.
- Shotgun (85) should reliably wake everything within ~10 open tiles. This is
  the intended downside vs the plasma/melee.
- Door muffle (25) means a closed door is worth ~4 tiles of distance —
  meaningful, but a shotgun blast still leaks through one closed door.
- NOISE_FLOOD_MAX_TILES caps BFS cost; tune for perf vs largest expected room
  span. With 80x45 grids, 18 is safe.

INTERACTIONS
============
1. Weapons (shotgun/plasma/pistol/melee idea files): each declares loudness;
   this is the system-level reason melee and a future silenced sidearm matter.
2. enemy_system.txt: noise is the second wake trigger beside LOS; enemies
   path to noise origin, enabling lure-and-ambush play and corpse-shot tricks.
3. hazard-chain-reactions + emergency-red-alert-pulse: explosions and the
   klaxon emit max-loudness noise, chaining aggro across rooms; the alarm
   masks the player's own audio cues (downside of tripping it).
4. doors.txt: closed doors muffle noise (DOOR_MUFFLE); opening a door is itself
   a small noise and removes the muffle for everything behind it.
5. billboard-prop-sprites / breakable barrels: breaking a prop is a deliberate
   noisemaker to bait enemies off a chokepoint (Pillar 3 emergence).

OPEN QUESTIONS

- Should reloading be a separate (small) noise, encouraging reload-in-cover?
- Pitch variation per enemy variant for audio identification (Pillar 7) — nice
