Deviations, all recorded deliberately:

  1. THE LINES ARE SHORTER THAN THE SPEC'S. A bark wraps to at most two 40-character lines
     (STORY_BARK_MAX_LINES x STORY_LINE_MAX_CHARS) and an exchange prompt to three, and section B's
     transmissions are 90-110 characters. Each was cut to the claim that MATTERS rather than split
     across panels:
       R2  "Gallery works are routine extraction. Auditory artifacts in your feed originate in you."
           -> "Operator. Gallery extraction is routine and within tolerance."
           The artifacts claim already IS the R2 demand exchange, a floor later, so keeping it here
           too would have been a D5 (say it once) violation. "Within tolerance" also points straight
           at the artefact that disproves it — the tolerance sheet that specifies live weight.
       R3  drops "your compliance record is under review" to "compliance is under review".
       R5  "your pattern is under review for write-off" -> "pattern under write-off review".
     R1 and R4 ship the spec's sentences intact.

  2. THE BRIEF SPLITS ACROSS THE TWO R1 CHANNELS. Section B puts the order on the gate bark and
     section E puts the mission in the exchange, but barks always precede the exchange they share a
     moment with (an exchange is held PENDING until no bark is on screen), so ORA's aside "that's the
     whole brief. Ten weeks and one paragraph" would have commented on a brief the player had not yet
     been given. The gate bark therefore carries the ten weeks and the dead crew — it IS the one
     paragraph her aside is about — and the exchange carries the attribution and the "why me".
     All three planted claims still ship, and all three are still in Region 1.

  3. THE SECOND ORA ASIDE IS AT R4, NOT NAMED IN THE SPEC (which says "2 new ORA asides" and writes
     only R1's). It hangs off the supersedes transmission — "New order, same reference number. I kept
     the old one." It is EVIDENCE, not explanation: it does not tell the player the mission was never
     a purge, which section B is explicit must be something they realise themselves.

  4. THE DESK PROBE STAYS AT R5 AND THE EVIDENCE MOVES TO R3. Section C asks for the probe "in the R3
     exchange"; there is no Organization exchange in R3 and section D forbids a fifth ("four exist,
     keep four"). Resolved by splitting the reveal in the order a player meets it: ORA volunteers the
     EVIDENCE unprompted from the Reliquary down (story.bark.org.desk, one-shot, on floor arrival),
     and the MECHANISM is the reply to the Core order's probe, rewritten from the thin
     "Nobody. That channel's automated." to section C's routing-headers answer. Both halves ship;
     only their order is swapped, and the idea enters play two regions earlier than it used to.

  5. THE R5 REWRITE WAS ALREADY IN PLACE. Section D says the last order "currently asks the player to
     confirm the purge"; the shipped prompt already read "Restraints are to be restored. Confirm."
     Left as it is, with the reason it matters written into the catalog comment.

  6. THE R1 PROBE'S CODEX UNLOCK WAS DROPPED, not moved. It used to unlock codex.contaminant, which
     is already archived twice over (by the gate order and by the vocabulary ladder's naming), so the
     new "Why me?" probe pays curiosity with the answer itself — the first hint that the hole in this
     print's memory has a shape.

PURPOSE
Fixes failure F7 of the fiction rather than of the UI: the Organization is currently a voice
that threatens and never explains. Five gate orders, escalating in menace, containing no
mission, no rationale and no checkable claims. A player cannot be betrayed by a briefing they
were never given. This order gives the Organization a job to hand out, a reasonable-sounding
case for it, and a set of specific claims the world later disproves — so the turn is something
the player CATCHES rather than something ORA announces.

============================================================
A. WHAT IS WRONG TODAY
============================================================
The five gate orders in full:
    R1  Operator. Go down. Purge the contaminant.
    R2  There's noise in your head. It's a fault. Ignore it.
    R3  You've stopped answering. We own your next body.
    R4  Pattern flagged. Reprint authorization: pending.
    R5  You're not an operator now. You're a loss.
As a tone curve this is good writing. As a story it has three holes:
  1. NO MISSION. Nothing states what the player is supposed to achieve, why it matters, or
     what happens to anyone if they fail. "Purge the contaminant" is not a goal; it is a mood.
  2. NOTHING FALSIFIABLE. Every line is an assertion about the player. None is a claim about
     the WORLD that the player can later walk into a room and see is untrue. The betrayal is
     therefore delivered by ORA telling you about it, which is second-hand.
  3. IT NEVER SOUNDS REASONABLE. It opens hostile, so the player never believes it, so there
     is nothing to lose. story/04 is explicit that the horror is banality — nobody in the
     chair is cruel, the chair is (and the codex entry story.codex.overseer says so beautifully,
     where almost nobody reads it).

============================================================
B. THE NEW SHAPE — five transmissions, and what each one is FOR
============================================================
  R1 HABITATION RINGS — THE BRIEFING (bark + the order-2 exchange)
     A job, with a reason a reasonable person accepts. Ten weeks of silence, a dead crew,
     something biological from the lower levels, restart the works. Cold, procedural, not
     cruel. THE PLANTED CLAIMS (each one is disproved later, in a room, by an object):
        "the crew were killed by the contaminant"      -> the rosters and the sedation logs
                                                          show the facility was killing them
                                                          for years before anything woke up
        "it came up from the deep levels"              -> everything down there is walled IN,
                                                          drilled INTO; nothing came up
        "you are the operator on file"                 -> the file says nine descents; the
                                                          player remembers four
     Bark, at the gate:
        story.bark.gate.rings=Operator. Descend, locate the contaminant, restore the works.
     ORA's aside, one-shot, immediately after (the model for how she handles their words):
        story.bark.gate.rings.ora=That's the whole brief. Ten weeks and one paragraph.

  R2 HARVESTING GALLERIES — THE CORRECTION
     The player is now standing in an abattoir the briefing did not mention. The Organization
     does not escalate; it CORRECTS THE RECORD, which is worse.
        story.bark.gate.galleries=Operator. Gallery works are routine extraction. Auditory
                                   artifacts in your feed originate in you. Continue.
     The claim ("routine extraction") is falsifiable by the tolerance sheet in the same region
     — the one that specifies live weight.

  R3 THE RELIQUARY — THE REMINDER OF OWNERSHIP
     First time it mentions the leash, and it does so as HR, not as a threat:
        story.bark.gate.reliquary=Operator. Reprint authorization is a condition of
                                   employment. Your compliance record is under review.
     Falsifiable claim: "reprint authorization is a benefit". The fuel intake sheet in the
     same region shows what the benefit is made of.

  R4 THE WOUND — THE INSTRUCTION NOBODY WILL SIGN
     The mask comes off, and the tell is that the order stops pretending to be about a
     contaminant:
        story.bark.gate.wound=Operator. Restraint assemblies are to be restored to service.
                               This supersedes the purge order.
     This is the line where a paying-attention player realises the mission was never a purge.
     It is the single most important transmission in the game and it currently does not exist.

  R5 THE CORE — THE WRITE-OFF
        story.bark.gate.core=Operator. Your pattern is under review for write-off. Return the
                              asset to restraint.
     "The asset" is the being. The word choice is the whole characterisation.

C. THE OVERSEER IS A DESK — surface it on a channel
============================================================
The best idea in the Organization's material is buried in a codex entry: the voice is not a
person, it is a role in rotation, reading from a script. Put it in play, once, as a PROBE
answer in the R3 exchange (a probe pays curiosity — order-4 of the old series):
    Q  Who is actually speaking?
    A  Nobody. Routing headers change every transmission. The phrasing never does. It's a
       desk, and whoever's sitting at it today.
And ORA's flat follow-up, one-shot, delivered on the next floor:
    story.bark.org.desk=I checked. Six different people have said the same paragraph to you.

============================================================
E. THE RULE THAT KEEPS IT COLD
============================================================
  - The Organization NEVER speaks twice on the same floor and never in a fight.
  - It never answers a question about itself. Its probe replies are procedural non-answers,
    which is characterisation, not evasion by the writer.
  - It never uses ORA's name and never addresses her. She is equipment.
  - It never insults the player. Every line is correct, filed, and defensible, which is the
    horror story/04 asks for.
  - Nothing it says is ever corrected on screen by narration. If a claim is false, the player
    must be able to walk into the room that proves it. If no such room exists, the claim does
    not ship.

============================================================
F. THE LIE LEDGER (build it, then check it)
============================================================
A small table in docs/story-ui-system.txt: every Organization claim, the region it is made in,
and the specific object/log/room that disproves it. Order-10 turns it into a test: for every
row in the ledger, the disproving artefact's string id must exist and be registered in a
region band at or before the region where the player can act on it. A claim with no
counter-evidence in the build is a claim the player can only be told about — which is the
failure this whole order exists to fix.
