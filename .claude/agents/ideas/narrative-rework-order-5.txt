TITLE: Narrative rework 5 — The bestiary voice
---

PURPOSE
ORA introduces the things trying to kill you, and every introduction does two jobs at once:
it tells you WHAT IT IS (which is the story — the moral geometry of the bestiary is the whole
premise) and HOW TO SURVIVE IT (which is the reason the player reads it). Today the six
family lines are pure flavour with no tactical content, and per-type knowledge is not
delivered at all.

READ FIRST
- order-1 (D1: teach, reveal, or land a beat — these lines do two), order-4 (the topic table)
- story/00-premise.md — "the system-as-story table": which family means what
- enemy/EnemyType.tacticalVerb(), EnemyFamily, EnemyRole, SpecialAbility
- docs/enemy-system.txt

============================================================
THE FIND: twenty tactical verbs already written, and nobody ever sees them
============================================================
Every EnemyType overrides tacticalVerb() with a real, well-written piece of advice —
    EYE_TYRANT: "SNIPER: break its line — step off its row or column to deny the shot."
    RIMESHELL_LANCER: "LANCER: armoured until it fires — bait the lance, step off the lane,
                       then burst it through the open shell."
— and `grep -rn "tacticalVerb()"` finds NO caller outside the enum itself. Twenty lines of
finished tactical design are dead data in the build.

This order does not print them raw (they are written in designer voice: caps-prefixed,
em-dashed, too long for a bark panel). It uses them as the SOURCE OF TRUTH for what each
line must convey, and ships an ORA translation of each. A test asserts every EnemyType with
a tacticalVerb has a bestiary row, so the two can never drift.

============================================================
A. THE FIRST-SIGHT LINE — one per FAMILY, rewritten
============================================================
Fires on ENEMY_FAMILY_FIRST_SEEN (already wired). One-shot per family, per save. The pattern
is: NAME IT (what it was) + WHAT IT DOES TO YOU. Both halves in one line.

  UNDEAD      Crew serials on those. Same format as the tags on your cuff.
              (second row, at the moment one closes) They're slow. Back up and let it walk
              into your shot.
  INSECT      Not escaped specimens. Grown here, out of the walls. They come in numbers -
              don't let them get around you.
  MACHINE     Site security. Still working, still on shift. Armoured from the front - it has
              to turn to hit you, so make it turn.
  ABERRATION  Nothing on file matches that. Whatever it is, it's slow and it hits hard -
              stay at range.
  DEMON       That's not crew and it isn't machinery. It's fast. Don't let it start the fight
              on top of you.
  GOLEM       Mineral, and moving. The walls are standing up. It's tough from the front and
              slow to turn.

Region-4 and below, the SAME families get their reframe lines (already the design, kept):
"That wasn't an enemy. That was a body defending itself." The tactical half is not repeated
down there — by Region 4 the player knows how to fight; what changes is what they are told
they are fighting. The bestiary line is where the premise's moral geometry actually reaches
a player who never opens a codex, so each family's later row must carry its column from
story/00-premise.md: undead + insect = the human cost, machine = the Organization's own
immune system, demon + golem = the planet defending itself.

============================================================
B. THE ARCHETYPE LINE — one per EnemyType, on first sight of that TYPE
============================================================
New subject key on the existing ENEMY_FAMILY_FIRST_SEEN trigger: EnemyType.name(). One-shot
per type per save, TACTICAL tier (order-4), so it is held during a combat spike unless the
type is a boss. Each is ORA's translation of that type's tacticalVerb, under 2x40 chars:

  PLAGUE_HULK        Big one. Don't trade punches - shoot it while it's walking.
  EYE_TYRANT         It only fires straight down a row or a column. Step off its line.
  GORE_BITER         They come as a pack. Find a doorway and make them queue.
  SHELL_BRUTE        It charges in a straight line. Step aside, then hit it while it recovers.
  MIRE_WRAITH        The acid stacks. Kill it first, argue later.
  IRON_STALKER       That's a step up. Spend something on it or walk around it.
  ACID_DRONE         It keeps backing off. Corner it - it can't kite through a wall.
  VOID_SHROUD        It goes for your back. Turn and face it before it gets there.
  GHOUL              Slow, and it never stops. Keep walking and never let it corner you.
  CRAWLER            Fast, thin. Drop it now, before the rest arrive.
  REVENANT           Fast and heavy. Do not let that one reach you.
  VORTEX_EYE         Short range only. Break its line or get right on top of it.
  BLIGHT_CORRUPTOR   Thick. Wear it down from range; don't stand in front of it.
  AURIC_SENTINEL     Those shards are its armour and its ammo. Strip them fast.
  CINDERFORGE_COLOSSUS  It re-hardens the second you stop shooting. Don't stop shooting.
  RIMESHELL_LANCER   Armoured until it fires. Bait the lance, step off the lane, then hit it.
  VERDANT_SPIRESOWER The spires heal it. Break those first or it never dies.
  THE_OVERSEER       (boss) It telegraphs everything. Watch one full cycle before you commit.
  THE_CORRUPTOR      (boss) Same deal - it tells you what's coming. Learn it, then punish it.
  HELL_BARON         (boss) Patterns, not reflexes. Read the mark, then move.

Rule: an archetype line names ONE behaviour. Where a type does two things, ORA says the one
that kills players (see order-3's one-idea-per-panel rule); the rest is codex.

============================================================
C. SPECIAL ABILITIES — taught by the enemy that first uses one
============================================================
Five abilities exist and none is ever explained. Each gets one line, fired the first time the
player SEES it resolve (not the first time it is telegraphed), one-shot ever, CRITICAL —
these are the ones that kill a player who does not know the rule:

  SELF_DESTRUCT  It's counting down to blow. Get two tiles clear or kill it now.
  SUMMON         It's calling more in. Kill the caller or you'll be here all day.
  AREA_STRIKE    That hits the whole cross, not just your tile. Get off the line.
  BUFF_SELF      It just made itself harder. Whatever you were doing, do it faster.
  DEBUFF_PLAYER  That did something to you, not to your health. Check the bar.

============================================================
D. THE FIRST BOSS — the one enemy that deserves a stop
============================================================
The first time a boss arena is entered, one bark (not an exchange — the player is about to
fight and a modal here is cruel):
    story.bark.boss.first=This one's on the plans. They built the room around it.
and, on its death, the region's own beat gets a home:
    story.bark.boss.first.dead=They knew it was down here. They built a door for it and kept
                               sending shifts past.
That second line is a REVEAL riding a moment the player is guaranteed to reach — exactly the
kind of spine beat order-8 is short of.

============================================================
E. WHAT THIS ORDER DELIBERATELY DOES NOT ADD
============================================================
- No bestiary screen, no enemy health-inspect panel, no "scan" verb. The codex already has a
  place for long enemy text if it is ever wanted; the delivery channel here is ORA.
- No per-kill commentary beyond what exists — kills are already rate-limited and rolled, and
  order-6 cuts several of them.
- No enemy line that reveals a stat. She says "slow", "thick", "fast" — never numbers. The
  HUD owns numbers.

  one survivable fact.

  grown here, and the golems are the planet standing up.
