STATUS: NOT IMPLEMENTED
CATEGORY: Systems
TITLE: Resource Scarcity Model — make every bullet and medkit a decision
---

Idea 3 of 6 in the balance series (see balance_order_1 for the full list).
Depends on ideas 1 and 2 (config + the four primitives, especially ammo
efficiency and TP).

=== THE PROBLEM IN ONE LINE ===

Right now resources are not scarce, so there are no resource DECISIONS. The
player fires the best weapon at everything, never runs dry, never has to choose
"shoot or save / fight or flee." Scarcity is what converts a shooter into a
roguelike. The goal is NOT to starve the player — it is to make supply land just
below comfort so that CLEVER spending is rewarded and careless spending is
punished.


=== THE CENTRAL EQUATION — SUPPLY vs DEMAND PER FLOOR ===

  DEMAND(floor) = total damage the player must output to clear it
                = sum over enemies of enemy.eHP            (idea 2)
  SUPPLY(floor) = total damage potential the floor HANDS the player
                = sum over ammo pickups of (boxSize * damagePerAmmoUnit)
                  + carry-over from previous floor
                + free melee damage (unlimited but slow & dangerous)

  THE SCARCITY RATIO:
      S = (SUPPLY excluding melee) / DEMAND

  TARGET:  S in [0.75, 0.95]  on a "fight everything" basis.
  Meaning: ranged ammo alone covers 75-95% of the damage needed to clear the
  floor IF you fight every enemy. The remaining 5-25% must come from melee
  (free but risky), avoidance (don't wake every enemy), or carried reserves.
  S >= 1.0  ->  ammo is effectively infinite, no decisions (current state).
  S < 0.6   ->  forced melee too often, feels unfair/grindy.

  WHY just-below-1: it forces the player to ASK "is this fight worth ammo?" on
  the margin. That single recurring question is the entire engagement loop of
  scarcity. It also makes avoidance and positioning (idea 4) valuable, because
  every fight you skip is ammo banked for one you can't skip.


=== WORKED MODEL FLOOR (using current numbers) ===

  A model early floor with a 120-TP budget might hold, e.g.:
      6x Gore Biter (18hp) + 3x Eye Tyrant (18) + 2x Shell Brute (38)
                            + 1x Plague Hulk (50)
  DEMAND = 6*18 + 3*18 + 2*38 + 1*50 = 108+54+76+50 = 288 damage.

  To hit S = 0.85, SUPPLY(ranged) should be ~245 damage.
  Current generator: LEVEL_GEN_AMMO_CHANCE_PER_ROOM 0.35, shells box = 12.
      If the floor has ~8 rooms -> ~2.8 ammo drops. If they roll shells:
      2.8 * 12 shells * 50 dmg/shell = 1680 potential damage  ->  S = 5.8 (!!)
  This is the bug, quantified: a SINGLE weapon's ammo on the floor provides ~6x
  the damage needed to clear it. There is zero scarcity. Reserve caps make it
  worse: 60 shells * 50 = 3000 banked damage ceiling.

  FIX DIRECTION (do the real tuning in implementation):
   - Cut per-room ammo drop frequency AND box sizes so per-weapon SUPPLY <
     DEMAND for that weapon alone (no single gun clears the floor).
   - Spread ammo across types so the player is pushed to use 2-3 weapons.
   - Lower reserve caps so banking is limited (can't hoard 50 floors of ammo).


=== THE FOUR LEVERS OF SCARCITY (all live in BalanceConfig) ===

  LEVER 1 — DROP FREQUENCY:  how often a pickup spawns at all.
      (LEVEL_GEN_AMMO_CHANCE_PER_ROOM and friends)
  LEVER 2 — DROP SIZE:       how much each pickup gives (ammo box sizes).
  LEVER 3 — RESERVE CAP:     the hoarding ceiling (AMMO_RESERVE_CAP_*).
  LEVER 4 — DEMAND:          enemy density/eHP per floor (the TP budget).

  RULE OF THUMB for ordering changes: tune LEVER 4 (demand) and LEVER 2 (drop
  size) first; they have the biggest, most predictable effect. Frequency
  (LEVER 1) adds variance — good for texture, bad as a primary dial. Caps
  (LEVER 3) only matter once supply is low enough that hoarding is possible.


=== PER-WEAPON SCARCITY: force diversification ===

  DESIGN RULE: no single weapon's ammo economy should cover a whole floor.
      perWeaponSupply(w) / DEMAND  <  0.6   for every weapon w.
  When every weapon is individually insufficient but the SUM is ~0.85, the
  player MUST switch weapons during a floor — which is exactly where build
  identity (idea 5) and tactical weapon-vs-enemy matchups (idea 4) come alive.
  Example intent: "shells handle bruisers, bullets handle chaff, save cells for
  the room you can't avoid." That sentence is the game being fun.


=== THE HEAL ECONOMY (HP is a resource too) ===

  Incoming damage per floor (if the player takes a realistic fraction of hits):
      INCOMING(floor) = sum over enemies of (enemyDPT * expectedTurnsEngaged)
                        * (1 - avoidanceFactor)
  HEAL SUPPLY(floor) = medkits found * heal value (STIM 18 / FULL 50)
                       + armour found (SHARD 8 / VEST 35)

  TARGET:  netHpDrain(floor) = INCOMING - HEAL_SUPPLY  >  0 on most floors,
           but  cumulativeDrain across a run  <  player eHP buffer + level-up HP.
  i.e. each floor should cost you some HP you don't fully recover, so HP slowly
  becomes precious and you can't face-tank — but the run total stays survivable
  for a skilled player. A medkit should be a "spend now or bank for the bad
  room?" decision (idea 2 contract C: price heals in survival-turns-bought).

  ANTI-FACE-TANK: if HEAL_SUPPLY >= INCOMING the player never feels HP pressure.
  Keep medkit drop chances (currently 0.50-0.70 in loot rooms) low enough that
  the average floor is a small net HP loss.


=== ANTI-HOARD MECHANISMS (so banking doesn't defeat scarcity) ===

  A player who hoards 5 floors of ammo has no scarcity. Counter with ANY of:
   1. LOW RESERVE CAPS (already exist — tune them down). Hard ceiling on banking.
   2. DIMINISHING PICKUPS: a pickup gives less if you're already near cap
      (overflow is wasted -> picking up at full is a non-choice, reinforcing
      "spend before you top up").
   3. DEPTH-INDEXED DEMAND OUTPACING SUPPLY SLOWLY: enemy eHP scales 1.08/depth
      (idea 1); if SUPPLY scales slower (e.g. LEVEL_GEN_DEPTH_AMMO_BONUS 0.20 is
      linear and gentler), S naturally tightens as you descend -> hoards get
      spent. This is the cleanest anti-hoard: the math erodes the stockpile.
  Recommendation: lean on #1 and #3; #2 is optional polish.


=== THE CREDIT SINK PROBLEM (prerequisite flag) ===

  Credits are awarded (5-40/kill, 3-7 chips/floor) but THERE IS NO SHOP to spend
  them (verified: roguelike_order_11 shop NOT IMPLEMENTED). Currency with no sink
  is not a resource — it is a meaningless counter. Until a sink exists, credits
  cannot participate in the scarcity economy.
  -> PREREQUISITE: a shop / vending / fabricator sink must exist before the
     credit economy can be balanced. This idea models ammo + HP scarcity (which
     CAN be done now); the credit half is BLOCKED on that sink. Note it loudly so
     it isn't forgotten. When the shop lands, prices follow the same method:
     price an item at (its TP-equiv or survival-turns-bought) * a scarcity markup.


=== TUNING PROCEDURE (how to actually set the numbers) ===

  1. Fix the floor TP budget per depth (idea 4 owns the encounter budget).
  2. Compute DEMAND = sum of enemy eHP for that budget.
  3. Set ammo SUPPLY so S = 0.85 floor-wide and < 0.6 per individual weapon.
  4. Set reserve caps to ~1.5 floors of that weapon's supply (limited banking).
  5. Set heal supply so the average floor is a 5-15% net eHP loss.
  6. Print the living table (idea 2) and read S and netHpDrain directly.
  7. Playtest; adjust LEVER 4 and LEVER 2 first.


=== PREREQUISITES ===
  - Ideas 1 & 2 (config + ammo-efficiency/eHP/TP formulas).
  - Idea 4's floor TP budget (they co-depend: scarcity is defined PER floor
    budget). Build idea 3 and idea 4 together if possible.
  - BLOCKED (credit half only): a shop/sink must exist. Ammo+HP half is unblocked.

=== DEFINITION OF DONE ===
  - SUPPLY/DEMAND, scarcity ratio S, and netHpDrain are formalized in GameMath
    and printed in the living balance table.
  - BalanceConfig's four levers are tuned so S in [0.75,0.95] floor-wide and
    < 0.6 per weapon, with average floor a small net HP loss, at depth 1.
  - Reserve caps limit banking to ~1.5 floors.
  - The credit-sink prerequisite is documented as a blocker for the credit half.
