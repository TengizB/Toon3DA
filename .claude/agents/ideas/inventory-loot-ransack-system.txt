TITLE: Slot Inventory, Loot Drops & Corpse Ransacking
---

OVERVIEW
========
A unified item-and-inventory backbone for the run. It introduces a small,
weight-limited slot inventory, ground loot that the player steps onto and picks
up, and the Doom RPG "ransack the corpse" verb — every dead enemy leaves a
lootable husk that may cough up ammo, credits, a medkit, or (rarely) a weapon
mod. This is the connective tissue the rest of the game economy hangs on:
medkits, ammo types, keycards, stims, and shop purchases all become concrete
items that live in slots and compete for space.

The design leans into the roguelike pillar of "meaningful choice under
pressure." Inventory is small enough that the player must constantly decide
what to carry and what to leave behind. Because every action costs a turn,
ransacking a corpse mid-fight is a genuine gamble — you spend a turn looting
while other enemies close in. Loot is generated, not placed: each run produces
a different distribution of drops, so route planning and risk assessment never
calcify into memorized solutions.

Thematically this is pure UAC scavenging: you are a lone marine stripping gear
off fallen comrades and prying ammo from twitching demon husks while the
facility burns. Dark humor is welcome in the flavor text ("You find half a
sandwich. And a clip." / "The zombie's pockets contained only regret and 6
shells.").

GAMEPLAY LOOP
=============
1. Player kills an enemy → enemy entity is removed from active simulation and
   replaced by a CORPSE prop on its tile (reuses the existing `m` corpse decal
   visual, tinted by enemy type).
2. Player steps onto OR faces-adjacent to a corpse/loot tile → a context prompt
   appears in the HUD status line: "[F] Ransack" or "[F] Pick up SHELLS x6".
3. Player presses F (the new INTERACT/LOOT key) → this consumes one turn:
   - For a corpse: roll the enemy's loot table once. Spawn 0-2 ground items on
     that tile (or auto-collect ammo/credits straight into inventory if space
     allows). Corpse becomes "ransacked" (visual desaturated, cannot loot again).
   - For a ground item: attempt to add to inventory. If a free slot exists or it
     stacks, collect it; otherwise show "INVENTORY FULL" and leave it on ground.
4. Enemies then take their turn as normal (looting is not free — the world
   advances).
5. Player opens the inventory overlay with TAB (does NOT cost a turn; it pauses
   input like a menu). Player can: inspect an item, USE a consumable (closing
   the menu and spending one turn on use), or DROP an item (spawns it on the
   current tile, costs one turn on menu close).
6. Stackable items (ammo, credits, stims) merge automatically. Unique items
   (weapons, keycards, weapon mods) each occupy one slot.

PLAYER EXPERIENCE
=================
The player feels the constant low hum of scarcity. Picking up a third medkit
when you already carry two means dropping something — maybe the spare keycard,
maybe rockets you were saving. The ransack verb adds a sharp tactical tension:
the satisfying click of stepping onto a fresh corpse is tempered by the fact
that bending down to loot lets the imp behind you take a free swing. Good
players learn to clear a room before ransacking; greedy players learn why that
is a rule. Opening the TAB inventory is a moment of calm planning — a small
"safe room" in menu form — which contrasts nicely with the turn-pressure of the
world.

The core decision this creates: "Is this turn better spent looting, attacking,
or retreating?" Every corpse is a question.

TECHNICAL NOTES
===============
New package: `…toon3d.item`.

New classes:
- `item/ItemType.java` (enum): SHELLS, BULLETS, ROCKETS, PLASMA_CELLS, CREDITS,
  MEDKIT_SMALL, MEDKIT_LARGE, STIMPACK, ARMOR_SHARD, KEYCARD_BLUE,
  KEYCARD_RED, KEYCARD_YELLOW, WEAPON_SHOTGUN, WEAPON_PLASMA, WEAPON_MOD_*, etc.
  Each enum constant stores: display name, isStackable, maxStackSize, weight,
  glyph/color for ground rendering, and a category for sorting.
- `item/ItemStack.java`: { ItemType type; int quantity; } — value-style holder.
  Pre-allocate; reuse rather than churn in hot paths.
- `item/Inventory.java`: fixed-size `ItemStack[]` (size = INVENTORY_SLOT_COUNT)
  plus a separate uncapped "ammo pouch" map if ammo should not eat slots (see
  OPEN QUESTIONS). Methods: `tryAdd(ItemType, qty)`, `remove(slot, qty)`,
  `use(slot)`, `findStack(ItemType)`, `isFull()`. No allocations in add/use.
- `item/GroundItem.java`: an entity-like { int tileColumn; int tileRow;
  ItemStack stack; } owned by a `GroundItemManager`.
- `item/GroundItemManager.java` (in `…toon3d.world` or `…toon3d.item`): holds
  the list of active ground items, answers `itemAt(column,row)`, handles spawn
  and pickup. Rendered as billboards by extending the existing PropRenderer
  path (ground items use the walkable-decal billboard style, ~0.20x wall).
- `item/LootTable.java`: maps an enemy type id → weighted list of possible
  drops. `rollDrops(enemyTypeId, random)` returns 0-2 ItemStacks. Use a fixed
  `java.util.Random` seeded per-run for reproducibility.
- `render/InventoryOverlayRenderer.java`: the TAB menu — a grid of slots drawn
  with the existing procedural-HUD shape/text style (no textures). Implements
  Renderable + Disposable.

Modified classes:
- `Level.java`: no new wall chars needed; loot is entity-side, not tile-side.
  (Optional: a `'$'` tile for a pre-placed credit cache if level designers want
  hand-seeded loot — see OPEN QUESTIONS.)
- `PlayerController.java`: add INTERACT/LOOT (F) and TOGGLE_INVENTORY (TAB) to
  the input priority order. Looting and item-use must route through the turn
  system so they advance the world exactly one turn (TAB open/close must NOT).
- `World.java` / `GlobalTickEventSystem` (see existing global-tick-event-system
  idea): looting/using emit a turn the same way a move does. Hook here.
- `HudRenderer.java`: render the context prompt ("[F] Ransack") in the left
  panel status line; reflect ammo counts from the new Inventory rather than
  ad-hoc fields.
- Enemy death (see existing enemy_system idea): on death, register a corpse with
  GroundItemManager and tag it with the enemy type id for later ransack roll.

Turn-based fit: nothing here is real-time. Looting is one action = one turn.
The inventory menu is a hard pause, like any roguelike pack screen. All spatial
queries (itemAt, corpseAt) are integer tile lookups — no float, no LOS needed
because the player must be ON or cardinally ADJACENT to the tile.

Constants to add (`Constants.java`):
- INVENTORY_SLOT_COUNT = 8 (placeholder)
- INVENTORY_MAX_WEIGHT = 40f (placeholder; only if weight enabled)
- LOOT_GROUND_ITEM_HEIGHT_FRACTION = 0.20f
- RANSACK_MAX_DROPS = 2
- AMMO_STACK_MAX_SHELLS = 60, _BULLETS = 200, _ROCKETS = 20, _CELLS = 120
  (placeholders, mirror Doom RPG ranges)

VISUAL DESIGN
=============
- Ground items: small flat billboards near the floor, color-coded by category —
  shells = brass/yellow, bullets = copper, rockets = olive, cells = cyan,
  medkit = white box with red cross, credits = pale green chip, keycards = the
  matching keycard color. A subtle 1-tile "shimmer" (brightness oscillation via
  the flickering-floor sine path) marks a fresh, uncollected item so it reads at
  a distance.
- Corpse states: fresh corpse uses the existing `m` decal at full color; once
  ransacked it desaturates ~40% and loses the shimmer, signaling "empty."
- Context prompt: drawn in the HUD left-panel status line in the CRT amber of
  the existing procedural HUD ("[F] RANSACK", "[F] PICK UP — SHELLS x6",
  "INVENTORY FULL" flashes red for ~0.4s of real time, purely cosmetic).
- TAB inventory overlay: an 8-slot grid centered on screen using the procedural
  HUD chrome panels (alpha ~0.9), each slot a square with the item glyph, a
  stack-count number bottom-right, item name on hover/selection, and a footer
  line listing weight used / max. Selected slot gets an amber border.

BALANCE NOTES
=============
- 8 slots is deliberately tight. If ammo lives in a separate uncapped pouch
  (recommended), slots are reserved for medkits, stims, keycards, weapons, and
  mods — making the squeeze about utility items, not bullets.
- Loot table weights (placeholder, per common zombie): 45% nothing useful (a few
  credits), 35% small ammo stack, 12% small medkit, 6% stimpack, 2% weapon mod.
  Tougher enemies shift the curve toward larger/rarer drops.
- Ransack should pay out enough to be tempting but not so much that grinding
  trash mobs trivializes the economy. Target: full clear of a floor yields
  roughly enough ammo to refill ~60% of expended rounds, never 100%.
- The free-hit cost of looting mid-combat is the natural balancer — keep loot
  generous and let the turn cost discipline the player.
- Flag ALL numbers above as placeholders pending playtest.

INTERACTIONS
============
1. Enemy system (enemy_system.txt): every enemy needs a loot-table id and must
   register a corpse on death. The ransack gamble interlocks with enemy turn
   timing — looting gives adjacent enemies a free action.
2. Medkit system (medkit-field-medicine-system.txt): medkits become inventory
   items. Using one from the TAB menu spends a turn and routes through that
   system's healing logic.
3. Keycard doors (uac-keycard-doors-security-tiers.txt): keycards occupy unique
   slots; the door check reads the Inventory instead of a boolean flag, so a
   dropped/lost keycard can actually lock you out (interesting tension).
4. Progression (levelup-stim-boon-choice-progression.txt): stims/boons can grant
   "+1 inventory slot" or "auto-collect ammo to pouch ignoring weight," turning
   inventory pressure itself into a build axis.
5. Weapon systems (shotgun, plasma-rifle): picking up a weapon you already own
   converts to ammo; weapon mods become rare ransack/loot rewards.

OPEN QUESTIONS
==============
- Does ammo consume inventory slots, or live in a separate uncapped pouch?
  (Recommendation: separate pouch — keeps slot pressure focused on choices.)
- Is there a weight system at all, or pure slot count? Weight adds depth but
  also UI burden; slot-only may be cleaner for a tight roguelike.
- Should ransacking require facing the corpse, or is standing-on-the-tile
  enough? Facing-adjacent is more "Doom RPG"; standing-on is simpler.
- Do unransacked corpses persist if the player leaves and returns, or decay
  after N turns? Decay adds urgency but may frustrate.
- Should a pre-placed `'$'` loot tile exist for level designers, or is all loot
  enemy/generation driven?
- Auto-pickup vs manual pickup for ammo/credits: auto reduces tedium but removes
  a (small) decision. Suggest auto for ammo/credits, manual for everything else.
