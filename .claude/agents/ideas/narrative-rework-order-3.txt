TITLE: Narrative rework 3 — The vocabulary ladder
---

PURPOSE
Fixes failure F2: nine undefined proper nouns in the first five minutes. Makes doctrine rule
D2 ("plain before proper") a mechanical property of the catalog rather than a thing writers
have to remember. Every term this fiction runs on gets ONE plain-language introduction, at a
moment the player can see the thing being named, and no channel may use the word before it.

READ FIRST
- order-1 (D2), order-2 (which already applies the ladder to the opening)
- assets/story/story-strings.properties (the offenders)
- narrative/BarkCatalog, StoryProgress (one-shot beat flags), CodexCatalog

============================================================
THE PATTERN — name the thing after you have described it
============================================================
Three steps, always in this order, and never more than one term per step:

  1. PLAIN — the thing is described in ordinary words, at the moment it is first relevant.
        "They keep a copy of you and grow a new body from it."
  2. NAMED — the word arrives attached to the description, once.
        "The paperwork calls that a reprint. You're on your second."
  3. USED — from then on the word is free, on every channel, including as jargon.
        "Print eleven. I know what that costs now."

Today the game starts at step 3 and never performs steps 1 and 2.

    story.term.erebus=The rock it's dug into is Erebus. Survey named it in an afternoon.
    story.term.contaminant=They call it the contaminant. That's the word on the form,

    story.term.yield=Yield is whatever these floors were cutting out and sending up.

Each intro line also unlocks its codex entry — the entries already exist and mostly already
carry these definitions in long form (story.codex.contaminant, .soul.reserve, .overseer, …).
This order changes nothing in the codex except which line unlocks it.

============================================================
ENFORCEMENT — data, not vigilance
============================================================
NEW HEADLESS TYPE: narrative/StoryTerm — an enum with, per value:
  - the term's match tokens (e.g. REPRINT -> {"reprint", "reprints", "print", "prints"});
  - the string id of its intro line;
  - the earliest StoryRegion its word may appear in;
  - the codex entry id it unlocks.
Plus narrative/StoryTermCatalog with one register() row per term. No switch statements; the
same registry discipline as every other catalog.

THE TEST (order-10 owns the file, this order owns the rule):
  For every row in every catalog (bark, exchange, boot card, framing chrome):
    resolve its text -> scan for term tokens -> for each hit, assert the row's region band
    does not start earlier than that term's "free from" region, and that the row is not the
    term's own intro line's predecessor on the same trigger.
  Case-insensitive, word-boundary matched, with an explicit ALLOW list for the intro lines
  themselves and for the codex (long text is where terms get explained, so the codex is
  exempt by construction).
The test reads the SHIPPED properties file, so a translator or a writer who reintroduces
"Cradle" into a Region-1 bark fails the build with the term named in the message.

WHY NOT A RUNTIME GATE: gating text at delivery time would mean lines silently vanishing in
play, which trades a comprehension bug for an invisible-content bug. The right time to catch
this is the build.

============================================================
THE SECOND HALF OF THE RULE — one new idea per panel
============================================================
A panel introduces AT MOST ONE unfamiliar thing. This is why order-2 splits the cold open into
three barks instead of one dense paragraph, and why the REPORT plate is taught on the second
death rather than the first. Concretely, for every row:
  - at most one term token from the table above (excluding terms already free two regions up);
  - at most one instruction (order-4's teaching lines obey this too);
  - never a term and an instruction in the same line unless the term IS the instruction.
LineQualityTest (order-10) counts tokens per row and fails rows carrying two.

DEVIATIONS FROM THE SPEC ABOVE, and why
  1. FIFTEEN TERMS, NOT SIXTEEN. "soul reserve" and "the reserve" are one row (RESERVE): the short
     form is a substring of the long one, so one token set covers both and two rows would have had
     to be kept in step by hand.
  2. RESERVE IS FREE FROM REGION 2, NOT REGION 3. The table above bands it to the Reliquary, but
     order-2 had ALREADY shipped "SOUL RESERVE ......... SUFFICIENT" on the reprint card's
     Harvesting-Galleries status block and given ORA two Galleries wake lines asking what the
     printers run on. Banding it to R3 would have meant undoing order-2's best mechanism (the
     wallpaper that becomes horrifying). So the WORD arrives in the Galleries — with a naming line
     that says only what it does mechanically (fuel, metered, finite) — and the Reliquary's cradle
     beat stays the reveal of what it is MADE OF. The ladder still holds; the rung just moved.
  3. THE OVERSEER LINE IS ORA, NOT SYSTEM. The spec annotated it (SYSTEM), but the line editorialises
     ("It's a desk, not a person") and docs/story-ui-system.txt states that SYSTEM lines never
     editorialize. Explaining what she is allowed to say IS ORA's charter (D6), so she says it.
  4. "operator" AND "the Organization" GOT NO NAMING LINE, as the spec's 13-line list implies. Both
     are introduced by USE, on the Region-1 briefing exchange — which is the Organization talking and
     addressing the player as the thing their file says they are. They borrow that line's string id.
  5. ONE NAMING PER MOMENT, which the spec did not specify. Without it, "the Deepworks" and "Erebus"
     would have arrived in the same breath on floor one, breaking the order's own second-half rule.
     requestNextIntro() therefore stops at the first UNNAMED term for the trigger, which spreads the
     namings across floors and regions with no counter and no conditional.
