THE DIAGNOSIS (why the current balance fails)
---------------------------------------------
The systems exist. They are tuned so FLAT that no decision matters. Three
structural problems, not one wrong number:

  P1  POWER GROWTH IS TOO SHALLOW ON BOTH SIDES.
      Weapon level = +10% damage (linear), enemy HP = x1.042 per floor, rarity by
      contract "never raises the power band" (R-WEAPON), and every level-up card
      is budget-EQUAL (R-CARD, 12 PP). A start weapon on floor 5 is ~1.2x slower
      to kill with than an on-level one: nobody notices. The contract made
      "nothing matters" a RULE.
  P2  FLOOR CONTENTS ARE INCONSISTENT BETWEEN FLOORS AND GENERATORS.
      Three generators place loot with three different codes (CavernGenerator only
      places 'H' inside a MEDICAL_BAY chamber that may not exist; LevelGenerator
      hard-codes a 0.70 stim chance; each uses per-room independent chances), so
      supply swings from overflow to nothing. Enemy COUNT is depth-invariant by
      construction and levels are large, so floors are empty and early floors are
      boring.
  P3  ENEMIES AND WEAPONS ARE TOO ALIKE.
      No damage classes, no enemy traits, no weapon-vs-enemy matchups; the only
      telegraphed attacks do drain-sized damage, so GUARD and armour never matter;
      toxic/beam/weaken are too small to feel; hazards deal flat damage that goes
      stale with depth. Automatic rifles at range dominate because nothing
      punishes them and nothing rewards the close-range risk.


WHAT THE OWNER WANTS (targets the series is measured against)
-------------------------------------------------------------
  T1  Levelling up, choosing and finding better weapons MATTER (K4 — the goal).
  T2  Falling behind is punished: a level-1 weapon cannot carry a floor-3 map; a
      higher-level weapon is MUCH stronger; enemy HP scales with it (B3, E4).
  T3  Floors are full: more enemies, real groups, a fight within the first steps
      of floor 1 (B1, B6, E5, I3, I4).
  T4  Supply is CONSISTENT: no overflow floors, no zero-heal floors, cave floors
      included. Deaths read as "my mistake", not "no resources" (B5, F3, A3).
  T5  Some attacks are strong enough that they MUST be avoided — telegraphed,
      communicated better (F1, I5). Toxic / beams / weaken / hazards matter (I6,
      I7). GUARD and armour are useful (F5).
  T6  Weapons have roles: shotguns worth the risk, the Incinerator viable, and
      switching weapon per enemy is worth it AND communicated (H1-H5).
  T7  Different runs build different characters (J3, H4).
  T8  A run is 25 floors then the game ENDS; a boss every 5 floors; floors 10-15
      very challenging but fair for a prepared player; first boss reachable
      without many attempts; mostly skill + resource management (C3-C5, D3).
  T9  Shops are affordable (D6). Elite / combat / cache feel different (D9).
  T10 Tools the owner can use ON THE PHONE: a per-floor/per-run stats screen he
      can copy, dev start-at-depth, dev master tuning dials (K3, A4).
  Blank answers (Part G ammo, J2 cards) are filled from playtest logs in order 8.


THE SERIES (build strictly in this order)
-----------------------------------------
  order-1  Phone dev tools: run log, dev start, tuning dials   (T10)
           Everything after it can be verified on the phone.
  order-2  The Power Ladder: levels and rarity that matter      (T1, T2)
           Steep weapon level/rarity, enemy scaling tied to it, level-gap
           penalty, rebased TTK, % heals, 25-floor audit horizon, affordable
           weapon level-ups.
  order-3  Full floors, fair supply: density + supply planner   (T3, T4, T9 part)
           Group templates, density by area, smaller early maps, ONE budgeted
           supply planner for every generator, guaranteed heal floor, COMBAT /
           ELITE / CACHE contents made distinct.
  order-4  Weapon roles and matchups, told to the player        (T6)
           Damage classes, enemy traits, matchup table, shotgun / incinerator
           identity, per-ammo economy, RESISTED/WEAK POINT feedback, switch hint.
  order-5  Dodge or die: heavy attacks, guard, armour, status   (T5)
           Lethal telegraphed HEAVY verbs, danger-tile telegraphs, guard riposte,
           armour as the status shield, status effects and hazards that scale.
  order-6  Builds that differ: perks, keystones, real rarity    (T7, T1)
           Tagged perks replace budget-equal cards; keystones at 5/10/15/20;
           abilities that are felt.
  order-7  The 25-floor run: finite descent, bosses, economy    (T8, T9)
           Region E "The Core", final boss at 25 ends the run, boss table and
           consistency, region difficulty curve, shop prices, win-rate bands.
  order-8  Feel pass from phone playtest logs                   (all; G, J2)
           Owner plays with order-1 tools; Claude reads logs, tunes dials, bakes
           them into BalanceConfig, re-bands the sim.

WHY THIS ORDER: tooling first so every later order can be checked on the phone.
The power ladder is the owner's top priority and every later number (density
TTK, supply demand, matchup multipliers, perk values, boss HP) is expressed
against the on-curve player it defines. Density/supply comes next because it
consumes the ladder's demand numbers and is the second-loudest complaint. Roles
and matchups (4) need density (groups make AoE weapons and traits readable).
Heavy attacks (5) need the matchup layer (shotgun cancels wind-ups). Builds (6)
need weapon classes and traits to tag against. Run shape (7) re-derives bosses
and the economy against the finished player model. The feel pass (8) is last by
definition.

Each order leaves the game building, playable and with both balance gates
green against ITS rules. Between orders the game may be mid-rebalance (e.g.
after order-2 the ladder is live but floors are still sparse); that is expected
and is why order-1 ships the dev dials first.


SERIES-WIDE CONVENTIONS
-----------------------
  - Every new number lands in util/BalanceConfig.java (new SECTION per order);
    every formula in util/GameMath.java with the Formula/Derivation/Edge-case
    block; content is register() rows, never a switch.
  - The audit horizon for every depth-swept rule becomes 1..RUN_FINAL_DEPTH (25),
    introduced in order-2.
  - The expected ("on-curve") player is ONE model, defined in order-2
    (GameMath.expectedPlayerAtDepth), and every later order prices against it.
  - Tests: only BalanceAuditTest / BalanceSimTest are extended; non-balance code
    (UI, renderers, narrative strings, dev tools) ships with NO new tests.
    Existing tests whose asserted behaviour legitimately changes are UPDATED in
    the same checkpoint (e.g. LevelGeneratorSnapshotTest, RouteMapGeneratorTest).
  - Docs: docs/game-balance-authority.txt and docs/game-balance-knowledge.txt are
    updated in the same checkpoint as any rule change; the domain doc of any
    touched subsystem likewise (route, generation, enemy, weapon, story UI).


OVERRIDE CLAUSE (series-wide; repeated verbatim in intent in every order)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The owner explicitly authorises this series to OVERRIDE ANY previously added
balance rule, band, waiver, policy, anchor or schema entry — util/BalanceSchema
rules / bands / waivers, util/BalanceConfig bands and anchors (SECTION 9 etc.),
sim/BehavioralBands S-* bands, sim policies, and every decision in the earlier
balance_order_*, new-game-balancr-order-* and game-difficulty-rebalance-fair-
challenge idea files — in favour of this series. When an order overrides an
older rule it must, in the SAME checkpoint:
  (a) name the rule / band / waiver being replaced;
  (b) state the new rule;
  (c) update BalanceSchema, BalanceAuditTest, BalanceSimTest,
      docs/game-balance-authority.txt and docs/game-balance-knowledge.txt so
      both gates stay green against the NEW rule.
It NEVER permits deleting, disabling, @Ignore-ing or weakening a test to make a
change pass: tests are UPDATED to assert the new rule (the Testing Policy allows
updating an existing test when behaviour legitimately changes, and allows
extending the balance-scope tests). Non-balance design rules (tile grid,
turn-based, cardinal-only ranged attacks, touch-only, one difficulty — see
order-1 on dev dials) are NOT overridable by this clause.
