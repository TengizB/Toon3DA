STATUS: NOT IMPLEMENTED
CATEGORY: Visual
TITLE: Story UI 9 — Atmosphere & mood: audio + hue, zero gameplay impact
---

PURPOSE
The mood bible. Defines the overall FEELING of the game and how atmosphere carries the
story as much as the words do. This is cross-cutting: it informs order-1 (visual language),
order-8 (framing screens), the environment art, and audio. Read it before making any
audiovisual mood decision. It is direction, not a single screen.

It is also, deliberately, the most CONSTRAINED order in the series. Everything below is a
coat of paint and a soundtrack. It does not touch the game.

============================================================
THE FOUR HARD CONSTRAINTS (read before anything else)
============================================================
These are not preferences. Any proposal in this file that conflicts with one of them is
wrong and gets cut, not negotiated. They exist because mood work has exactly one failure
mode: it starts as a filter and ends as a balance change nobody signed off on.

C1 — ENEMY PLACEMENT IS UNTOUCHABLE.
     No change to where enemies spawn, how many spawn, which archetypes spawn, spawn
     density per room/floor/region, wake radius, aggression, or AI. `EnemyManager`,
     `EnemyConstants`, `GameBalance`, `LevelGenConstants` enemy-count params and every
     enemy spawn symbol in every level file are OUT OF SCOPE for this order. Full stop.

C2 — LONELINESS IS NEVER BOUGHT WITH EMPTY ROOMS.
     "Emptier level, fewer things in it" is the lazy version of this pillar and it is
     BANNED. A room stripped of enemies is not atmospheric, it is a worse room: it removes
     decisions, drains tension and quietly nerfs the floor. Loneliness in this game is a
     SOUND and a VOICE problem, not a population problem — see PILLAR 1 for the six
     techniques that are actually allowed.

C3 — THE GAME NEVER GETS DARKER.
     The player must be able to see, at all times and at least as clearly as today: walls,
     doors, floor markings, props, hazards, items on the ground, and above all ENEMIES —
     including the ranged ones on the player's cardinal line, which is life-or-death
     information. Mood is expressed through HUE and SATURATION at constant perceived
     brightness. Never through luminance. The specific numbers this order may not lower
     are listed in the LEGIBILITY CONTRACT below.

C4 — ZERO GAMEPLAY AND ZERO DIFFICULTY IMPACT.
     Nothing here may change damage, health, ammo, drop rates, XP, sight lines, field of
     view, ray reach, turn costs, or any decision the player makes. The proof obligation
     is mechanical, not a promise: see VERIFICATION at the bottom. If a mood idea can only
     land by changing a number that affects play, the idea is deleted.

THE INTENDED FEELING (one line)
Lonely, wrong, and quietly grieving — cosmic horror by RESTRAINT, not gore. The dread comes
from realizing the "monster" is a victim and you are its betrayer, not from jump scares.

============================================================
THE FIVE PILLARS OF MOOD (each rewritten to obey C1–C4)
============================================================
1. LONELINESS — you are one small thing, deep, alone but for a voice in your ear and a
   voice in your head. Delivered ONLY by these means:
     a) The drone and the silence around it (see SOUND). Loneliness is 80% audio.
     b) ORA is the only companion, and she is not there. The story channels already
        establish that every other voice reaches you through a speaker, a terminal, or
        your own skull — nobody is standing next to you and nobody ever will be.
     c) There are no allies, no rescued NPCs, no friendly faces, no chatter channel, and
        there must never be. The absence of a companion mechanic IS the pillar.
     d) The crew is present only as PAPERWORK — terminal logs, ORA's take on them, codex
        entries. You are always reading the words of people who are already gone.
     e) Room ACOUSTICS, not room contents: reverb tail and footstep spacing sell "this
        space is big and you are the only thing in it" without removing one enemy.
     f) The reprint counter. The number climbing is the loneliest object in the game.
   NOT delivered by: fewer enemies, emptier rooms, longer corridors, bigger levels,
   removed props, or reduced loot. (C1, C2)

2. WRONGNESS — something is subtly off before you can name it: a held note under the
   machinery, a corridor that feels watched, a light that shifts hue when the planet is
   near. Carried by sound and colour temperature, never by hiding things from the player.

3. RESTRAINT — silence and space do the work. The game is not a constant audio assault;
   horror lands because it is rare and earned. Restraint here means the MIX gets quiet,
   never that the FLOOR gets empty. (C2)

4. GRIEF — deep down the mood turns from fear to sorrow. The Wound and Core should feel
   less like a monster's lair and more like standing inside something in pain. Expressed
   in warm organic hues, the heartbeat under the drone, and ORA's already-written tone
   shift — not in dimming the lights.

5. THE TURN (mood tracks the story's grey) — it STARTS almost mundane and corporate (false
   safety, fluorescent normalcy) and CURDLES as the truth surfaces. Atmosphere is part of
   "the grey is staged, not stated": the world stops feeling like a job and starts feeling
   like a crime. The curdle is a colour and audio journey from COLD-CLEAN to WARM-ORGANIC
   at flat brightness — the palette changes character, not exposure.

============================================================
MOOD PROGRESSION BY REGION (hue, sound — brightness stays flat)
============================================================
Read every row as: same amount of light, different colour of light, different sound.
  Region 1 Habitation Rings — cold clean fluorescent; desaturated corporate greys and
     blues; hum of failing machinery; almost ordinary. False safety.
  Region 2 Harvesting Galleries — wet, organic, industrial-abattoir; sickly reds and meat
     tones creeping into the grey; dripping, the same low note the specimens scream on.
  Region 3 The Reliquary — sterile but humming with power; sharp clean whites over the
     Cradle banks (this region is the BRIGHTEST, and that is the horror — a well-lit
     abattoir reads worse than a dark one); the drone gains a pulse.
  Region 4 The Wound — no longer facility; raw living tissue, drills in flesh; deep reds
     and bioluminescent teals, a slow heartbeat felt more than heard; the light now reads
     as coming from a body rather than from a fixture, at the same intensity as Region 1.
  Region 5 The Core — overwhelming, intimate, vast; you are INSIDE the mind. Sound and
     light resolve into something almost tender. Awe over terror. Bright, not dark.

============================================================
COLOUR & LIGHT DIRECTION
============================================================
- Global drift: cold/desaturated at the top → warm/saturated organic at the bottom. The
  player should feel they have left "facility" and entered "body". This is a CHROMA
  journey. Total luminance across the five regions stays within a narrow band (see the
  LEGIBILITY CONTRACT); measure it, do not eyeball it.
- Reuse and extend existing systems, do not reinvent: `EffectConstants` (ambient light,
  vignette, transitions), `RenderConstants` red-alert pulse, `WallRenderer` distance
  shading, `FloorCeilingRenderer` backdrop, `HitVignetteRenderer`.
- Add per-region ambient TINT parameters (hue/saturation multipliers) to
  `RenderConstants`/`EffectConstants` — never hardcoded, and never a brightness multiplier
  below 1.0. If a region wants to feel heavier, it shifts hue and gains saturation; it
  does not lose light.
- Region tint applies to the WORLD BACKDROP layer only — walls, floor, ceiling. It is NOT
  applied to enemy sprites, enemy health bars, item pickups, hazard props, the HUD, or any
  story panel. Those keep their existing colour so contrast against the tinted world goes
  UP, not down. A red enemy in a red region must be MORE readable after this order than
  before it, never less.
- The old draft of this file contained the line "darkness is a tool". It has been deleted
  and is not coming back. Limited sight lines, shadow and "not-far-enough light" are not
  available techniques here: every one of them is a stealth difficulty change. (C3, C4)

============================================================
SOUND & MUSIC (this is where most of the mood actually lives)
============================================================
Audio does the work that darkness is not allowed to do. Budget accordingly: if this order
ships with only the audio half built, it has still delivered most of its value.
- A LOW PLANETARY DRONE is the spine. Faint at the top, deepening with descent; deep down
  it carries a slow heartbeat/pulse. When the planet is "near" (before it speaks), the
  drone swells and the world quiets around it.
- SILENCE is used deliberately — cut the mix to near-nothing in key moments; let footsteps
  and the drone breathe. Never wall-to-wall music.
- Per-voice audio motifs (ties to order-7 Part D speaker stings, reusing `StoryAudio` —
  never a second audio owner): ORA warm/human, planet low/organic, Organization
  cold/electronic.
- Enemy-family sound identity: undead (human, wrong), insect (that shared scream-note),
  machine (cold servo/alarm), demon/golem (deep, planetary, pained — NOT generic monster
  roars; they should sound like the planet, because they are). Audio identity ONLY: no
  change to enemy stats, AI, or where they stand. (C1)
- An enemy's sound may only ADD information, never replace it. A sound cue is a bonus for
  players with audio on; the same enemy stays exactly as visible as it is today for a
  player with the phone muted. Nothing in this game may be audio-only.
- Music is minimal and ambient; it SWELLS only at story beats and the Core. No
  action-movie score; this is dread and grief, not spectacle.

============================================================
SCREEN EFFECTS IN SERVICE OF DREAD (subtle, never cheap, never in the way)
============================================================
- Subtle film grain / low-light noise for unease. Off by default is acceptable; togglable
  is mandatory. Grain amplitude is capped so a 1×1 tile feature never becomes ambiguous.
- "Wrongness" cues when the planet reaches for you: a brief hue/desaturation shift, a sign
  reading wrong for a moment, the drone catching. Hard rules: rare; never a flash-bang;
  never a full-screen luminance drop; never applied to enemy sprites or the HUD; never
  fired while any enemy is awake (reuse the `BarkSystem.setCombatSpike()` signal that
  already exists rather than inventing a second combat detector); always suppressed by the
  existing "reduced motion" setting.
- Deep down, a heartbeat-synced vignette pulse (reuse `HitVignetteRenderer` / red-alert
  pulse machinery). Gentle, edge-only, and its peak alpha must sit BELOW the existing
  low-HP vignette floor so it can never be mistaken for a damage cue or mask one.
- Combat screen-shake/impact (`ImpactEffectSystem`) is unchanged by this order. It must
  not disturb story panels (order-1) or become the dominant mood — restraint holds even in
  fights.

============================================================
PACING OF DREAD (a MIX and STORY-CHANNEL rhythm, not an encounter rhythm)
============================================================
- Alternate tension and lull IN THE AUDIO AND THE STORY CHANNELS. After an intense fight
  or a heavy reveal, the mix opens up and the drone comes forward. The lull is a quiet
  soundtrack over the same fight the game was always going to give you.
- This order NEVER inserts a lull by removing, delaying, or thinning an encounter, and
  never changes floor length or room count to make room for a quiet stretch. (C1, C2, C4)
- The encounter rhythm is already owned elsewhere — the route map and the balance
  simulator. This file rides that rhythm; it does not author it.
- Do not stack scares. The scariest tool available to us is a quiet room that is fully lit
  and still feels occupied.
- Let the boot card / reprint moments be small pockets of calm-dread, not punishment.

============================================================
THE LEGIBILITY CONTRACT (the concrete floor under C3)
============================================================
Mood is a coat of paint over a readable game, never a fog over it. Concretely, this order
may NOT lower any of the following, in any region, under any effect, at any moment:
  - `RenderConstants.WALL_SHADE_MIN_BRIGHTNESS`
  - `RenderConstants.ENEMY_SPRITE_SHADE_MIN_BRIGHTNESS` (may only go UP)
  - `RenderConstants.MAX_LIGHTING_SHADE`
  - `RenderConstants.COLUMN_SHADE_MIN`
  - the floor/ceiling ambient colours' luminance
  - `Constants.RAY_MAX_LENGTH_CELLS` or `PLAYER_FIELD_OF_VIEW_DEGREES` — these are
    gameplay reach, not mood (C4)
and may NOT raise any always-on vignette alpha during `RunPhase.PLAYING`.
  - Story-UI panels always stay legible over any atmosphere (dark panel behind text,
    order-1) — atmosphere never bleeds under a panel.
  - Enemy health bars, the HUD, ground items and the mini-map are never tinted, dimmed,
    grained, or vignetted by this order.
  - Nothing critical is ever conveyed by colour alone; a hue shift is always redundant
    with something already drawn or written.

============================================================
ACCESSIBILITY
============================================================
- Settings, alongside order-6's existing narrative knobs: reduce grain/noise, reduce
  screen-shake, reduce flashing-adjacent effects, reduce colour intensity (drops region
  tint toward neutral for colour-vision comfort), master/music/ambient volume, and
  "reduced motion".
- Mood degrades gracefully: with every effect minimized and audio off, the game is fully
  playable, fully legible and exactly as difficult. That is the acceptance bar for the
  whole order — if turning the atmosphere off changes how hard the game is, the
  atmosphere was doing gameplay work it was never allowed to do.
- No photosensitivity hazards: no rapid full-screen luminance oscillation, ever.

============================================================
WHAT THIS ORDER ACTUALLY TOUCHES (scope fence)
============================================================
MAY touch:  `RenderConstants` / `EffectConstants` (new region tint + grain + pulse params),
            `FloorCeilingRenderer` and `WallRenderer` colour application, a new region-mood
            renderer/overlay if one is needed, `render/StoryAudio` (extended, not replaced),
            `docs/story-ui-system.txt`, the accessibility settings surface.
MUST NOT touch: `enemy/**`, `EnemyConstants`, `GameBalance`, `BalanceConfig`, `sim/**`,
            `level/LevelGenerator` + `LevelGenConstants`, `route/**` economics, any
            `assets/levels/*.txt`, weapon stats, item drop tables, `PlayerController`,
            `Constants` FOV/ray reach.

============================================================
VERIFICATION (how "no gameplay impact" is proven, not promised)
============================================================
1. `./gradlew test` passes, and the balance audit's numbers are byte-identical to the
   pre-change run.
2. `./gradlew balanceSim` produces an IDENTICAL report to the pre-change run for the same
   seed matrix — same TTK, same deaths, same band results. A single differing number means
   this order leaked into gameplay and must be reverted to the point where it does not.
3. The diff touches no file in the MUST NOT TOUCH list above. This is checkable by reading
   the diff's file list alone and should be checked that way.
4. Side-by-side screenshots per region, before/after, with mean-luminance sampled: the
   after-image is never darker than the before-image, and enemy-against-wall contrast is
   equal or higher in every region.
5. A run played with all atmosphere settings minimized is indistinguishable, in every
   gameplay-relevant respect, from the game as it exists today.

============================================================
DEFINITION OF DONE
============================================================
- Each region reads with its intended palette and sound; the drone deepens with descent and
  swells when the planet nears; silence and lulls are used; mood curdles from
  corporate-normal to grieving-body across the descent.
- Brightness is flat across the descent and no region is darker than the game is today;
  enemies, hazards and items are at least as readable in every region as they are now.
- Enemy placement, counts, AI and every balance number are provably untouched (VERIFICATION
  1–3), and the loneliness pillar is carried entirely by audio, voice and absence-of-allies
  (PILLAR 1 a–f) — never by a thinner floor.
- All effects are togglable, degrade gracefully to a fully legible game, and never harm
  readability or fairness.
- `docs/story-ui-system.txt` updated in the same commit.
